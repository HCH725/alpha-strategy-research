## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The paper defines information leakage as "news released about an upcoming green bond issue before the issue is publicly announced by the issuing firm," disseminated "through different communication channels (e.g. news media) to influence market behaviours before formal corporate announcements." Two stated objectives: (i) equity market reaction to green bond announcements, and (ii) whether firms "leverage positive investor sentiment toward green bonds and leak green bond-related news early." The authors motivate firm-level leaking with Sletten (2012) — managerial desire to maintain a high stock price — and note that Bloomberg News / Bloomberg First Word screens "often contain anonymous 'tips'" that carry the ticker, so privileged information is broadcast to Bloomberg Professional subscribers before the corporate release.

Reported channel interpretation: population-level price impact is initially muted but turns significantly negative one trading week after the leak; the negative reaction is **concentrated in financial issuers** and in **leaks arriving pre-market or within the first hour of trading**, while non-financial leaks are reacted to positively (non-significantly). Explanations offered by the authors: (a) the announcement of new debt financing triggers adverse-selection pricing (Myers–Majluf 1984; Miller–Rock 1985), so investors read a green-bond issue as a negative signal about profitability/financial stability rather than a sustainability commitment; (b) for financial issuers, regulation constrains the "transformative" use of green proceeds, so the same news is read as a negative signal; (c) for non-financials, higher Tobin's Q and free cash flow make the issue read as a positive transformation opportunity; (d) timing follows Patell–Wolfson (1982) — favourable news is released during trading hours, unfavourable news outside them; (e) volume spikes confirm information asymmetry being resolved (Chae 2005; Lei–Wang 2014). The authors explicitly ask why arbitrageurs do not trade the pattern and answer with Shleifer–Vishny (1997) limits-to-arbitrage: heterogeneous, geographically dispersed, illiquid equities and noisy payoffs. They also state that **"establishing the specific diffusive effect of leaked news is difficult"** and that **pre-leakage leakage cannot be ruled out**.

Scale (source-reported, §5 + Appendix C): the leaks found **account for 7.5% of all green bond announcements made by publicly traded firms over the study period** (our count: 259 / 3,462 = 7.48%, consistent).

### Research interpretation

Hypothesis in falsifiable form: when material, issuer-specific information appears on a publicly readable wire **before the issuer's own announcement**, informed trading produces a **short-horizon negative drift on the named issuer's equity**, conditional on (i) issuer sector being a financial institution and (ii) the headline landing pre-market or in the first trading hour; the effect is accompanied by an abnormal volume spike and is **substantially reversed over days 11–21**, which implies the tradable window is short and that a hold past ~10 trading days destroys the edge. This is a *pre-announcement information-asymmetry / limits-to-arbitrage* mechanism, not an earnings-drift, forced-flow or sentiment mechanism.

Component roles (source-reported unless marked):
- **Regime / context:** none printed by the source; the sample itself is regime-concentrated (our count from Figure 3: 2016–2022 leak counts 7 / 19 / 13 / 30 / 45 / 99 / 46; 45+99 = 144 of 259 = 55.6% of leaks fall in 2020–2021, matching the §4.1.1 statement "56% of the leaks were recorded in 2020-2021").
- **Primary signal (source-reported):** a qualifying leak headline — Bloomberg News or Bloomberg First Word, containing `"Mandate"` AND `"Green"`, carrying a Bloomberg-assigned `Green Bond` article label, with a primary ticker attached, timestamped **at least one business day before** the firm's public green-bond announcement and with that announcement occurring **within two months** of the headline.
- **Confirmation filters (source-reported as sample screens, not as trade filters):** issuer is a public ultimate parent; ≥ "multiple" trade orders in the hour after the leak (the word "multiple" is never quantified — `underspecified`); 24-hour volume after the leak > 10,000 units; factor coverage available (90% of the surviving sample).
- **Reported conditioning splits (source-reported):** financial vs non-financial issuer; early-market (pre-market or first trading hour, n=120) vs late-market (after the first hour, n=118).
- **Exit / risk logic:** **none printed.** `[0,1] … [0,10]`, `[11,21]`, `[21,30]` are **event-study measurement windows**, not holding rules; the significant positive `[11,21]` cell means any tradable exit choice is `research-proposed`.
- No source claim assigns alpha to any component separately; ablation is left to falsification.

## Signal

**Source-reported (event-study construction, not a trading rule).**
- Event dates: **day 0 = timestamp of the leaked headline** (leak event) or the **public announcement date** (announcement event). Headline timestamps are compared with exchange opening hours; day-of-week is reported in GMT (Figure 3, left).
- Leak classification (§2.2): search `Mandate` + `Green` + `Green Bond` label on Bloomberg News / Bloomberg First Word -> 2,036 candidate headlines -> **1,197** after removing duplicates and follow-up "update" articles (earliest retained) -> **1,050** with a primary ticker -> keep those where the same ticker announces an upcoming green bond **within two months** and the headline is **≥ 1 business day before** the announcement -> **259 leaks**.
- Sample ladder: 259 leaks -> 3 firms removed (IPO within the prior three years) -> **256** (Table 1) -> liquidity screens remove 18 -> **238** (§2.4, Appendix C) -> market loading factors available for **214** (90% coverage) -> **214 events from 159 companies across 23 countries** (§2.4).
- Abnormal return models (§3.1): market-adjusted `AR_i,t = R_i,t − R_m,t` (Brown–Warner 1985, country-specific market index), plus **CAPM (Eq. 2), Fama–French 3-factor (Eq. 3) and Carhart 4-factor (Eq. 4)** as robustness; factor loadings from CRSP/French (2023) with **hand-collected loadings for South Africa, China and India**.
- Event windows (§3.1.2): announcement day 0 -> baseline `[-10,10]` with estimation window `[-310,-11]`; **leak day 0 -> baseline `[0,10]` with estimation window `[-300,-1]`**; both extended to `[11,21]` and `[21,30]` following Flammer (2021).
- Intraday measurement: market-adjusted returns binned every **5 minutes across the first 8 trading hours** (Table 2), with the qualifying-instrument count falling as the horizon lengthens (n 238 -> 23 by hour 8).
- Abnormal volume (§3.2, Jansen 2015): `log turnover = ln(trading volume / shares outstanding)`; intraday baseline = average 5-minute volume over the **96 bins (8 trading hours) preceding the leak**; daily baseline = average daily volume over the **month before** the release (T = 20).
- Regression design (Table 7): `CAR[0,1]` and `CAR[0,2]` regressed on bond characteristics (issue size, term, coupon, embedded option, number of bonds) and firm characteristics (first-time issuer, ROA, debt/equity, free cash flow, Tobin's Q, market cap, total assets), with time-of-day, day-of-week, year and region controls; n = 100 (non-financials) and 114 (financials).

**Underspecified in the source (must not be read as reproducible):** no entry price, no entry time-of-day convention relative to the headline timestamp, no position sizing, no weighting, no holding-period rule, no exit, no re-entry, **no shorting or borrow treatment at all**, no definition of "multiple trade orders", no stated variance estimator (Tables 2–3 print `(std. err.)` and Tables 4–6 print `t-stat` without naming the estimator — clustered, robust or otherwise), **no stated tail convention for the stars/p-values** (see Evidence: our count), no tail/outlier treatment for the CARs, and no statement of whether day 0 returns are close-to-close or headline-to-close.

**Research-proposed tradable skeleton (NOT source-reported).** `research-proposed`: on a qualifying leak headline, **short the named issuer at the first tradable price after the headline timestamp is observable** (pre-market headline -> next session open; intraday headline -> next 5-minute bar close), equal notional per event, exit at the close of trading day **2** (variant: day **10**, with an explicit hard exit before day **11** because of the printed `[11,21]` reversal), no leverage, participation capped at **10% ADV** (`research-defined`), cost ladder as in F3. Borrow availability across the 23-country universe is an unmodelled constraint. This skeleton exists only so the falsification plan has something concrete to attack; the source neither prints nor endorses it.

## Required data

- **Signal source (proprietary):** Bloomberg Terminal `Bloomberg News` and `Bloomberg First Word` headline archive with **exact publication timestamps**, primary ticker tags, and the Bloomberg `Green Bond` article classification, plus the search operators `Mandate` and `Green`. Not redistributable; a replication requires terminal access.
- **Event universe (proprietary):** Bloomberg fixed-income database records for all active/matured bonds flagged `Green Bond / Loan`, announced 2007-05-22 → 2023-01-29 (7,375 bonds; 3,462 with a public ultimate parent; 2,631 same-day-condensed announcement events), including the **public announcement date** used to define the leak.
- **Fundamentals:** S&P Capital IQ Pro — total assets, ROA, debt/equity, free cash flow, market capitalisation (three-year averages, or trailing 12-month figures when the announcement falls in the first 45 days of the year).
- **Market fields:** intraday **5-minute** bars and volume for the first 8 trading hours, daily OHLCV, shares outstanding, per-exchange trading calendar and timezone (leaks are timed against exchange opening hours; day-of-week reported in GMT).
- **Benchmark data:** country-specific market indices for 23 countries; CRSP / Ken French factor returns; hand-collected loadings for South Africa, China and India.
- **Point-in-time / availability:** the headline timestamp is the honest availability time, **but the source's own leak label is retrospective** (it requires a green-bond announcement within the following two months) — using the label as a live signal is look-ahead and is therefore `research-proposed` to rebuild from headline text alone.
- **Missing-data behavior as printed:** 3 firms dropped for short listing history; 18 dropped on liquidity screens; 24 dropped for missing market loadings (238 -> 214); Appendix C marks many exchanges "Not Covered" for factor loadings (e.g. Abu Dhabi, Chile, Colombia, Peru, Argentina, Turkey, Botswana, Bratislava, Budapest, Bursa Malaysia, Dow Jones Industrial Average, Euronext Dublin/Lisbon, First North Stockholm, Indonesia, Johannesburg, KOSPI, Mexico, Namibia, Vilnius, Iceland, Nigeria, Philippine, Qatar, Sao Paulo, Saudi, Taiwan, Tel Aviv, Thailand) while only US exchanges, Tokyo, Shanghai, Toronto are marked "Exact".

### Negative evidence

1. **No cost, fill, borrow, capacity, turnover or P&L model exists** (scan above); every printed number is gross and un-costed, and the implied trade is a **short** on equities in 23 countries with no borrow treatment anywhere.
2. **The source is an event study, not a strategy.** There is no entry, exit, sizing, holding, stop or portfolio construction anywhere in the pinned text (`backtest`, `portfolio`, `position` all 0 hits); the entire trading layer is `research-proposed`.
3. **Population intraday impact is entirely insignificant.** Table 2 Panel A (n = 238) shows no significant price change at any horizon; significance exists only inside the sector/timing splits, i.e. the headline effect is conditional, not general.
4. **The daily response is delayed and mostly insignificant.** For leaked news, day 0 (p 0.341) and day 1 (p 0.175) are insignificant; only day 2 reaches 5% (−0.21%, p 0.014) under the CAPM, and days 3–10 are all insignificant.
5. **The drift reverses.** `[0,10]` −0.58% / −0.78% is followed by **`[11,21]` +0.72% / +0.86%** (both significant at 5%), which the source reads as "a significant mid-term reversal … suggesting an initial overreaction." Our count: `[0,21]` net ≈ **+0.08 pp**, so any hold past day 10 erases the effect.
6. **Anticipation contaminates day 0.** Footnote 13 documents `[−3,−1]` cumulative abnormal returns of −34 bp / −38 bp before the public announcement, and §5 states that "the presence of pre-leakage leakages cannot be ruled out" — the printed leak timestamp may not be the first information time, so the measured post-leak drift can understate (or duplicate) pre-existing drift.
7. **Direction depends on issuer sector.** Non-financial leaks are reacted to positively (non-significantly); only financial issuers show a persistent negative intraday reaction. A blanket "short the leaker" rule is not supported by the printed split.
8. **Timing flips the sign.** Late-market leaks produce **significant positive** intraday returns (1 h +12.80 bp**) and significant volume *drops*, the opposite of the early-market pattern; a single undifferentiated rule would be wrong half the time (n = 118 vs 120).
9. **Significance is thin and one-tailed.** Many cells carry a single star at 10%; **all six financial-issuer F-stats in Table 7 are insignificant**; the p-values we recomputed are one-tailed while the source states no tail convention.
10. **Zero multiplicity control and no stated variance estimator.** Tables 1–7 plus Appendices A–D print many windows x four return models x two issuer splits x two timing splits with `Bonferroni`, `Benjamini`, `multiple testing`, `deflated`, `bootstrap`, `cluster`, `Newey`, `heterosked` all **0 hits**; `point-in-time`, `look-ahead`, `out-of-sample`, `placebo`, `robust` (as a design word) are likewise absent (`robustness` appears once, meaning "use FF3/Carhart instead of market-adjusted").
11. **Retrospective signal definition (look-ahead by construction).** A headline only counts as a leak if the firm announces a green bond *within the following two months*; that label is unavailable in real time. Any tradable reading must be rebuilt from the headline text alone (`research-proposed`), and the source does not report how that degraded signal would perform.
12. **Sample concentration and regime dependence.** Our count from Figure 3: **55.6% of leaks fall in 2020–2021** (COVID-era, when §4.1.1 notes "debt financing was encouraged"), **38.2% in 2021 alone**, and **59.1% on Monday/Tuesday**; by venue, our count of Appendix C shows **93 of 259 leaks (35.9%) come from just three markets** — NASDAQ OMX Stockholm (50, of which 46 passed the liquidity screen), Tokyo (26, 21 passing) and NYSE/Nasdaq combined (17, 15 passing) — while many exchanges have no factor coverage at all.
13. **Liquidity screens and factor coverage select the sample.** 3 + 18 firms removed for listing history and liquidity, then 24 more for missing loadings (256 -> 238 -> 214); "multiple trade orders in the hour" is never quantified, so the screen itself is `underspecified`.
14. **Source-internal count and source-attribution conflicts** (recorded in frontmatter `contradictions`): the Introduction's **231 leaks from 170 firms** never reconciles with 259 / 256 / 238, and Table A1 attributes global-sample CARs to **CRSP**.
15. **Source quality / reproducibility:** preprint only (arXiv + SSRN working paper), no peer-review statement, **no data-availability, code or replication statement**, proprietary Bloomberg and S&P Capital IQ Pro inputs, `CC BY-NC-ND 4.0` (no derivatives, non-commercial), and the acknowledged "supplementary data" from a third party is not described.

## Falsification plan

Every threshold below is **research-defined**; every operational rule not printed by the source is **research-proposed**. Each test names data, sample/regime, metric, decision rule and the action on failure.

- **F1 — Point-in-time headline replication** (`research-proposed` rule, `research-defined` threshold). Rebuild the signal from **headline text alone** (`Mandate` + `Green` + `Green Bond` label + ticker), with **no knowledge of any subsequent announcement**, on Bloomberg (or a second wire) data frozen at the headline timestamp, 2016–2022. Threshold: `CAR[0,2] <= −0.20 pp` with one-sided `t <= −2.0` versus a matched non-leak control set. **Action on failure:** the printed result is an artefact of the retrospective leak label; the tradable reading is withdrawn.
- **F2 — Cost ladder with borrow** (`research-proposed` cost lines, `research-defined` thresholds). Re-book the short leg at **0 / 5 / 10 / 20 / 30 bp per side** plus a **50 bp / year stock-loan fee**, at **10% ADV** and **25% ADV** participation. Threshold: net > 0 at 10 bp and net `CAR[0,2] <= −0.15 pp`, with no sign flip before 30 bp. **Action on failure:** no investable claim; the record stays an event finding.
- **F3 — Sector-mechanism falsification** (`research-defined`). Require the **financial-issuer** subsample to deliver `CAR[0,2] <= −0.30 pp` with `t <= −2.0` while the non-financial subsample is `>= 0`; separately require the printed ROA loading (Table 7 Panel B, 18.260\*) to keep its sign. **Action on failure:** the "regulation-constrained green finance" mechanism is rejected even if an aggregate effect survives.
- **F4 — Timing falsification** (`research-defined`). Pre-market / first-hour leaks only: intraday CAAR over the first **5 trading hours** must be `<= −10 bp` with `z <= −1.96`; late-market leaks must be `>= 0`. **Action on failure:** the Patell–Wolfson timing channel is not the driver; treat timing as noise.
- **F5 — Pretrend / anticipation repair** (`research-proposed`). Require a joint `[−20,−1]` test **not** to reject (`p > 0.10`) **and** `[−3,−1] >= −0.10 pp` before crediting any post-leak drift (the printed `[−3,−1]` is −34/−38 bp). **Action on failure:** classify the pattern as pre-existing anticipation rather than post-leak repricing.
- **F6 — Reversal / exit test** (`research-defined`). Hold the short through day **21**. Threshold: `[0,21] <= −0.30 pp` net of costs. **Action on failure** (our count of the printed cells suggests failure: `[0,21] ≈ +0.08 pp`): hard-exit at day 10 or earlier, and downgrade the strategy to a 2–5 day event.
- **F7 — Placebo timestamp battery** (`research-defined`). 1,000 pseudo-headline timestamps per ticker from the same calendar years, matched on weekday and time-of-day, run through the identical windows. Threshold: observed `|CAR[0,2]|` above the **95th percentile** of the placebo `|mean CAR|` distribution with placebo means inside **±0.10 pp**. **Action on failure:** calendar/weekday structure (59% Monday/Tuesday) explains the result.
- **F8 — Benchmark robustness** (`research-proposed`). Market-adjusted vs CAPM vs FF3 vs Carhart must keep the **same sign at `[0,2]`, with at least 3 of 4 at `|t| >= 2.0`**. **Action on failure:** the effect is a factor-loading artefact.
- **F9 — Second wire / second market test** (`research-defined`). Replicate the identical pipeline on a **non-Bloomberg news source** (Reuters/Refinitiv or an exchange RNS feed) and on a single well-covered market (US or Japan only). Threshold: same-signed `[0,2] <= −0.15 pp` with `p < 0.05` in at least one of the two. **Action on failure:** the finding is a Bloomberg-feed-specific or market-specific artefact.
- **F10 — Forward out-of-sample window** (`research-defined`). Freeze every rule and evaluate **2023-01-01 onward for >= 40 new qualifying headlines**. Threshold: mean `CAR[0,2] <= −0.15 pp` with `t <= −2.0` and >= 55% of events negative. **Action on failure:** the 2016–2022 effect does not persist.
- **F11 — Multiplicity audit** (`research-defined`). Apply **Benjamini–Hochberg at q = 0.10** across every printed window x model x split combination (Tables 2–7 and Appendices C–D), counting the four return models as four looks. **Action on failure:** mark the finding exploratory and lower `confidence` to `low`.
- **F12 — Capacity / small-name audit** (`research-defined`). Require >= 80% of signal weight on names above **USD 5 million** median daily dollar volume (research-defined floor) and cap at 10% ADV. Threshold: the `[0,2]` estimate shrinks by **< 50%** relative to the printed −0.47 pp. **Action on failure:** the effect lives in untradable names; implementation withdrawn.

## Crypto portability

`unproven`.

There is no crypto evidence in the source, and the nearest analogues are structurally different: the signal depends on a **licensed real-time news wire carrying an anonymous, ticker-tagged "mandate" tip before a corporate debt issuance**, and on **exchange opening hours** (intraday bins are defined against session opens; day-of-week is GMT). Crypto has 24/7 sessions, no opening bell, no "pre-market" bucket, no issuer-equity event tied to a bond mandate, and no equivalent regulatory disclosure clock for a *pre-announcement* leak — token-listing or unlock leaks exist but are different mechanisms with their own records. Cost-wise, the source models nothing at all (no fees, borrow, funding, mark price, liquidation or venue fragmentation), so a 24/7 short on a fragmented venue with easy borrow but manipulable mark prices cannot inherit any printed number. Portability here would be a **ported hypothesis**, not crypto empirical evidence.
