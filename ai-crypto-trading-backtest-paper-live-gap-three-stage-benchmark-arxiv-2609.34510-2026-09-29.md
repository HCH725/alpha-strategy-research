## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

- The paper's framing claim is an **evaluation-realism claim, not a return premium**. It decomposes evaluation realism into two axes: **temporal realism** (historical data cannot show whether performance persists on unseen future markets, a concern that grows "as models are extensively tuned and selected using historical data") and **execution realism** (offline evaluation simplifies how decisions become orders and realised returns, whereas deployment faces "latency, slippage, liquidity constraints, and market impact").
- **Three-stage protocol (Section 3.3):** (1) **historical backtesting** on OKX data, train 2021-01-01 to 2023-12-31, validation 2024-01-01 to 2024-12-31, test 2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31, with configuration selected on validation and frozen thereafter; (2) **prospective exchange-based paper trading** on OKX 2026-06-04 to 2026-09-21, where "future market movements are unknown when decisions are made"; (3) **real-money live trading** on OKX 2026-08-24 to 2026-09-21 with real capital. Each later stage keeps the trading formulation and the selected configuration fixed while the timeline and execution environment move closer to deployment.
- **Evaluated methods (Section 3.1, Appendix A, Table 6):** 32 AI methods plus a passive baseline - 24 ML (LightGBM, CatBoost, Linear, XGBoost, MLP, TabNet, TCN, GRU, LSTM, SFM, GeneralPtNN, ALSTM, TFT, Transformer, KRNN, Localformer, TRA, AdaRNN, TCTS, ADD, DoubleEnsemble, GATs, Sandwich, IGMTF), 2 RL (MacroHFT, EarnHFT), 3 LLM (DeepSeek, Qwen, FinGPT) and 3 agents (FinMem, FinAgent, TradingAgents), with **Bitcoin buy-and-hold as the passive market baseline** ("Bitcoin Buy&Hold adopted as the market benchmark"). Methods are rebuilt from their public codebases where available and adapted to one unified framework; configuration selection is done "exclusively through historical backtesting over model hyperparameters and trading configurations, including trading windows, position limits, and leverage".
- **Family signal constructions (Section 3.2, Table 6):** ML methods consume 52 engineered OHLCV factors (**Alpha52**), predict the future return of every candidate asset over a predefined horizon, rank the cross-section, take the **top-k** and build a **long-only** portfolio (asset selection yes, news no, risk-control instructions no). RL methods consume the same Alpha52 factors but act on a **predefined asset, ETH**, in a **long-only** setting (no asset selection, no news, no risk control). LLM methods and agents consume method-specific prompts built from market data and textual news and return an action: DeepSeek and Qwen return JSON with buy/sell/hold, contract quantity, stop-loss, profit target and invalidation condition (market data yes, no factors, no news, **long/short**, risk control yes); FinGPT is **long-only**, news-only, evaluated **daily with a one-day lag**, each asset in its own capital bucket, long/flat; FinMem, FinAgent and TradingAgents are **long/short** with asset selection and news (FinMem/FinAgent/TradingAgents print no risk-control flag in Table 6).
- **Randomness control (Section 3.1):** a fixed random seed (42 where supported, Appendix B.3) for non-LLM methods; each historical backtest for LLM methods and trading agents is **run three times and averaged**.
- **Metrics (Section 3.3):** cumulative return, Jensen's alpha against the Bitcoin buy-and-hold benchmark, Sharpe ratio, maximum drawdown and volatility.
- **Stated finding:** "strong historical performance does not reliably transfer to prospective trading", "paper trading provides a closer approximation to live performance than backtesting but still leaves a meaningful gap", and "none of the evaluated methods sustains market-beating performance in live trading".

### Research interpretation

- Hypothesised mechanism in falsifiable terms: **the ranking that a historical backtest produces is dominated by sample-specific fit and by the particular 2025 market path, so it carries little information about prospective performance; the residual gap between a prospective paper environment and a real-money environment is driven by realised execution differences that the backtest cost model never contains.** The two stated components are: *temporal shift* (train/validation/test window vs unseen future) and *execution shift* (simulated fills vs exchange-hosted orders). Under this reading the benchmark's real contribution is a **measurement instrument for alpha decay across deployment stages**, and its negative live result is evidence that backtest-selected AI methods carry no persistent excess return over Bitcoin in this window.
- A second, weaker reading is also consistent with the printed evidence: the observed "gap" is partly a **selection artefact** - the funnel selects a handful of methods on one sample and then evaluates them on a very short live window, so winner's-curse reversion alone would produce a negative live alpha even with no execution effect. The source does not run the random-selection control that would separate these two readings.
- **The tradable extension - any specific entry/exit rule, position-sizing rule, horizon, universe filter or threshold that a practitioner would need - is entirely `research-proposed`; the source specifies none of them for a single strategy, because the object is a benchmark, not a strategy.** See Signal and Falsification plan.
- Competing explanations the source does not exclude: selection bias at each funnel stage (see contradiction 4); a 28-day live window; one venue; incomplete cost model (fees only); model hygiene (a mixed Pro/Flash DeepSeek trajectory, source-disclosed); no significance testing anywhere; no random- or uniform-selection placebo cohort.
- This is **already crypto evidence** (OKX, ten crypto assets, perpetual futures), so it is a native crypto result rather than a port. See Crypto portability.

## Signal

There is no single trading rule in this source; the "signal" is the **staged evaluation funnel plus three family-level decision rules**. What is printed:

- **Stage 1 - historical backtest.** Universe: ten large-cap crypto assets on OKX (Appendix B.2): **BTC, ETH, XRP, DOGE, ADA, TRX, LINK, LTC, HBAR, OKB**, ordered by market capitalisation at benchmark-construction time. Data split 2021-01-01/2023-12-31 train, 2024 validation, 2025 test; **all reported backtest results are on the held-out 2025 test period**. Decision step `t` uses only information available at that time. Sequence models use a **60-bar lookback**; Appendix B.3 states "We main test 5 minutes, 15 minutes, 1 hour and 4 hours, and finally choose the best time frame for simulate trading".
  - **ML rule:** predict future return per asset from Alpha52 factors, rank, take **top-k**, long-only portfolio. **`k`, the prediction horizon, the chosen timeframe per method, the rebalance cadence, the capital base and the weighting scheme are not printed** - the ML rule is `underspecified` for reconstruction.
  - **RL rule:** predefined asset ETH, long-only, method-native action space mapped to orders. Position adjustment details are `underspecified` (delegated to each original method).
  - **LLM/agent rule:** method-specific prompt -> discrete action (buy/sell/hold, or BUY/SELL/HOLD/CLOSE, or a five-level rating) -> executor converts to a contract quantity under deterministic exposure caps.
  - **Baseline:** Bitcoin buy-and-hold opened at the start of the evaluation period and held.
- **Stage 2 - prospective paper trading, 2026-06-04 to 2026-09-21.** Cohort = 14 methods (8 ML: XGBoost, MLP, TabNet, TCN, LSTM, TRA, DoubleEnsemble, GATs; 6 non-ML: MacroHFT, DeepSeek, Qwen, FinGPT, FinMem, FinAgent), stated to be "the top-performing methods from each category based on their backtesting results" (see contradiction 4). Decisions are made prospectively against newly arriving data; orders are submitted to the exchange environment with simulated capital.
- **Stage 3 - real-money live trading, 2026-08-24 to 2026-09-21.** Cohort = 4 methods (LSTM, TRA, XGBoost, Qwen), stated to be the highest paper-trading alpha as of 2026-08-23 (see contradictions 1 and 4; the selection statistic itself is never printed).
- **Prospective executor for the LLM/agent systems (Appendix B.3, `Prospective execution settings`):** poll every **300 seconds**, at most **one decision per newly closed UTC 4-hour bar**, isolated margin with fixed **2x leverage**, per-asset absolute notional capped at **15% of current account equity** and total gross notional across **four assets** capped at **60%**, order changes below **10 USDT** skipped (direct baselines only; FinAgent follows exchange minimum-size and lot-size rules). Prompt-proposed stop-losses, profit targets and sizing suggestions are recorded for analysis but "do not override these deterministic exposure limits or create exchange-native conditional orders". **Which four assets are traded in the prospective stage is never named** - `data gap`.
- **Direction flags (Table 6):** long/short for DeepSeek, Qwen, FinMem, FinAgent, TradingAgents; long-only for every ML method, both RL methods, FinGPT and the buy-and-hold baseline.
- **Everything operational that a researcher would still have to choose - entry price, order type beyond "taker", `k`, horizon, timeframe per method, sizing for ML/RL, re-entry, stop rules, capacity filter - is `research-proposed`, not source-reported.**

## Required data

- **Instrument / market type:** ten large-cap crypto assets on **OKX**; longs executed as **spot**, shorts as **perpetual futures** (Section 3.3); the 2025 direct-model backtests use **BTC-USDT perpetual contracts** (Appendix B.3).
- **Venue:** OKX only, for market data, paper trading and live trading. Multi-venue = not present.
- **Timeframe:** OHLCV at 5-minute, 15-minute, 1-hour and 4-hour resolutions are all tested; the winning timeframe is chosen per method but never printed. Sequence lookback = 60 bars. Direct-model prompts additionally consume **3-minute** bars (latest ten closes plus indicators computed from fifty 3-minute bars).
- **Fields:** open, high, low, close, volume; the 52 Alpha52 derived factors; **funding rate** and **open interest** (fed to the DeepSeek/Qwen prompts); news text (FinGPT, FinMem, FinAgent, TradingAgents); account equity, cash and signed positions; recent decision history.
- **Point-in-time:** Stage 1 uses a chronological train/validation/test split with only information available at each decision step; Stage 2 and Stage 3 are genuinely prospective. Publication-lag handling for news is `not stated in source`.
- **Timestamp:** decision bars are **UTC 4-hour bars** for the prospective LLM/agent executor; FinGPT runs daily with a one-day lag; other families' cadences are `data gap`.
- **Missing data:** null, stale, suspended and bad-print handling = `not stated in source`; imputation rules = `not stated in source`.
- **Funding / fee / spread needs:** taker fees are specified (see Execution assumptions). **Funding payments, spread, borrow and impact are not specified as costs anywhere in the pinned text** - funding appears only as a *prompt input feature* (`funding rate`), never as a P&L term. Never read as zero.

## Execution assumptions

- **Source-reported:**
  - Order type: **all trades submitted as taker orders** "to facilitate faster execution against available market liquidity" (Section 3.3).
  - Fees: **long = spot, 0.10% per trade; short = perpetual futures, 0.05% taker per trade**, stated to follow the OKX taker fee schedule, and "we apply the same transaction costs in historical backtesting".
  - Conflicting fee/instrument statement for the 2025 direct-model backtests: **BTC-USDT perpetuals, 10x isolated leverage, 10,000 USDT initial equity, 0.10% transaction fee** (Appendix B.3) - recorded as contradiction 5.
  - Prospective risk limits: **2x isolated leverage**, **15% per-asset / 60% total gross notional**, **10 USDT** minimum order-change skip, **300-second** polling, at most one decision per closed UTC 4-hour bar.
  - Order-change conversion: the executor subtracts the current signed position, converts the notional difference to contract units using the exchange contract value, and **rounds down to the permitted lot size**.
  - Measured execution discrepancies (not modelled in the backtest): **latency** (seconds from decision to execution), **price difference** (per mille, intended vs executed price) and **order value deviation** (per mille of account capital), reported for paper trading (Table 3) and live trading (Table 5).
- **If the source omits them, it says so or leaves them silent; the following are `data gap` and are never treated as zero:** fill model and queue position, partial fills and failed orders, signal-to-order delay inside the backtest, latency inside the backtest, bid-ask spread, market impact, participation/capacity, turnover, borrow availability and fees, **perpetual funding payments as a cost**, slippage, leverage and initial capital for the ML and RL families, capital base and the amount of real capital deployed in Stage 3, and any margin/liquidation model.

### Negative evidence

1. **No live method beats the market.** Over 2026-08-24 to 2026-09-21, one of four deployed methods is profitable (Qwen +3.72%), all four have negative Jensen alpha (-90.88, -284.03, -57.90, -28.10), only Qwen has a positive Sharpe (0.34), and the median return is -0.56% while Bitcoin buy-and-hold gained 11.63% (Table 4, Section 4.3).

The mechanism the benchmark is named for is a rank inversion, not a level shift.
3. **Backtest and paper disagree over the same calendar period.** Mean absolute gaps of 6.25 pp cumulative return and 14.05 pp alpha across 14 methods, with a 26.42 pp gap for Qwen, and disagreements in **both** directions (10 of 14 higher in paper trading) - so backtesting does not merely overstate, it mis-ranks (Section 4.2).
