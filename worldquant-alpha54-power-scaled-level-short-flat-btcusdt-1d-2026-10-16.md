## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

Coarse mean-reversion/momentum building blocks. The paper frames its universe coarsely: `one can think of alpha signals as based on mean-reversion or momentum`. Alpha#54 is a single-print delay-0 level block with no delay/delta and no cross-sectional operator — the paper prints the formula and the delay-0 close-trade class and nothing else about its behaviour. The paper's delay-0 Alpha#42 discussion gives the flavour (`rank(vwap - close)` lower when the stock runs up, `contrarian position taken close to the close`) without printing any behavioural mechanism for #54 itself. The paper ships no stop, target, or risk leg of any kind.

### Research interpretation

Single-bar power-scaled short-or-flat book, never long by arithmetic. Write the score as `score[t] = N[t] / D[t]` with `N[t] = -(low[t] - close[t]) * open[t]^5 = (close[t] - low[t]) * open[t]^5` and `D[t] = (low[t] - high[t]) * close[t]^5 = -(high[t] - low[t]) * close[t]^5`. On any valid bar with `high > low`, `close > low`, `open > 0`, `close > 0` (the crypto normal case), `N > 0` and `D < 0`, so the score prints strictly negative — a short firing bar. When `close == low` (close pinned at the low with `high > low`), `N = 0` and the score prints `0` (flat). When `high == low` (degenerate zero-range bar, forcing `close == low`), the quotient is `0/0` → NaN → flat by record rule. When `close <= 0` or non-finite inputs occur (impossible on BTCUSDT but fenced for determinism), `_safe_div` maps the non-finite quotient to NaN → flat. The `open^5 / close^5` factor is a scale-free power scaling: both legs are the same quote currency raised to the same power, so price-level units cancel and the factor hovers near `1` when open and close are close, amplifying the `(close - low) / (low - high)` level when the open runs far above the close. Magnitude is ignored by this record — only the sign trades (single-unit short-or-flat), so power-driven magnitude explosions on tiny `(high - low)` denominators never flip the side and never size the book. Unlike sibling worldquant-alpha53 (a 9-bar change of a close-location ratio, two-sided), this rule has no change, no lookback, and no long leg — every defined bar's sign is short or flat. Unlike sibling worldquant-alpha46 (a 20/10-day acceleration gate with fixed ±1 legs), this rule has no gate and no fixed legs. Unlike pool `cmf-velocity`/`chaikin` (volume-weighted smoothed oscillators), this rule never reads volume and never smooths. The failure mode is plain: any bar closing above its low pins the book short with no guardrail until a `close == low` or NaN bar flattens it — disclosed, not upgraded.

## Signal

Exact rule as pinned (any other series, exponent, divisor, negation, fill, frame, book, or tie treatment is a different, unpinned rule):

- Inputs (paper A.2): `open[t]`, `high[t]`, `low[t]`, `close[t]` on completed `1d` bars only. No `volume`, no vwap/adv/returns/market-cap/sector, no cross-sectional rank, no second asset or timeframe is read by any order-gating line.
- Power-scaled level (paper Appendix A.1, pinned impl lines 464–465): `score[t] = (-(low[t] - close[t]) * open[t]^5) / ((low[t] - high[t]) * close[t]^5)` via `_safe_div` (non-finite → NaN, lines 185–189) — same-bar construction, single print, no delay/delta, undefined exactly when the quotient is non-finite per the `_safe_div` code (zero-range `high == low`, non-positive or non-finite `close`, non-finite inputs), never seeded or backfilled.
- Direction mapping (source-grounded, fenced in Negative evidence 1): `score[t] < 0` → short signal; `score[t] == 0` → flat signal (fires when `close == low` prints exactly, i.e. `-0.0 == 0`); non-finite → NaN → flat signal by record rule, never interpolated. `score[t] > 0` never occurs on valid bars by the sign proof above — the long side is explicitly disabled by arithmetic, not by researcher choice, and no long order exists in this spec. The entry fills at the same bar's close, one single unit, only if the book is currently flat (see position rule).
- Exit: the first flat signal (`score == 0` or NaN) flattens the full short unit at the bar close — hold-or-flatten is the sole exit path by construction (there is no opposite long leg to reverse into). No stop-loss, no take-profit, no trailing, no time exit (the paper ships no risk leg — carried over exactly, disclosed in Provenance adaptation (4), never upgraded into a source claim).
- Direction: short-flat only overall (above-low closes hold short; at-low or undefined prints hold flat; long explicitly disabled).
- Position rule (predeclared researcher pin): single-unit short-or-flat; an already-short book treats further short-signal bars as no-ops (no pyramiding, no averaging, no scaling by score magnitude); flat-regime bars while already flat are no-ops. Re-entry re-arms on the next short-signal bar after a flat exit only (no cooldown specified — explicitly none, not invented). Pre-first-signal bars (before the adopted warmup completes) and any NaN-score bars are flat by record rule.

## Limitations

- No price stop, no time stop: any bar closing above its low pins the book short, and only an at-low or undefined bar flattens it, with no guardrail before the next print, by the carried-over paper design — the record's principal risk.

- Single-print noise: the level reads one bar with no smoothing — single-print wicks swing the sign between short and flat bar to bar with no averaging — disclosed, not smoothed.
- Power-scale opacity: the `open^5 / close^5` factor has no paper-printed economic rationale — carried over exactly, never rationalized into a volatility or liquidity claim.

- Performance evidence is absent: nothing here is calibrated, fitted, or tuned to any backtest.
