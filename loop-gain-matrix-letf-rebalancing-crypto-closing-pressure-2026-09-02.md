# Leveraged ETP Rebalancing Loop-Gain Matrix — Coupled Cross-Asset Closing Pressure

## Provenance

- **Paper**: Jihwan Woo, "The Loop-Gain Matrix: Coupled Rebalancing Feedback and the Blind Spots of Scalar Stability Monitoring"
- **arXiv**: 2608.22768v1 [q-fin.TR]
- **Published**: 2026-08-24
- **Authors**: Jihwan Woo
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22768
- **PDF**: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.22768v1

This paper is a peer-reviewed-quality working paper introducing a novel matrix-valued framework for monitoring leveraged exchange-traded product (LETF/ETP) rebalancing feedback. The paper applies the framework to both Korean single-stock LETFs and the U.S. MSTR-Bitcoin-Coinbase complex. The crypto result is a null (no significant effect for the MSTR-BTC complex), but the framework itself and the methodology are novel and directly relevant to understanding crypto market structure around leveraged products.

## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

Leveraged ETPs rebalance daily at or near the close. When multiple leveraged products exist on correlated underlyings, their rebalancing creates a coupled feedback system. The paper models this as a matrix gain system L where:

- **Scalar monitoring blind spot (i) — Cycle amplification**: For nonnegative coupling, the spectral radius ρ(L) ≥ max_i l_ii (the largest diagonal element). Under two-way coupling, the inequality is strict. This means scalar per-product monitoring systematically underestimates total system feedback.
- **Scalar monitoring blind spot (ii) — Transmitted displacement**: Even under one-way coupling, a leveraged product's rebalancing displaces the underlying price, which transmits to other products holding that underlying. This is invisible to the receiver's own scalar gain.

The paper derives a reduced-form estimator of L using only prices and public fund assets (no signed order flow needed) via cross-asset overnight reversals.

### Research interpretation

The hypothesized alpha mechanism is **predictable institutional flow at close** generated by leveraged product rebalancing, amplified by cross-asset coupling. When the loop-gain matrix spectral radius is high (indicating strong coupled feedback), the system is more prone to:

1. **Closing price pressure**: Leveraged products' mandatory rebalancing at close creates directional demand/supply that moves the underlying.
2. **Cross-asset transmission**: Rebalancing in product A moves underlying X, which transmits to product B (also holding underlying X), creating secondary closing pressure.
3. **Amplification during stress**: When market volatility increases, leveraged products' rebalancing needs grow, potentially creating a self-reinforcing cycle.

The alpha hypothesis is that monitoring the loop-gain matrix spectral radius (rather than scalar per-product gains) can identify regimes where closing price pressure from leveraged rebalancing is amplified and potentially predictable.

Components:
- **System monitor**: Loop-gain matrix spectral radius ρ(L) computed from cross-asset overnight reversals and public fund AUM data
- **Alpha signal**: High ρ(L) in a complex involving a crypto-underlying indicates amplified closing pressure regime
- **Trading hypothesis**: Position ahead of close when ρ(L) exceeds threshold, direction determined by rebalancing flow direction (long underlying when leveraged products are net long; short when net short) [research-proposed]

## Signal

- **Formation timestamp**: End-of-day (close), using overnight reversal data and fund AUM snapshots. Timezone: exchange-local (UTC for Binance, UTC-4 for U.S. markets).
- **Lookback**: Paper uses T=250 trading days for estimation. The reduced-form estimator uses cross-asset overnight reversals over a rolling window. Exact window length is not specified beyond T=250 simulation horizon.
- **Entry**: [research-proposed] When ρ(L) exceeds a threshold (paper demonstrates sensitivity but does not declare a specific trading threshold), take a position aligned with the rebalancing flow direction ahead of close.
- **Exit**: [research-proposed] Close position at or shortly after the close, capturing the rebalancing-driven price displacement.
- **Holding period**: [research-proposed] Intraday to overnight — position entered near close, exited at next open or shortly after.
- **Parameters**: The loop-gain matrix L is estimated from public fund AUM and cross-asset overnight reversals. The spectral radius ρ(L) is the key parameter. Paper reports RMSE 0.005 for ρ(L) recovery at T=250 in simulation.
- **Underspecified**: The paper does not provide a complete trading rule (entry threshold, position sizing, stop-loss). The signal construction is presented as a monitoring framework, not a standalone trading strategy.

## Required data

- **Instrument**: Leveraged ETPs (e.g., BITX, BITI, MSTR options, MicroStrategy stock) and their underlying (BTC spot/perpetual).
- **Universe**: Assets with leveraged ETP products correlated to crypto (MSTR, Coinbase, Bitcoin spot ETFs, leveraged Bitcoin ETFs).
- **Venue**: Primary listing venue for the leveraged product and the underlying's primary venue (e.g., Binance for BTC spot, NYSE/Nasdaq for MSTR).
- **Timeframe**: Daily close prices for both leveraged products and underlyings.
- **Fields**: Close prices for leveraged products and underlyings; public fund AUM (assets under management) for leveraged products; no signed order flow required.
- **Point-in-time**: Fund AUM must be available at or before close. AUM reporting lag may vary by fund.
- **Timestamp**: Exchange-local timezone, daily close.
- **Missing-data**: Paper does not specify handling of missing AUM data or trading halts.
- **Funding/fee/spread**: Not explicitly modeled in the paper. The reduced-form estimator uses prices only.

## Execution assumptions

- **Signal-to-order timing**: [research-proposed] Position entered ahead of close to capture rebalancing flow. Exact timing not specified by source.
- **Order type**: [research-proposed] Market order at close.
- **Fill model**: [research-proposed] Assume close-price fill.
- **Fees**: Not modeled by source. [research-proposed] Include standard maker/taker fees and spread.
- **Slippage**: Not modeled by source. [research-proposed] Include close-price slippage proportional to order size.
- **Impact / capacity**: The paper's key finding is that venue depth matters: the U.S. MSTR-BTC complex showed null results because the closing venue (Binance) is "far deeper" than the Korean LETF venue. This suggests capacity constraints are material.
- **Leverage**: [research-proposed] Not specified. The alpha comes from the underlying, not from leveraging.
- **Latency**: [research-proposed] Requires real-time AUM monitoring and close-price positioning.

## Evidence

### Source-reported

1. **Simulation**: Spectral radius recovery RMSE 0.005 at T=250. Lead-lag confounder yields 2% false-alarm rate. In a calibrated blind-spot configuration, scalar monitor reports "safe" while matrix monitor reports "unsafe" on 100% of paths.
2. **Korean LETF episode (2026)**: Transmission from SK Hynix complex into Samsung Electronics' closing price detected. DiD z=-2.82; exact randomization p=0.0055 against 182 control pairs. Approximately 41% of Samsung's closing displacement variance is imported — invisible to its own "moderate" gain of 0.24. Effect scales with sender's rebalancing capital.
3. **U.S. MSTR-Bitcoin-Coinbase complex**: The same estimator returns nulls. The paper attributes this to the closing venue (Binance) being "far deeper" than the Korean venue.

Source-reported results are from the paper's simulation and empirical analysis. The Korean LETF result is the only positive empirical finding. The crypto (MSTR-BTC) result is a null.

### Independently reproduced

Not independently reproduced.

### Negative evidence

The U.S. MSTR-Bitcoin-Coinbase complex returned null results despite comparable capital to the Korean complex. The paper attributes this to venue depth — deeper venues absorb rebalancing flow without significant price impact. This is a material negative result for crypto application: the mechanism may not produce tradeable alpha on deep, liquid venues like Binance BTC.

## Falsification

1. **Venue-depth threshold**: [research-defined] Determine the minimum venue depth (measured by average daily volume, spread, or order book depth) below which leveraged ETP rebalancing creates detectable closing pressure. Failure: no price displacement detectable on any crypto venue regardless of depth.
2. **Cross-asset transmission in crypto**: [research-defined] Test whether leveraged Bitcoin ETP rebalancing transmits to correlated assets (MSTR, Coinbase, Ethereum) in a manner analogous to the Korean LETF episode. Failure: null transmission across all crypto-related pairs.
3. **Spectral radius threshold for regime detection**: [research-defined] Determine whether ρ(L) above a threshold predicts statistically significant closing price displacement. Failure: ρ(L) has no predictive power for closing returns.
4. **Out-of-sample**: Test the reduced-form estimator on a rolling out-of-sample window across different market regimes (bull, bear, high-vol, low-vol).
5. **Fee sensitivity**: [research-defined] Include realistic transaction costs (spread, slippage, market impact). Failure: any alpha from closing-pressure prediction is consumed by costs.

## Crypto portability

**Adapted**

The paper explicitly tests the MSTR-Bitcoin-Coinbase complex and finds null results, attributing this to Binance's deep liquidity. The framework is portable to crypto conceptually, but the empirical evidence suggests the effect may be too small on deep crypto venues to generate tradeable alpha.

Crypto-specific portability considerations:
- **Spot vs. perpetual**: The underlying for leveraged crypto ETPs is typically BTC spot, not perpetual. Funding rate dynamics on perps may interact with rebalancing flow.
- **24/7 session**: Crypto markets never close, so "close" is defined by the ETF/ETP listing venue's hours (e.g., NYSE close for MSTR, 4:00 PM ET). BTC spot on Binance trades through the close.
- **Venue fragmentation**: BTC trades on dozens of venues. The rebalancing flow may hit different venues than where the alpha signal is measured.
- **Liquidity**: Deep BTC spot venues may absorb rebalancing flow without detectable impact (as the paper suggests).
- **MSTR as proxy**: MicroStrategy (MSTR) holds ~600K BTC and trades on Nasdaq. Leveraged MSTR products (e.g., MSTX, MSTU) rebalance daily, creating closing pressure on MSTR, which in turn affects BTC spot via MSTR's BTC holdings. This is a more promising venue than direct BTC leveraged ETFs due to Nasdaq's closing auction mechanics.
- **ETF flow interaction**: The existing record on Bitcoin spot ETF net flows (`bitcoin-us-spot-etf-net-flow-next-day-drift-2026-09-01.md`) addresses flow-driven drift; the loop-gain matrix adds a structural feedback layer to this mechanism.

## Limitations

- **Null crypto result**: The paper's only crypto-relevant finding is a null (MSTR-BTC complex). The positive result is from Korean equity LETFs, which may not generalize.
- **Venue depth dependency**: The mechanism appears to require insufficient venue depth to absorb rebalancing flow. Deep crypto venues (Binance, Coinbase) may be too liquid.
- **Not a standalone trading strategy**: The paper presents a monitoring framework, not a trading rule. Entry/exit thresholds, position sizing, and risk management are unspecified.
- **Sample period**: The Korean LETF episode is a single episode (2026). Generalizability is unclear.
- **Reduced-form estimator**: The estimator uses only prices and AUM; it does not observe actual rebalancing flow. Measurement- convention sensitivity is acknowledged.
- **Publication bias**: The paper is a working paper, not peer-reviewed.
- **Data gap**: Actual rebalancing flow data (not just AUM) would strengthen the analysis.
