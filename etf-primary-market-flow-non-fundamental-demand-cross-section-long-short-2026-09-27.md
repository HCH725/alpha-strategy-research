## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The paper's thesis (Abstract, Section 1, Section 2, Remarks 2, 6, 7, 8) is a **microfoundation for reading ETF primary-market flows as a signal**, not a trading rule:

- ETF shares and their underlying basket share one unobservable fundamental value Ω. A divergence between the ETF price and NAV is therefore a *relative* mispricing whose fundamental component cancels out, so "trades that exploit the mispricing (i.e., arbitrage trades) imply the realization of this non-fundamental excess demand" (Section 2, p. 9).
- In the model, authorised participants (APs) optimise `max δ_i (p_2(δ_i) − π_2(δ_i))` (Eq. 8) and the equilibrium aggregate flow is **Δ* = (ε_etf − ε_nav)/(λ + η)** (Lemma 1, Eq. 9), which contains **no fundamental shock ω_1** and is linear only in the *difference* of the two non-fundamental demand shocks. **Remark 2: "ETF flows are symptomatic of non-fundamental demand distortions."**
- Because AP arbitrage removes *relative* mispricing (Proposition 1, Eq. 11: ψ* → 0 as N → ∞) but leaves *fundamental* mispricing (Eq. 12), **Remark 6: "ETF flows almost always signal a fundamental mispricing that subsequently reverses."**
- **Remark 7** fixes the sign: if ETF-share demand is more sensitive to non-fundamental shocks than the basket (σ_e > σ_n), **flows negatively predict returns**; the empirical coefficients are consistently negative, which the source reads as σ_e > σ_n. **Remark 8** predicts ETF-share and NAV return predictability should be qualitatively the same, which Tables 3 and 4 confirm.
- **Remark 3** predicts where the signal is strongest: ETFs with **volatile flows** and with *different clienteles* trading the shares vs the underlying. The source maps this onto (i) **leveraged ETFs** (shares bought by retail for short-horizon magnified exposure, backed by total-return swaps traded by institutions) and (ii) **high-activity ETFs** (frequent creations/redemptions).
- **Performance claim (source-reported):** "A portfolio which is short high flow ETFs and long low flow ETFs earns excess returns of **1% to 4% per month**" (pinned draft Abstract); Section 1 restates the one-month horizon as **0.8% to 2.0% per month**; Table 3 Panel A prints one-month long-short returns of **1.519%/1.134%** ($50M+ equal-/value-weighted), **2.025%/1.154%** (mature), **4.452%/4.409%** (mature leveraged) and **0.175%/0.110%** (mature unleveraged), with three-month and six-month horizons also reported. Table 6 reports annualised Sharpe ratios of **0.60 (one-month)** and **0.99 (six-month)** for the mature-sample trading strategy. Section 5 adds that ETF investors underperform: in SPY the IRR falls **37 bp/year** below the single-share return and the SGAR (**5.43%**) falls **148 bp/year** below its simulated expectation (**6.91%**), against a stated management fee of **9 bp/year**.
- The source explicitly lists its own modelling simplifications (Section 2, p. 20): **zero opportunity cost of arbitrage capital**, no dynamic AP game, and no cross-ETF interaction.

### Research interpretation

- **Hypothesized mechanism (falsifiable form, `research interpretation`):** *non-fundamental demand reversal.* A demand shock that hits ETF shares but not the basket (sentiment, retail/short-horizon flow, cheap-basket trading) forces APs to create shares; the observable creation marks a cohort of ETFs whose price is above fundamental value, so that cohort underperforms and the redemption cohort outperforms over the following 1-6 months. The tradable object is the **cross-sectional D1-minus-D10 spread**, not the model itself.
- **What the evidence in the source actually supports (`research interpretation`):** the spread is **not a symmetric two-sided signal at all horizons**. Table 5 (date fixed effects, lagged controls) shows Deciles 2-9 are mostly indistinguishable from Decile 1 while Decile 10 is hugely negative at every horizon, and at the six-month horizon Deciles 5, 6 and 7 **outperform** Decile 1 (+1.75*, +2.16**, +1.74*). The six-month long-short return is therefore **entirely a short-D10 effect**, and even at one month the D10 leg contributes about −1.86 of the roughly −1.86-to-+0.70 cross-sectional range. The mechanism story ("redemption ETFs are cheap") is much weaker than the story "creation ETFs are expensive".
- **Competing mechanism the source's own results make live (`research interpretation`):** an **activity/liquidity conditioning** story. Table 3 Panel B shows the long-short return is **negative and significant in the low-activity tercile** (−0.586** at one month, −0.795* at three months) and positive only in the high tercile. If flows signalled non-fundamental demand uniformly, low-activity ETFs should show a weaker version of the same sign, not the **opposite** sign. A liquidity, small-size or primary-market-friction explanation is therefore a live rival, and the source does not test it.
- **Role of each component if one tried to trade it** (all `research-proposed`, none source-specified as an executable rule): *Signal layer:* prior-calendar-month percentage change in shares outstanding, cross-sectionally ranked into deciles. *Universe layer:* ETFs above $50m AUM, optionally the "mature" and "high-activity" screens. *Portfolio layer:* long Decile 1, short Decile 10, value- or equal-weighted. *Holding layer:* 1, 3 or 6 months, with the six-month version an equal weight of the last six one-month cohorts. *Risk layer:* none exists in the source — no stop, no volatility target, no beta or factor neutralisation, no capacity cap.

## Signal

Two layers must be separated: the **signal definition** (source-reported in detail) and the **tradable rule** (source-reported only as a portfolio sort, with material operational gaps).

**Signal definition (source-reported, Section 3-4, Equations 15-17)**

- **Instrument:** US-listed ETFs (all categories: broad equity, sector equity, bond, international, commodity/currency, mixed/municipal via the Lipper "Bonds" bucket).
- **Universe screens, in order (source-reported):**
  1. Bloomberg daily price / NAV / shares-outstanding / volume panel; require **at least twelve months of observations** (so ETFs launched in 2016 are excluded — the paper has data on 1,707 ETFs at end-2016 but reports **1,515** in the sample).
  2. **$50m screen:** included from the first month in which end-of-month market capitalisation exceeds $50 million. Removes **27%** of ETFs, which together hold **< 1%** of market cap.
  3. **"Mature" screen:** $50m+ *and* a month in which **at least one-half of trading days** had reported creation or redemption activity. Removes roughly half of the remaining ETFs but only **10%** of market cap.
  4. **"High activity" screen (Panel B only):** tercile split on the prior month's **number of days with any change in shares outstanding**; top tercile = high activity.
  5. Leveraged vs unleveraged split (leveraged = 2X/3X and −1X/−2X/−3X funds; cash creations/redemptions, derivative-backed).
- **Signal formula (Equation 15):** `ETFFlow_{j,t} = SharesOutstanding_{j,t} / SharesOutstanding_{j,t−1} − 1`, computed **at monthly frequency** on the change in shares outstanding. The source gives three reasons for not going higher-frequency (Section 3): (i) accounting conventions differ across funds (**T vs T+1** registration) and "the change from T+1 to T accounting, or vice versa, is not public"; (ii) APs can strategically delay creation to exploit failure-to-deliver rules, waiting **until T+6** (citing Evans, Moussawi, Pagano & Sedunov 2017); (iii) arbitrage activity itself creates price pressure, which monthly aggregation is meant to de-emphasise.
- **Sort:** monthly, on the prior month's `ETFFlow`; **Decile 1 = most negative flow (most redemptions), Decile 10 = most positive flow (most creations)**. Sign convention verified in Provenance (Decile 1 − Decile 10 = Long-Short).
- **Portfolio:** long Decile 1, short Decile 10, both **equal-weighted and value-weighted (by ETF AUM)**; returns measured over the following **one, three and six months**; overlapping horizons handled with **Newey-West standard errors with lag equal to the horizon in months** for the sorts and **Driscoll-Kraay** standard errors (same lag) for the panel regressions.
- **Comparator signal the model also predicts:** `ETFPremChange` (Equation 16) = change in the ETF premium (price/NAV − 1). **This signal fails** (Figure 2 panels b/d/f: returns "generally positive, but not statistically different from zero"; relegated to Internet Appendix Table IA8).
- **Panel regression (Equation 17):** `Performance_{j,t+h} = β·FlowMeasure_{j,t} + γ_t + δ·V_{j,t} + ε`, with **date fixed effects** and lagged fund controls (two lags of ETF return, ETF premium, ETF share volume, ETF market cap); `ETFFlow` and characteristics standardised (subtract sample mean, divide by sample SD). Abnormal returns use **FF3 + momentum (Carhart 1997)** (Eq. 18).
- **Trading-strategy construction (Table 6):** value-weighted long-short; the one-month strategy **turns over every month** on the prior month's flow; the six-month strategy **equally weights the last six one-month cohorts**, so **one-sixth of the portfolio turns over every month**.

**Tradable rule — `underspecified`, never guessed here**

- **Formation timestamp / timezone:** `not stated in source`. The signal is monthly and uses "the prior month's" flow, but no formation day, no market-close convention and no timezone are printed.
- **Availability of the signal:** `underspecified` and **material**. Shares-outstanding changes are registered on **T or T+1** with the convention changing over time and "not public", and APs may delay creation to **T+6**. The source acknowledges this as its reason for using monthly data but **never quantifies the resulting observation lag**, so the month in which a flow becomes publicly knowable is not established.
- **Entry/exit price, order type, latency, partial fills, tie handling, delisting and corporate-action treatment:** `not stated in source`.
- **Weighting and neutrality:** EW and VW are both reported (source-reported); whether the spread is beta-, sector- or factor-neutral is `not stated in source`.
- **Parameters summary — all source-reported:** $50m AUM floor; ≥50% active-days maturity rule; 12-month minimum history; monthly frequency; 10 deciles; activity tercile split; horizons 1/3/6 months; Newey-West/Driscoll-Kraay lag = horizon; Carhart-four-factor abnormal returns; FF3/Carhart/FF5/q-factor libraries (Section 3). **Nothing in this list is Scout-tuned.** Anything not printed — formation day, execution price, order type, cost ladder, leg weighting neutrality, sizing — is **`research-proposed`**.

### Negative evidence

1. **No transaction-cost model exists anywhere in the source** (Methods-level read of Sections 3, 4, 4.1, 4.2, 5 plus Tables 1-7, Figures 1-3, Appendix, and a whole-text word scan of the 56-page pinned PDF; see the table in Execution assumptions). 2. **The strategy's turnover rate is never reported**, despite a full monthly re-sort of a long-short pair (or 1/6 monthly for the six-month spec). Cost sensitivity is unmeasured.
3. **The signal flips sign in the low-activity tercile** (Table 3 Panel B): −0.586** at one month, −0.795* at three months equal-weighted; Table 4 (NAV returns) shows −0.625** and −1.014**. If creation/redemption flow signalled non-fundamental demand unconditionally, the low-activity cohort should show the *same* sign with smaller magnitude. It does not. The effect is therefore conditional on primary-market activity — an unexplained heterogeneity the source does not test.
4. **The effect is a Decile-10 story, not a smooth cross-sectional relation.** In Table 5 the continuous `ETFFlow` coefficient is **never significant** (−0.03/−0.04/−0.05, t ≈ −1.1 to −1.3) and the source says so explicitly: "a non-linear relation between lagged ETF flows and subsequent performance". Rule: rebuild `ETFFlow`, the $50m and mature screens, the decile sort and the Table 3 long-short exactly as specified, with no re-tuning. Metric: one-, three- and six-month long-short returns with Newey-West t at the horizon. **Fail if** the mature-sample one-month or six-month spread is ≤ 0, or |t| < 1.96, in the post-2016 window. **Action:** report the hypothesis as not generalising and stop treating it as a candidate.
- **F2 — Timestamp and publication-lag audit (decisive for validity).** Re-run F1 three ways: (i) as printed (current-month share count), (ii) lagging the flow one full month, (iii) reconstructing flow from each fund's **actual public reporting date** under both T and T+1 conventions and dropping months whose publication is ambiguous. **Fail if** the sign flips, the spread falls by more than 50%, or |t| < 1.96 under any convention. This is the largest unquantified issue the source itself raises.
- **F3 — Cost ladder (decisive for tradability).** Measure one-way monthly turnover of the value-weighted D1/D10 pair from absolute weight changes (`research-proposed`), then apply **0/1/5/10/20/30 bp one-way** plus a commission schedule and a separate stock-loan fee on the short leg. Use Table 2's 10 bp mature-sample bid-ask spread as the realistic midpoint. **Fail if** the strategy's Sharpe falls by more than 50% at 10 bp one-way, or the mean annual return turns ≤ 0 at or below 10 bp one-way.
- **F4 — Turnover and capacity audit.** Report average and median one-way monthly turnover for both the one-month and one-sixth-rolling specs, then cap each leg at **10% (and 20%) of average daily dollar volume**. **Fail if** target weights cannot be reached inside the 10% participation cap, or required annual turnover exceeds 500% of the cohort's traded volume.
- **F5 — Activity-tercile mechanism test (decisive for the mechanism).** Re-run the sort in **five** primary-market-activity buckets rather than three and regress the long-short return on the bucket rank. **Fail the non-fundamental-demand reading if** the low-activity buckets continue to show a significantly *negative* spread (|t| ≥ 1.96) or if the slope on activity is not positive and significant — the sign flip would then be better explained by liquidity, size or category than by non-fundamental demand.
- **F6 — Decile-vs-continuous ablation (decisive for signal shape).** The source reports that the continuous measure is never significant. Test (a) a rank-transformed continuous flow, (b) winsorisation of `ETFFlow` at the 1st/99th percentiles, and (c) a 5-decile re-sort. **Fail the paper's broad claim that "ETF flows predict returns" if** only the raw Decile-10 dummy survives and the effect disappears under winsorisation — that would make the result an extreme-outlier study rather than a signal.
- **F7 — Two-leg decomposition.** Report each leg's alpha separately against the same date-fixed-effect benchmark. **Fail if** the long leg's alpha is ≤ 0 or |t| < 1.96 at six months (the source's own Table 5 implies it will), since the marketed object is a *spread*, not a single short.
- **F8 — Placebo / circular shift.** Circularly shift the monthly `ETFFlow` panel relative to returns by k ∈ {1..12} months, 1,000 draws, preserving the cross-sectional structure. **Fail if** the genuine alignment's |spread| is not above the 95th percentile of the null.
- **F9 — Risk-model robustness.** Require the spread to survive **FF5, the q-factor, plus a flow-factor control** (a factor-mimicking portfolio long high-flow/short low-flow ETFs), and to stay positive after adding a size-spread and a one-month-reversal control. **Fail if** the six-month alpha falls below 100 bp annualised or |t| < 1.96.
- **F10 — Competing-explanation horse race.** Add bid-ask spread, short-interest percentage, AUM, turnover, Lipper category fixed effects, and the ETF premium level as controls. **Fail if** the Decile-10 coefficient shrinks by more than 50% and loses significance (|t| < 1.96).
- **F11 — Subperiod stability.** Require the mature-sample spread to be positive and significant (|t| ≥ 1.96) in at least 3 of 4 subperiods: 2007-2011, 2012-2016, 2017-2020, 2021-2026. **Fail otherwise.** (The source already shows two sign/magnitude failures inside its own first two subperiods.)
- **F12 — Multiplicity control.** Apply Benjamini-Hochberg at **q < 0.10** across the headline battery (Table 3 Panels A-B, Table 4 Panels A-B, Table 5's nine columns, Table 6's eighteen cells). **Fail if** the mature-sample one- and six-month spreads do not survive.
- **F13 — Underlying-stock transport test.** Replicate at the index-constituent level (the claim of the source's unreadable Internet Appendix Section 6) using point-in-time index membership and the same monthly flow deciles mapped to ETF constituents. **Fail if** the stock-level spread has |t| < 1.96 or differs in sign from the ETF-level spread.
- **F14 — Borrow gate (`research-proposed`).** Verify that ≥ 80% of a randomly sampled 500-name panel drawn from the Decile-10 cohorts is borrowable at a stock-loan fee ≤ 100 bp/yr with ≥ 90% availability. **Fail if** fewer than 80% are borrowable, since the headline spread requires the short leg.
- **F15 — Crypto port gate (`research-proposed`, only if a port is ever attempted).** Require Newey-West |t| ≥ 1.96 on the ported spread **and** a positive net-of-10 bp Sharpe ≥ 0.30 over ≥ 3 years of crypto data, with an explicit point-in-time substitute for shares outstanding. **Fail otherwise** and keep portability at `unproven`.

## Crypto portability

**unproven.**

- The source contains **zero crypto evidence**: no crypto instrument, no exchange, no spot/perpetual/futures contract, no funding, no 24/7 session, no on-chain data. Every result is from US-listed ETFs on traditional assets between 2007 and 2016.
- **Mechanism-level obstacle:** the signal *is* the authorised-participant primary market — APs delivering a basket for newly created shares. Native crypto spot and perpetual markets have **no AP creation/redemption mechanism and no NAV to arbitrage against**; the closest analogue is the premium/discount cycle of crypto ETPs, which is a thin cross-section (a handful of US spot-BTC/ETH ETFs plus European products) and whose flows are already covered in this repository by records with different sources (`bitcoin-us-spot-etf-net-flow-next-day-drift-2026-09-01.md`, `tradingview-bitcoin-etf-issuer-normalized-flow-zscore-2026-09-22.md`). Any port built here would be a **new hypothesis (`research-proposed`)**, not a transfer of this one.
- **Data-dependency obstacle:** `ETFFlow` is a *regulatory-adjacent daily shares-outstanding* series with a T/T+1 and T+6 publication ambiguity. Crypto has no equivalent supply-change disclosure with a comparable structure; exchange-level open interest and basis are different variables with different dynamics.
- **Market-structure differences that would have to be re-derived:** 24/7 sessions and exchange-specific candle boundaries versus a US-listed fund that only creates/ redeems during US hours (a crypto ETP's flow is stamped in US hours while its underlying trades continuously); funding and mark/index price; leverage, liquidation and margin; venue fragmentation and per-venue survivorship; borrow availability for the short leg; custody, withdrawal and stablecoin/quote-currency effects; and the 1-to-6-month horizon itself, which crosses many crypto regime changes.
- Because neither the mechanism nor the signal has been demonstrated in crypto by the source, portability is **`unproven`**; this is not authorization to trade anything.

- `data gap` — data-cleaning rules live in Internet Appendix Section 9; survivorship, delisting and corporate-action handling for the ETF return panel are `not stated in source`.
- `underspecified` — the observation/availability timing of the monthly flow: T vs T+1 registration with a convention that "is not public", plus discretionary AP delay to T+6, all acknowledged by the source but never quantified.
- `underspecified` — formation timestamp and timezone, execution price, order type, latency, tie handling, neutrality convention, and the definition of the risk-free rate used for Table 6's Sharpe ratios (the French risk-free rate is named only for the SGAR calculation).
- `underspecified` — why the low-activity tercile has the **opposite** sign, and why the six-month horizon is carried entirely by the short leg.
- `not stated in source` — code availability, data availability, replication package, conflict-of-interest and funding disclosures; and the published article's full text, which is paywalled (so the difference between the pinned April 2020 draft and the July 2021 published version is known only at the abstract level).
- **Version boundary:** every quantitative claim here comes from the SSRN **April 17, 2020** draft (SHA-256 `1afa5e6e…`). The peer-reviewed *Review of Finance* abstract states a different headline range (**1.1-2.0%** vs the draft's **1% to 4%**); the published tables were not read and are not quoted anywhere in this record.
- Identification is correlational: the source documents that a flow sort co-moves with subsequent ETF returns; it does not identify the non-fundamental-demand channel, and its own activity-tercile sign flip and continuous-variable insignificance are consistent with a liquidity/category proxy.
- Sample scope: **US-listed ETFs, January 2007 - December 2016**, after a $50m floor (drops 27% of funds), a ≥50%-active-days maturity screen (drops about half of what remains) and a 12-month history requirement; results are strongest precisely in the leveraged and high-activity corners where retail/specialist clientele dominate.
- Publication-bias and multiplicity concerns apply: many sample/weighting/horizon combinations, three different stated headline ranges, an unreadable supplement carrying load-bearing robustness, and no multiplicity correction.
- Capacity, borrowability and turnover of the long-short spread are entirely unaddressed.
- This is a research capture of a public working paper. It is not evidence that the spread survives after costs, survives a publication-lag point-in-time rebuild, survives 2017-2026, or exists in crypto.
