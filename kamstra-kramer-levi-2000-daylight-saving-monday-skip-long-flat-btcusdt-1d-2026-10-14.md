## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

Sleep-desynchronosis risk-shunning as a weekend-effect amplifier, fenced to the paper's 1967–1998 equity samples: minor sleep imbalances `cause errors in judgment, anxiety, impatience, less efficient processing of information, and loss of attention`; clock shifts produce `desynchronosis … whether time "stretches" or "shrinks"` (spring loss and fall gain both count, with fall empirically stronger); the conjectured path is desynchronosis → `greater anxiety` → `prefer safer investments and shun risk` on `the trading day following such a disturbance`, pushing prices down when the disturbance is systematic — `Such a linkage is, of course, a conjecture`; the paper `sets out to evaluate the empirical association`, finding spring weekends at `200 to 500 percent` of ordinary weekends, stronger fall effects, joint-test significance beyond 1% (U.S./U.K.) and 5% (Canada), and a `$31 billion` U.S. one-day implication. All mechanism framing fenced to the paper's samples, never adopted as a crypto claim here.

### Research interpretation

Legislated-clock-change Monday skip, long-only, twice-yearly cadence. Unlike every-Monday calendar records the trigger is not the weekday but the statute table: a regular non-DST Monday prints long here and long in monday-drift (agreement), while a clock-change Monday prints flat here and long there (opposite triggers on exactly the paper's event days). Unlike formation-sign records (JT/TSMOM/Novy-Marx) no price is ever read: a deeply oversold DST Monday still prints flat, and a euphoric regular Monday still prints long. Unlike count/composition records (GM 8-of-11, FIP daily signs) there is no statistic to accumulate. Unlike volume/turnover records volume never appears. Unlike extremum records (MAX) no tail is measured. The bet, as derived, is purely that the documented first-trading-day-after-clock-change weakness ports to BTCUSDT Monday-UTC-day sessions — with only ~2 events/year, no guardrail outside those days, and the paper's own German insignificance plus the Pinegar dispute riding along undisguised.

## Limitations

- Single-asset port: the paper's evidence is a multi-index equity study; BTCUSDT clock-change-Monday behavior is unevidenced by the source and may differ arbitrarily — no performance is claimed here for exactly this reason.
- Mechanism mismatch: whatever anxiety-driven risk-shunning sits behind the equity effect has no established crypto analogue (24/7 global tape, no open/close session, non-U.S. participant majority, perpetual-funding holders, stablecoin quote, thin early history) — the port bets on calendar persistence, not on a verified crypto mechanism, and the paper itself fences the linkage as conjecture.

- Rule-break rigidity: the 2007 statute change means no tested sample ever traded the dates this record trades; a future statute change would obsolete the pinned table (a re-pinned table would be a new record, never a silent patch).
