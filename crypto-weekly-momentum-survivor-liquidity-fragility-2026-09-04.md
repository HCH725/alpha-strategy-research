# Crypto Weekly Momentum Survivor-Liquidity Fragility and Transient-Coin Dependence

## Provenance

Primary source: Klaus Grobys, Davide Sandretto, and Janne Äijö, *On survivor cryptocurrency momentum*, *Finance Research Letters* 92 (2026), article 109602. DOI: `10.1016/j.frl.2026.109602`. The article was accepted 2026-02-01 and available online 2026-02-02.

The full published article is publicly available under CC BY through the University of Turin and University of Vaasa institutional repositories. The empirical sample runs from the first week of January 2017 through the fourth week of August 2024 and contains 398 weekly observations.

Source-identity / pool-level deduplication check:

- Repository search found no record containing DOI `10.1016/j.frl.2026.109602`, the exact paper title, `SCMP`, `PCMP`, or the same survivor-versus-transient cryptocurrency momentum mechanism.
- This record is not a generic momentum record. Its distinctive hypothesis is that **weekly cross-sectional crypto momentum is not robust inside a time-invariant liquid survivor universe, while apparent profitability in a rotating top-30 universe depends on trimming and coins that are only temporarily investable**.
- Existing pool momentum records may share the broad momentum family, but this source contributes materially different universe construction, liquidity/survivorship evidence, drawdown evidence, transaction-cost sensitivity, and direct negative evidence.
- No second record is created from the same source under a different narrative.

Source/data as-of: fourth week of August 2024.

## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The authors test whether cryptocurrency momentum is a pervasive return effect comparable to momentum in liquid G10 currencies, or whether previously reported crypto momentum profits are partly generated by unstable, temporarily popular, or eventually illiquid/defaulted coins.

They define a time-invariant **survivor** universe consisting of cryptocurrencies that remained among the top 100 altcoins by market capitalization over the full January 2017-August 2024 sample. Only nine free-floating cryptocurrencies satisfy this criterion: BTC, ETH, XRP, DOGE, LTC, XLM, ETC, XMR, and NEO. USDT is excluded because it is not free-floating.

The source argues that stable liquidity and market relevance should provide a cleaner environment for testing whether momentum itself is persistent. If momentum disappears among survivor coins but appears in a rotating universe containing temporarily investable coins, the apparent premium may be linked to financial frictions, discontinuous price paths, changing popularity, and limits to arbitrage rather than a stable return-generating mechanism.

### Research interpretation

The falsifiable research hypothesis is primarily a **fragility / negative-evidence hypothesis**:

1. In a point-in-time, continuously liquid crypto universe, 30-day cross-sectional momentum should be materially weaker than headline results obtained from broader rotating universes.
2. Large reported crypto momentum payoffs may concentrate in assets that enter a high-attention/high-market-cap state temporarily and later lose liquidity or market relevance.
3. Trimming extreme returns can mechanically turn an insignificant strategy into a statistically significant one, so any momentum result that requires ex-post trimming should be treated as fragile until the corresponding live risk-control rule is specified point-in-time.
4. If the only profitable branch relies on assets that later become hard to trade or disappear, backtest alpha may be partly an investability illusion rather than executable momentum.

This does not prove that all crypto momentum is invalid. It provides a direct test of whether momentum survives realistic universe stability and liquidity constraints.

## Signal

### Source-reported survivor momentum construction (SCMP)

- **Universe:** nine survivor cryptocurrencies: BTC, ETH, XRP, DOGE, LTC, XLM, ETC, XMR, NEO.
- **Sample:** first week of January 2017 through fourth week of August 2024; 398 weekly observations.
- **Formation lookback:** past 30 daily prices.
- **Skip convention:** the most recent daily price quotation is skipped when computing the prior-month formation-period return.
- **Formation return:** source equation uses the closing price on the previous trading day and the closing price 30 trading days earlier.
- **Ranking:** sort the nine survivor coins into terciles by formation-period return.
- **Long leg:** equal-weight the three coins with the highest formation-period returns.
- **Short leg:** equal-weight the three coins with the lowest formation-period returns.
- **Portfolio:** zero-cost winner-minus-loser survivor cryptocurrency momentum portfolio (`SCMP`).
- **Holding period:** one week.
- **Rebalance:** beginning of each week.
- **Inference:** Newey-West standard errors with five lags.

The source does not state an exchange-specific execution timestamp, exact timezone, bid/ask convention, order type, fill rule, funding treatment, or borrow mechanism. These remain **underspecified** for live implementation.

### Source-reported plain momentum construction (PCMP)

- **Universe:** the 30 cryptocurrencies with the highest market capitalization at the end of each December.
- **Reconstitution:** annual, based on the year-end top-30 market-cap set.
- **Formation concept:** same prior 30-day momentum framework used in the paper.
- **Ranking:** quintile sorts.
- **Portfolio:** zero-cost winner-minus-loser plain cryptocurrency momentum portfolio (`PCMP`).
- **Holding / rebalance cadence:** weekly, consistent with the source methodology.

The precise treatment of ties, missing observations, delistings within the year, exchange mapping, and unavailable shorts is **underspecified** in the normalized execution sense.

### Source-reported trimming analysis

The paper evaluates raw returns and trimmed return distributions. It reports trimming variants that remove 1%, 5%, and 10% of the return distribution. Earlier methodological discussion describes the focal trimming as excluding observations outside the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles (5% total trimming).

Trimming is an **ex-post statistical treatment**, not a live executable stop or winsorization rule. It must not be silently converted into a trading rule.

### Research-proposed testable operationalization

Any executable adaptation is **research-proposed**:

1. Reconstitute a point-in-time investable universe using only information available before each rebalance.
2. Preserve the source 30-day formation horizon and one-week holding horizon.
3. Run parallel branches: (a) stable/high-liquidity survivor-like universe, (b) annual top-30 market-cap universe, and (c) broader liquid point-in-time universe.
4. Do **not** trim realized future returns in the tradable strategy. If tail control is desired, define an ex-ante liquidity, volatility, participation, or stop rule using information available before entry.
5. Treat any ex-ante threshold, liquidity floor, volatility cap, stop, borrow filter, funding filter, sizing rule, exchange selection, or execution timing as **research-proposed**.

## Required data

- **Price data:** daily closes for formation-period returns and weekly portfolio return measurement.
- **Market capitalization:** point-in-time market cap for annual top-30 selection and survivor/stability analysis.
- **Listing history:** listing, delisting, suspension, symbol changes, redenominations, forks, migrations, and contract replacements.
- **Liquidity:** point-in-time dollar volume, bid-ask spread, order-book depth, and preferably market-impact estimates.
- **Venue:** source uses CoinMarketCap aggregate price data; an executable replication must specify one or more actual trading venues.
- **Shortability:** spot borrow availability and borrow cost, or perpetual/futures contract availability and funding.
- **Fees / spread / impact:** maker/taker fees, commissions, bid-ask spread, slippage, market impact, and funding/borrow.
- **Universe PIT integrity:** annual ranking and all eligibility filters must use contemporaneously observable data; no later survivor status may be used to choose assets in a causal backtest.
- **Timestamp:** exact 24/7 crypto daily and weekly boundary must be specified. The source does not provide an exchange-specific UTC execution clock sufficient for live reconstruction.
- **Missing data:** no silent imputation. Assets unavailable at formation or execution should be handled by a predeclared skip/eligibility rule.

## Execution assumptions

### Source-reported

The source is an empirical momentum study, not an exchange-specific execution study. It does not specify canonical order type, latency, limit/market fill model, leverage, collateral, liquidation rules, venue fragmentation, funding, borrow locate, spread, slippage, or impact.

For the PCMP transaction-cost exercise, the authors apply stylized costs of 25, 50, and 75 basis points and calculate break-even costs. These are sensitivity assumptions, not reconstructed historical execution costs.

### Research interpretation

A valid execution test must separately specify:

- signal observation timestamp and timezone;
- first executable price after signal formation;
- long and short venue eligibility;
- borrow/perpetual availability;
- funding and basis;
- maker/taker fees;
- spread, slippage, and impact;
- participation cap and minimum depth;
- handling of coins that delist or lose derivative markets during the holding week;
- cross-venue fragmentation and stablecoin quote effects;
- margin and liquidation constraints.

Because the paper's central concern is that profits may depend on temporarily investable coins, **fillability and exitability during deteriorating liquidity are part of the hypothesis, not optional implementation details**.

## Evidence

### Source-reported

For the nine-coin survivor strategy (`SCMP`), the source reports an average weekly zero-cost payoff of approximately **0.36%**, with `t = 0.75`; the return is economically positive but statistically indistinguishable from zero.

For the raw top-30 plain momentum portfolio (`PCMP`), the source reports approximately **0.56% per week**, also statistically insignificant in the untrimmed data.

The source reports a severe maximum-drawdown contrast in the raw strategies: approximately **-211.74% for PCMP** versus **-56.28% for SCMP**, highlighting extreme tail behavior and the instability of the rotating-universe momentum strategy.

After trimming 5% of the return distribution, the paper reports PCMP gross average return of approximately **0.93% per week** with statistical significance at the 1% level. Under the paper's stylized transaction-cost exercise:

- gross 5%-trimmed PCMP: approximately `0.93%` per week;
- after 25 bps cost: approximately `0.72%` per week, significant at the 5% level;
- after 50 bps cost: approximately `0.51%` per week, not statistically significant;
- after 75 bps cost: approximately `0.30%` per week, not statistically significant;
- reported break-even transaction cost for the 5%-trimmed variant: approximately `111.01 bps`.

A regression of the trimmed top-30 momentum portfolio on survivor momentum reports a positive survivor-momentum loading but an intercept of roughly **0.85%-0.88% per week**, statistically significant at the 5% level. The authors therefore reject the idea that the trimmed plain momentum payoff is simply leveraged survivor momentum.

The source concludes that the profitability of plain crypto momentum is highly sample-dependent and appears associated with coins that are temporarily active/investable, consistent with market frictions and limits to arbitrage.

### Negative evidence

- Survivor-coin momentum is not statistically significant in the source sample.
- Raw top-30 momentum is not statistically significant before trimming.
- The raw PCMP exhibits an extreme reported maximum drawdown of about `-211.74%`, which is economically incompatible with a simple unconstrained long-short implementation.
- Statistical significance emerges only after ex-post trimming of extreme observations; trimming realized future returns is not an executable trading rule.
- The 5%-trimmed PCMP loses statistical significance in the source's stylized exercise at 50 bps and 75 bps transaction costs.
- The source explicitly characterizes the profitable effect as highly sample-dependent.
- The authors argue that temporarily tradable / popularity-driven coins and financial frictions may explain the apparent premium, which creates direct doubt about live scalability and exitability.
- The survivor universe is defined ex post by remaining in the top 100 throughout the entire sample. It is useful for identification but cannot itself be used as a causal live selection rule without look-ahead.
- Aggregate CoinMarketCap closes do not establish executable venue-specific returns.
- Evidence ends in August 2024; persistence in the post-ETF / post-2024 market structure is unproven.

## Falsification plan

1. **Point-in-time universe reconstruction.** Build annual top-30 and liquidity-screened universes using only data available at each formation date. **research-defined falsification threshold:** reject a robust momentum interpretation if positive expectancy disappears when future survivor information and delisted-coin hindsight are removed.
2. **No-ex-post-trimming test.** Reproduce momentum without deleting or trimming realized future returns. **research-defined falsification threshold:** classify the apparent premium as non-tradable statistical fragility if significance exists only after ex-post return trimming.
3. **Ex-ante tail-control replacement.** Replace trimming with predeclared, point-in-time volatility/liquidity/participation caps. **research-defined falsification threshold:** reject the claim that trimming proxies for an executable risk control if no reasonable ex-ante rule recovers positive net expectancy.
4. **Strict post-source OOS.** Test from September 2024 onward with fixed formation and holding rules. **research-defined falsification threshold:** reject persistence if the long-short spread is non-positive after costs over the OOS sample or reverses across major subperiods.
5. **Liquidity/stability stratification.** Compare stable top-market-cap/high-depth assets against transient entrants and lower-liquidity assets. **research-defined falsification threshold:** weaken the friction mechanism if momentum is equally strong and net-profitable in the stable/high-liquidity cohort.
6. **Cost and shortability stress.** Use venue-specific fees, spreads, slippage, funding/borrow, and unavailable-short handling. **research-defined falsification threshold:** reject tradability if conservative net expectancy is non-positive or if more than 20% of intended short notional is historically unavailable at formation time.
7. **Exitability stress.** Measure depth deterioration during losing legs and delisting/popularity collapses. **research-defined falsification threshold:** reject practical exploitability if modeled liquidation impact consumes the median gross edge in the transient-coin cohort.
8. **Parameter perturbation.** Test adjacent formation windows (e.g. 21, 30, 42 days) and holding periods (e.g. 3, 7, 14 days) without retuning by subperiod. **research-defined falsification threshold:** weaken the mechanism if positive results occur only at one narrow lookback/holding pair.
9. **Placebo ranking.** Shuffle cross-sectional ranks within each formation week while preserving the universe and return distribution. **research-defined falsification threshold:** reject momentum-specific information if the observed spread is not materially stronger than the placebo distribution.
10. **Competing explanation controls.** Control for size, liquidity, volatility, listing age, recent attention, and idiosyncratic jump exposure. **research-defined falsification threshold:** weaken pure momentum interpretation if winner-minus-loser returns are fully explained by transient-liquidity or jump-risk exposures.
11. **Venue robustness.** Reconstruct on Binance, Coinbase/Kraken where available, and a consolidated executable-price dataset. **research-defined falsification threshold:** reject generalization if the effect is driven by one venue/data aggregation convention.
12. **Capacity test.** Apply conservative participation limits based on executable depth. **research-defined falsification threshold:** reject scalable alpha if positive net returns vanish at economically modest portfolio sizes.

Failure should result in rejection or reclassification, not unconstrained retuning.

## Crypto portability

**direct**.

The source directly studies cryptocurrency cross-sectional momentum. No traditional-market transplantation is required.

However, portability to a modern institutional crypto implementation is **adapted / unproven** because the source uses aggregate spot-market data and does not model:

- perpetual funding and basis;
- derivative availability for every short leg;
- exchange fragmentation;
- stablecoin quote effects;
- 24/7 rebalance timestamps;
- delistings and contract discontinuities during the holding period;
- real-time liquidity deterioration;
- custody and venue failure risk;
- market impact for transient coins.

The most important portability lesson is negative: a momentum rule that appears strongest in unstable coins may be least portable to a realistic executable portfolio.

## Limitations

- **not independently reproduced**.
- **contested:** broader crypto-momentum literature reports both strong and null effects; this paper specifically shows fragility under survivor/liquidity controls.
- **look-ahead by design for survivor identification:** the nine-coin survivor universe is selected using knowledge that each coin remains in the top 100 through August 2024. This is acceptable for the source's identification question but not as a live universe rule.
- **ex-post trimming:** statistical trimming removes realized tail observations after the fact and is not executable.
- **underspecified execution:** exact exchange, timestamp, order type, short mechanism, leverage, fill, and funding are not provided.
- **aggregate-price limitation:** CoinMarketCap data do not represent a guaranteed executable price on any single venue.
- **transaction-cost simplification:** 25/50/75 bps sensitivity does not reconstruct time-varying spread, slippage, borrow/funding, or impact.
- **tail instability:** raw PCMP maximum drawdown is reported near `-211.74%`, indicating leverage/path assumptions require careful reconstruction.
- **small survivor cross-section:** only nine coins meet the full-sample survivor definition.
- **sample dependence:** the source explicitly reports that the trimmed plain momentum result is highly sample-dependent.
- **sample end:** August 2024; modern ETF-era and 2025-2026 market structure are not covered.
