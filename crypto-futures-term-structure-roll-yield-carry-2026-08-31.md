# Crypto Futures Multi-Horizon Term Structure Carry and Roll Yield

## Provenance

- **Primary Academic Source:** Maik Schmeling, Andreas Schrimpf, and Karamfil Todorov, "Crypto Carry", *Management Science* (2026, Forthcoming; previously *BIS Working Paper No. 1087*, 2023). DOI: [10.1287/mnsc.2024.01524](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2024.01524) / SSRN: [4427497](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4427497).
- **Asset Pricing Foundation:** General multi-asset carry definition of Koijen, Moskowitz, Pedersen, and Vrugt (JFE 2018), extended to cryptocurrency futures term structure curves.
- **Empirical Scope:** Multi-horizon fixed-maturity calendar futures (weekly, bi-weekly, monthly, quarterly, bi-quarterly) and spot prices on Binance, OKX, Bybit, Deribit, and CME from 2019 to 2024.

## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported
Cryptocurrency futures curves trade in persistent and pronounced contango (futures price $F_t(T) > S_t$), with annualized basis spreads frequently exceeding $20\%\text{--}60\%$ per annum during bullish regimes. The steep upward slope of the futures term structure is generated by strong demand for leveraged upside exposure from retail speculators who are willing to pay a hefty convenience premium for embedded leverage. Limits to arbitrage—specifically counterparty risk, margin hair-cuts, capital fragmentation across venues, and balance-sheet capacity constraints of institutional arbitrageurs—prevent the immediate flattening of the futures curve. Consequently, market participants who take the other side (earning the term structure carry through shorting distant high-basis futures against spot or rolling short futures down the curve) capture substantial risk-adjusted roll yield.

### Research interpretation
The strategy is a **multi-horizon term structure carry and roll-yield harvesting strategy**:
1. **Carry Decomposition:** For a futures contract with time-to-maturity $\tau = T - t$, the generalized carry is defined as $C_t(\tau) = \frac{S_t - F_t(\tau)}{F_t(\tau) \cdot \tau}$. Under constant spot prices, the expected return from holding the futures position is directly determined by the convergence of the futures price to spot ($F_T \to S_T$), yielding roll-down decay.
2. **Curve-Slope Carry Rotation:** Different assets exhibit varying degrees of term structure slope (contango vs backwardation). By constructing a cross-sectional carry portfolio that goes long futures curves with the highest carry (flattest or backwardated curves) and short futures curves with the lowest carry (steepest contango), the strategy extracts carry premia while neutralizing broad market beta.
3. **Cash-and-Carry Roll-Down:** In a single-asset setting, the cash-and-carry trader shorts the highest-slope maturity contract $\tau_2$ while holding spot, rolling the position as maturity approaches to harvest steady basis decay without directional market exposure.

## Signal

- **Annualized Basis per Maturity $\tau$:**
  $$B_{i,t}(\tau) = \frac{1}{\tau} \left(\frac{F_{i,t}(\tau) - S_{i,t}}{S_{i,t}}\right)$$
  where $\tau \in \{7/365, 14/365, 30/365, 90/365, 180/365\}$ corresponds to available weekly, monthly, and quarterly contracts.
- **Term Structure Slope (Roll Yield Rate):**
  $$Slope_{i,t}(\tau_1, \tau_2) = \frac{B_{i,t}(\tau_2) - B_{i,t}(\tau_1)}{\tau_2 - \tau_1}$$
  measuring the steepness of contango between near ($\tau_1$) and far ($\tau_2$) maturities.
- **Cross-Sectional Carry Factor Rank:**
  - For each cryptocurrency $i$ in the universe, compute the generalized carry score:
    $$CarryScore_{i,t} = - B_{i,t}(\tau_{\text{quarterly}})$$
  - Sort the universe cross-sectionally:
    - **Long Portfolio:** Top 30% highest carry (assets with lowest contango or deepest backwardation).
    - **Short Portfolio:** Bottom 30% lowest carry (assets with highest contango / steepest upward curve).
  - Rebalance weekly (or bi-weekly matching contract cycles).
- **Alternative Single-Asset Basis Roll Execution:**
  - For BTC and ETH, initiate Cash-and-Carry when $B_{i,t}(\tau_{\text{quarterly}}) > r_{\text{borrow}} + \text{Threshold}_{\text{entry}}$ (e.g., basis $> 12\%$ annualized).
  - Unwind or roll to next deferred quarter when remaining time-to-maturity $\tau \le 7\text{ days}$ or basis converges to within $\le 2\%$.

## Required data

- **Universe:** BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP, LTC, BCH, DOGE, ADA calendar futures and underlying spot pairs.
- **Venues:** Binance Futures, OKX Deliverable Futures, Deribit Futures, Bybit Futures.
- **Timeframe:** Daily settlement and hourly snapshots of spot and futures curve prices across all traded delivery expiries.
- **Fields:** Spot price ($S_t$), futures contract price ($F_t(\tau)$), contract expiry timestamp ($T$), open interest ($OI(\tau)$), order book depth, lending/borrowing interest rates ($r_{\text{borrow}}$ on stablecoins).

## Execution assumptions

- **Trade Legs:** 
  - Cross-sectional version: Long near/deferred futures of high-carry assets, Short near/deferred futures of low-carry assets (beta-neutral, zero-net-investment).
  - Cash-and-Carry version: Long physical spot (or spot margin), Short delivery futures.
- **Roll Protocol:** Roll positions 5–7 days prior to expiry to avoid delivery pin risk and illiquidity during final settlement windows.
- **Margin & Capital Efficiency:** Unified margin / Portfolio Margin accounts required to offset spot collateral against short futures margin requirements.
- **Transaction Costs:** 2–4 bps maker/taker futures fee; 4–6 bps spot trading fee; 1–3 bps roll-over spread friction.

## Evidence

### Source-reported
- Schmeling, Schrimpf, and Todorov (2026) document that a cross-sectional crypto carry strategy across major cryptocurrency calendar futures achieves an annualized Sharpe ratio of $1.50$ to $2.10$ before transaction costs, and remains above $1.15$ after full transaction fees and margin costs.
- The carry factor is uncorrelated with traditional equity and fixed-income factors, and explains cross-sectional futures return dispersion beyond naive spot momentum.
- The basis spread is highly persistent with typical half-lives of 14 to 30 days, allowing low-frequency weekly/monthly rebalancing with minimal turnover drag.

### Independently reproduced
Not independently reproduced.

### Negative evidence
- **Margin Liquidation & Basis Blowout Risk:** During extreme market rallies (e.g., Q1 2021 bull run), retail demand can drive contango spreads to widen rapidly (e.g., basis expanding from 20% to 50%), generating severe interim mark-to-market drawdowns on short futures legs before eventual convergence.
- **Exchange / Custodial Risk:** Cash-and-carry requires holding collateral on centralized exchanges for extended holding periods (weeks to months), exposing capital to exchange credit risk.
- **Curve Illiquidity in Altcoin Delivery Futures:** Beyond BTC and ETH, altcoin calendar futures suffer from fragmented liquidity and wide bid-ask spreads on far expiries ($\tau > 90\text{ days}$).

## Falsification plan

1. **Margin Stress & Basis Widening Simulation:** Stress-test the short futures leg under a $+30\%$ sudden basis blowout over a 48-hour window. If simulated margin calls force premature position liquidation, the carry strategy's risk parameters are falsified.
2. **Roll Friction & Liquidity Degradation:** Simulate rolls on the final 3 days prior to expiration using realistic order book depth. If roll transaction costs exceed $25\%$ of annual carry yield, reject feasibility for contracts outside BTC/ETH.
3. **Carry Predictability Horizon:** Perform multi-horizon Fama-MacBeth predictive regressions of forward $h$-day futures returns on term structure slope $Slope_t$. If the slope coefficient is statistically insignificant ($t < 1.96$) at horizons $h \in [7, 30\text{ days}]$, the term-structure predictability hypothesis is rejected.

## Crypto portability

**Direct**: Exclusively designed for cryptocurrency calendar futures markets (Binance/OKX/Deribit quarterly delivery contracts) and spot-perp/calendar basis structures.

## Limitations

- **not independently reproduced**: Requires historical multi-expiry futures term structure datasets across Binance and OKX.
- **contract availability constraint**: Only a subset of crypto assets (top 10–20) have active, liquid fixed-maturity calendar futures listings.
- **collateral interest rate drag**: Rising DeFi/CeFi stablecoin borrow rates reduce net carry margins during high-rate macro regimes.
