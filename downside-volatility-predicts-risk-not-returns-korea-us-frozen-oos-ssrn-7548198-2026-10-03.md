## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The paper is explicitly a replication and validation study, not an anomaly proposal: `The paper is a replication and validation study; it does not propose a new anomaly.` (Section 1). It puts two questions under one design in two markets, Korea and the United States:

1. **Risk channel (the claim that survives).** Negative returns are followed by higher volatility than positive returns of the same size - the leverage effect (Black, 1976; Christie, 1982). Splitting realized variance into upside and downside semivariance (Barndorff-Nielsen, Kinnebrock and Shephard, 2010; Patton and Sheppard, 2015) is reported to forecast volatility better than symmetric benchmarks. The source's mechanism for the surviving result is therefore: **a large decline carries information about the magnitude of tomorrow's variance, symmetrically about both the upside and the downside component of that variance.**
2. **Return channel (the claim that fails).** A parallel literature reports that directional asymmetry computed from high-frequency data - realized skewness (Amaya et al., 2015) and signed-jump measures - forecasts the cross-section of returns, and that lottery-type characteristics (maximum daily return, idiosyncratic volatility; Bali et al., 2011; Ang et al., 2006) are linked to low subsequent returns. When only daily returns are available these measures must be approximated, and the source's stated economic question is whether that directional information predicts returns once short-term reversal (Jegadeesh, 1990; Lehmann, 1990; Nagel, 2012) is controlled for.
3. **Short-sale channel.** Binding short-sale constraints may delay the incorporation of negative information (Miller, 1977; Diamond and Verrecchia, 1987), so the 2023-2025 Korean ban is used to ask whether the cross-sectional pricing of the asymmetry and lottery measures changed - a difference-in-differences question, not a trading rule.
4. **Regime-conditional leverage.** The common presumption is that the leverage effect strengthens in turbulent markets; the source tests this with an online regime indicator and reports the opposite direction in the U.S.

### Research interpretation

Normalized as a falsifiable two-part hypothesis with a shared measurement layer:

- **H-risk (magnitude):** `HAR` / `LHAR` forecasts built from daily Parkinson range variance and sign-split squared daily log returns carry out-of-sample information about next-period variance that a symmetric `HAR` benchmark does not. Candidate mechanism: heterogeneous persistence of downside shocks plus rebound dynamics after sharp declines. This is a **risk-forecast** claim, and the source's own practice recommendation is `If volatility is predictable but returns are not, volatility forecasts are best used to size positions.` (Section 8).
- **H-return (direction):** daily-frequency directional asymmetry (monthly relative signed jump variation `RSJ = (RS+ - RS-)/RV`, weekly realized skewness from the week's five daily returns, and stock-level predicted upside/downside semivariance shares) forecasts the cross-section of returns **beyond** same-period return reversal. Mechanism candidates the source entertains and then rejects: lottery demand / variance risk premium priced off skewness, and delayed incorporation of negative information under short constraints.
- **Component roles (hybrid structure of the allocation half):**
  - Regime: two-state online statistical jump model on volatility features (half-lives 5, 10, 21 days; penalty lambda = 5), re-estimated monthly on past data only and filtered online - turbulent share 25.5 percent (U.S.) and 12.2 percent (Korea) in the development sample.
  - Primary signal: `LHAR` / `log-LHAR` next-day (U.S., horizon h1) or next-month (Korea, horizon h22) log realized-variance forecast.
  - Position sizing: volatility targeting, exposure scaled by target / forecast volatility (rule `S1v2`).
  - Alternative (falsified in-sample-to-out-of-sample) regime rule: hold the ETF in calm regimes and cash in turbulent ones (rule `S1 A`).
  - Cross-sectional selection (falsified): rule `S4` buys stocks with high predicted upside semivariance and sells when the predicted upside-to-downside ratio deteriorates; rule `S5` is the frozen weekly-realized-skewness decile design.
- The source does **not** claim that each component contributes alpha; the paper's own conclusion is the opposite: `asymmetry is useful for forecasting risk, not returns`. Do not assume any component adds return; ablation gates F4-F11 below exist for exactly that reason.

## Signal

Everything in this section is **source-reported** unless explicitly tagged `research-proposed`.

**Formation timestamp and availability.** Index/ETF rules operate on daily bars: the variance forecast for day `t+1` is formed after the close of day `t` and, in the allocation backtests, executed with `Daily rebalancing with a one-day execution lag` (Table 10 note) - i.e. signal at close of `t`, trade at/after the next open, a **next-bar** convention. Regime state `D_t` uses `the previous day's online regime` (Table 11 note) and is `re-estimated monthly on past data only`. Cross-sectional signals are formed at month end (monthly `RSJ`, `MAX`, `IVOL`, `REV`) or at week end (weekly realized skewness) and held for one month / one week. Timezone and exchange-session convention for Korean and U.S. daily bars is **not stated in source** (`data gap`).

**Lookback / parameterization (all source-reported, all fixed on the development sample):**

- Daily variance: Parkinson (1980) range estimator in percent-squared from open, high, low, close. Semivariances: squared daily log returns split by sign, `RS+_t = r_t^2 1{r_t > 0}` and `RS-_t = r_t^2 1{r_t < 0}`.
- `LHAR` regressors: `logRV_t`, `logRV^(w)` (5-day average), `logRV^(m)` (22-day average), plus `L_t = max(-r_t, 0)` (today's decline in percent) and its 5-day and 22-day averages. Estimated as `logRV_{t+1} = c + b_d logRV_t + b_w logRV^(w)_t + b_m logRV^(m)_t + gamma_d L_t + gamma_w L^(w)_t + gamma_m L^(m)_t + eps_{t+1}` (Equation 1).
- `RLHAR` adds the turbulent dummy `D_t` and its interactions with `logRV_t` and `L_t`.
- Forecast protocol: `expanding-window walk-forward scheme re-estimated every 22 trading days after a 1,000-day burn-in`, evaluated with QLIKE loss and Diebold-Mariano tests. Development-sample evaluation windows: U.S. Sample A `N` 4027 (LHAR) and Korea Sample A `N` 3952; Sample B starts later because the regime indicator needs its own burn-in (`N` 3027 / 2952).
- Semivariance regression (Equation 2): future average semivariance over `h` in {1, 5, 22} days on past `RS+` and `RS-` at the three horizons; total effects `Sigma beta+` and `Sigma beta-` and their difference, Newey-West lags `max(22, 2h)`.
- Monthly `RSJ = (RS+ - RS-)/RV` from daily returns; monthly `MAX` (maximum daily return), `IVOL`, and monthly `REV`. Weekly realized skewness computed from the week's five daily returns; a robustness variant computes it over the past 22 trading days.
- Frozen cross-sectional selection criteria (source-reported, pre-specified): `neutralized IC |t| >= 2, same sign in both halves, monotone quintiles, positive long leg`. None of the five candidate signals met them in either market.
- Frozen rule sets (Table 12): `S1` regime switch with jump-model lambda = 5, frozen 2026-09-27 09:32:48, SHA-256 first 12 `c6bf0fe3c487`; `S2S3` empty (no signal selected), frozen 2026-09-28 15:18:51, `44136fa355b3`; `S4` semivariance stock selection, frozen 2026-09-28 21:44:08, `84a1faf3991e`; `S5` weekly skewness design, frozen 2026-09-29 13:42:08, `8292324739ed`; `S1v2` volatility-targeting variant, frozen 2026-09-29 21:06:20, `a4a0323a5979`.

**Entry / exit / holding period.**

- Allocation rules: `S1 A` holds the index ETF in the calm regime and cash in the turbulent regime; `S1v2` scales exposure by the ratio of a target to forecast volatility (U.S. `S1v2 = LHAR h1`, Korea `S1v2 = LHAR h22`); comparators are naive 22-day realized-volatility targeting, a 200-day moving-average rule, and buy-and-hold. Daily rebalance, one-day execution lag, `a 10-point no-trade band for volatility-targeting rules`. U.S. cash earns the 3-month T-bill rate; Korean cash earns zero. Holding is continuous (the position is the rule's target weight).
- `S5` weekly skewness: sort stocks into deciles at the end of each week, hold `decile 1 (lowest skewness) minus decile 10` for the next week. Long-only variant holds decile 1. Rebalance weekly, i.e. 52 two-leg turnovers per year before overlap.
- `S4`: buy stocks with high predicted upside semivariance and sell when the predicted upside-to-downside ratio deteriorates. **The precise deterioration threshold is not stated in source** - the rule is named but not fully specified in the printed artifact (`underspecified`), and its full definition lives only in the unpinned-unfetched `config/frozen/S4.json` (see gate F2).
- Direction rules: a `reversal` rule predicts the opposite of the past dominant direction of variation; the baseline always predicts the sample majority. Window `k` for the past share and horizon `h` for the future share run over daily and intraday (60-, 10-, 5-minute) data; the full grid of 84 specifications is **not enumerated** in the printed artifact (`data gap`).

**Position sizing.** Source-reported: sign-only / target-weight scaling in the allocation rules; equal- and value-weighted decile portfolios in the cross-sectional rules. No leverage, margin, Kelly or risk-parity sizing appears anywhere (census `kelly` 0, `leverage` 9 all in the leverage-effect sense). Whether the regime-switch rule holds full notional or partial is implied by `holds the ETF ... and cash` (full/cash, i.e. 1/0) but is **not explicitly stated** (`underspecified`).

**Re-entry / precedence / ties.** Tie handling in the decile sorts, simultaneous-signal precedence, and the treatment of stale or missing bars are **not stated in source** (`data gap`). Missing forward returns in the S4 comparison are `set to zero` (Table 8 note) - a source-reported and materially consequential choice.

**Research-proposed (not in the source):** using this signal family on crypto, converting the 5/22/22-trading-day windows onto a 24/7 clock, using the Parkinson range on 24/7 candles, and any position cap or stop beyond the source's own no-trade band. All such choices are `research-proposed` and none may be attributed to the source.

### Negative evidence

1. The paper's own headline null: `Overall, asymmetry is useful for forecasting risk, not returns`, and `The paper is a replication and validation study; it does not propose a new anomaly.`
2. None of the five candidate cross-sectional signals met the pre-specified selection criteria (neutralized IC `|t| >= 2`, same sign in both halves, monotone quintiles, positive long leg) in either market (Section 5.1).
3. Every net weekly-skewness long-short cell is negative: U.S. development `-3.9`, U.S. test `-11.8`, Korea development `-47.2`, Korea test `-76.7` bps per week (Table 6).
4. The skewness Fama-MacBeth coefficient collapses under controls: U.S. development `-6.35` to `-0.25` (p `0.88`), Korea development `-10.96` to `-2.84` (p `0.33`) (Table 6 Panel B).
5. Weekly skewness computed from five daily returns is essentially a re-measurement of the week's own return (rank correlation `0.760` U.S. / `0.816` Korea against `about 0.3` for the 22-day measure); with the 22-day measure the gross long-short spread is insignificant in every sample (Section 5.2, Table 17).
6. Two-leg turnover of about `360%` per week erases the development-sample U.S. spread and converts the Korean spread into a `47 bps` per week loss (Section 5.2).
7. The long-only lowest-skewness decile underperforms the equal-weighted universe in all four cells: `25.7` vs `29.8`, `16.6` vs `23.1`, `-15.7` vs `10.4`, `-29.3` vs `18.3` (Table 6 Panel A).
8. Direction of tomorrow's variation cannot be forecast: `The reversal rule never beats the baseline significantly and is significantly worse in 24 cases` (Table 7).
9. The single Holm-surviving direction coefficient (U.S. development `beta = -0.067`) has a reversal hit rate of `52.8%` against a `54.9%` baseline - i.e. significance without predictive value - and vanishes out of sample (`beta = -0.008`, p `0.723`) (Section 6.1).

## Crypto portability

**`adapted` (mechanism plausibly portable, sign and economic value `unproven`).**

The source contains **zero** crypto content: no cryptocurrency, perpetual, futures, funding, mark or index price, liquidation, venue or 24/7 reference appears anywhere in the pinned text (`crypto` 0, `bitcoin` 0, `ethereum` 0, `perpetual` 0, `funding` 0). Under the repository rule that a traditional-asset mechanism may not be labeled `direct` unless the source itself demonstrates it in crypto markets, this record is `adapted`, and the crypto version is a `research-proposed` ported hypothesis rather than crypto empirical evidence.

Portability risks that must be resolved before any port:

- **Session structure.** The source's universes are exchange-traded indices and stocks on a roughly 252-session calendar with an explicit close-to-open overnight gap. Appendix C's only suggestive finding - that the daily downside-upside difference `is positive for overnight moves at all horizons with unadjusted p-values of 0.02 to 0.03` and `suggests that the daily asymmetry may originate in overnight gaps` - is exactly the feature crypto 24/7 markets do not have; without overnight gaps the hypothesized origin of the asymmetry may simply not exist. Candle-boundary conventions (UTC day vs exchange day) are `research-proposed`.
- **Window mapping.** The `5`-day, `22`-day and `252`-session conventions map to `research-proposed` crypto equivalents (e.g. 120-hour and 528-hour windows on 24/7 hourly bars); no source guidance exists.
- **Instruments and frictions.** Spot versus perpetual differences, funding payments, mark/index price divergence, auto-deleveraging, listing and delisting churn, stablecoin quotation effects, venue fragmentation, custody and withdrawal risk, and borrow/short availability on crypto are all unaddressed by the source and are `data gap` here.
- **Cost model.** The source's `2 / 8 / 5 / 10` bps one-way assumptions and its `0.18%` Korean equity transaction tax have no crypto analogue; taker/maker schedules, spread and impact on 24/7 books would be entirely `research-proposed`.
- **Cross-sectional layer.** The `S4`/`S5` stock-selection designs depend on a stable, delisted-inclusive equity panel; crypto listing churn and survivorship would need an explicit point-in-time universe rule, which is `research-proposed`.
- **What could port.** The risk half (HAR/LHAR-style downside-semivariance forecasting used only for sizing) is the most plausible port, because it needs only OHLC and makes no claim about direction; even there, the source's own Korean test-sample failure (`0 of 27`) shows the risk result is already fragile across a single market change, so a crypto port starts at `unproven`.

Crypto portability is not authorization to trade.

- Identification: `RSJ` and daily realized skewness are statistically inseparable from same-period reversal at daily frequency, so the return-channel null is a statement about the daily approximation, explicitly not a refutation of the high-frequency literature.
- Sample and regime: one index pair and two stock panels, one 2021-2026 test window that the source itself describes as unusual (Korean index `rose about 180% between September 2023 and September 2026, with eleven daily moves beyond +/- 8%`); the Korean test-sample risk replication fails outright.

- Execution/capacity: no spread, slippage, impact, borrow or capacity model at all; long-short results are net of a fixed bps cost only, with borrow explicitly not deducted.
- Source quality: SSRN preprint with `13` downloads and `0` citations; single independent researcher; extensive declared AI assistance in code, analysis and drafting; internal rather than external freezing.
- Reproducibility: Korean raw data cannot be redistributed under the KRX Open API terms; the replication package's frozen-hash and access-log claims were **not** re-derived by this run (gates F2 and F3 are open).
