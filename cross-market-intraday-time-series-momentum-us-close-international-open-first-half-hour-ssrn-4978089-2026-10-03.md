## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The authors' story is an overnight-information-diffusion and lead-lag argument (PDF Sections 1 and 4.1):

- The U.S. market `plays a leading role in the international stock market` (attributed to Rapach, Strauss and Zhou 2013), so U.S. closing information is `an important component of overnight information for other international stock markets, thereby acting as a significant overnight influence on other markets upon their opening`.
- Because of time-zone differences `the US market close typically precedes the opening of international markets`, and `the previous day's trading hours for US stocks do not overlap with and lead the same day's trading hours for other markets` (asserted from Figure 1, which is itself absent - contradiction 1).
- The first half hour is chosen `because it is the first freely tradable period after overnight period, featuring higher liquidity and lower transaction costs, with a large concentration of institutional traders trading purely based on overnight information during this period`, while `overnight information is usually assimilated and reflected in stock returns within the initial half-hour of market trading` (citing Barclay and Hendershott 2003, Gao et al. 2018, Lou et al. 2019).
- Behavioural channel: `many institutional investors place significant emphasis on the closing prices of the US stocks, particularly the last half-hour's trading, which are deemed crucial for trading decisions`, and `cross-market investors often adjust their international stock portfolio allocations based on these US market closing insights`; Vayanos and Woolley (2013) and Bogousslavsky (2016) are invoked for cross-market rebalancing and infrequent-rebalancing momentum.
- Stylised timing facts printed in Section 2.2: `the overnight time span between the closing of a trading day and the opening of the next day is 17.5 hours, and the intraday trading span is only 6.5 hours in comparison`, and high-frequency traders `typically avoid holding positions overnight`.

The source claims statistical predictability in-sample and out-of-sample and economic significance for the CITSM rule, and conditions profitability on market characteristics (liquidity, information uncertainty, information discreteness). All of that is a `source-reported` claim, not a fact established here.

### Research interpretation

Falsifiable reading of the same mechanism, in our words:

- **Regime / horizon:** a cross-market, overnight, session-boundary spillover: information arriving at the dominant reference market's close is only partially digested overnight and is re-priced in the peripheral markets' opening half-hour. This is a *lead-lag / slow-diffusion* channel, not a within-market microstructure habit.
- **Signal construction:** a binary sign transfer - the peripheral market takes the opposite or same side as the reference market's final 30 minutes, with no magnitude term, no threshold and no cross-sectional ranking.
- **Competing explanations that must be ruled out:** (a) a pure opening-auction microstructure effect (the first half-hour of any session is high-volatility and spread-rich, so a sign coin-flip could show drift through asymmetric execution); (b) the overnight-return channel already documented by Lou, Polk and Skouras (2019), i.e. the U.S. last half-hour is merely a noisy proxy for the global overnight risk factor; (c) a beta/size or sector exposure of the international indices to the U.S. close rather than an information-transfer premium; (d) the source's own full-sample extreme-price filter leaking future dispersion into the data preparation.
- **What would make it alpha:** the sign rule must survive net costs, borrow, timing slippage at the open, the parent single-market intraday-momentum benchmark, and multiplicity across 15 markets, three horizons and three conditioning variables. None of that is demonstrated in the source; it is what the falsification plan tests.

## Signal

Normalized construction, as far as the source prints it. Anything not printed is marked `research-proposed` or `underspecified`.

- **Signal variable and formation timestamp (source-reported).** `R_L,t = P_close,t / P_L30,t - 1` (Equation 2) is the last half-hour return of the U.S. index on day t-1, where `P_L30,t` is `the first price in the last 30 minutes before closing` and `P_close,t` is the closing price. The signal is known at the U.S. close of day t-1 and is acted on at the international market's open on day t. Time zone is given only through the Figure 1 UTC discussion (Figure absent), and the exact timestamp of the `P_F30` print is `not stated in source` -> `underspecified`. `research-proposed` convention for any test: stamp both the signal and the entry at the exchange-local official open, convert to UTC, and assert signal-time < entry-time on every session (Gate F2).
- **Target return (source-reported).** `R_F,t = P_F30,t / P_Open,t - 1` (Equation 1), `where P_Open,t is the opening price on day t and P_F30,t represents the last price in the first 30 minutes after the opening of day t` (Section 2.2). The paper deliberately drops overnight information from both legs: `Our method diverges from previous research by omitting overnight information from the trading signal of the half-hour returns`.
- **Entry (source-reported, Equation 10).** `if the timing signal from the US last half-hour is positive, we create a long position at the opening of the international stock market; alternatively, we create a short position at the market open` - i.e. long when `sign(R_US_L,t-1) > 0`, short when `sign(R_US_L,t-1) <= 0`. Zero is grouped with the short side by the printed inequality.
- **Exit (source-reported).** `We then close the position at half an hour after the opening`, so the position is held for exactly one half-hour and is never held overnight (`high-frequency traders typically avoid holding positions overnight`).
- **Holding period and cadence (source-reported).** One 30-minute position per trading day per market, re-formed every session; no overlapping positions are described. Position sizing, leverage and weighting across the 15 markets are `not stated in source` -> `data gap`.
- **Benchmarks (source-reported).** `BH` buys the index at the start of the sample and holds to the end; `AL` goes long at every open and closes 30 minutes later. The paper's economic-significance claim is relative to these two.
- **Conditioning variables (source-reported, used for explanation rather than for the base rule).** Intraday volatility (Equations 11-12), information discreteness - `The information discreteness (ID) is defined following Lim et al. (2018)`, with `ID_t = sign(r_F_t) x (%neg_t - %pos_t)` written here in ASCII from Equation 13 - and the Corwin-Schultz (2012) high-low percentage spread averaged over overlapping 5-minute intervals (Equations 14-17) as the liquidity / information-uncertainty proxy. Using these to *select* markets or days is `research-proposed`; the source only reports sorts.
- **Parameters (all source-reported, fixed by the paper).** 30-minute intervals; sign-only (no threshold); 16 MSCI developed indices with 15 traded; USD-denominated index data; ETF variant over 13 ETFs after dropping EIRL and PGAL; Newey-West (1987) standard errors; Welch-Goyal (2008) out-of-sample R-squared with Clark-West (2007) MSPE adjustment, initial estimation window = the market's first two years, both rolling and expanding; Fama-MacBeth (1973) cross-sectional regressions; recession dummies from the OECD/NBER indicator on FRED; four cost scenarios 0 / 0.02% / 0.06% / 0.10% per trade and a three-tier dollar fee schedule (Section 7).
- **Fully specified or underspecified?** The sign rule and the two 30-minute return definitions are reconstructable in principle; the *tradable* rule is `underspecified` - index identities, sample dates, trading hours, entry price convention (official open print versus first tradable quote), cross-market weighting, sizing, tie handling around zero, holiday/half-day sessions, daylight-saving transition days and execution mechanics are all `not stated in source` or `data gap`.

## Execution assumptions

- **Source assumptions.** Index results (Sections 4-6) are back-tests on index returns with **no** friction model of any kind (census: `slippage`, `market impact`, `latency`, `fill`, `borrow`, `gross`, `net of` all 0). The ETF variant (Section 7) is the only execution-realistic block, and it does three things: it crosses the **spread** by computing the long profit from the opening **ask** to the 30-minute **bid** and the short profit from the opening **bid** to the 30-minute **ask**; it adds **commission and fee costs into each trading transaction** using the Gao et al. (2018) schedule (`a higher fee of $10 per trade for casual traders and a lower fee of $2 per trade for active traders when transaction sizes exceed $10,000, in addition to a moderate transaction fee of $6 per trade`); and it reports four per-trade cost scenarios - `no cost (NC), low cost of 0.02% per trade (LC), medium cost of 0.06% per trade (MC), and high cost of 0.10% per trade (HC)`.
- **Our reading.** Index-based numbers are **gross of every friction**; ETF-based numbers are spread-inclusive and commission-inclusive at the stated scenarios but still exclude impact, latency, borrow, short-sale restrictions, participation and financing. No figure in this record is net-of-everything.
- **`research-proposed` operational choices for any test (none of these come from the source):** enter at the first tradable quote after the official open (with official-open and +5-minute variants as timing controls); exit at the 30-minute mark with a market-on-close-to-the-window rule; cost ladder 0 / 5 / 10 / 20 / 50 bps one-way *plus* the source's 0.02 / 0.06 / 0.10% per-trade scenarios; explicit borrow cost of 0 / 50 / 100 bp per year on the short leg and a long-only variant; participation cap of 20% of first-half-hour ETF volume with the implied capacity reported; equal weighting across the 15 markets and a single-market-reported variant; an assertion that the signal timestamp precedes the entry timestamp in UTC on 100% of sessions.
- **Failure handling:** if the signal cannot be evaluated before the entry price on any session, or a market's first half-hour has no two-sided quote, that market-day is dropped and the drop rate is reported rather than silently re-weighted (`research-defined`).
- **Latency / fills / partial fills:** `data gap` in the source; a 30-minute holding window with open-auction entry is unusually sensitive to latency, so any result must be re-run with a delayed entry (`research-proposed`, Gate F2).

### Negative evidence

1. The pinned artifact contains **no tables and no figures at all** - Tables 1-12 and Figure 1 exist only as placeholders and the PDF holds zero image objects - so every printed statistic, the index list, trading hours, sample dates, t-statistics and standard errors are unverifiable from the primary source.
2. The sample window is never stated in prose; `we use the longest available sample period for each market` is the only guidance, so cross-market results pool heterogeneous, unknown windows and no reader can determine the economic regime being claimed.
3. Cost fragility is severe and monotone: at `0.10% per trade` only 3 of 13 ETF markets stay positive, and the rule trades **once per market per session** (roughly one round trip per day, ~250 per year per market), so a 10 bp per-trade drag compounds into a very large annual haircut that the source never annualises.
4. The abstract and conclusion assert cost-robustness without qualification while the body reports the 3-of-13 high-cost outcome (contradiction 2).
5. Turnover is never reported (`turnover` appears only as a session-liquidity aside and a reference title), so no reader can convert the gross annualised figures into net figures, and there is no capacity or participation analysis (`capacity` appears only in a reference title, `participation` 0).
6. The short leg is created at every open (`we create a short position at the market open`) with **no** borrow availability, borrow cost, recall risk or short-sale-restriction modeling (`borrow` 0, `short sale` 0, `shorting` 0), and shorting a national index is not directly implementable at all - only the ETF variant is, and even there borrow is unmodeled.
7. No execution realism beyond the ETF bid/ask cross: `slippage`, `market impact`, `latency`, `fill`, `maker`, `taker` and `execution` are all 0, yet entry is at the opening print and exit is 30 minutes later - the two highest-variance, most spread-sensitive minutes of the session.
8. Point-in-time leakage in data preparation: extreme prices are removed using `three standard deviations from the average volatility of the data over the entire sample period`, a statistic computed over the whole sample including the future; the effect of rebuilding the filter recursively is never tested.
9. No multiplicity control anywhere: `multiple testing`, `bonferroni`, `holm`, `benjamini`, `fdr`, `deflated`, `confidence interval`, `bootstrap` and `p-value` are all 0, while the paper runs 15 markets x several regressions x 3 horizons x 3 characteristics x sorts x strategies.
10. The out-of-sample design starts from only `the first two years sample of each market`, and the expanding-window results are the weakest (7 of 15 markets), which is the classic signature of a fragile predictive relation.
11. The headline `88%` Australian annualised alpha and the `>20%` Australian/Austrian returns are extraordinary for a strategy invested 30 minutes per day; with no table, no t-statistic and no annualisation convention printed, they are unverifiable and prima facie implausible.
12. The annualisation convention for a position that is invested only 0.5 hours out of a 6.5-hour session is never defined, and no risk-free rate appears anywhere (`risk-free` 0), so the construction of the reported Sharpe ratios is `underspecified`.
13. Cross-sectional sorts run on 15 assets split 30/40/30 (about 4 / 7 / 4 names per group) and Fama-MacBeth regressions on the same 15 assets are extremely low-powered; the reported R-squared values of 15.81%, 17.68% and 36.45% are therefore fragile.
14. The double-sorting result that would adjudicate contradiction 3 is explicitly withheld (`not reported for brevity but are available upon request`), so the conditioning rule cannot be checked.
15. The exclusion rules are inconsistent across samples: index markets are included regardless of history length while ETFs require `at least ten years`, so the index and ETF results describe different populations.
16. The timing claim (`the previous day's trading hours for US stocks do not overlap with and lead the same day's trading hours for other markets`) rests on an absent figure and is never tested on daylight-saving transition days, half-days or late-opening sessions; no session-level audit is reported.
17. Source quality: a single unreviewed SSRN preprint (3 versions, posted 7 Oct 2024), an AI-assisted-writing declaration, no code or data release (`github` 0, `zenodo` 0, `replication` 0), and a landing abstract that omits one sentence present in the PDF abstract.
18. Publication-bias and novelty concerns: the paper positions itself as `the first to provide evidence on cross-market intraday momentum` while building directly on Gao et al. (2018) and Li et al. (2022), never benchmarks against those parent single-market intraday-momentum strategies, and reports no deflated-Sharpe or holdout check for the 15-market search.

## Falsification plan

Each gate states a **Threshold** (a `research-defined falsification threshold`, not a source value) and an **Action** that follows failure. Nothing here is claimed by the source.

- **F1 - Prose-claim reproduction.** *Threshold:* on minute data for the 16 MSCI developed indices, re-derive the source's prose claims - 11 markets with a positive U.S. coefficient, 10 of them significant at 1%, 13 of 15 markets with positive annualised CITSM returns, 12 with positive Sharpe, and the Section 7 cost ladder (13/13 positive at NC and LC, 8 at MC with 5 beating BH, 3 at HC) - within one market of each stated count. **Action:** if any count differs by more than one market, the Source-reported block is treated as unverifiable and status stays `research-only` with `not independently reproduced` retained.
- **F2 - Signal-timing and look-ahead gate.** *Threshold:* (a) the U.S. signal timestamp precedes the international entry timestamp in UTC on 100% of sessions, including daylight-saving transition weeks, and (b) entering at the first tradable quote after the open (rather than the official open print) retains at least 50% of the source's stated outperforming-market counts. **Action:** if any session has the signal arriving after entry, or the delayed-entry variant drops below half, classify the finding as timing-look-ahead contaminated and stop.
- **F3 - Cost ladder.** *Threshold:* one-way costs of 0 / 5 / 10 / 20 / 50 bps **plus** the source's 0.02 / 0.06 / 0.10% per-trade scenarios and the three-tier dollar fee schedule; require a positive mean net annualised return in at least 8 of 15 markets at 10 bps one-way and in at least 5 of 13 markets at the source's 0.06% scenario (mirroring the source's own 8-of-13 at MC but with our execution). **Action:** on failure, report the strategy as cost-fragile and reject further work.
- **F4 - Turnover and capacity gate.** *Threshold:* daily one-round-trip trading implies roughly 250 one-way turns per year per market; require the whole book to deploy with participation capped at 20% of first-half-hour ETF volume and with reported capacity above 10 million USD notional per market. **Action:** on failure, mark capacity `unproven` and downgrade `confidence` to `low`.
- **F5 - Borrow and long-only gate.** *Threshold:* the short leg is borrowable at <= 100 bp per year in at least 80% of sessions per market (by volume weight), and a long-only variant retains at least 50% of the long-short spread. **Action:** on failure, the result is recorded as long-leg-dependent and no short-leg claim may be carried forward.
- **F6 - Multiplicity gate.** *Threshold:* Benjamini-Hochberg at q = 0.10 across 15 markets x 3 regressions x 3 horizons x 3 characteristics x 2 sort families, plus a deflated-Sharpe check if a backtest is built. **Action:** on failure, the CITSM effect is reported as indistinguishable from multiple testing.
- **F7 - Sub-period stability gate.** *Threshold:* split each market's realized sample into three equal calendar blocks; the CITSM mean must be positive in at least two of three blocks in at least 10 of 15 markets, with |t| >= 2 in at least one block for the pooled panel. **Action:** on failure, report regime dependence and drop any claim of a persistent premium.
- **F8 - Untouched forward window.** *Threshold:* a frozen 2024-01 to 2026-12 window (post-dating the 7 Oct 2024 posting) shows a positive pooled net mean at 10 bps one-way with |t| >= 2. **Action:** on failure, treat the whole result as an in-sample artifact of the unreported sample window.
- **F9 - Placebo and benchmark-ablation gate.** *Threshold:* the sign rule must beat three controls - a random-sign rule with the identical position count, the market's own last-half-hour momentum (the single-market ITSM of Gao et al. 2018 / Li et al. 2022), and the always-long AL benchmark - by at least 3% per year net at 10 bps in a pooled panel. **Action:** on failure, the cross-market content is not identified and the record collapses to a restatement of opening-window volatility.
- **F10 - Incremental-value gate.** *Threshold:* CITSM adds >= 20% of its gross annualised return over the better of BH and AL after costs, replicating the source's Table 8 alpha claim in at least 8 of 15 markets. **Action:** on failure, the strategy is a beta/timing artifact rather than alpha, and the Source-reported alpha block is marked non-reproduced.
- **F11 - Conditioning-ablation gate.** *Threshold:* the Section 6 claims must hold out of sample - high-volatility terciles keep a positive L-S in at least 10 of 15 markets, low own-spread terciles keep a positive S-L, and the U-shape-versus-monotone US-spread question (contradiction 3) resolves in favour of exactly one rule. **Action:** on failure, drop all conditioning claims and keep only the unconditional sign rule under test.

## Crypto portability

**Portability: `adapted` (sign and magnitude `unproven`).**

- The abstract mechanism - a dominant reference venue's final session window being digested by peripheral venues at their next session open - transfers to crypto in principle: the U.S. equity close is a well-known risk-on/off event for crypto, and BTC/USDT can act as the reference asset for altcoin or venue-specific opens.
- What does not port directly: crypto has **no open**, so the paper's `first half hour after the opening` (Equation 1) is undefined; there is no official close-to-open gap; 24/7 sessions remove the overnight window that the whole mechanism requires; the sign rule depends on a *calendar* asymmetry that crypto does not have.
- If redefined as a venue/session proxy (e.g. the CME futures open, or the U.S. cash-session close as the reference window and a UTC-defined 30-minute crypto window as the target), the construction becomes `research-proposed` and loses source backing for the timing convention.
- Crypto-specific frictions the source never addresses, all `data gap`: perpetual funding and settlement, mark/index price divergence versus index/ETF spot, liquidation and auto-deleveraging, venue fragmentation (which venue's open?), listing churn and survivorship, stablecoin quotation, and borrow/short availability on individual tokens.
- Sign risk is real: this repository's own session-boundary records report both continuation and reversal at session cuts in crypto (see Related Wiki records), so the sign must be re-established before any port. Conclusion: `adapted` for mechanism, `unproven` for sign, size and cost survival; crypto portability is not authorization to trade.

## Limitations

- `underspecified`: sample start/end dates, the exact index list, trading hours, entry and exit price conventions, cross-market weighting, sizing, tie handling around a zero signal, holiday/half-day sessions, daylight-saving transitions, the annualisation convention for a 30-minute daily position, the Sharpe ratio's risk-free convention (none printed), and the version identity among the three SSRN versions.

- `not stated in source`: any stop, take-profit, sizing rule, leverage limit or portfolio-construction rule across the 15 markets; the source presents one position per market per session plus BH and AL benchmarks.
- `not independently reproduced`: every performance number in this record; only source integrity, census counts, digest arithmetic and internal prose consistency were checked.
- `unproven`: the overnight-information-diffusion mechanism as distinct from an opening-auction or global-overnight-beta explanation; the `88%` and `>20%` headline returns; any claim that the effect survives net costs in most markets; the conditioning rules (contradiction 3); crypto portability.
- Source-quality limitations: one unreviewed SSRN preprint with three versions, an AI-assisted-writing declaration, no code, no data, no ORCID, a landing abstract that differs from the PDF abstract, and landing-versus-PDF affiliation artifacts for two of the four authors.
- Identification limitations: heterogeneous unreported sample windows across 15 markets, a full-sample statistic inside the cleaning pipeline, a U.S.-only recession dummy applied to international markets, no factor or beta adjustment anywhere, and no control for the parent single-market intraday-momentum strategy.
- Scope: developed-market equity indices only, one signal market (the U.S.), one horizon (30 minutes), one position per session, and results that are gross for the index variant and spread/commission-only for the ETF variant.
