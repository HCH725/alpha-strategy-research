# PRISM-VQ: Vector-Quantized Discrete Latent Factors with Financial Priors for Cross-Sectional Stock Ranking

## Provenance

- **Primary paper:** arXiv:2605.13407v1, submitted 13 May 2026
- **Authors:** Namhyoung Kim (RiskX Corp. + Hanyang University), Jae Wook Song (Hanyang University)
- **Venue:** IJCAI 2026 (Accepted Paper)
- **Code:** https://github.com/finxlab/PRISM-VQ — commit SHA 7d02635d0cdeec2f4e7278a9e73acd0eb67a8fa6 (2026-08-19)
- **License:** MIT
- **Data source:** Qlib platform; Alpha158 feature set; JKP Global Factor Library (Jensen et al. 2023)
- **Sample period:** Train 2009–2019, Validation 2020–2021, Test 2022–2024 (strictly out-of-sample)
- **Universe:** CSI 300 (China) and S&P 500 (U.S.), using contemporaneous index constituents per date
- **Test period:** 2022–2024 (2 years OOS)

## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

PRISM-VQ proposes that cross-sectional stock return predictability can be decomposed into interpretable factor contributions by combining (1) domain-informed financial prior factors (value, size, momentum, etc. from the JKP library), (2) vector-quantized discrete latent factors learned from cross-sectional structure, and (3) a structure-conditioned Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) that generates time-varying factor loadings. The vector quantization acts as an information bottleneck that suppresses idiosyncratic noise while capturing robust cross-sectional market structure. Discrete codes serve as both latent factors and routing signals for temporal expert specialization, enabling the model to adapt to different market regimes.

The framework is explicitly a **factor model** with a dynamic factor structure: y_i = α_i + β'_p,i f_p + β'_l,i f_l,i + ε_i, where f_p are expert prior factors and f_l are learned latent factors. Loadings are generated by nonlinear mappings conditioned on discrete VQ codes.

### Research interpretation

The core hypothesis is that a discrete information bottleneck (VQ codebook) provides stronger regularization than continuous latent representations under low signal-to-noise ratio conditions typical of financial data. The combination of prior factors as stabilizing anchors with learned discrete codes as regime-conditioned routing signals addresses the dual challenge of non-stationarity and overfitting in cross-sectional return prediction. The MoE architecture allows different experts to specialize in distinct market regimes while maintaining overall model coherence.

This is a **portfolio construction signal** — the model predicts cross-sectional stock rankings for long-short portfolio construction. The signal is the predicted return ranking, which determines top-K/N portfolio selection.

## Signal

- **Formation timestamp:** Daily; predictions generated each trading date t using data available at t
- **Lookback window:** T = 20 trading days of Alpha158 features (158 dimensions)
- **Prediction target:** 5-day forward return: y_i,t = (price_i,t+5 - price_i,t+1) / price_i,t+1
- **Additional auxiliary targets:** 1-day to 9-day forward returns (used in Stage 1 training via L_pred)
- **Expert prior factors:** P = 13 factor returns from JKP Global Factor Library (value, size, momentum, profitability, investment, quality, low-risk, low-leverage, accruals, etc.), computed as 20-day cumulative values using information up to t-1
- **Long entry:** Top-K stocks ranked by predicted forward return (K = 30 for primary results; K = 40, 50 tested in robustness)
- **Short entry:** Bottom N stocks dropped from portfolio (N = 5); no explicit short selling in the primary evaluation
- **Exit / rebalance:** TopK-DropN strategy; portfolio rebalanced daily
- **Holding period:** Daily rebalancing
- **Architecture (two-stage):**
  - Stage 1 (Spatial Learning): GRU encoder → cross-asset Transformer → VQ codebook (K = 512 codes, ds = 128) → discrete codes + contrastive learning + reconstruction + auxiliary prediction
  - Stage 2 (Temporal Learning): Temporal Transformer with RoPE (dt = 64) → structure-conditioned MoE (top-1 routing for CSI 300 with Me = 2 experts; top-4 for S&P 500 with Me = 8 experts) → dynamic factor loadings → return prediction
- **Fully specified:** Yes, architecture and hyperparameters are detailed in the paper and code

## Required data

- **Universe:** CSI 300 and S&P 500 constituents (contemporaneous, avoiding survivorship bias)
- **Venue:** Qlib data infrastructure (Chinese and U.S. equity markets)
- **Market type:** Equity (spot, long-only primary evaluation)
- **Timeframe:** Daily bars
- **Fields:** Alpha158 factors (158 features derived from OHLCV data), JKP Global Factor Library (13 factors)
- **Point-in-time:** Features computed using data available at each date t; JKP factors computed using information up to t-1
- **Timestamp:** Trading dates aligned to market calendar
- **Missing data:** Forward-fill with Fillna; RobustZScoreNorm normalization; CSRankNorm for cross-sectional target normalization

## Execution assumptions

- **Transaction costs:** 5 bps at open + 15 bps at close (20 bps round-trip) — source-reported, applied consistently to all methods
- **Signal-to-order timing:** Next-day execution implied by daily rebalancing with open-to-close cost model
- **Order type:** Market order assumed
- **Fill model:** 100% fill assumed
- **Slippage:** Not separately modeled; subsumed in the 20 bps round-trip cost
- **Impact / capacity:** Not modeled; source reports robustness to varying cost levels (10–50 bps round-trip)
- **Leverage / margin:** Not specified (long-only, no leverage)
- **Shorting:** Not explicitly evaluated; the short leg is the bottom-N dropped stocks
- **Turnover:** Mean daily turnover reported; grows ~14x from N_drop=1 to N_drop=15. Default N_drop=5 chosen for favorable net-of-cost balance
- **Latency:** Not specified; daily rebalancing implies end-of-day execution

## Evidence

### Source-reported

All performance figures below are from the paper's Table 1, computed on the 2022–2024 out-of-sample test period:

**CSI 300:**
- RankIC: 0.0646
- RankICIR: 0.4224
- Annualized Return (AR): 30.77%
- Maximum Drawdown (MDD): 19.24%
- Sharpe Ratio (SR): 1.5694
- Statistically significant vs. all 10 baselines at p < 0.05 (block bootstrap, 10K resamples)

**S&P 500:**
- RankIC: 0.0141
- RankICIR: 0.1208
- Annualized Return (AR): 14.42%
- Maximum Drawdown (MDD): 16.16%
- Sharpe Ratio (SR): 0.6701
- 58.4% RankIC improvement over strongest baseline (DTML)
- Statistically significant vs. 7 of 10 baselines at p < 0.05

**Robustness to portfolio size (K=40, K=50):**
- CSI 300: AR 27.85% / 25.35%, SR 1.48 / 1.37
- S&P 500: AR 13.96% / 12.42%, SR 0.67 / 0.61

**Cost sensitivity (20–50 bps round-trip):** Smooth, monotonic degradation; gains not driven by excessive turnover.

**Ablation (Table 2):** Removing the codebook causes largest degradation (RankIC drops 26.9% on CSI 300, becomes negative on S&P 500). Removing prior factors reduces RankIC by 15.2%/78.0%. Replacing MoE with single network reduces RankIC by 9.3%/42.6%.

All figures are source-reported and have not been independently reproduced.

### Negative evidence

None identified in the reviewed sources; absence is not evidence of no negative result.

The S&P 500 Sharpe of 0.67 is substantially weaker than CSI 300's 1.57, suggesting the signal may be less powerful in more efficient markets. The RankIC of 0.0141 on S&P 500 is low in absolute terms, though statistically significant.

## Falsification plan

1. **Out-of-sample extension:** Extend test beyond 2022–2024 into 2025+ data; verify performance persistence
2. **Parameter perturbation:** Vary codebook size K, number of experts Me, temporal dimension dt; verify robustness
3. **Cost stress test:** Increase round-trip costs to 50–100 bps; verify returns survive friction
4. **Alternative universe:** Test on non-index constituents, small-cap stocks, or emerging-market equities
5. **Ablation of codebook:** Remove VQ bottleneck entirely; verify that continuous alternatives underperform (partial evidence in paper's ablation)
6. **Regime breakdown:** Evaluate performance in bull vs. bear vs. sideways sub-periods separately
7. **Turnover control:** Impose turnover caps; verify returns are not entirely driven by high-turnover tail
8. **Failure threshold:** RankIC < 0.005 on S&P 500 or Sharpe < 0.3 after costs would indicate signal decay
9. **Action on failure:** If rank prediction degrades below threshold, investigate whether the VQ codebook has overfit to training-period structure

## Crypto portability

unproven

The framework is designed for equity markets with well-defined factor structures (value, momentum, quality, etc.) and liquid index constituents. Crypto markets lack many of the financial prior factors (e.g., book-to-market, accruals, profitability ratios) that anchor the model. Cross-sectional crypto ranking exists (top-20 coins by market cap) but the universe is smaller, more volatile, and has different microstructure. Vector quantization over crypto cross-sections may capture regime structure, but the economic interpretation of learned codes would differ substantially. Direct adaptation is not supported; any crypto portability would require re-specifying prior factors and validating from scratch.

## Limitations

- **Equity-specific prior factors:** The model relies on JKP Global Factor Library factors (value, momentum, quality, size, etc.) that are not available in crypto markets
- **CSI 300 vs. S&P 500 gap:** Performance is substantially weaker on S&P 500 (SR 0.67 vs. 1.57), suggesting limited portability to efficient markets
- **Sample period:** Only 2 years of OOS data (2022–2024); insufficient for regime robustness assessment
- **Not independently reproduced:** All results are source-reported only
- **Long-only evaluation:** No explicit short-selling or long-short portfolio construction reported; the short leg is only "bottom N dropped"
- **Daily rebalancing:** Assumes end-of-day execution; intraday dynamics and partial fills not modeled
- **Data availability:** Qlib data infrastructure may not be accessible to all researchers
- **Computational cost:** Two-stage training with VQ + MoE + Transformer is non-trivial; requires GPU training
- **Market-specific MoE configuration:** Different optimal hyperparameters for CSI 300 (Me=2, top-1) vs. S&P 500 (Me=8, top-4), suggesting market-specific tuning is required
- **Turning point risk:** The VQ codebook captures structure from the training period (2009–2019); codebook may not generalize to structural regime breaks outside this window
- **Data gap:** Detailed turnover statistics per sub-period not reported in the paper; only aggregate daily turnover
- **Data gap:** Sub-period (bull/bear) performance breakdown not reported
