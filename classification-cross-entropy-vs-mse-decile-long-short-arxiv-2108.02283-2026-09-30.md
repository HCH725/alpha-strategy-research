## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The authors' stated story (Section 1, Section 2.2, Section 5): nearly all financial-ML portfolio-construction work minimizes mean squared error, but "the payoff of a long-short decile strategy depends on which decile portfolio each stock is assigned to". Cross-entropy is a strictly proper scoring rule (Section 2.2, citing Gneiting and Raftery 2007), so training on it calibrates an honest probability vector over return states, and the portfolio rule reads exactly that vector; MSE instead spends the model's limited capacity fitting the *magnitude* of returns inside a decile rather than the *boundaries between* deciles. The source's own diagnostics are offered as the mechanism evidence: the regression confusion matrix (Table OSM5 Panel A) is near-uniform across the eight middle deciles (diagonal entries 10.58%-11.37%, indistinguishable from the 10% random baseline) with tail recall only 19.43% (class 1) and 16.90% (class 10), whereas the classification ensembles concentrate accuracy in the extreme classes; out-of-sample accuracy is 16.571% (hard) and 16.931% (soft) against 12.454% for regression, and top/bottom-class precision jumps from 16.90%/19.40% (regression) to 20.98%/29.68% (soft construction) (Table OSM5 Panels D and E). The source also attributes the value-weighted gap to *what* each objective reads: feature importance puts `idiovol`, `retvol`, `baspread` and `maxret` in the classification model's top five while `mve` and `mom1m` head the regression model's list (Table OSM5 Panel F), and 60-month rolling Fama-MacBeth regressions of a correctness indicator show `idiovol` and `maxret` significant only under the soft construction (Table OSM5 Panel G, average window adjusted R2 0.11 soft vs 0.01 regression).

### Research interpretation

Falsifiable restatement: **the loss function is a first-order design choice for cross-sectional long-short construction because a strictly proper classification loss optimizes the exact object the portfolio rule consumes (extreme-decile membership), while a magnitude loss does not.** Component roles (`research interpretation`):

```text
Regime / conditioning: none (the claim is unconditional across subperiods and NBER regimes)
Primary signal: per-stock predicted probability of top-decile membership q(10) for the long leg
                and of bottom-decile membership q(1) for the short leg, from a 10-class
                cross-entropy model on 102 rank-normalized characteristics + 2-digit SIC
Baseline / control: identical model families, data, features and hyperparameter grids trained
                under mean squared error on the same targets (the matched regression pair)
Confirmation: equal-weight stacking of GBT + RF + NN probability vectors; logistic-regression
                vs OLS pair isolates the loss effect with zero added model complexity
Risk / exit: none specified by the source - monthly rebalance only (this is a signal claim,
                and risk management is not part of the hypothesis)
```

Competing explanations that must be tested rather than assumed away: (i) the classification book is a **high-idiosyncratic-volatility, wide-spread, low-profitability, young-growth tilt** (Table OSM6: long leg `idiovol` +0.795, `baspread` +0.795, `retvol` +0.827, `maxret` +0.767, `absacc` +0.415 z-scores above the regression long leg, with `roaq` -0.738, `ep` -0.650, `mve` -0.270) -- i.e. the reported alpha may be an illiquidity / lottery / shortability factor rather than loss-function efficiency, and the source controls only for CAPM, FF3, FF5 and FF6; (ii) a **scalar, constant cost haircut cannot price a book that deliberately sits in wide-spread names**, so the "survives transaction costs" claim is only as good as a flat 30 bps assumption; (iii) the edge is concentrated on the **short leg at the two ends of the sample** (Table OSM3), which is exactly where borrow is hardest.

## Signal

All items below are `source-reported` unless explicitly marked `research-proposed`, `research-defined` or `underspecified`.

- **Label / target (Section 2.2):** at each date, stocks are sorted into **10 return deciles on realized returns**; the model is trained to predict each stock's **following-month** decile. Baseline `|D| = 10`; robustness specifications at **2 classes (above/below median), 3 classes (terciles), 5 classes (quintiles)** (Section 4, Table 3).
- **Classification objective (Eq. 1):** categorical cross-entropy `L_cls = - sum_i,t sum_d y_i,t(d) ln q_i,t(d)` with `y` the one-hot realized-decile indicator and `q` the predicted probability vector summing to one.
- **Regression objective (Eq. 2):** `L_reg = (1/S) sum_i,t (r_i,t - r_hat_i,t)^2` on the stock's realized **excess** return; the standard Gu-Kelly-Xiu decile sort on predicted return (buy highest decile, short lowest).
- **Portfolio construction -- the `soft` construction (Section 2.2, Section 2.6), which is the source's main rule:** rank **all stocks separately on `q(10)`** and take the **top 10%** into the long leg; rank **all stocks separately on `q(1)`** and take the **top 10%** into the short leg. Because each class portfolio is formed independently, "a stock can appear in multiple class portfolios or in none"; **a stock qualifying for both legs is excluded from both** (Section 2.6 and footnote 6; footnote 6 adds that including them "does not influence our conclusion" in unreported results). The alternative `hard` construction assigns each stock to its single argmax class (Table 3 Panels C-D); the source reports soft > hard at every class count and in both weightings.
- **Ensemble (Section 2.4.4, OSM.2.5):** the stacking ensemble is **simple equal-weight averaging** of GBT, RF and NN -- averaging the three 10-dimensional **probability vectors** for classification, averaging the three scalar forecasts for regression.
- **Models and grids (Section 2.4, OSM.2.2-OSM.2.4, Appendix Table A1):** GBT and RF search **maximum tree depth in {1, 2, 4, 8}**, each candidate grown to **1,000 trees** (GBT with early stopping after 5 rounds); RF feature sampling = **sqrt(feature count) for classification and one-third for regression**, row sampling **0.632**. NN is a fully connected tanh MLP over the printed architecture grid (1 layer of 8/16/32/64/128; 2 layers of 128-64, 64-32, 32-16, 16-8; 3, 4 and 5-layer combinations as printed in Table A1), up to **100 epochs**, **Adam with learning rate 0.01**, early stopping after 5 epochs without improvement; the source states Adam is reported because it "improves the regression side of the comparison more than the classification side, which makes our reported gap the conservative one", with Adadelta results in Table OSM2. Linear benchmarks: **logistic regression** (classification) vs **OLS** (regression). Most frequently selected hyperparameters across the 42 windows are printed in Appendix Table A2 (e.g. classification GBT depth 4 in 30/42 windows, RF depth 8 in 42/42, NN `[16, 8]` in 16/42).
- **Training / retraining schedule (Section 2.5 and OSM.2.6) -- internally inconsistent, see Provenance and Contradiction 1:** models are updated **each June** on an **expanding** window; Section 2.5 says fitting uses all data through that June, "the following 12 months serve as a holdout period for tuning hyperparameters", and OOS predictions come from "the subsequent 12 months after the holdout period"; OSM.2.6 says for OOS period `t`, training runs through the end of `t-2` with the 12 months of `t-1` as validation, repeating **42 times**. Section 3 states the first training window is **July 1962 to June 1983** and that OOS evaluation spans **July 1983 to December 2024 (498 monthly observations)**. Recorded as printed; the schedule is `underspecified` in the sense that it cannot be implemented unambiguously without picking one of the three readings (`research-proposed` for any replication: run all three readings, per F10).
- **Evaluation (Section 2.6):** both **value-weighted and equal-weighted** long-short portfolios; Sharpe for legs is `E(r_p - r_f)/sigma(r_p) x sqrt(12)` (Eqs. 3-4); for the long-short it is `E(r_ls)/sigma(r_ls) x sqrt(12)` treating it as a zero-investment portfolio; factor alphas from time-series regressions of the monthly zero-investment return (or the leg's excess return) on CAPM, FF3F, FF5F, FF6F with **Newey-West HAC standard errors at 6 lags** (footnote 8: 12 lags do not change the conclusion). Note: Table 1's `Average Return` is an **annualized raw (gross) return**, so the market row's Sharpe (0.533 value-weighted) is computed on the excess return while the raw average is 0.117 -- the two are not interchangeable.
- **Holding period / exit / re-entry:** **monthly rebalance only.** No stop, no take-profit, no maximum holding period, no re-entry rule, no signal-strength threshold and no cash rule is specified -> `underspecified`.
- **Position sizing:** value-weighted or equal-weighted decile legs; **no leverage, gross-exposure cap, per-name cap or risk budget is specified** -> `data gap`.
- `research-proposed` operationalisations (none appear in the source): form at the last trading day of month `t-1` using characteristics published by then, execute at the next session open with market orders; per-name participation cap of 10% of 20-day ADV; skip any name with fewer than 95% valid observations in the formation window; treat a missing characteristic as "not eligible" rather than imputing.

### Negative evidence

Source-carried (all inside arXiv `2108.02283v7`):

1. **The friction model is a scalar, constant haircut, not a cost model.** OSM.5.3 deducts `Turnover x 30 bps` from the full-period mean return with no dependence on the name, the spread, the size of the order or the side; the whole-document census above shows `slippage`, `commission`, `market impact`, `participation`, `latency`, `market order`, `limit order` and `fee(s)` all at **0**. Order type, fill, execution price, signal-to-order delay and capacity are `data gap`.
2. **The cost model is mis-specified relative to the book's own tilt.** Table OSM6 shows the classification long leg sits **+0.795 z above** the regression long leg on `baspread` and **+0.795** on `idiovol`, with `retvol` +0.827 and `maxret` +0.767 -- i.e. a flat 30 bps/trade is applied to precisely the names for which 30 bps is least plausible. The classification book also loads **-0.650** on `ep`, **-0.738** on `roaq` and **-0.270** on `mve`, so a meaningful part of the spread may be an illiquidity/lottery/shortability premium that the four factor models used (CAPM, FF3, FF5, FF6) do not span.
3. **Borrow is unrealistically flat.** OSM.5.4 charges 50 bps/year uniformly with **no locate-failure rule, no short-sale cap and no no-trade constraint**, on a short leg whose `baspread` difference vs the regression short leg is **+1.117 z** and whose `idiovol` difference is **+1.073 z** (Table OSM6).
4. **Turnover is high and is the entire cost.** Table 5: value-weighted 1.440 and equal-weighted 1.381 for the classification stacking ensemble (the source reads 144% as replacing roughly three-quarters of the book each month), against a market portfolio at 0.088 / 0.122. Equal-weighted classification turnover is **higher** than its regression counterpart (1.381 vs 1.242), so in the equal-weighted scheme classification pays more for its edge.
5. **No capacity, liquidity or participation analysis anywhere** (3 `capacity` hits, all non-friction; 3 `liquidity` hits, all prose). A 531-name decile leg rebuilt monthly cannot be assumed fillable at scale.
6. **No point-in-time, delisting or survivorship treatment is stated**; the only missing-data rule is "exclude observations with missing current returns", which is exactly the rule that can silently drop delisted names.
7. **No multiple-testing control** despite a hyperparameter grid (4 tree depths x a 20-cell NN architecture grid), two optimizers (Adam, Adadelta), five matched model pairs, two weightings, four factor models, four class counts, two construction rules, a large-cap subsample, four subperiods, two NBER regimes and a second battery of alternative regression targets (OSM.8). The census finds `deflated`, `Benjamini`, `FDR`, `multiple testing`, `multiplicity` and `p-hacking` all at **0**; only Newey-West 6-lag t-statistics are reported.
8. **Headline sharpes are gross.** Table 1's value-weighted 2.080 and equal-weighted 3.767 are before any cost; only Table OSM4 / Figure 2 are net, and net-of-TC30+BC50 the equal-weighted classification Sharpe is still **3.450** (Table OSM4), a number that should be treated as implausible for a 498-month monthly US equity long-short until an independent cost and capacity model says otherwise.
9. **Attenuation inside the sample.** Value-weighted classification Sharpe falls `3.733 -> 2.628 -> 1.323` across the first three decades before recovering to `1.578` (Table OSM3); Section OSM.4 explicitly links the declining pattern to the anomaly-attenuation literature (Chen and Zimmermann 2022; Chordia, Subrahmanyam and Tong 2014; Green, Hand and Zhang 2017; McLean and Pontiff 2016).
10. **The edge is short-leg heavy at the sample ends.** Classification VW short-leg Sharpe 1.321 (1983-93) and 0.709 (2014-24) versus long-leg 0.485 and 0.376 (Table OSM3); after costs the regression VW short leg collapses to Sharpe **0.036** while the classification short leg holds **0.533** (Table OSM4) -- i.e. the residual net edge lives on the side with the hardest borrow.
11. **Recession months favour the baseline:** NBER recession VW Sharpe `1.868` (classification) vs `2.148` (regression) over 40 months (Table OSM3 Panel E); with only 40 observations the source itself calls the split noisy.
12. **One of the 50 headline comparisons favours regression** (equal-weighted RF Sharpe 2.398 vs 2.441), and the source acknowledges it (Section 3.1).

3. **F3 - Executable-cost ladder.** Replace the scalar haircut with a path-dependent cost model at `k in {5, 10, 20, 30, 50}` bps one-way plus half-spread crossing (research-proposed grid). *Fail if:* classification net value-weighted Sharpe <= regression net value-weighted Sharpe at `k = 20` bps (research-defined) -> the "survives transaction costs" claim is rejected.

5. **F5 - Capacity gate.** Cap each leg at **10% of 20-day ADV** (research-proposed) and re-run. *Fail if:* classification value-weighted Sharpe degrades by more than **0.50** (research-defined) -> the effect is a small-cap artefact of the printed construction, not scalable alpha.
