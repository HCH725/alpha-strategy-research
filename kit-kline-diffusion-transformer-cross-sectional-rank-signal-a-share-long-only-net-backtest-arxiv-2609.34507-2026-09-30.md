## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The authors reformulate candlestick forecasting as **conditional path generation via flow matching**: each OHLCV bar is encoded into an invertible five-dimensional log-ratio state `x_t = (r_gap, r_body, r_up, r_dn, v_t)` = `(ln(O_t/C_{t-1}), ln(C_t/O_t), ln(H_t/max(O_t,C_t)), ln(min(O_t,C_t)/L_t), ln((V_t+1)/(E_t+1)))` where `E_t` is a strictly causal volume EMA (Equation 3, Section 3.2). History and horizon are placed in one token sequence with the history left un-noised and returned bit-identical; the backbone is a Diffusion Transformer whose block adds QK-Norm, RoPE and SwiGLU with role-aware dual AdaLN modulation (Sections 3.3-3.4, Equations 4-7). Window-constant identity signals (market, sector, instrument, timescale) modulate every layer through one shared AdaLN trunk evaluated twice (`t` for target tokens, `t=0` for context tokens); per-bar calendar signals (intraday clock, session position, day of week, month, day of year, event flags) are added directly to token embeddings (Section 3.3). Training minimises masked MSE on target positions only with a logit-normal flow-time schedule (Equation 8); inference integrates the learned ODE from `z_1 = ε ~ N(0,I)` with an Euler solver under dual classifier-free guidance with weights `w_id`, `w_hist` (Equation 9); `M` independent samples are decoded through the invertible map to give an ensemble of `M` plausible OHLCV trajectories from which "any distributional statistic can be computed directly" (Section 3.5). The stated contribution is that non-autoregressive one-shot generation removes the error accumulation of autoregressive decoders and that the log-ratio representation removes tokenizer loss and price-scale heterogeneity (Sections 1, 2).

For trading, Section 4.3 states: "we rank names by predicted terminal return and replay a long-only book on A-share over a one-year period (2025.05-2026.04). Top-N and rebalance frequency are selected on validation and then frozen. Buys that would close at a limit-up are skipped. Costs are 12.5 bp on the buy side and 22.5 bp on the sell side (commission 2.5 bp, stamp 10 bp, slippage 10 bp per side)."

### Research interpretation

The falsifiable hypothesis is a **cross-sectional short-horizon return-rank signal**, not a pricing model and not a risk premium. Decomposed by component role:

- **Representation / signal generator**: KiT-L (284.7M parameters) consumes `L_c` history bars and emits `L_h` future bars; at inference the ensemble mean terminal close-to-close log-return over the horizon is the raw score, and the ensemble standard deviation of one-bar log-returns is the volatility score (Section 4.2, Appendix A.2).
- **Cross-sectional ranking**: names are ranked by that score inside a market; the paper's headline number is an **equal-weight average of three market-level RankICs** (research-derived - see Evidence).
- **Portfolio construction**: long-only top-N A-share book, Top-N and rebalance frequency chosen on validation and then frozen (values not printed).
- **Execution / friction**: a fixed two-sided basis-point ladder plus a limit-up skip.
- **Absent**: no regime filter, no confirmation filter, no stop, no volatility targeting, no hedging leg, no market-neutral construction.

Three candidate economic channels could produce the observed rank correlation, and the paper does not discriminate between them: (a) **instrument-identity persistence** - the identity embeddings can learn instrument-level drift/volatility/size/liquidity characteristics that persist across the 120-day to 250-bar context and therefore proxy a slow cross-sectional premium; (b) **calendar/session structure** - the clock/session/day-of-week/event injections can encode intraday and turn-of-period seasonality; (c) **intrabar microstructure information** - high/low/shadow geometry and volume-vs-EMA carry short-horizon order-flow and liquidity information that close-only inputs discard. Channel (c) is directly supported by the paper's own Appendix E ablation: replacing the log-ratio state with a close-only log price drops the mean return RankIC from `0.0571` to `0.0183` (a **68.0 percent** reduction, research-computed). Because every baseline in Appendix A.2 is explicitly "close-only", a substantial part of the reported edge is an **information-set advantage rather than an architecture advantage**; that attribution is a missing link, not an established result.

Not established by the source: any cost-of-capital or behavioural explanation, any microstructure identification, any stationarity argument, and any evidence that the rank correlation is stable outside the single 99-day test window.

## Signal

**Fully specified parts (source-reported).**

- **Formation timestamp / lookback**: at each anchor the model reads `L_c` completed history bars at that resolution and predicts `L_h` future bars. Table 4 (Appendix A.1): 1 minute `1200/120`, 5 minutes `960/96`, 15 minutes `800/64`, 30 minutes `640/40`, 1 hour `400/20`, 2 hours `360/20`, 1 day `250/20` (history/horizon in bars). History is un-noised; warm-up handling and whether endpoints are inclusive are not stated (underspecified). Timezone and session calendar are not stated (data gap).
- **Score**: draw `M = 16` samples per case and average them as the predicted trajectory (Section 4.1); terminal return = sum of close-to-close log returns over the horizon; volatility score = sample standard deviation of one-bar log returns (Appendix A.2). Dual CFG weights default to `1`, i.e. the plain conditional model (Section 4.1, Appendix D).
- **Ranking and entry**: "rank names by predicted terminal return" and buy the `Top-N` of that ranking, long-only, on A-shares; skip a buy that would close at a limit-up (Section 4.3). `Top-N` is printed as a symbol only - **the value of N is never stated** (underspecified). Ranking direction is implied by "top-N" plus long-only but the paper never writes "descending" (research inference, labelled).
- **Rebalance / exit**: `rebalance frequency` is stated to be "selected on validation and then frozen" - **the frequency itself, the candidate grid, and the weighting scheme inside the book (equal-weight vs value-weight) are never printed** (underspecified). No stop, no take-profit, no maximum holding period, and no explicit exit price are stated (data gap); the exit is implicitly the next rebalance.
- **Evaluation metric**: IC = cross-sectional Pearson correlation of predicted vs realized terminal return; RankIC = the corresponding Spearman rank correlation; "Higher IC and RankIC are better" (Section 4.1). Computed on the test window 2026-01-01 to 2026-04-10 for Tables 1, 2, 5, 6, 7 and Figures 7-9.
- **Baselines** (Appendix A.2, all close-only): Kronos-base (102M official checkpoint, `K=8`), Kronos-base-FT ("the same architecture finetuned on our mixed-market training split; we record the checkpoint with the **best test-set scores**"), Sundial `thuml/sundial-base-128m` (`K=16`), `amazon/chronos-bolt-small` and `amazon/chronos-2` (`K=1` point readouts), `google/timesfm-2.5-200m-pytorch` and `google/timesfm-3.0-pytorch` (`K=1`), historical-return bootstrap (`K=16`), a **fixed-coefficient** GARCH(1,1) `v_{t+1} = 1e-8 + 0.05 r_{t+1}^2 + 0.90 v_t` with no likelihood fitting (`K=16`), and TimeGrad / Diffusion-TS / TSFlow retrained per resolution **on A-share windows only** with targets ending by 2023-12-31, 2024 validation targets, 10,000 optimizer updates ("Terminal-10k"). All validation runs record inference seed `91000`.

**Research-proposed operationalisation (NOT source-reported; required to make the rule testable, and labelled research-proposed here and nowhere presented as the paper's rule):** descending rank; entry at the next bar's open after the signal bar closes; equal weighting within the top-N; exit the name at its next scheduled rebalance; no intraperiod trading; position count fixed before the test window; the limit-up skip applied against the previous close as the reference price.

**Reconstructibility verdict:** the *forecast* side is reconstructible in principle from the paper; the *trading* side is **not** - missing `Top-N` value, rebalance frequency, weighting, entry price convention, exit rule and data vendor mean the printed backtest cannot be reproduced from the source alone, and the code that would resolve this does not exist at the pinned commit.

### Negative evidence

1. **Selection-window contamination of the trading result.** Research-computed: 244 of the 364 backtest days (67.0%, 8.0 months) lie inside the declared validation window, and the Top-N and rebalance frequency were "selected on validation and then frozen". The label "Test annualized net return" therefore covers a majority-validation-period window (this is the `contradictions` entry above).
2. **Model selection also touched that window.** Early stopping runs on "mean validation loss ... no longer improves" over 2024-2025, so the first eight months of the backtest are inside the model-selection period as well (research interpretation of Sections 4.1 + 4.3).
3. **Only 99 calendar days of declared test data support every IC table** (2026-01-01 to 2026-04-10) - roughly 65 A-share trading sessions (research-computed).
4. **Tiny anchor counts.** Figure 7: A-share `n=3` at 1d and `n=6` at 2h; Figure 9: US `n=3` at 1d and `n=12` at 2h; Figure 8: crypto `n=4` at 1d. Daily and 2-hour RankIC rest on 3-6 anchors (source panel annotations).

## Falsification plan

All thresholds below are **research-defined falsification thresholds** unless explicitly attributed to the source; none of them is a source claim. Each gate has a failure condition and a fixed action. **No-retuning rule:** once gates F3-F12 are opened, no parameter, window, Top-N value, rebalance frequency, guidance weight or cost assumption may be changed to rescue a failure; a failed gate downgrades the record to untradable research and must not be re-tested with adjusted thresholds on the same data.

- **F1 - Printed-value reproduction (blocked on code).** When (if) code and weights are released at a pinned commit, regenerate Tables 1, 2, 5, 6, 7 and Figures 7-9 from the released corpus. *Fail:* any KiT cell differs from the printed value by more than `0.0005`. *Action:* reject the record's evidence section entirely.
- **F2 - Direction and per-market sign gate (research-defined).** Rebuild the three per-market RankIC series on the same declared test window. *Fail:* US mean RankIC is `<= 0` at four or more of seven resolutions, or crypto 1m remains `< 0.010`. *Action:* restrict the hypothesis to A-shares and mark US/crypto as refuted for this mechanism.
- **F3 - Point-in-time leakage gate (research-defined).** Rebuild features and labels from unadjusted (or correctly point-in-time adjusted) prices with no information after each bar's close. *Fail:* mean return RankIC falls below `0.045` (printed `0.0571` minus 21%). *Action:* treat the original number as leakage-contaminated.
- **F4 - Clean-window gate (research-defined).** Evaluate on a frozen forward window beginning 2026-10-01 for at least 12 months, with no retraining on the evaluation period. *Fail:* mean return RankIC `< 0.030`, or `<= 0` in at least two of the three markets. *Action:* mark the signal as non-stationary.
- **F5 - Selection-window gate (research-defined).** Re-run the Section 4.3 backtest on a window strictly after the declared validation end (i.e. starting 2026-01-01 or later) with Top-N and rebalance frequency chosen only on 2024-2025. *Fail:* net annualized return `<= 0` or net Sharpe `< 0.50`. *Action:* treat the printed +65.2% / Sharpe 1.70 as validation-contaminated.
- **F6 - Cost ladder (research-defined).** Charge 0 / 5 / 10 / 20 / 35 / 50 bp per side on the A-share top-N book. *Fail:* net Sharpe `< 0.50` at the source's own 12.5 / 22.5 bp ladder, or net Sharpe `< 0` at 2x the source ladder (25 / 45 bp). *Action:* reject as non-tradable.
- **F7 - Executability gate (research-defined).** Next-bar-open fills with a 50% participation cap and no same-bar close fills. *Fail:* net Sharpe drops by more than `0.50` versus the source's reported 1.70 (5-minute) or 2.46 (2-hour). *Action:* mark execution assumptions as not realizable.
- **F8 - Capacity gate (research-defined).** Scale book size until net Sharpe falls by 0.50 from the F7 baseline; report the AUM at that point. *Action:* publish the capacity ceiling; no failure threshold is pre-set because no capacity number exists in the source.
- **F9 - Like-for-like information-set gate (research-defined).** Compare KiT against at least one baseline given the identical OHLCV input, identical training corpus (all three markets) and a checkpoint selected on validation only - i.e. removing the Appendix A.2 asymmetries. *Fail:* KiT's mean RankIC edge over that baseline is `< 0.015`. *Action:* attribute the original edge to input and tuning rather than architecture.
- **F10 - Weighting gate (research-defined).** Recompute the headline as a product-weighted and as a market-cap-weighted mean rather than the equal-market mean derived in Evidence. *Fail:* weighted mean `< 0.030`. *Action:* withdraw the `0.0571` headline as an artefact of equal market weighting.
- **F11 - Multiple-testing gate (research-defined).** Apply Benjamini-Hochberg at `q < 0.10` across the full family of 13 configurations x 7 resolutions x 2 metrics x 3 markets, plus a deflated-Sharpe adjustment over the backtest configuration search. *Fail:* fewer than half of KiT's claimed leading cells survive. *Action:* downgrade all "state-of-the-art" wording to uncorrected exploratory results.
- **F12 - Placebo gate (research-defined).** Within each market-date cross-section, permute the identity and calendar conditioning 1,000 times and recompute mean return RankIC. *Fail:* the observed `0.0571` does not exceed the 95th percentile of the permutation distribution. *Action:* treat the conditioning channels as non-informative.
- **F13 - Benchmark gate (research-defined).** Against the equal-weight universe, CSI 300 and CSI 500 on a fully post-validation window, with a block-bootstrap 95% confidence interval on the excess return. *Fail:* the interval includes zero. *Action:* mark the economic-relevance claim as unsupported.
- **F14 - Second-vendor gate (research-defined).** Rebuild the 1-minute to 1-day panels from an independent data vendor with a declared session calendar. *Fail:* sign agreement with the printed RankIC in fewer than 6 of 7 resolutions. *Action:* attribute the result to vendor-specific data construction.

## Crypto portability

**`adapted`** (forecast-level mechanism demonstrated by the source in crypto; crypto *trading* performance unproven).

- **What ports mechanically**: the model, the log-ratio encoding and the rank score are instrument-agnostic, and the source itself trains on and reports 28 crypto products over seven resolutions (Figure 8 crypto return RankIC `+0.0055 / +0.0394 / +0.0242 / +0.0199 / +0.0501 / +0.0926 / +0.1957`). This is source-reported crypto *forecast* evidence, so the mechanism is not a pure TradFi import.
- **What does not port**: there is **no crypto backtest anywhere in the source**; every traded number in the paper is an A-share cash long-only book. Crypto-specific friction is entirely unmodelled - taker/maker fees, quoted spread, partial fills, venue fragmentation across exchanges, 24/7 candle and anchor boundaries versus the equity session calendar used for the calendar embeddings, index/mark-price construction for any perpetual-based implementation, and the `24/7` term appears 0 times in the pinned text.
- **Weak crypto signal where it matters most**: crypto 1m RankIC is `+0.0055` (Figure 8) and the highest-frequency resolutions are exactly where crypto costs dominate; crypto 1d `+0.1957` rests on `n=4` anchors.
- **Point-in-time and universe risks carry over**: backward-adjusted prices and an unstated 28-product inclusion rule have no crypto analogue (no corporate actions) but also no stated delisting/stablecoin-outage handling, and survivorship in a 28-product sample is a live concern.
- Crypto portability is **not** authorization to trade; it states only that the mechanism can be evaluated in crypto with new data and a new cost model.
