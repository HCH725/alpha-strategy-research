# TradingView Heikin-Ashi Supertrend with Real-Price Execution

## Provenance

Public TradingView open-source strategy **Heikin Ashi Supertrend**, published by `jordanfray` on 2022-11-02. Stable source URL: https://www.tradingview.com/script/9z16eauD-Heikin-Ashi-Supertrend/. Source reviewed 2026-09-17.

The TradingView page explicitly distinguishes synthetic Heikin-Ashi prices used to form the Supertrend signal from standard candle close prices used for trade entry and exit. The page warns that running an ordinary Supertrend directly on a Heikin-Ashi chart can create unrealistic backtest results because fills would then use synthetic rather than tradable prices.

GitHub dedup on current `main` found no strategy record containing canonical TradingView script ID `9z16eauD` or the normalized Heikin-Ashi-Supertrend / real-price-execution construction.

## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The author uses Heikin-Ashi candles to smooth price action for Supertrend formation while deliberately keeping execution on normal candle close prices. The intended signal is a Supertrend reversal derived from the smoothed Heikin-Ashi series, while actual entries/exits are priced using ordinary market candles.

### Research interpretation

The falsifiable hypothesis has two separable parts. First, Heikin-Ashi transformation may suppress high-frequency bar noise enough that Supertrend regime flips contain more persistent directional information than Supertrend calculated on raw candles. Second, any apparent edge must survive execution on real prices; synthetic Heikin-Ashi fills are not economically tradable and can mechanically distort backtests.

This makes the construction materially different from a generic Supertrend rule: the alpha candidate is the **synthetic-series signal / real-series execution separation**. A valid test must compare it with a raw-price Supertrend baseline under the same execution and cost model.

## Signal

Source-reported logic recoverable from the public description:

- **Signal series:** Heikin-Ashi candles are generated by the strategy and used to calculate Supertrend.
- **Primary directional event:** Supertrend reversals on the Heikin-Ashi-derived series generate long and short entry signals.
- **Execution price series:** normal candle close prices are used for entries and exits rather than Heikin-Ashi synthetic prices.
- **Risk/exit features:** the source includes configurable profit target, stop loss, and functionality to move the stop after a trade reaches a configurable percentage profit.

The public description reviewed in this Scout cycle does not expose the exact default Supertrend ATR length/multiplier, exact reversal operator, profit-target percentage, stop-loss percentage, stop-move threshold, stop destination, pyramiding/re-entry semantics, or whether a reversal itself closes/reverses an existing position. These are **underspecified** and are not invented here.

Signal formation is bar-based, but exact same-close versus next-bar order semantics are not explicitly stated in the reviewed prose. `research-proposed` causal convention for later testing: form the Heikin-Ashi/Supertrend state from information available at completed bar close and execute no earlier than the next tradable bar unless inspection of the public Pine source establishes a different leakage-safe order.

## Required data

Minimum required inputs are timestamped standard OHLC bars. Heikin-Ashi OHLC must be derived causally from the standard series; Supertrend additionally requires sufficient high/low/close history for its ATR calculation. The strategy does not require synthetic Heikin-Ashi market data from an external venue.

The reviewed source does not restrict the rule to a specific instrument, venue, market type, or timeframe. Volume, funding, basis, open interest, trades and order-book data are not part of the stated signal.

Point-in-time handling is material: Heikin-Ashi and Supertrend values at each signal timestamp must be generated only from information available by that timestamp. Standard market prices, not synthetic Heikin-Ashi values, must be used for fills and P&L.

## Execution assumptions

The source is unusually explicit about one execution constraint: **do not use Heikin-Ashi synthetic prices as entry/exit fill prices**. The chart should use standard candles while the strategy internally constructs the Heikin-Ashi signal series.

Exact signal-to-order timing, market versus limit orders, fees, spread, slippage, impact, latency, leverage, margin, short borrow/funding and partial-fill behavior are not specified in the reviewed public description. Later reproduction must model them explicitly.

Any test that fills at Heikin-Ashi OHLC is not a faithful economic reproduction of this hypothesis and should be rejected as an execution artifact.

## Evidence

### Source-reported

The author states that the signal/price separation corrects unrealistic backtesting behavior that occurs when the built-in Supertrend is run directly on Heikin-Ashi candles and synthetic prices are used by the strategy tester. The reviewed public description does not provide a traceable Sharpe ratio, CAGR, drawdown, win rate or other performance statistic that should be carried into this record.

### Independently reproduced

Not independently reproduced.

### Negative evidence

The source itself identifies synthetic-price execution as a major backtest-validity failure mode. Heikin-Ashi smoothing also introduces transformation lag, so cleaner regime flips may occur later than raw-price signals and may lose edge after realistic costs. No independent evidence was reviewed in this Scout cycle; absence of further negative evidence is not evidence of robustness.

## Falsification plan

1. Recover the exact public Pine parameters and order semantics before claiming exact reproduction; unresolved values remain `underspecified` or explicitly `research-proposed`.
2. Compare Heikin-Ashi-derived Supertrend against an otherwise identical raw-OHLC Supertrend baseline, with both executed on standard market prices.
3. Include a diagnostic synthetic-fill variant only to quantify backtest inflation; it must never be treated as tradable evidence.
4. Evaluate long and short legs separately across trend and range regimes and multiple liquid instruments/timeframes.
5. Stress fees, spread and slippage. The hypothesis is materially weakened if the Heikin-Ashi signal does not improve out-of-sample risk-adjusted continuation or drawdown behavior versus raw Supertrend after accounting for added lag and costs.
6. Ablate the profit target, stop loss and stop-move layer so risk management is not mistaken for predictive alpha.

## Crypto portability

`adapted`. The source describes a generic TradingView construction rather than presenting crypto-specific empirical evidence. The mechanism can be tested on crypto OHLC because both Heikin-Ashi and Supertrend are price-derived, but this is a ported hypothesis until independently reproduced on crypto.

Crypto testing must account for 24/7 candle boundaries, venue fragmentation, spread/slippage, and — for perpetuals — funding, mark/index conventions, leverage and liquidation mechanics. Standard venue prices must remain the execution series.

## Limitations

- Exact Supertrend ATR length/multiplier and several risk parameters are **underspecified** in the reviewed prose.
- Exact order/fill timing is **underspecified**.
- Instrument, venue and timeframe are **underspecified**.
- Heikin-Ashi smoothing can reduce noise but also adds lag; whether the trade-off is beneficial is unproven.
- No source-reported performance statistic is adopted here.
