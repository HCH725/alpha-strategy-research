## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The author's stated mechanism has three linked parts, all from Sections 1, 2 and 3:

1. **Objective-channel.** An automated alpha-mining loop turns factor discovery into a search in which the reward function is the research objective (Section 1). If that reward is gross in-sample return quality - described by the author as the default in almost every published or informally circulated pipeline - the search systematically favours short-horizon, high-turnover signals, because fast cross-sectional effects carry attractive raw Sharpe ratios that are largely compensation for the spread and impact a real trader would pay (Section 1, citing Novy-Marx and Velikov, 2016 and Korajczyk and Sadka, 2004).
2. **Response-channel.** Telling the agent about turnover and cost *during* the search, not only at final evaluation, changes what it finds: penalised runs converge to long-lookback, heavily smoothed, structurally simpler expressions (`decay_linear` in 100 percent of fold-seed instances versus 26 percent, mean lookback 209 trading days versus 29, mean 6.1 AST operator nodes versus 9.6), which is the stated mechanism for the turnover collapse of 22x to 38x (Section 3, fourth finding, Table 2).
3. **Monetisation-channel.** Whether the discovered factor survives cost is decided by pace, not by gross strength: in the volatility category the execution-aware agent's own gross Sharpe is *lower* than the cost-blind baseline's (0.48 versus 0.73) yet its net outcome is far better, so `a moderately weaker pre-cost signal traded at a sustainable pace outperforms a stronger one traded at an unsustainable pace` (Section 3, third finding). The author's own caveat: `a reward penalizing turnover will mechanically produce low-turnover factors`, so the substantive question is whether the trade-off improves realised out-of-sample performance (Section 4).

### Research interpretation

Falsifiable restatement of the hypothesis this record captures, in our terms rather than the author's:

- **Treatment claim (the alpha-relevant claim):** with model, universe, search budget, starting expression, sampling seed and cost model held fixed, changing only the reward from `Gross_IR` to `Gross_IR - 0.5 * Turnover - 8 * Cost_Impact` produces lower daily turnover and higher stitched out-of-sample net Sharpe. Source-reported magnitude: mean fold-level net-Sharpe improvement `+9.96` with cluster-bootstrap 95 percent CI `[8.3, 11.6]` over 252 paired fold-level comparisons, positive in all 12 seed-alpha by sampling-seed combinations (exact sign test `p = 0.0005`), with execution-aware superior on 87 to 97 percent of individual folds in every category (Section 3, second finding). This is a claim about *what a search objective selects*, not a claim that any one signal forecasts returns.
- **Mechanism claim:** the improvement is mediated by expression structure (long-window smoothing operators), so a reward that only shrinks turnover without changing structure should reproduce it. The paper's own penalty sweep supports a threshold rather than a smooth frontier: any turnover penalty collapses turnover, and turnover is then largely insensitive to its magnitude (Section 3, fifth finding, Table 3).
- **Tradeability claim (narrow):** one of four seed categories, the low-volatility-anomaly seed, yields a net-profitable daily dollar-neutral long/short cross-sectional US equity factor under the source's own full cost model - net Sharpe `+0.35` (three-seed range `+0.33` to `+0.37`), CAGR `3.5-4.1 percent`, annualised volatility `13.1-13.3 percent`, maximum drawdown `-33 percent` to `-40 percent`, over a 20.6-year stitched out-of-sample record at 1.4 percent per-day turnover (Section 3, third finding; Table 1). The other three categories stay net-negative (`-0.38`, `-0.25`, `-0.10`).
- **Component roles (this is a search-system record, not a multi-component trading system):** treatment = the two reward penalty terms `gamma1 = 0.5`, `gamma2 = 8` applied identically in complexity penalty to both arms; search = Qwen2.5-Coder-14B-Instruct Q4_K_M served by llama.cpp at temperature 0.7, 50 rounds, AST-whitelisted expression grammar plus six anti-pattern rules; universe = reconstructed point-in-time S&P membership with a per-fold liquidity cap; portfolio = daily-rebalanced dollar-neutral long/short book at 200 percent gross with weights lagged one day; costs = Corwin-Schultz high-low spread plus square-root impact; evaluation = 21 rolling walk-forward folds with a frozen winner scored once per held-out test year. Do not assume every component contributes alpha; an ablation of the four seed categories and of the cost model versus the turnover term separately is exactly what Table 3 only partially provides (only for the volatility category and one sampling seed).

## Signal

**Formation timestamp and availability.** One observation per trading day. A candidate factor expression is evaluated on day `t` inputs; `Target weights derived from a factor score computed on day t's inputs are lagged one day before being applied, so a position earns the close[t]-to-close[t+1] return rather than the return already realized when the signal was formed; no same-day price information enters the reward` (Section 2). Timezone, exchange session and the exact close convention are `not stated in source`.

**Lookback.** Expression windows are restricted by guardrail to `[2, 252]` trading days for every windowed operator, with `delta`/`delay` exempt (Appendix A.2). Source-reported for discovered factors: execution-aware winners have a mean lookback of 209 trading days and sit `at or near the 252-day ceiling` (Section 3, fourth finding); the cost-blind baseline mean is 29 days. The source does not state how the per-expression lookback is aggregated into those two means; our recomputation under a stated convention (mean, within expression, of that expression's window arguments, then mean over instances) gives 208.7 and 28.6, which round to the printed 209 and 29 (see `### Independently reproduced`).

**Entry.** Each candidate becomes a `daily-rebalanced, dollar-neutral long/short portfolio` (Section 1) at `200%-gross` exposure (Section 4). The mapping from factor score to long and short weights - equal weight, rank-proportional, or a capped top/bottom split - is `underspecified`; the source never prints it, so position construction cannot be reproduced from the manuscript alone (it may exist in the unexecuted `src/` modules, which this run deliberately did not run).

**Exit.** There is no separate exit trigger: the position is re-targeted every trading day, and the effective holding period is the inverse of turnover, printed as 1.0 to 1.1 days for the cost-blind baseline and 24.2 to 71.2 days for the execution-aware runs (Table 1). Stops, take-profits and time exits do not exist in the source.

**Holding period, re-entry, overlap.** Continuous daily re-targeting of one book; no overlapping-position logic, no re-entry rule, no cash buffer. `Not applicable` rather than missing.

**Parameters (all source-reported unless marked).** Reward penalties `gamma1 = 0.5` (turnover) and `gamma2 = 8` (cost), plus an identical expression-complexity penalty above an 8-node budget in both arms (Section 2); penalty sweep grid of nine additional settings - `gamma1` in `{0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1.0}` with `gamma2 = 0`, `gamma2` in `{2, 4, 8, 16}` with `gamma1 = 0`, and `(0.25, 4)` - over the volatility category at sampling seed 1001 (Table 3); LLM temperature `0.7` with `top_p 0.9` (repository README), validated against 0 and 0.3 which both collapse after 1 to 2 rounds (Appendix A.3); search budget `50 rounds` per fold; grammar whitelist `cs_rank, ts_rank, zscore, decay_linear, sma, stddev, arithmetic` over `open/high/low/close/volume/vwap` (Section 2); six anti-pattern rules (Appendix A.2); lookback range `[2, 252]`; seed alphas from the repository's `grid_summary.csv`: reversal `rank(-1 * delta(close, 1))`, volume `rank(-1 * zscore(volume, 20))`, volatility `rank(-1 * stddev(delta(close, 1), 20))`, momentum `rank(delta(close, 60) / (stddev(delta(close, 1), 60) + 1e-5))`; LLM sampling seeds `1001, 2002, 3003` (repository README); liquidity cap `top-150 by trailing two-year average dollar volume` reconstituted six times per fold (Appendix A.1); cost parameters `Corwin-Schultz bounded to 1-100 bps`, impact coefficient `Y = 0.5`, participation measured against `21-day average dollar volume`, flat-cost comparison columns `5 / 10 / 20 bps`. Anything not on that list - order type, fill price, score-to-weight rule, sizing, signal-to-order delay - is `research-proposed`.

**Whether the rule is fully specified:** no. The *search objective* is fully specified; the *portfolio* is not. Reproducing Table 1 therefore additionally requires the score-to-weight mapping, the close convention and the exact fill price, all `underspecified` in the manuscript.

## Execution assumptions

**Source-reported (Section 2, Appendix A.1 and A.4; census over the pinned 34,520-character text layer).**
- Bid-ask spread is estimated per stock-day with the Corwin and Schultz (2012) high-low estimator, `bounded to 1-100bps`; impact follows `Impact = Y * sigma_daily * sqrt(participation)` with `Y = 0.5` (Grinold and Kahn, 2000) and participation measured against 21-day average dollar volume. `Daily cost drag is this one-way cost applied to that day's dollar weight change, summed across the book and subtracted from gross return.`
- Turnover is drift-adjusted against the pre-rebalance weight so passive drift is not double-counted; the *reported* turnover is the one-way halved figure while `the cost engine itself is charged on the full, un-halved weight change, since realized transaction cost is paid on both legs of every rebalance` (Appendix A.4).
- Execution lag: weights computed on day `t` are applied one day later, so returns are close-to-close with an explicit one-day delay; order type, fill price and partial-fill handling are `not stated in source`.
- Robustness columns re-price the same return streams at flat `5`, `10` and `20 bps` one-way (Section 2, Figure 3).
- Gross exposure is `200%` with daily rebalancing and `no no-trade band` (Section 4), so `the factor expression itself is the only turnover-reduction lever available to the search`.

### Negative evidence

1. The cost-blind arm loses its entire notional in all four categories: cumulative return `-100%` with net Sharpe `-9.74`, `-11.24`, `-4.67` and `-6.61` (Table 1), so the flagship result is a demonstration of *failure* of gross-rewarded search, not of alpha.
2. Three of the four execution-aware categories remain net-negative after the treatment: `-0.38`, `-0.25` and `-0.10` net Sharpe with cumulative returns `-77%`, `-55%` and `-47%` (Table 1); only the volatility seed clears zero.
3. The single net-positive result is modest and untested for significance in absolute terms: net Sharpe `+0.35` on `13.1-13.3%` volatility with maximum drawdown `-33% to -40%` implies a Calmar ratio of about `0.1`, and the source reports no t-statistic, p-value or confidence interval *for that factor's own Sharpe* - the only interval in the paper covers the paired improvement, not the absolute level.
4. A `+9.96` improvement measured against a baseline that sits at `-4.67` to `-11.24` is compatible with a still-negative strategy; the treatment-effect statistic must not be read as absolute performance (Sections 3, 4).
5. The cost model's impact coefficient is not fitted: `The cost model's impact coefficient (Y=0.5) is not fit to realized execution data` (Section 4), and the spread is an estimator (Corwin-Schultz high-low) rather than an observed quoted spread; the median half-spread of `6.7bps` is therefore model output.
6. The flat 5/10/20 bps columns `share the model family used inside the reward`, so they `establish robustness to the assumed cost level rather than to the cost model's functional form` (Section 4, source's own words).
7. Commissions, exchange fees, borrow and stock-loan cost, funding, margin, latency, participation caps and capacity are all absent (census above), so every printed net number is net of two modelled frictions only; for a daily 200-percent-gross long/short book the missing short-leg borrow cost alone could be material (`data gap`).
8. Universe coverage is incomplete where it matters most: `price coverage is incomplete pre-2010 (roughly 75-90% of true constituents)`, which the source argues does not bias the between-arm comparison but concedes `may affect absolute return levels in early folds` (Section 4).
9. Breadth is capped for compute: `~150-220 tickers per fold` out of a `~500-name` index, with `the full ~500-name universe is a natural robustness check not yet run` (Section 4, source's own words).
10. The whole comparison rests on one local model at one quantisation - Qwen2.5-Coder-14B-Instruct Q4_K_M - with three sampling seeds, and the source had to raise temperature to 0.7 because 0 and 0.3 `collapse to a fixed point after round 1-2`; model-specificity is untested (`data gap`).
11. No multiplicity control anywhere: `benjamini 0`, `fdr 0`, `bonferroni 0`, `multiple testing 0`, with 4 categories x 3 seeds x 10 penalty configurations plus a 50-round search per fold; the single `p=0.0005` sign test is the only formal test in the paper.
12. Contradiction 1: the prose claims execution-aware modal recurrence of `14-22 of 63` while Table 2 prints `9` for the reversal cell, and the shipped `wfo_folds.csv` files reproduce `9`; the structure argument therefore overstates its own consistency.
13. Contradiction 2: the SSRN author affiliation and the manuscript affiliation disagree, and the manuscript body asserts no affiliation at all.
14. Contradiction 3: the Data availability statement contradicts the title page of the same PDF about whether the repository URL is present in this version.
15. The score-to-weight rule that turns a factor score into a dollar-neutral book is never stated, so Table 1 cannot be reconstructed from the manuscript alone even with the right data (`underspecified`).
16. The reported turnover is halved while the cost charge is not (Appendix A.4); any reader comparing `1.4%/day` against an external turnover definition will mis-scale both the turnover and the cost by 2x unless they notice the convention, and the convention is stated only in an appendix.
17. The `85-90% of NAV per year` cost claim only closes under that halved convention with about 252 sessions; neither is stated beside the sentence (see `### Independently reproduced`).
18. Point-in-time membership comes from an unnamed `public historical-components record`, and the primary price archive is a `local multi-vendor price archive` that is explicitly `not itself publicly redistributable`; exact replication therefore depends on vendor access the source does not version (`data gap`).
19. The source itself flags the residual risk: negative net Sharpe in three categories `should not be read as evidence that these predictor families are categorically unprofitable net of cost. They reflect an intentionally unforgiving stress test, uncontrolled 200%-gross daily rebalancing` (Section 4) - i.e. the printed negatives are properties of a deliberately harsh harness.

21. Sample framing: one market (US equities), one index family (S&P), one frequency (daily), one payoff convention (dollar-neutral long/short, 200 percent gross), with test years `2006 through` the fold-20 window ending `2026-08-13` - no crypto, no futures, no options, no other asset class.

The repository also contains earlier six-fold exploratory tables (`results/tables/table1*.csv`) with different numbers, including a `Seed-Only (no LLM)` row, none of which appear in the manuscript; which artefact generation corresponds to which printed claim is `not stated in source` beyond the paths named here.

## Crypto portability

`adapted`.

- The mechanism - placing the execution-cost penalty inside the reward of an automated factor search - is universe-agnostic in principle, but every piece of evidence in the source is US cash equities: a reconstructed S&P membership panel, Corwin-Schultz high-low spreads on exchange sessions, a dollar-neutral long/short book, and test windows on a trading-day calendar. The source contains no crypto analysis of any kind, so the label cannot be `direct`.
- What ports cleanly: the AST factor grammar over OHLCV plus vwap, the drift-adjusted turnover accounting, the matched-seed reward ablation, and the walk-forward protocol are all data-shape compatible with crypto daily bars.
- What does not port without rework: (i) universe construction - crypto has severe listing, delisting and survivorship churn, so a point-in-time membership analogue and a liquidity cap must be defined (`research-proposed`); (ii) cost - crypto venues charge explicit maker/taker schedules and perp funding rather than a spread-and-impact pair, so the Corwin-Schultz plus `Y = 0.5` engine must be replaced or validated against observed crypto spreads; (iii) calendar - the source annualises over trading days, while crypto trades 24/7, so candle boundaries, the daily close convention and every annualisation factor change; (iv) market structure - spot versus perpetual, funding carry, mark-versus-index divergence, liquidation and leverage behaviour, cross-venue fragmentation and stablecoin numeraire are all absent from the source and remain `data gap`.
- Horizon caveat: the treatment's benefit appears precisely because baseline turnover is 49-101 percent per day; crypto's higher realised volatility and different fee tiers could shift that break-even substantially, so the sign of the treatment effect in crypto is `unproven` until F5, F8 and F12 are re-run on a crypto panel.
- Staleness caveat: the pinned stitched record ends `2026-08-13`; any claim about the same effect after that date is `unproven` until F14 is run.
