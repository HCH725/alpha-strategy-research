## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The thesis does not sell a directional credit view; it builds a **risk-management overlay** — a dynamically timed *short* exposure to investment-grade credit that a bond-fund manager turns on when stress signals fire and off when they subside. The source's stated mechanism (Abstract, §1.1, §2.1, §6):

1. **Downside convexity of IG credit ETFs.** During crises (GFC 2008–2009, COVID March 2020) IG spreads widen sharply and liquidity dries up ("sellers outnumbering buyers"), so IG bonds fall "equally large or larger drawdowns than similar duration high-yield bonds" (Abstract). The spread/liquidity premia "occur during market drawdowns and tend to move non-linearly with the market" — the thesis names this non-linearity **downside convexity** — and "can effectively be captured through a short position established in IG Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs)" (Abstract). Rising ETF short availability plus the repo market has "made short selling of bonds … increasingly accessible and cost-effective" (§1.1), offering an alternative to rolling puts / vol strategies.
2. **Three partially orthogonal stress signals predict when the short pays (§3).** *Credit risk* from option-implied risk-neutral distributions of LQD (risk-neutral probability/expected magnitude of LQD drawdowns in excess of duration-matched IEF over 3 months); *liquidity* from the market-value-weighted duration×spread of IG CDX constituents in TRACE (high value = market is "liquidity-demanding" → short on; §3.2); *momentum* from duration-neutral LQD/HYG cumulative returns with a one-month lag (§3.3). Source-stated orthogonality: pairwise Pearson correlation "less than 25%", and a VAR(0) equation-by-equation OLS gives "adjusted R-squared ≤ 0.05" (§3.4).
3. **Combination and timing (§4).** Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA) learns hedge weights by maximizing correlation between the joint signal and hedged-portfolio duration-neutral returns; a second-step regression forecasts next-day hedged return; the hedge is entered when the optimal hedge weight is negative **and** the forecast z-score exceeds an upper threshold, and exited when the forecast z-score falls below a lower threshold (hysteresis, Eq. 4.13). Source interpretation of what CCA learns (§5.1, §6): "CCA learns to activate the hedge when correlations from signals to hedged returns begin to spike, and exit at the peak or when correlations revert back to normal"; it "favours medium horizon credit and liquidity risk combined with short-term momentum, presumably loading on credit and liquidity risk shocks to enter the hedge and short-term reversal to exit."
4. **Why ETFs rather than CDX (§5.1, §6):** "credit default swaps do not benefit from the same downside convexity that credit ETFs possess" — IG CDX alone does not cancel the 2020 drawdown and adds minimal Sortino (verified in the source's own backtest; see Negative evidence).

### Research interpretation

**Falsifiable hypothesis captured here:** *a bond fund's duration-neutral spread returns can be protected — net of bid-ask spread costs, short-funding costs, and a 10%-of-ADV volume cap — by shorting IG/HY credit ETFs (LQD ± HYG) exactly when an option-implied credit-risk signal, a TRACE-derived liquidity signal, and a lagged duration-neutral momentum signal jointly forecast negative next-day hedge returns, because credit-ETF downside convexity pays during spread-widening episodes and the three signals lead those episodes.*

- **Type of record:** a **timed tail-hedge / defensive overlay** with an embedded predictive-timing claim, not a standalone alpha factory. Per repository rules the two roles are kept separate: the *hedge* (short IG exposure) is risk management; the *alpha-relevant claim* is that the **entry/exit timing signal** (credit+liquidity+momentum via CCA) has predictive content for short-hedge returns — i.e., the hedge is better than the same short held unconditionally, and better than an untimed control.
- **Hypothesized mechanism in our words:** crisis-conditioned mean reversion of *credit risk premia* captured through a convex (non-linear) instrument: spread-widening episodes cluster, credit ETFs fall more than linearly in those episodes, and option surfaces + trading-quality metrics + lagged flows lead the clustering by enough to time entry/exit at daily frequency.
- **Component roles (hybrid structure):** Regime/stress gauge: risk-neutral credit-risk signal (LQD vs IEF drawdown decomposition). Confirmation: TRACE liquidity factor (duration×spread on IG CDX names). Third leg: duration-neutral momentum/reversal (LQD/HYG, 12m-1m-style with 22-business-day offset). Combination: CCA weights + second-step forecast (or the paper's baseline two-step OLS). Risk/exit: z-score hysteresis off-threshold, `|W_hedge| ≤ 1` cap, volatility scaling `min(σ_fund/σ_hedge, 1)` (one year of duration-neutral vol), and a 10%-of-252-day-SMA volume cap on position change (Eqs. 4.15–4.16). Do not assume every component contributes — the source's own single-signal ablation shows momentum alone barely helps (Table 5.1, MSG 0.099 / MSG+CSG 0.063 ΔSortino).

## Signal

**Signal object:** a daily long/flat *short* overlay on hedge instruments (LQD, optionally HYG/JNK/IG CDX or a PC1 basket) applied to a bond mutual fund's duration-neutral spread returns. Everything marked *source-reported* is stated in the pinned PDF.

- **Formation timestamp / tradability:** daily signals; "we are using **yesterday's signals to predict tomorrow's** hedged return, preventing data leakage and avoiding look-ahead bias" (§4.2). Exact publication timestamps / timezone convention for Bloomberg and TRACE inputs: **not stated in source** → data gap (US market session is implied by US ETF/TRACE data but not specified).
- **Lookback / warm-up:** rolling CCA/OLS training window searched over **{20, 40, 60, 125, 250}** business days (§5.1, Figure 5.1); two-step OLS variant fixes a **90-day** (one quarter) regression window (§4.1); momentum signal uses a **252-day** lookback for its z-score and a cumulative-return window offset by **22 business days** ("12m-1m momentum"-style lag, Eqs. 3.13–3.15, Figure 3.7); liquidity signal sums IG CDX constituents "up to **3 years** after being first included" (§3.2); credit-risk option surface is **3-month** LQD calls (§3.1, Table 3.1). Warm-up handling and endpoint inclusivity: **underspecified**.
- **Three inputs (source-reported):**
  1. **Credit risk (§3.1):** fit cubic splines (boundary conditions selected day-by-day where IV is flat; clamped at chosen strikes, cf. Malz 2014) to Bloomberg LQD 3-month call implied volatilities → call value function → risk-neutral CDF/PDF by numerical differentiation (Eqs. 3.8–3.10, monotone/bounded CDF enforced numerically); duration effects removed by mapping the **IEF** 1%-probability drawdown onto the LQD CDF; two variants both plotted (Figures 3.4–3.5): (a) probability that LQD draws down more than IEF within 3 months ("Step 2"), (b) expected LQD drawdown in excess of IEF (integrate LQD PDF to that point). Which variant feeds each backtest: grid-search choice is **underspecified in-source** (both are "worth investigating").
  2. **Liquidity (§3.2, Eq. 3.12):** `F_Liquidity = Σ(MktVal_i,t × Duration_i,t × Spread_i,t) / Σ MktVal_i,t` over IG CDX constituents (≈130 names, 6-month rebalance), spread = corporate-bond YTM minus maturity/coupon-matched Treasury YTM, on cleaned TRACE prints + WRDS monthly depository attributes. High = liquidity-demanding → short on.
  3. **Momentum (§3.3):** duration-neutral returns `R̃` (Appendix Eqs. A1–A4: two Treasuries bracketing target duration, weights sum to 1, subtract duration-driven return); `C̃_n,t = R̃_{t−22} / R̃_{t−n−22} − 1` then z-score (Eqs. 3.13–3.15); Figure 3.7 shows LQD and HYG signals with 252-day lookback.
- **Combination and entry (primary method = CCA, §4.2):** first canonical pair between `X = (F_Liquidity, F_Momentum, F_Credit)` and `Y = (R̃_fund, R̃_hedge)`, hedge weight normalized per unit of fund (`w_fund = 1`); second-step regression `R̃*_hedged,t+1 = ω + β(Xa)_t + ε_{t+1}` → next-day forecast → z-score `ẑ_CCA,t+1`. **Entry (on):** `W*_hedge,t < 0` **AND** `ẑ_CCA,t+1 > γ_CCA,upper`. **Exit (off):** `ẑ_CCA,t+1 < γ_CCA,lower` **while on** (hysteresis; thresholds need not be symmetric; full rule in Eq. 4.13). **Sizing:** `|W_hedge| ≤ 1` (never exceed fund notional) and `|W_hedge| ≤ min(σ_fund/σ_hedge, 1)` with σ = last year's duration-neutral return volatility; multiple hedge instruments are combined through **PC1 of hedge returns** (first principal component) and distributed by `W_hedges,t · PC1` (§4.2).
- **Baseline combination (§4.1):** two-step OLS — rolling regression of next-day hedge duration-neutral return on one-day-lagged signals; hedge ON if `R̂_hedge,t+1 < 0` **AND** `p(F-statistic) ≤ γ_OLS`; weight `W = −β_hedge · 1_hedge` from a fund-on-hedge regression (Eqs. 4.2–4.6). Source reports CCA "outperforms the 2-Step OLS method" on hedged risk characteristics (§5.1) but gives no head-to-head table → quantitative gap for OLS results.
- **Parameters (source-reported, tuned in-sample by sequential grid search — Figures 5.1–5.3, Tables 5.1–5.3):** grid-search procedure: lookback first (local optimum **125 bd** for liquidity-only), then `γ_CCA,upper` (parabola peaking at **2.0** for that pass), then `γ_CCA,lower` (local optimum **−3.0**, "could realistically be either −2.0, −2.5 or −3.0"). Final headline configuration for all three signals on PIMIX hedged with LQD (Table 5.1 row `LSG, MSG, CSG`): **lookback 40 bd, γ_lower = −1.50, γ_upper = +2.50**. Under transaction+funding costs the optimum stays at (40, −1.50, +2.50) until `fbps = 200`, where γ_lower moves to **−1.00** (Table 5.2). With a $10bln fund and volume cap the optimum moves to **lookback 125, γ_upper 3.0** (Table 5.3). DODIX + LQD + IG CDX at fbps=20: **lookback 60, γ_lower −1.50, γ_upper +2.50** (§5.3). All fixed vs tuned status: **all of the above are tuned values chosen on the backtest sample itself** (source-described grid search; no out-of-sample split is stated) → **in-sample tuning is a material limitation, not a Scout label**.
- **Holding / re-entry:** overlay is daily; the source introduces the lower-threshold exit rule explicitly "to lower hedge turnover" and keep the hedge "on for longer" (§4.2); no maximum holding period, no stop-loss/take-profit on the hedge, no cooldown beyond the hysteresis band → otherwise **underspecified**.
- **Parameter provenance:** every threshold/window above (20/40/60/125/250, 90, 252, 22, 3y, 3m, ±1.5/2.5/2.0/3.0/−3.0/−1.0, 10% ADV, `|W|≤1`, vol-scaling) is **source-reported** with the section/table/figure anchor given. **Scout-generated operationalization: `research-proposed: none`** — this record contains **zero** Scout-created entry, exit, filter, stop, sizing, or execution rules; all Scout-chosen acceptance/failure cutoffs appear only in the Falsification plan and are labelled `research-defined`.

## Execution assumptions

- **Signal-to-order timing:** yesterday's signals → tomorrow's hedged return (§4.2); activation/deactivation decisions are made on this daily lag. Same-bar vs next-bar execution beyond that daily lag: **underspecified** (no intraday execution model).
- **Order type / fill model:** **not stated in source** → data gap. Position changes are implicitly spread across days when the volume cap binds (Eqs. 4.15–4.16: trade at most `min(V′_252/10, V_t)` shares per day, so entry/exit "gradually accumulates (or removes) over multiple consecutive trading days, minimizing market impact"). Partial fills, order types, and latency: **underspecified**.
- **Fees / spread:** **modeled** — transaction cost = live bid-ask half-spread charged only on days the hedge is established or closed: `1_trade · (P_ask − P_bid)/(P_ask + P_bid)` with bid/ask taken symmetric about mid ("As a simplification … considers the best bid and ask symmetric about the midprice", Eq. 4.14 and its discussion).
- **Slippage / impact:** explicit slippage beyond the bid-ask spread is **not modeled** — the source's own framing is that the base framework "doesn't account for trading slippage, volume informed realistic position taking, or short hedge position funding costs", and it then addresses costs via bid-ask, funding parameter, and the 10%-of-252-day-SMA volume cap (§4.2, Eqs. 4.14–4.16). Market impact beyond that volume cap: **not modeled → data gap** (do not read as "zero impact").
- **Funding / borrow:** short-funding cost is a **flat parameter** `fbps` applied on days the short is held (`1_hedge · fbps/1000`, Eq. 4.14). Source states empirical funding costs "can vary from as low as 20bps to as much as 800bps on rare days with large redemptions" and are "relatively insensitive to market downturns" (§5.1); headline cost tables use **fbps ∈ {0, 20, 50, 100, 200}** (Table 5.2), DODIX run uses **fbps = 20** (§5.3). A **time-varying borrow-fee series is not used** → material gap between the flat parameter and real locate/repo pricing.
- **Capacity:** volume cap = 10% of 252-day SMA daily volume (Eq. 4.16); source quantifies: a $500mln manager starting 2012 needing 10% of ADTV would need ≈35 trading days to accrue the LQD short vs ≈2 days in today's volumes (§1.2); a $10bln fund example implies ≈1,000 trading days to put on the hedge single-instrument (§5.1); bottleneck identified "around **$5bln** or greater" for single-instrument LQD (§5.1).
- **Leverage / margin / shorting:** hedge notional capped at fund notional and vol-scaled (§4.2); shorting assumed accessible via ETF securities-lending/repo market (§1.1); margin/liquidation mechanics: **not stated → data gap**.

### Negative evidence

Carried by the source itself (all `source-reported`):

- **IG CDX as sole hedge fails:** "IG CDXs alone do not suffice for avoiding large drawdowns, and make minimal contribution to Sortino gains" (§5.1); Figure 5.8 shows the CDX-timed hedge "does not cancel out the 2020 drawdown despite the hedge being active"; timing "is not as precise"; mechanism claim: "credit default swaps do not benefit from the same downside convexity that credit ETFs possess" (§5.1).
- **Capacity collapse at scale:** $10bln single-instrument → ΔSortino falls 0.744 → 0.437 → **0.223** (Table 5.3); bottleneck "around $5bln or greater"; the $10bln single-instrument hedge activates 4 times in ~11 years with a >2-year first ramp (§5.1).
- **Conservative-fund case is marginal:** DODIX gains are "marginal, lower, and decay much faster"; negative carry "quite expensive between 2016-2019"; protection requires acting "within a two week action window" (§5.3).
- **A slow grind-down episode defeats the hedge:** late-2019 "both funds draw down approximately 11%" while the hedge is on; a mid-episode rally "hurts the hedged portfolio" and the prolonged episode "induces higher funding costs" — "No hedge can be 100% effective" (§5.2).
- **Momentum leg alone is nearly useless:** MSG ΔSortino **0.099**, MSG+CSG **0.063** vs 0.787 joint (Table 5.1) — the signal set is not uniformly informative.
- **Cost sensitivity is monotone:** ΔSortino 0.787 → **0.618** as fbps goes 0 → 200 (Table 5.2), and the tuned off-threshold itself moves — parameter stability degrades with cost level.
- **Fragile parameter plateau:** off-threshold optimum "could realistically be either −2.0, −2.5 or −3.0" (§5.1); the source itself frames the grid search as finding "a local optimum" rather than "a global model optimum" (§5.1).
- **Outperformance concentration:** "The majority of the outperformance comes from avoiding the large drawdown during the 2020 COVID pandemic" (§5.1) — single-episode dependence is acknowledged in-source.

Identified by the Scout (labelled distinctly):

- **In-sample tuning, no stated out-of-sample split:** all headline parameters are chosen by sequential grid search on the same backtest sample on which ΔSortino is reported (§5.1, Figures 5.1–5.3); no walk-forward, holdout, or pre-registration is described → performance is optimistically biased by construction; magnitude unknowable from the source alone (**research-defined**): out-of-sample ΔSortino ≤ 0 under the fixed headline parameters would falsify the timing claim.
- **No statistical inference on the hedge effect:** no t-statistics, confidence blocks, or multiple-testing adjustments accompany any ΔSortino; heat-map cells are point estimates only.
- **Backtest window truncated at 2022-10 (or 2022-09):** the 2022–2023 banking-episode tightening, post-2022 spread regime, and everything after are untested; the window discrepancy (Jul 2013–Oct 2022 vs Jan 2013–Sep 2022) is unresolved in-source.
- **Flat funding parameter vs real borrow dynamics:** a constant `fbps` cannot express locate squeezes precisely when the hedge wants to be on; the source's own claimed range (20–800bps) is anecdotally sourced, not a vintage fee series.
- **Slippage and market impact beyond the 10%-ADV cap are not modeled** (see Execution assumptions) → net figures are optimistic to an unquantified degree.
- **Contrary repo evidence (different source, adjacent object):** `fourier-residue-sign-magnitude-equity-reversal-decomposition-2026-09-04.md` reports that LQD/HYG daily prices are "indistinguishable from random walks" for mean-reversion purposes (OTC matrix pricing) — not a direct contradiction (this thesis's claim is about *short-hedge timing*, not mean reversion in spot), but a reminder that LQD/HYG price formation is OTC-driven and its own microstructure record in this repository is thin.

## Falsification plan

Thresholds and actions below are **`research-defined`** (Scout-generated); none of them comes from the thesis, which states no pre-registered failure rules. Each test: data / sample / metric / threshold / action.

1. **Out-of-sample timing test (primary).** Data: PIMIX-or-proxy duration-neutral spread returns + LQD/HYG/IEF/TRACE-equivalent inputs. Sample: freeze the source headline parameters (40 bd, −1.50, +2.50, all three signals, fbps = 50, 10% ADV cap) and run untouched post-2022-10 data (≥ 24 months). Metric: ΔSortino₂₅₂ hedged vs unhedged. **Threshold:** ΔSortino ≤ 0 → timing claim **fails**; 0 < ΔSortino < 0.3 → materially weakened (less than ~40% of the in-sample 0.732). Action: keep record research-only, record OOS result as negative evidence; no retuning rescue (one prespecified configuration only).
2. **Placebo / shuffled-timing test.** Same data; randomly circular-shift the activation indicator 1,000 times (preserving on-period lengths). Metric: distribution of placebo ΔSortino vs actual. **Threshold:** actual ΔSortino below the 95th percentile of placebo → timing skill claim **fails**. Action: same as test 1.
3. **Ablation out-of-sample.** Joint three-signal vs best single signal (credit or liquidity), OOS. **Threshold:** joint ≤ best single → the source's orthogonality/additivity reading (Table 5.1) **fails** materially. Action: downgrade mechanism claim to "credit/liquidity stress timing only".
4. **Cost stress ladder.** fbps ∈ {50, 100, 200, 400, 800} (800 = source's stated extreme) and bid-ask widened ×2. Metric: ΔSortino at each rung with frozen parameters. **Threshold:** ΔSortino < 0.1 at fbps = 200, or < 0 at any rung ≤ 400 → "cheap tail hedge" claim **fails** for that cost regime. Action: record cost ceiling explicitly; no adoption beyond it under any circumstances.
5. **Capacity ladder.** Fund sizes {$0.1B, $1B, $5B, $10B} under the 10%-of-252-SMA rule, single LQD then LQD+HYG. **Threshold:** ΔSortino ≤ 0 at $1B single-instrument (source implies ~0.437 there) → capacity narrative **fails** earlier than source claims; confirm-or-refute the stated ~$5B bottleneck. Action: bind any future candidate to its measured size ceiling.
6. **Instrument control (source's own control, replicated).** IG CDX-only timed hedge vs LQD-timed hedge, same parameters. **Threshold:** CDX ≥ LQD in ΔSortino → source's downside-convexity explanation **fails**; CDX < LQD as reported → mechanism reading survives. Action: document which instrument carries the convexity.
7. **Episode concentration audit.** Recompute ΔSortino with 2020 (and separately each crisis year) excluded. **Threshold:** remaining-sample ΔSortino ≤ 0.1 → the effect is single-episode and **fails** the "robust over a wide array" reading (§6). Action: relabel as crisis-episode-dependent.
8. **Data-window reconciliation.** Re-run under both in-source windows (Jul 2013–Oct 2022 and Jan 2013–Sep 2022). **Threshold:** headline ΔSortino differs by > 0.15 between windows → window sensitivity **fails** robustness reading. Action: pin one window and mark the other as superseded in any derivative record.

Cross-cutting rule (research-defined): tests must be run on fixed, prespecified configurations; **no test may be rescued by unconstrained retuning**; if required data (TRACE, Bloomberg IV surfaces) cannot be obtained, report **not run**, never pass. Any failed test keeps the record at `research-only / not-implemented / not-approved`.

## Crypto portability

**`unproven`** — the source contains **zero** crypto evidence: the entire study runs on US IG credit funds, US credit ETFs, US Treasury ETFs and TRACE corporate-bond prints. Nothing in the primary source demonstrates this mechanism in crypto markets, so it must not be labelled `direct`; it is a **ported hypothesis at best**, and even `adapted` overstates readiness because the signal inputs do not map:

- **No counterpart instruments/fields:** IG credit risk (risk-neutral LQD-vs-IEF drawdown decomposition), TRACE corporate-bond spreads, IG CDX constituents, and bond-fund spread returns have **no direct crypto equivalent**. Crypto has no duration-matched Treasury-bond benchmark to strip duration, no IG-credit complex, and no TRACE.
- **Possible (untested) ports, all research-proposed if ever attempted:** stress-short timing via shorting BTC/ETH **perpetuals** during liquidity crises (funding-rate drag replaces `fbps`; liquidation/bankruptcy risk replaces securities-lending risk); option-implied risk-neutral distributions from **BTC options** as a stand-in for the credit-risk signal; order-book depth/spread deterioration as a stand-in for the TRACE liquidity signal. Each substitution changes the mechanism (crypto drawdowns are liquidity/delevering-driven rather than credit-spread-driven) → the ported hypothesis must be re-derived, not inherited.
- **Crypto-specific risks if attempted:** spot vs perpetual basis and funding (costs spike precisely in stress, inverting the "relatively insensitive to downturns" funding assumption the source relies on); 24/7 sessions vs the source's US-session daily data (signal timestamps and candle boundaries differ); venue fragmentation (which venue's IV surface / depth / funding is canonical); mark/index price and oracle differences; borrow/short availability and squeeze/liquidation mechanics for perp shorts; listing/survivorship of "bond-like" yield instruments (stablecoin yield token drawdowns are protocol, not credit-spread, risk).
- Crypto portability is **not** authorization to trade; a crypto analogue would require its own record, its own primary evidence, and its own falsification plan.

- **Risk-management vs alpha:** the overlay is, by the source's own framing, **hedging** (risk management). Its alpha-relevant content is the *timing signal* only; a real allocation decision must not confuse the short-IG position itself (a cost-carrying hedge) with a predictive return source.
