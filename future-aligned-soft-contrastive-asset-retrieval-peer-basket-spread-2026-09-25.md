## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The paper's stated thesis is that asset similarity has been defined *backward* — Pearson correlation of historical returns, DTW on normalized price series, or GICS/sector membership — and that "these criteria are backward-looking: they identify assets that have moved together, not those that will." Its proposed remedy is **future-aligned supervision**: each asset's raw 64-day × 6-feature window is encoded into a 384-dimensional embedding by a patch-based Transformer, and the training target for an anchor asset *i* is the **pairwise Pearson correlation of the H = 64-day future daily return series** with every other asset in the same-date batch, passed through a temperature-scaled softmax (`τ_t = 0.05`). A second temperature-scaled softmax (`τ = 0.01`) is applied to the cosine similarities of the embeddings, and the loss is the **KL divergence** between the two per-anchor distributions (Eq. 7–10). The authors describe this as preserving a graded scale — "assets that are 80% correlated in future returns receive proportionally more 'pull' than those at 30%" — instead of InfoNCE's binary positive/negative assignment. At inference, retrieval is plain cosine nearest-neighbour search in that space.

For the downstream trading evaluation the source is explicit about the intent and the limit: the spread-trading backtest is *"a stylized spread trading backtest (Gatev et al., 2006) designed to measure signal quality rather than realized profitability"*, and the released code's module docstring states the economic premise directly — *"if a retrieval model correctly identifies similar assets, the spread between a query asset and its peer basket should be tight and mean-reverting, enabling profitable trading"* (`source-reported`, Section 5.6 and `src/retrieval/spread_backtest.py` docstring). The source reports **gross** Sharpe for all fifteen methods, and for FASCL additionally turnover, break-even one-way cost and net Sharpe at fixed one-way costs.

### Research interpretation

Stated as a falsifiable hypothesis: **a representation trained so that embedding similarity tracks *future* return correlation selects peer baskets whose daily spread (query minus equal-weight peer basket) mean-reverts more strongly, and with lower residual dispersion, than baskets selected by observation-space similarity (historical Pearson, DTW), by self-supervised/foundation-model embeddings, or at random — out of sample in calendar 2024.** Three separable components carry the claim, and ablation shows they do not contribute equally:

```text
Signal / retrieval:  FASCL embedding of the trailing 64-day window -> cosine top-K peers
                     (K in {1,5,10,20}), same window period only, own ticker excluded
Position rule:       position = -sign(cumulative spread to date), recomputed daily, no deadband
Execution:           next-day relative to the signal (positions[d] <- -sign(cumspread[d-1]);
                     P&L[d] = positions[d] * spread[d])
Cost layer:          flat one-way basis points charged on traded notional (code only)
```

- The **edge lives in the supervision, not the architecture**: the source's own Table 4 ablation moves FRC@1 from 0.4427 (FASCL) to 0.1667 when the contrastive objective is replaced by pointwise return regression, and to 0.2875 when the *same* soft-contrastive machinery is fed observation-space similarity instead of future return correlation, while pooling, depth, temperature and input-channel changes move it by ≤4.5% (≤14% for the projection head). Our reading: this is a claim about a *label*, not about a model class.
- The **absolute Sharpe level is mostly not retrieval**: the random-retrieval arm already prints gross Sharpe 3.00–4.13 (Table 5). The generic long-short/short-long mean-reversion overlay on a diversiﬁed cross-section therefore supplies most of the level; what FASCL adds over the strongest observation-space baseline is a much smaller increment (`our count`: +1.17 / +0.11 / +1.05 / +1.05 gross Sharpe over Pearson at K = 1/5/10/20).
- The source itself flags the main competing explanation: *"Historical return correlation already partially predicts near-future co-movement through momentum effects (Jegadeesh and Titman, 1993); explicit future-aligned optimization improves on this strong baseline."* So part of the FRC/IC advantage may be a momentum/style persistence effect rather than a distinct retrieval mechanism (`research interpretation`).
- The metric the paper leans on for cross-sectional quality, **IC@K, is explicitly not a trading signal**: *"The consensus is formed from the peers' realized returns, so IC@K diagnoses how well retrieval groups assets that move together; it is not a forecast and is not tradable as stated."* (`source-reported`, Section 4.3.)

## Signal

All operational items below are `source-reported` from the pinned HTML unless explicitly marked `code-derived`, `research-proposed` or `underspecified`.

- **Formation timestamp.** At each evaluation window, every asset's trailing **T = 64 trading days** of daily data is encoded and all assets in the *same* window period are compared (`same-period retrieval`, Section 4.2). The retrieval timestamp is therefore the window observation date; the traded horizon is the **H = 64 future trading days** that follow. Timezone and session-close convention are **not stated → `data gap`**; the underlying data are daily exchange-session bars.
- **Lookback.** T = 64 trading days, no warm-up stated. Inputs are **z-scored per feature across the time dimension inside each window** (removing absolute price/volume scale), split into **N = T/P = 16 non-overlapping patches of P = 4**, linearly projected (Conv1d, kernel = stride = 4) to D = 384 with layer norm, plus learnable positional embeddings. The reported model **mean-pools the patch tokens** (no `[CLS]` contribution).
- **Encoder.** L = 8 pre-norm Transformer blocks, 8 attention heads, FFN expansion ratio 4, GELU, dropout in the FFN; ≈14M parameters (`source-reported`, Section 3.2).
- **Training objective and schedule.** Soft contrastive KL loss with **τ = 0.01, τ_t = 0.05**; AdamW (β = 0.9/0.999, weight decay 0.05), cosine learning-rate schedule from 1e-3 to 1e-6 with **10 epochs linear warm-up**, **100 epochs × 300 steps**, **batch B = 4,096**, gradient clip at norm 1.0, bfloat16 mixed precision. Batches are **same-date batches** (one random date, then B assets), so pairwise future-return correlation is a cross-sectional signal at a fixed point in time. Supervision uses **H = 64 future trading days**; training windows end in 2022 with supervision extending into Q1 2023 (`source-reported`, Sections 3.3–3.4, 4.1, 5.1).
- **Retrieval.** Cosine similarity in embedding space; **top-K with K ∈ {1, 5, 10, 20}**; candidates restricted to assets observed in the **same time window**; the query's own ticker is **excluded** (`source-reported`, Section 4.2).
- **Long entry / short entry (spread overlay).** For each query *q*, the retrieved top-K peers form an **equal-weight basket**; the daily spread is `s(q,d) = r(q,d) − mean_k r(k,d)` over d = 1..H (Eq. 15). The position at day d+1 is **`−sign(Σ_{d'≤d} s(q,d'))`**, i.e. a unit position in the spread: long query / short basket when the cumulative spread is negative, and the reverse when positive. The daily P&L is that position times the same-day spread; because the position for day d is computed from cumulative spread through d−1, execution is **next-day relative to the signal** (`source-reported` Eq. 15 and Section 5.6; `code-derived` from `src/retrieval/spread_backtest.py`: `positions[1:] = -sign(cum_spread[:-1])`, `pnl = positions * spread_daily`).
- **Exit / holding period.** **No exit rule other than the daily sign flip, no stop, no take-profit, no maximum holding period** is stated anywhere → `underspecified`. The overlay runs across the H = 64-day future window; overlapping windows are pooled (see Execution assumptions).
- **Position sizing.** One unit of spread exposure per query; **portfolio-level P&L is the arithmetic mean of daily P&L across all queries** (`source-reported`, Section 5.6). No capital base, no netting across queries, no risk budget, no per-name weight cap → `underspecified`.
- **Turnover mechanism.** "The mean-reversion position is the sign of the cumulative spread, recomputed daily, so it can flip often; **each flip trades both legs**" (`source-reported`); the code charges gross notional 2.0 per full flip (query leg + basket leg) with **no deadband** (`code-derived`).
- **Pre-processing guard.** Daily returns entering the backtest are **clipped at ±50%** "to guard against data artifacts such as mis-adjusted splits" (`source-reported`, Section 5.6; `RETURN_CLIP_THRESHOLD = 0.5` in code).
- **Parameters.** Every numeric choice above (T, H, P, D, L, heads, τ, τ_t, batch, epochs, learning rates, K set, ±50% clip) is `source-reported`. **No threshold, entry trigger, stop, universe filter or sizing rule in this record is invented by the Scout.** Anything not listed is marked `underspecified` rather than filled in.

- **Timestamp / timezone.** Daily bars, exchange session; timezone, clock and out-of-order handling **not stated → `data gap`**.

## Execution assumptions

- **Signal-to-order.** Daily. The position applied to day d is computed from cumulative spread through d−1, so the overlay is **next-bar (next-day) execution relative to the signal**; there is no same-bar execution anywhere (`code-derived`, cross-checked against Section 5.6's prose "position at day d+1").
- **Order type / fill model.** **Not stated anywhere → `data gap`.** No market-vs-limit distinction, no fill probability, no partial-fill or failed-order handling, no latency or signal-to-order delay model (`transaction cost / fill / latency` word scan of the pinned text returns 0 hits for `latency`, `fill`, `limit order`, `market order`).
- **Fees / spread / slippage (the ONLY cost that exists in the source).** A **flat one-way basis-point charge on traded notional**, computed in code at **{1, 5, 10, 20} bp** and printed in Table 5 only as **break-even cost, net Sharpe @ 5 bp, and net Sharpe @ 10 bp**. The paper calls 5 bp "conservative for liquid US equities" (`source-reported`, Section 5.6 and Table 5 lower panel; `code-derived` net computation `net = pnl − (bps/1e4) * traded`, annualisation `× √252`).
- **Turnover.** Average daily traded notional per unit of spread exposure **0.32 / 0.32 / 0.32 / 0.31** at K = 1/5/10/20 (`source-reported`, Table 5). `our count`: that is ≈ **80.6 / 80.6 / 80.6 / 78.1** units of traded notional per unit of spread exposure per 252-day year (0.32 × 252), gross of any netting, since no netting across queries is described.
- **What is explicitly omitted by the source.** *"a stylized backtest omits market impact and capacity effects"* and *"the spread trading backtest is a stylized signal-quality measure that omits market impact and capacity constraints, so any strategy built on it would need further risk controls and cost modeling"* (`source-reported`, Section 5.6 and Ethical Considerations).
- **What is never mentioned at all → `data gap`, never treated as zero.** Word-boundary scan of the pinned v2 HTML: `bid-ask` 0, `slippage` 0, `commission` 0, `fee`/`fees` 0, `borrow` 0, `short sell`/`short-sell` 0, `dividend` 0, `latency` 0, `fill` 0, `ADV` 0, `participation` 0, `leverage` 0, `liquidat` 0, `funding` 1 (arXiv funder boilerplate only), `margin` 3 (all prose "margin"/"wide margin", unrelated), `transaction` 2 (both inside "net of transaction costs" / "Turnover and transaction costs"), `market impact` 2, `capacity` 2, `turnover` 4. So **spread, slippage, borrow/locate fees for the short leg, dividend/ex-date effects, latency, participation limits, leverage/margin and capacity are unmodelled, not zero**.
- **Shorting / borrow.** The overlay is short one leg on every unit; **no borrow availability, hard-to-borrow screen, locate fee or stock-loan cost appears anywhere → `data gap`**.
- **Leverage / margin.** Not stated → `data gap`.
- **Capacity.** Not stated; explicitly conceded as omitted.
- **Portfolio construction (critical caveat).** P&L is the **arithmetic mean of per-query daily P&L**, i.e. an equal-weight cross-sectional overlay with no capital base, no netting of offsetting query positions, no position limits and no reconciliation of the three overlapping window periods. `code-derived`: `src/retrieval/evaluator.py::_compute_spread_backtest` receives the **whole test split** (all window periods pooled) and `spread_backtest.py` stacks every query into one `(Q, 64)` matrix and averages down the query axis **for each window-relative day index d**. Because the three window periods start on different calendar days, **day index d mixes calendar dates from different periods**, so the printed "portfolio time series" is window-relative rather than a calendar-aligned equity curve (`research interpretation` from the released code; the paper's own wording is only "averaging daily P&L across all queries, and we report the annualized Sharpe ratio of the resulting portfolio time series").

### Negative evidence

1. **Cost realism.** Only a flat one-way bp charge exists. Spread, slippage, borrow/locate fee on the short leg, dividends, latency, fills, participation and impact are all unmodelled; the source concedes only **market impact and capacity**. Everything else is `data gap`, never zero (word-scan evidence in Execution assumptions).
2. **Break-even is thin.** 8.3–11.7 bp one-way. At **10 bp** — a realistic all-in one-way for anything below the largest caps, and below a typical retail spread-plus-commission on less liquid names — **three of four K configurations go net negative** (Table 5), which sits directly against the source's own sentence *"the signal therefore survives realistic cost levels."*
3. **Most of the absolute Sharpe is not the retrieval signal.** Random retrieval posts gross Sharpe **3.00–4.13** (Table 5); the generic spread mean-reversion overlay plus cross-query averaging supplies the bulk of the level, and the increment over Pearson at **K=5 is only +0.11** (`our count`).
4. **No significance test on the trading result.** The paired bootstrap (N=1,000) covers **FRC@K only**; there is **no confidence interval, bootstrap or hypothesis test on any Sharpe difference**, and a word scan finds **0 hits for `seed`, `error bar`, `multiple runs`, `standard deviation across runs`** → training-run variance is unreported (`data gap`).
5. **One test year.** All main results are calendar **2024**; robustness only re-slices that year. The source concedes *"a single year contains only a limited range of them."* No multi-year out-of-sample result exists for the trading overlay.
6. **IC@K is explicitly non-tradable** (source, Section 4.3), yet it is one of the three headline future-behaviour metrics.
7. **Momentum / style confound.** The source states historical correlation already predicts near-future co-movement through momentum; FASCL's FRC/IC margin and the spread's mean reversion may both be partly a persistence/style artifact (`research interpretation`).
8. **Point-in-time universe risk.** The ticker list comes from a listing file fetched at download time with **no stated snapshot date** and no survivorship/delisting treatment discussed → conditioning of a 2010–2024 sample on a later listing file is a live risk (`research interpretation` from `download_ohlcv_all.py`; the paper only mentions delisted tickers when explaining 86% sector coverage).
9. **Window-relative P&L.** `our code-derived finding`: the Sharpe is computed on an index-time series that pools three calendar-distinct window periods, not on a calendar-aligned equity curve, with no capital base or netting (see Execution assumptions).
10. **Printed metric set is incomplete.** The released code computes `cum_return`, `max_drawdown`, `win_rate`, `net_sharpe_1bps` and `net_sharpe_20bps`, but **Table 5 prints only Sharpe, turnover, break-even, net@5bp and net@10bp** → drawdown, win rate, cumulative return, and the 1 bp / 20 bp net rungs are `data gap` in the paper even though they exist in code.
11. **Baseline numbers not reproducible from the release.** The README withholds all fourteen baselines' code (three upstreams unlicensed) and checkpoints/evaluation outputs are omitted → only FASCL's own rows can be re-run from the pinned SHA (`source-reported`, README).
12. **Short-leg feasibility unstated.** No borrow list, no hard-to-borrow screen, no stock-loan cost for a unit short leg across up to ~5.6k names.
13. **Environment not pinned.** `pyproject.toml` lists `numpy, pandas, pyarrow, scikit-learn, matplotlib, tqdm, torch, yfinance, requests, wandb, pyyaml` with **no version pins** (`code-derived`) → a future re-download via `yfinance` need not reproduce the 2010–2024 panel.
14. **Code/prose scope tension on ETFs.** Section 4.1 says the universe retains "a small number of exchange-traded funds and other listed instruments"; the released NYSE branch filters `ETF == "N"` and the docstring says "filters to common stocks" (`source-reported` vs `code-derived`) — unresolved, recorded rather than reconciled.
15. **Cross-record contrary evidence inside this repository.** Spread/mean-reversion edges here are repeatedly shown to degrade under honest cost and feasibility treatment: `crypto-stat-arb-cointegration-fdr-tiering-drifting-hedge-turnover-2026-09-13.md`, `crypto-perpetual-pairs-trading-kalman-cointegration-falsification-2026-09-11.md`, `pairs-trading-adf-stationarity-filter-walk-forward-falsification-2026-09-12.md`, `sp500-avellaneda-lee-residual-reversion-implementability-falsification-2026-09-13.md`, and `dtw-ocp-clustering-pairs-trading-regime-falsification-2026-09-13.md` — the last one being an observation-space similarity selection rule of exactly the family FASCL benchmarks against.

## Falsification plan

Every threshold below is a **`research-defined falsification threshold`** or **`research-proposed`** operationalization invented by this Scout, not by the source.

- **F1 — Point-in-time universe and dividend repair (`research-proposed`).** Rebuild the universe from listing history as of each window start (including names that delist later) and recompute returns on a stated adjustment basis (split-only vs total-return). **Fail if** FASCL's FRC@1 margin over historical Pearson falls below **+5% relative**, or its K=1 gross spread Sharpe falls below Pearson's (`research-defined`).
- **F2 — Honest cost ladder (`research-proposed`).** Charge 0/5/10/20/30 bp per side **plus** paid half-spread and market impact at 20% ADV participation **plus** a 50 bp/year stock-loan fee on the short leg. **Fail the tradability claim if** break-even one-way cost is **< 15 bp** or net Sharpe at 10 bp one-way is **≤ 0** at any K (`research-defined`). The source's own 10 bp rung already fails this at K = 5/10/20.
- **F3 — Baselines under identical costs (`research-proposed`).** Re-run Pearson, DTW and Random through the same cost panel the source runs only FASCL through. **Fail if** FASCL's net Sharpe at 10 bp does not exceed every observation-space baseline by **≥ 0.30** (`research-defined`).
- **F4 — Calendar-aligned book (`research-proposed`).** Stitch queries into one calendar-time portfolio with an explicit capital base, netting of offsetting query positions, a position limit per name and a stated rebalance rule. **Fail if** calendar-aligned Sharpe is **< 0.7 ×** the reported window-relative Sharpe (`research-defined`), which would show the printed 6.93 is partly an averaging artifact.
- **F5 — Multi-year out-of-sample (`research-proposed`).** Rolling test years 2019–2023 (outside the 2010–2022 training window), encoder retrained per year. **Fail if** FRC@1 stays **< 0.35** or the gross Sharpe advantage over Pearson is **< +0.30** in **≥ 2 of 5** years (`research-defined`).
- **F6 — Seed variance (`research-proposed`).** 5 independent training seeds; report mean ± sd of FRC@1 and of spread Sharpe. **Fail if** the K=5 FASCL−Pearson Sharpe gap (**+0.11**, `our count`) lies within **1 sd** of zero (`research-defined`).
- **F7 — Significance on the trading metric (`research-proposed`).** Stationary/block bootstrap, 1,000 draws with 20-day blocks, on the **daily P&L difference** FASCL − Pearson. **Fail if** the 95% CI of the mean daily difference is **not strictly above 0 at every K** (`research-defined`).
- **F8 — Momentum/style ablation (`research-proposed`).** Residualise the spread against beta, size, industry and the query's own trailing momentum before applying the position rule. **Fail if** the gross Sharpe advantage over Pearson shrinks by **> 50%** (`research-defined`), which would attribute the edge to persistence rather than future-aligned retrieval.
- **F9 — Turnover/deadband ablation (`research-proposed`).** Add a deadband (position only flips when |cumulative spread| exceeds 0.5 sd of the spread distribution) and a 20-day maximum holding period. **Fail if** break-even cost does **not rise above 15 bp** or net Sharpe at 10 bp remains **≤ 0** (`research-defined`).
- **F10 — Feasibility screen (`research-proposed`).** Exclude names without borrowable inventory and cap participation at 20% ADV. **Fail if** **≥ 20%** of the query set becomes infeasible (`research-defined`), since that would make the overlay unimplementable as specified.
- **F11 — Supervision placebo (`research-proposed`).** Circularly shift the future-return-correlation supervision targets by **≥ 60 trading days** before training (so the label can no longer describe the asset's own future) and retrain. **Fail the mechanism claim if** placebo FRC@1 stays **≥ the historical-Pearson baseline of 0.3981** (`research-defined`), i.e. the advantage survives without genuine future alignment.
- **F12 — Multiplicity audit (`research-proposed`).** Apply Benjamini–Hochberg at **q < 0.10** across the full printed grid (15 methods × 4 K × 4 metrics × 4 horizons). **Downgrade confidence to `low` if** fewer than half of FASCL's "best in every cell" claims survive (`research-defined`).
- **Action on failure.** Any failure of F1–F4 or F7 leaves the artifact as research material only: no intake promotion, no production candidate entry, no implementation. Failures of F5, F6, F8 or F11 additionally require re-labelling the mechanism as momentum/style persistence rather than future-aligned retrieval.

## Crypto portability

**`adapted`** — structural portability only; **empirically unproven** (the source demonstrates US cash equities exclusively, so this is a ported hypothesis, not crypto evidence).

- **What maps directly.** The signal needs only a cross-sectional universe with daily OHLCV-like bars, a short leg, and a mean-reversion spread — all available on crypto spot and perpetual markets. Unlike accounting- or listing-based equity factors, FASCL uses no book value, no index breakpoints and no corporate-action calendar.
- **Required re-specification (`research-proposed`).** Replace the short leg with **perpetual-futures shorts** (or spot borrow where available); charge **funding** on the short leg every 8 h; define candles on each venue's own UTC boundaries and state how a 24/7 clock interacts with the "daily" return and the sign-flip cadence; raise or volatility-scale the **±50% return clip**, since legitimate >50% daily moves are common in small-cap tokens and the current clip would silently censor the very spread moves being traded; aggregate venues or fix a single venue, since retrieval assumes one coherent return panel.
- **Crypto-specific risks the source does not model at all.** Funding and carry (0 mentions in the pinned text), mark/index price divergence between the spot legs of the spread, liquidation and margin on the short leg, venue fragmentation and per-venue survivorship/listing churn (far faster than NASDAQ/NYSE), borrow availability differing sharply between majors and long-tail tokens, 24/7 sessions invalidating the "next trading day" execution convention, and index-price manipulation on thin venues.
- **Empirical status.** No crypto result exists in the source, and no crypto claim is made here: portability of the *signal construction* is argued structurally; portability of the *returns* is `unproven`.

## Limitations

- `not independently reproduced` — every performance figure is third-party, source-reported, and none was recomputed by us.
- `underspecified`: exit/stop/holding rules beyond the daily sign flip; capital base and netting; position limits; order type and fill model; latency; timezone/session convention; dividend and split-adjustment convention; retraining cadence and live label lag (the supervision needs 64 forward days); the exact date of the listing snapshot; K-sensitivity outside {1, 5, 10, 20}.
- `data gap`: affiliations; seeds/repeats/variance; significance of the Sharpe differences; drawdown, win rate and cumulative return (computed in code, not printed); net Sharpe at 1 bp and 20 bp (computed in code, not printed); borrow/locate; spread and slippage; capacity and ADV; funding/dividend handling; data vendor beyond "public sources" (vendor identified only from the released script as Yahoo Finance via `yfinance`).
- Single test year (2024) and a single market (US listed equities); the robustness section re-slices that same year only.
- The comparison against 14 baselines is only partially reproducible from the release (baseline code withheld), and the significance claim covers FRC only.
- The headline Sharpe of 6.93 is a **window-relative, equal-weight, no-capital, no-netting overlay** with a random-arm level of 3.00–4.13; it must not be read as an implementable account-level Sharpe.
- Universe conditioning risk from an unstated listing snapshot date (`research interpretation` from code).
- ETF-scope tension between Section 4.1 and the released download script (recorded, not reconciled).
- Code/prose scope note: the repository retains a Mamba block path selectable by `block_type`, but the paper reports the Transformer setting throughout (`source-reported`, README) — do not treat Mamba results as source-reported; none exist.
