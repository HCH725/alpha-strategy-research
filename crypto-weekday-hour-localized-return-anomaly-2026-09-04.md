## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The authors revisit day-of-the-week effects using hourly rather than daily data because a continuously traded 24/7 market can make a daily average look persistent even when the effect is generated by only a few intraday intervals.

The source reports that apparent weekday effects are not broad, persistent daily phenomena. Instead, statistically significant average returns are concentrated in a limited number of asset-specific hours. The effect is more visible among the actively traded cryptocurrencies and largely absent among the less-active group.

The source therefore interprets crypto calendar predictability as **localized and short-lived**, not as a stable market-wide weekday anomaly.

### Research interpretation

The falsifiable mechanism is an **aggregation-induced calendar anomaly**:

1. a small number of recurring weekday-hour windows contain localized order-flow, liquidity, participation, or information-arrival effects;
2. daily aggregation averages those windows together with many neutral hours, creating the appearance of a broad weekday premium;
3. because the windows differ by asset, the mechanism is more consistent with asset-specific microstructure or participation schedules than with a universal “Monday effect” or similar calendar factor.

This interpretation does **not** establish why any particular hour earns abnormal returns. The causal channel is **unproven**. Possible explanations such as exchange liquidity cycles, regional participation, scheduled institutional activity, funding/derivatives mechanics, or recurring information releases are hypotheses for later testing, not source-reported facts.

## Signal

The source is an empirical calendar-anomaly study rather than a fully specified executable strategy. Therefore the trading signal below is deliberately separated into source-reported observations and a `research-proposed` operationalization.

### Source-reported observations

The reviewed public sources report the following statistically significant weekday-hour concentrations in UTC:

- Bitcoin: Monday, especially 06:00 and 18:00 UTC.
- Ethereum: Wednesday, especially 01:00, 02:00, 07:00, and 20:00 UTC.
- Tether USDt: Sunday, with negative effects around 10:00 and 21:00 UTC.
- BNB: Friday, including 09:00 UTC and late-day hours 20:00–23:00 UTC.
- Cardano: Saturday 00:00 UTC.
- The journal-study summary reports mean weekday-level hourly returns of approximately 0.0166% for Bitcoin Monday, 0.0225% for Ethereum Wednesday, 0.0694% for BNB Friday, -0.0022% for Tether Sunday, and 0.0210% for Cardano Saturday.
- Public reporting of the study additionally lists representative hourly means including approximately 0.1103% for Bitcoin Monday 06:00 UTC, 0.1200% for Ethereum Wednesday 20:00 UTC, -0.0261% for Tether Sunday 10:00 UTC, 0.3717% for BNB Friday 09:00 UTC, and 0.1947% for Cardano Saturday 00:00 UTC.

The source uses hourly log returns and Newey-West HAC standard errors with 24 lags; it also reports robustness work including year fixed effects. The exact full coefficient table, multiple-testing treatment across all 168 weekday-hour cells per asset, and every sample start/end timestamp are **underspecified** in the reviewed public-access material.

### Research-proposed operationalization

For testability only, not as source-reported trading advice:

- Formation timestamp: one second before the relevant UTC hour begins, using only information available at that time.
- Entry: at the first executable quote after the target UTC hour opens.
- Direction:
  - long for positive source-reported weekday-hour windows;
  - short only where the source-reported hourly effect is negative and shorting is feasible.
- Holding period: exactly one hour, exiting at the first executable quote after the next UTC hour boundary.
- Re-entry: once per qualifying weekday-hour; no overlapping position in the same asset.
- Position sizing: equal risk contribution across simultaneously active signals, using ex-ante volatility estimated only from trailing data.
- No stop-loss, take-profit, or additional indicator filter in the baseline test.
- Any liquidity, volatility, momentum, funding, or regime filter added later must be labeled `research-proposed` and tested as an ablation rather than assumed to improve the source effect.

The one-hour entry/exit rule, position sizing, and portfolio construction are all **research-proposed** because the source does not publish an executable strategy specification.

## Execution assumptions

### Source-reported

The source evaluates return anomalies statistically. It does not establish a complete executable fill model, order type, latency model, participation cap, short-borrow convention, or net-of-cost trading strategy in the public material reviewed here.

### Research-proposed

For a conservative test:

- Order type: taker marketable order at the hour boundary baseline; passive-limit sensitivity test separately.
- Signal-to-order delay: 1 second, 5 seconds, 30 seconds, and 60 seconds.
- Fill model: executable bid/ask, not mid-price.
- Participation cap: maximum 1% of trailing one-hour venue volume.
- Shorting: only via instruments where borrow or perpetual shorting is operationally available.
- Funding: include realized perpetual funding if perps are tested.
- Fees/spread/slippage: subtract venue- and timestamp-appropriate costs.
- Failure handling: if the target hour lacks clean quotes or the instrument is unavailable, skip the trade; do not impute.
- No leverage in the baseline research test.

All execution rules above are `research-proposed`.

## Evidence

### Source-reported

The source studies 12 cryptocurrencies with hourly data spanning 2013–2025, with starting dates varying by asset.

Reported broad weekday effects among the actively traded group are asset-specific:

- Bitcoin: positive Monday effect.
- Ethereum: positive Wednesday effect.
- BNB: positive Friday effect.
- Tether USDt: negative Sunday effect.

Among the less-active group, Cardano is reported as the only asset with a statistically significant weekday-level effect, on Saturday.

However, the authors report that a joint F-test does not support a broad market-wide weekday pattern. Their intraday decomposition shows that the daily effects are driven by only a small number of hours rather than by all or most hours of the day.

The source therefore supplies **negative evidence against naive daily calendar strategies** while simultaneously identifying narrower weekday-hour hypotheses that can be tested prospectively.

### Negative evidence

- The source's own central result is that the apparent day-of-week effects are not persistent across the full day.
- Joint tests fail to support a broad, universal weekday effect across the crypto market.
- Effects are localized and asset-specific rather than common across the 12-asset universe.
- Less-active cryptocurrencies show little evidence of weekday effects.
- The public source material reviewed here does not establish net profitability after spreads, fees, slippage, latency, short financing, or multiple-testing correction across all weekday-hour cells.
- The narrow set of statistically significant windows creates a substantial data-mining risk: with 168 possible weekday-hour cells per asset, false discoveries are plausible unless selection and inference are leakage-controlled.
- The exact cause of the localized windows is unproven.

## Falsification

All thresholds in this section are **research-defined falsification thresholds** unless explicitly described as source-reported.

1. **Prospective walk-forward replication**
   - Data: exchange-native hourly or finer BTC/ETH/BNB/ADA data after the source sample.
   - Rule: estimate qualifying weekday-hours on a trailing training sample, freeze them, then trade only the subsequent test block.
   - Failure threshold: reject a specific weekday-hour signal if its net mean return is non-positive over at least 52 independent occurrences or if its 95% bootstrap confidence interval includes a net mean return below -2 bps.

2. **Multiple-testing control**
   - Data: all 168 weekday-hour cells per asset.
   - Metric: Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery-rate adjusted p-values.
   - Failure threshold: downgrade the anomaly family if none of the source-reported windows survive FDR at q = 0.05 in an independent sample.

3. **UTC-phase placebo**
   - Shift each source-reported target hour by ±1, ±2, and ±3 hours.
   - Failure threshold: if neighboring placebo hours perform similarly or better after costs, reject the claim that the edge is localized to the reported clock hour.

4. **Day-label placebo**
   - Keep the hour fixed but permute weekday labels within calendar weeks.
   - Failure threshold: if the observed coefficient lies inside the central 95% of the permutation distribution, reject that weekday conditioning adds information.

5. **Cost and latency stress**
   - Baseline: executable bid/ask plus fees.
   - Stress: 5, 10, 20, and 30 bps round-trip all-in costs; delays of 1, 5, 30, and 60 seconds.
   - Failure threshold: reject tradability if the net effect is non-positive at a 10 bps round-trip assumption or after a 30-second delay.

6. **Venue replication**
   - Test Binance, Coinbase, OKX, Kraken, and another liquid venue where historical data are available.
   - Failure threshold: if the effect appears only on one venue and disappears on at least two other liquid venues, classify it as venue-specific rather than portable crypto alpha.

7. **Regime stability**
   - Split by bull/bear trend, realized-volatility tercile, and pre/post major market-structure changes.
   - Failure threshold: if sign reverses in two or more major regimes, downgrade from seasonal alpha to regime-dependent anomaly.

8. **Liquidity and activity explanation**
   - Regress target-hour returns on contemporaneous and lagged volume, spread, realized volatility, funding, and major scheduled macro-event dummies.
   - Failure threshold: if the weekday-hour coefficient becomes economically negligible (< 1 bp before costs) after controls, prefer the competing microstructure/event explanation.

9. **Asset-level replication**
   - Apply the same methodology to assets not in the original 12-coin sample.
   - Failure threshold: if no new liquid asset shows an FDR-surviving localized weekday-hour effect, treat portability beyond the original universe as unproven.

## Crypto portability

**direct**, because the primary study is itself on cryptocurrency markets.

Portability remains conditional:

- 24/7 trading makes UTC anchoring essential; local exchange/session clocks must not be substituted silently.
- Spot, perpetual, and futures markets can have different clock-time mechanics because of funding settlement, institutional participation, and derivatives hedging.
- Cross-venue fragmentation may shift or erase a weekday-hour pattern.
- Stablecoins such as USDT and USDC have different return and microstructure properties from directional crypto assets; results should not be pooled mechanically.
- Listing ages vary materially, so survivorship and sample-length differences matter.
- Funding and borrow costs can dominate a very small one-hour anomaly.
- Exchange outages, maintenance, quote quality, and historical fee tiers must be modeled.
- Any claim that the same hour remains profitable in 2026+ is **unproven** until tested prospectively.

## Limitations

- The source establishes statistical return patterns, not a fully specified executable strategy.
- Exact per-asset sample start/end timestamps are **underspecified** in the public material reviewed here.
- Exact all-cell coefficient tables and the source's full multiple-testing treatment are **data gap / underspecified** from the accessible materials reviewed in this Scout run.
- Causal mechanism is **unproven**.
- Historical Investing.com aggregates may not reproduce exchange-native executable prices.
- One-hour anomalies may be too small to survive spread, fees, slippage, and latency.
- Calendar effects are particularly exposed to data mining, non-stationarity, and publication bias.
- The observed active-versus-less-active difference may reflect data quality, trading intensity, or sample length rather than a stable economic mechanism.
- No independent reproduction has been performed.
