## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

Forecast-deviation trend acceptance with zero-latch reversal discipline (bilingual mirror prose plus pinned Pine, educate-only warning fenced above): each bar's close is compared against its own n-period linear regression forecast `xLG` — the least-squares end-point value over the trailing window — and the percentage deviation `xCFO` prints positive when the market stands above its fitted trend (under-estimated by the forecast, momentum acceptance) and negative when it stands below (over-estimated, weakness). Latching the deviation against zero means a leg is entered only when price escapes to its side of its own regression line, and held while the deviation merely touches zero — the opposite-side escape is the exit. There is no stop, no target, no flat state by design after the first signal — the opposite escape is the exit. The design bets that a close persistently above (below) its own short-horizon linear fit identifies an accepted trend better than any fixed-average cross, and that the highest-expectancy posture is to always hold the side of the last escape.

### Research interpretation

Single-series oscillator-latch system, two-sided, always-in-position after the first signal, with a sticky hold on exact-zero prints. Unlike band systems that need a tolerance zone (PFE ±50, Keltner rails, STARC bands), this rule has one moving part — the forecast deviation itself — and the zero line converts it into a three-state latch (long / hold / short) where the hold state inherits the last escape. Unlike cross systems that trade the moment one line passes another, there is no second series at all: the regression line is consumed inside the deviation, so the trigger is a level (zero), not a cross. Unlike the sibling Chande records (CMO band-fade, Kroll ATR rails), nothing here measures move-asymmetry or volatility range — the only question asked is whether price sits above or below its own least-squares fit. The 14-bar fit makes the reference adaptive but slow: entries arrive after the deviation has confirmed, exits arrive after it has decayed through zero. The price of the always-in posture is indefinite adverse rides: a leg whose opposite escape never prints is held forever. The bet is on regression-deviation persistence, not on any momentum extreme, volume print, second series, or calendar effect.

## Signal

Exact rule as pinned (all inputs frozen — any other value is a different, unpinned rule):

- Declarations (derived): single-position state machine over `BTCUSDT 1d` with same-bar-close execution and house sizing/costs (see Execution assumptions). At most one open leg; state ∈ {flat, long, short}; flat exists only before the first signal and under the warmup rule.

Only `close` is read anywhere.
- State update (source-native, evaluated once per completed bar): `pos(t) = 1` iff `xCFO(t) > 0`; `−1` iff `xCFO(t) < 0`; else `pos(t−1)` (sticky hold on exact-zero prints; both triggers are strictly mutually exclusive — no bar can fire both, no priority rule needed).
- Entry long: on `pos` transitioning to `1` (from flat: open long; from short: atomic reversal — close short plus open long as a single reversal event, matching Pine's opposite-side `strategy.entry` reversal). Refires of the `pos == 1` state while already long are no-ops (Pine-default no-pyramiding). Entry short: mirror on transition to `−1`.
- Exits: the opposite transition only (long exits exactly when `pos` flips to `−1` and vice versa). No stop, no target, no trailing, no time exit, no cooldown — pinned as written (the script ships zero `strategy.exit`/`strategy.close` legs and names no stop anywhere; the mirror's Risk/Optimization prose proposes adding stops only as future work, which confirms none exist now), explicitly none, not invented. The block contains no `close_all` anywhere, so there is no flat path after the first signal at all — `possig == 0` is reachable only on the seed bar(s) before any signal, where the book is already flat.
- Direction: two-sided with an explicit pre-first-signal flat state (plus the 30-bar warmup flat by record rule). `reverse = false` preserves both sides; a long-only or short-only reading would contradict the prescribed sell leg, and enabling `reverse` would invert both sides — each is a different, unpinned rule.
