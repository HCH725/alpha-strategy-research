## Provenance

Primary sources read end to end during this cycle (2026-10-17), pinned:

- Paper (live-fetch arXiv PDF this cycle, 22 pages / 47,294 chars extracted, 244,416 bytes, sha256 `1f9c21afe32dcb3ee77b31548acdaea00451fbfa1c0ee10c907867bcc736fce9`, byte-identical to the three prior cycles' fetch): `Zura Kakushadze`, `101 Formulaic Alphas`, arXiv `1601.00991` (manuscript dated `December 9, 2015` — record `source_as_of` is that manuscript date, never a fetch date; the paper states the formulas `are also computer code` once the operators are defined). Alpha#28 prints in Appendix A as `scale(((correlation(adv20, low, 5) + ((high + low) / 2)) - close))`. Operator pins from the paper's own definitions section (A.1): `scale(x, a) = rescaled x such that sum(abs(x)) = a (the default is a = 1)`; `correlation(x, y, d) = time-serial correlation of x and y for the past d days`; `delay(x, d) = value of x d days ago`; `delta(x, d) = today's value of x minus the value of x d days ago`; `ts_{O}(x, d) = operator O applied across the time-series for the past d days; non-integer number of days d is converted to floor(d)` (all Alpha#28 windows are integers, so no flooring is exercised); inputs (A.2) `open, close, high, low, volume = standard definitions for daily price and volume data`, `adv{d} = average daily dollar volume for the past d days` (dollar definition fenced in Negative evidence 5). Timing pin from §2: `delay-1 means that the alpha is traded on the day subsequent to the date of the most recent data used in computing it` (e.g. traded today `starting at the open`); footnote 11 names exactly four delay-0 alphas (`42, 48, 53 and 54`, traded at or as close as possible to the close of the computed day) — Alpha#28 is not among them, so it is delay-1 by the paper's own exclusion. Direction pin from the §2 worked example: `Alpha#101 ... is a delay-1 momentum alpha: if the stock runs up intraday (i.e., close > open and high > low), the next day one takes a long position` — a positive score maps to long, a negative score to short; the paper's own taxonomy adds that `a mean-reversion alpha has a sign opposite to the return on which it is based`, which is exactly this rule's typical-price leg (see Signal). The scale-to-sign reduction is exact for direction (see Derived-record declaration): `scale` divides every asset's composite by one shared strictly-positive divisor `sum(abs(x))`, so `sign(scaled_i) = sign(composite_i)` for every asset with a nonzero composite — the side map used here is lossless with respect to the paper's direction convention, and only cross-sectional magnitude (unused by this record's single-unit book) is dropped.

## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

Coarse mean-reversion/momentum building blocks. The paper frames its universe coarsely: `one can think of alpha signals as based on mean-reversion or momentum`, where `a mean-reversion alpha has a sign opposite to the return on which it is based`. Alpha#28 is a mean-reversion block with a volume-trend tilt: the `((high + low) / 2) - close` leg scores the opposite of the close's position within the day's range (positive when the close prints in the lower half — a faded close), while the `correlation(adv20, low, 5)` leg adds the trailing 5-bar co-movement of smoothed activity with lows (positive when rising activity accompanies rising lows). The paper ships no behavioural, structural, or risk-premium mechanism beyond this measurement construction, and no stop, target, or risk leg of any kind.

### Research interpretation

Typical-price reversal with a ±1 adv–low correlation tilt, two-sided, threshold-free, continuous-score. On most bars the composite's sign follows the typical-price leg: a close in the upper half of the range scores negative (short the extended close) and a close in the lower half scores positive (long the soft close) — textbook intraday mean-reversion in the paper's own taxonomy. The correlation tilt (bounded in `[-1, 1]`) can only flip bars where the close prints within 1 USDT of the typical price — on the BTCUSDT scale that is rare, so the tilt is usually a nudge, never the driver; this is disclosed, not upgraded (see Limitations, Falsification F1). Unlike sibling worldquant-alpha6 (a negated raw 10-bar open-vs-volume level correlation with no average, no additive price leg, and no scale) this rule correlates a 20-bar volume average against lows over 5 bars and adds a range-position leg inside a scale. Unlike sibling worldquant-alpha7 (an `adv20 < volume` gate selecting between a 60-bar time-rank contrarian leg and a `-1` short default) this rule has no gate, no branch, and no fixed leg — `adv20` enters only inside the correlation. Unlike sibling worldquant-alpha12 (a 1-bar `sign(volume-change) × (−price-change)` product with no window and no average) this rule's memory is 24 bars deep and its volume enters only as a 20-bar smoother. Unlike sibling worldquant-alpha26 (a negated trailing-maximum envelope of rank-trajectory correlations — an agreement object) this rule adds its correlation to a price level and scales once, with no rank transform and no envelope. Unlike pool Bollinger/band records (mean-plus/minus-dispersion envelopes on closes) there is no mean, no dispersion, and no band — the reversal here is measured against the bar's own typical price, never a moving average. The failure mode is plain: a trending close that keeps printing in the same range half pins the book against the trend with no scaling and no guardrail, while the tilt that was meant to condition on activity is denominated too small to matter on this venue — disclosed, not upgraded.

## Signal

Exact rule as pinned (any other window, series, transform, scale treatment, fill, frame, book, or tie treatment is a different, unpinned rule):

- Inputs (paper A.2): `high[t]`, `low[t]`, `close[t]`, `volume[t]` on completed `1d` bars — the klines print as-is (base-asset volume, no quote conversion, no normalization). No `open`, no vwap/adv-external/returns/cap/sector, no cross-sectional rank, no second asset or timeframe is read by any order-gating line (`adv20` is derived from the same `volume` series, never fetched).
- Activity smoother (pinned impl lines 100–102, 235–236): `adv20[t] = mean(volume[t−19 .. t])` — trailing 20-bar simple average of the kline volume series. Undefined before 20 consecutive finite bars exist, by the `_rolling` all-finite-block convention. Paper-dollar definition fenced in Negative evidence 5; this record implements the pinned implementation literally.
- Tilt (paper A.1, pinned impl lines 120–140): `tilt[t] = correlation(adv20, low, 5)[t]` (trailing 5-bar Pearson correlation of the smoother against lows; all-finite 5-block required, else NaN; a defined all-constant window prints `0.0` per the pinned WorldQuant convention, fenced in Negative evidence 6). Undefined before the 24th completed bar (first bar with 5 defined smoother prints: bars 20–24).
- Composite (paper Appendix A, pinned impl lines 353–354): `raw[t] = tilt[t] + (high[t] + low[t]) / 2 − close[t]` — the unitless `[-1, 1]` tilt added to the USDT-denominated typical-minus-close exactly as printed (no rescaling invented; the paper's own `scale` erases units cross-sectionally). Undefined (NaN) while any required window is incomplete — flat by the warmup rule, never traded.
- Direction mapping (source-grounded, fenced in Negative evidence 1): `raw[t] > 0` → long signal; `raw[t] < 0` → short signal; `raw[t] == 0` → flat signal (exact-zero prints, e.g. a close printed exactly at typical with a `0.0` tilt — deterministic flat, never a held conviction; the pinned single-asset `scale` prints NaN on an exact-zero composite, which is likewise flat). The entry fills at the next bar's open, one single unit, only if the book does not already carry that side (see position rule).
- Exit: the first opposite signal reverses the full unit in one step (close plus opposite entry at the same next-bar-open fill); a zero print flattens the full unit at the next open — reversal-or-flatten is the sole exit path by construction. Consecutive same-side bars extend one continuous hold, never re-enter. No stop-loss, no take-profit, no trailing, no time exit (the paper ships no risk leg — carried over exactly, disclosed in Provenance adaptation (5), never upgraded into a source claim).
- Direction: two-sided (both signs are routinely attainable — the typical-price leg takes both signs whenever the close prints on either side of the bar's mid-range; verified structurally in-cycle). Shorts are native to USDT-M margin (no uptick rule, no borrow desk).
- Position rule (predeclared researcher pin): single-unit; an already-long book treats further long-signal bars as no-ops (no pyramiding, no averaging), and symmetrically for shorts; flat-regime bars while already flat are no-ops. Re-entry re-arms on the next opposite or flat-exit signal only (no cooldown specified — explicitly none, not invented). Pre-first-signal and any NaN-score bars are flat by record rule.

## Limitations

- No price stop, no time stop: a close that keeps printing in the same range half pins the book against the trend with no scaling and no guardrail before the range position flips — the record's principal risk.
