## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The paper's own account, in its own terms:

- **Who trades and why.** 'Recurring payment obligations such as pensions, dividends, and salaries are excessively concentrated at the turn of the month', so 'institutions and other investors with month-end obligations sell securities prior to month-end for settled cash, generating temporary price pressures' (§1, citing Etula, Rinne, Suominen and Vaittinen 2020, *Dash for cash*, RFS 33:75–111).
- **What they sell.** Investors 'sell most heavily the stocks that are trading far below their 52-week highs'. The paper names these **'dispensable stocks'** because they are 'salient underperformers, commonly offer tax-loss benefits when sold, and they pay little in dividends' (§1, citing the companion working paper Nathan, Suominen and Tasa 2026, *The intramonth momentum cycle*, reference-listed as 'Working paper' with **no identifier → `data gap`**). Distance below the 52-week high is explicitly treated as 'a proxy for that trait rather than the trait itself' (§3.1).
- **How it reaches factor returns.** A factor's *dispensability exposure* `df` = the intended-signed, value-weighted difference in the two legs' average dispensability scores (equation 7). A **positive** `df` means the factor is *short* more-dispensable stocks, so month-end selling *raises* its return; a **negative** `df` means it is long them, so the same selling *lowers* it (§3.1). The paper's predicted signature is a positive slope in PreTOM, approximately zero in Rest, and a negative slope in Post (§3.2), i.e. partly **temporary** pressure that partially reverses — the pattern it reports (Table 4).
- **Why it is not competed away.** Framed through Grossman and Miller (1988) and Duffie (2010): liquidity suppliers need a concession to carry inventory, and capital moves slowly; Vayanos and Woolley (2013) supply the uncertainty-of-magnitude channel. The source's own summary: month-end liquidity demand is **'predictable in timing but uncertain in magnitude'** (§4.1).
- **The window is not picked from factor returns.** PreTOM `[τ−9, τ−4]` is 'pinned down by the aggregate market return path, the only consecutive run of trading-day positions with negative average market excess returns' (§2.2, building on Etula et al.'s τ−8…τ−4; in this sample the run starts one day earlier at τ−9), averaging **−4.1 bps/day** of FF market excess return inside the window against **+5.7** outside (Table 1).
- **Causal handle.** The 28 May 2024 T+2→T+1 settlement change moved the binding deadline one trading day earlier, so the selling (and the price pressure) should move from τ−4 to τ−3 — which is what the difference-in-differences in §5/Table 9 finds.
- **Scope claim.** The mechanism 'organizes 134 of the 152 factors', explains 72% of the PreTOM cross-section against 1.0% outside it, accounts for 83% of the mean of the zoo's first principal component, 39% of the value–momentum correlation and 22% of realised factor-momentum return (§8). The paper is explicit that this 'does not collapse the factor zoo into a single factor'.

### Research interpretation

- **Hypothesised mechanism (falsifiable form):** a **recurring, settlement-timed liquidity-supply premium**. Six times a year-ish month, a predictable-flow imbalance hits a specific, identifiable subset of stocks (far below their 52-week high); whoever is on the other side of that flow is paid a concession that is positive in expectation inside the window, partially reverses around month-end, and is near zero outside it. The cross-section of *which* factor wins is mechanical given holdings, not forecast: **the sign of the payment is determined by the sign of `df`**.
- **Component roles (normalised, hybrid structure):**
  - **Calendar regime:** PreTOM `[τ−9, τ−4]` relative to the last trading day of month `m` (source-reported, ex-ante observable).
  - **Cross-sectional signal:** factor/stock dispensability exposure `df` / `δ` from month-end price vs trailing 252-day maximum (source-reported).
  - **Monthly state variable:** `LDS_preTOM_m`, the least-minus-most-dispensable decile return spread over the window — an **ex-post** measure of the realised concession, useful for conditioning but not tradable in advance (source §4.1: 'predictable in timing but not in magnitude').
  - **Risk / exit / sizing:** **absent from the source.** No stop, no volatility target, no position size, no exit rule is given for any calendar-timed implementation → **`underspecified`**; any such rule introduced below is `research-proposed`.
- **What the mechanism does *not* claim:** it is not a directional forecast for the whole factor zoo (40 of 152 factors load with the *wrong* sign for PreTOM and *lose* there), it is not a claim that anomaly premia are artefacts (§3: the concentration *increases* from 45% to 49% as premia decay), and it is not a claim of a tradeable magnitude (§4.1).
- **Nearest tradable hypothesis (this is our construction, not the source's):** hold, only during `[τ−9, τ−4]`, the factor group with significantly positive `df` (the 94 'short-dispensable' factors) and be flat or on the other side otherwise. The source neither proposes, sizes, nor backtests this rule, and explicitly states in §7.3 that its own `df`-sorted row **'is not an investable signal'** — an ex-post benchmark only. Everything about entry, exit, sizing, costs and thresholds for that rule is `research-proposed` and must be falsified before it is believed.

## Signal

Everything in this section is either quoted-normalised from the pinned PDF (marked source-reported) or explicitly labelled `research-proposed`.

**Formation timestamp and tradability (source-reported).**
- Characteristic values are observed **at each month-end** `m` from the JKP library and sorted into deciles 'using the previous month-end value' and then 'held … fixed through the month' (§2.1).
- The dispensability score `δ_{i,m}` is computed at the month-end close of month `m` and, in the paper's own words, 'we observe `δ_{i,m}` at the end of month `m` and use it to explain returns in month `m+1`' (§3.1, equation 6) → lag-one-month, no look-ahead in the paper's design.
- The window itself is a deterministic function of the calendar: `τ` = last trading day of month `m`; PreTOM = the six trading days `[τ−9, τ−4]`; **the window is knowable ex ante** (§2.2).
- Timezone, exchange calendar source and the exact close used (regular-session close vs consolidated close) are **not stated → `data gap`**.

**Lookback (source-reported).**
- Dispensability: `max_{s∈T_m} P_{i,s}` over the trailing **252-trading-day** window `T_m`, then proportional distance from that maximum, standardised cross-sectionally within month `m` (equation 6).
- Factor characteristics: each JKP characteristic's own lookback (e.g. momentum `ret_12_1` = 12-minus-1 months), taken from the JKP Factor Details file; **per-characteristic windows are not restated in this paper → `data gap`, refer to the JKP source**.
- Warm-up, inclusive/exclusive endpoint conventions and tie handling are **not stated → `data gap`**.

**Entry / exit / direction (source-reported for the paper's own portfolios, absent for any calendar-timed rule).**
- The paper's portfolios: monthly decile sorts, value-weighted by prior-day market cap, **held fixed through the month**, long D10 / short D1 with the orientation `s_f` from JKP (equation 1). It never exits intra-month and never trades the window separately.
- **No entry price, no exit price, no order type, no next-bar/same-bar convention, no holding-period cap and no re-entry rule is given for a PreTOM-only trade → the implementable signal is `underspecified` at exactly the point a trader would need it.**

**Parameters that ARE specified (source-reported).** `PreTOM = [τ−9, τ−4]` (6 trading days); `Post = [τ−3, τ+3]` (7 trading days, overlapping Rest by default); `Rest` = all days outside PreTOM (§2.2) **but** Rest excludes Post in Table 12 (see contradiction); `LDS` deciles formed at the prior month-end from the raw price-to-high ratio with NYSE breakpoints, `Disp = D1`, `NonDisp = D10` (§4.1 footnote 19); taxonomy cut at `|t(df)| > 1.96` giving **94 / 40 / 18** groups summing to 152 (Table 6); the 52-week-high factor is excluded wherever `df` is a regressor (152 tested); Table 2 uses the balanced 141; bootstrap inference `B = 5,000` month-block draws with both returns and exposures recomputed inside each draw (Table 3/4/5 notes).

**`research-proposed` operationalisation (for falsification only — not in the source).** A minimal calendar-gated rule that makes the hypothesis testable:
1. At the close of month `m`, compute `df` for every factor from its published JKP holdings/characteristics (no return information), and classify into `df>0` (94), `df<0` (40), `|t|≤1.96` (18).
2. **Enter** the `df>0` long–short decile portfolios at the **close of the trading day `τ−9`** and **exit at the close of `τ−4`** each month; hold cash the rest of the month. Mirror image for the `df<0` group.
3. `research-proposed` sizing: equal risk per factor, capped at 1% of capital per factor leg, gross exposure ≤ 100%.
4. `research-proposed` execution: next-day market-on-open after the signal day, with a cost ladder applied in every test (F2 below).
5. All of steps 1–5 are **`research-proposed`**: the source specifies no entry, exit, sizing, execution or cost rule whatsoever for a calendar-timed trade.

## Required data

## Execution assumptions

- **From the source:** nothing. No order type, no fill model, no signal-to-order delay, no next-bar/same-bar convention, no latency, no participation cap, no position limits, no leverage, no margin, no borrow model, no fee schedule, no spread or slippage model, no impact model, no capacity number, no turnover statistic (term-scan table in Provenance). - **What implementers must add (`research-proposed`, all of it):** signal-to-order lag (assumed next-session open in the operationalisation above), market orders, an explicit commission + half-spread model, a borrow/locate screen and fee for the short leg, a participation/ADV cap, and a capacity ladder. - **Structural execution caveat:** the portfolios are monthly-formed and the *window* is only 6 of ~21 trading days, so any calendar-gated implementation roughly triples monthly trading frequency relative to a buy-and-hold factor position (research-computed observation: entering and exiting a monthly portfolio once extra per month) — turnover is consequently **higher than the source's design implies**, and the source prints no turnover at all.

## Crypto portability

**`adapted` — a ported hypothesis with a heavily modified clock; crypto performance is `unproven`, and the source contains zero crypto evidence.**

- **Settlement dependency (the core):** the mechanism is *US equity settlement mechanics* — T+3 → T+2 (2017) → T+1 (2024) — and month-end *settled cash* obligations for institutional books. Crypto spot and perpetuals settle essentially instantly; there is **no T+N cash-raising clock**, so the exact PreTOM `[τ−9, τ−4]` geometry has no structural analogue and must not be copied across.
- **What might port:** crypto has its own month-end calendar effects — fund NAV dealing dates, quarterly/annual redemptions, CME/derivative expiry clusters, index inclusion flows, and staking-unbonding cycles. A ported test would have to *re-derive* the window from crypto-native flows rather than assume month-end (a `research-proposed` requirement, not a source claim).
- **The signal itself does port mechanically:** distance below the trailing 252-day high is computable on any 24/7 series, and a 'dispensable/crowded-out asset' cross-section exists among alts. But its *economic meaning* (tax-loss selling, dividend-poor underperformers, institutional cash raising) is equity-specific; in crypto the nearest analogue is forced deleveraging and redemption-driven alt selling, which is a **different mechanism** covered elsewhere in this repository (liquidation/flow records).
- **Session structure:** 24/7 candles destroy `τ` as a calendar anchor; any window must be defined on an exchange-local month-end or on funding/expiry timestamps, and candle-boundary/timezone choices become first-order (`data gap` in the source).
- **Costs and shorting:** perp funding, maker/taker schedules, borrow/locate on short legs and liquidation/ADL risk have no counterpart in the source's (empty) cost model; a crypto port would start from a *stricter* cost regime, not a looser one.
- **Survivorship / listing churn:** crypto listing and delisting churn is far faster than equity delisting, and the source's point-in-time universe rules are already unstated — the port multiplies that `data gap`.
- **Label:** **`adapted`** — mechanism borrowed, clock re-derivation required, and **not** crypto empirical evidence. Any crypto implementation must re-run F1–F12 on crypto-native data before it is discussed as a candidate.
