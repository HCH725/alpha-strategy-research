## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The authors' stated rationale (§1, §3.3, §5): news sentiment captures "prevailing sentiment and market mood" that numerical time-series features miss, and this complementarity is largest when price dynamics are driven by supply/demand shocks rather than by their own history. They argue (i) a finetuned lightweight LLM (SFT'd Qwen3 8B) reads English **and** Chinese headlines better than FinBERT, (ii) not all news is equally informative — headlines reporting **events that already occurred** carry information while **forward-looking** statements (forecasts, guidance) are already priced, consistent with the Efficient Market Hypothesis they cite (Fama 1970; Tetlock 2007), and (iii) Reuters coverage outperforms Dow Jones and China News Service on identical topics because of information quality, not only topic mix. They report that sentiment adds the most value in high-volatility regimes, is sufficient on its own in medium regimes, and is indistinguishable from tabular-only models in low regimes.

### Research interpretation

Hypothesized mechanism, stated falsifiably: **event-driven information diffusion in a thinly covered commodity**. New factual disclosures (production outages, inventory prints, supply disruptions) are digested slowly by a market whose marginal participant trades on price history, so the sign of the month's aggregated headline sentiment predicts the next month's price drift; forecast-type text carries no such drift because it is already in the price. The volatility-regime claim implies a **conditional** rather than unconditional alpha: sentiment should add nothing in calm regimes and should dominate in stressed regimes, i.e. the effect is a stress-period liquidity-provision/attention channel, not a permanent risk premium.

Component roles (hybrid structure as described by the source):

```text
Regime:        ex-post 3-bin volatility conditioning (6-month rolling sd of monthly
               aluminum returns × √12; thresholds 8.40% / 19.30%)  [reporting lens only]
Primary signal: sign of the monthly mean headline sentiment score, Ss_t ∈ {−1,0,+1}
Alternative:    sign of (predicted price t+1 − actual price t) from a walk-forward
               LSTM-family forecaster on tabular + sentiment features, Sn_t ∈ {−1,0,+1}
Filter (source-reported): topic (12 zero-shot LLM labels) and event type
               (forward-looking vs occurred) used to restrict which headlines enter Sent_t
Risk / exit:   none specified by the source — position is re-derived each month
```

The regime bin is a *reporting* partition in the source (results are reported per bin), not a tradable filter; turning it into an entry filter would be `research-proposed`. No component is assumed to contribute alpha: the topic/event filter in particular was selected by search (see Negative evidence) and requires ablation before any weight is placed on it.

## Signal

All rules below are source-reported unless explicitly labelled.

- **Formation timestamp:** monthly. `Sent_t` = the average sentiment score of **all headlines published during period t** (§3.3). Each headline is labelled positive = +1, negative = −1, neutral = 0 by the finetuned Qwen3 8B (same prompt as the finetuning stage) or by FinBERT for the comparison arm; `Sent_t` is the count-weighted mean of those labels across the month's headlines (worked example in §3.2: 6 positive, 2 neutral, 2 negative → (6·1 + 2·0 + (−1)·2)/10 = 0.4).
- **Tradable time / alignment:** **`underspecified` in source.** §3.3 only says the generated signals "are multiplied by the aluminum return realized for each period". The **price-based** arm is clearly next-month in the worked examples (Feb-2020 features → March-2020 trade), but the paper never states explicitly whether the month-t sentiment signal is applied to month-t or month-t+1 return. If it is month t, the strategy uses the month's full headline set before the month's return is realized — a look-ahead that must be tested, not assumed either way.
- **Lookback:** `Sent_t` uses only month t headlines (no trailing window). Volatility binning uses a **6-month rolling** standard deviation of monthly aluminum returns, annualized by ×√12 (this is the only place √12 appears).
- **Long entry:** `Ss_t = +1` if `Sent_t > 0`. **Short entry:** `Ss_t = −1` if `Sent_t < 0`. **Flat:** `Ss_t = 0` if `Sent_t = 0` exactly (Eq. 1). Price-based arm: `Sn_t = +1` if `P^{pred}_{t+1} > P^{true}_t`, `−1` if `<`, `0` otherwise (Eq. 2).
- **P&L rule:** the signal in {−1,0,+1} is **multiplied by the realized aluminum return of that period** (§3.3) — i.e. unit notional, unlevered, symmetric long/short, no sizing rule, no stop, no take-profit.
- **Exit / holding period / re-entry:** no explicit exit exists; each month a fresh signal is formed, so holding period is one month by construction and overlapping positions cannot occur. Precedence rules for simultaneous signals: not applicable (single signal), `not stated in source` for anything finer.
- **Parameters:** sentiment threshold = sign at 0 (fixed, not tuned); rolling window 6 months (fixed); volatility cut-points **8.40%** and **19.30%**, derived by the authors as the 20% and 50% points of the *observed full-sample* annualized-volatility range 1.13%–37.47% — i.e. **defined ex-post on the whole sample (in-sample partitioning)**; resulting bins 66 low / 106 medium / 28 high months (200 classified months after warm-up).
- **Forecast arm hyperparameters (source-reported, Appendix B):** 5 architectures (LSTM, BiLSTM, GRU, ConvLSTM, TFT) × 4 feature sets (no sentiment, Reuters, Dow Jones, China News Service) × 4 rolling input windows (1, 3, 6, 12 months) = 80 configurations; grid over hidden size {16, 32, 64, 128, 256} and layers 1–6 with dropout fixed at 0.1 → 2,400 combinations searched, "only the best result for every hidden size and number of layers combination is collected, leaving 80 best results"; MSE loss, Adam, gradient clipping, learning-rate scheduling; **walk-forward validation** (train on a rolling past window, predict the next step, slide forward); "the best-performing model for each window size is retrained on the full dataset".
- **Topic/event filter (source-reported):** Reuters headlines are zero-shot classified by **Qwen3 8B base** into 12 topics (Price Movement, Environmental, Market Analysis, Production Output, Macroeconomic, Inventory Stocks, Demand Outlook, Supply Disruption, Company News, Trade Policy, Geopolitical, Other) and split into forward-looking vs occurred. A per-topic portfolio keeps only that month's headlines of the chosen topic when forming `Sent_t`; months with no headline of that topic are excluded from that topic's portfolio (so topic portfolios have differing sample sizes). The "Best Combo" arm is the best of **all 4,094 subsets of 2..11 topics**, chosen on the same sample used to report its Sharpe.
- **Reconstruction status:** the sentiment-only arm (sign of monthly mean tri-source/Reuters headline sentiment, monthly rebalance, unit notional) is **reconstructable in logic but not in data**: the Factiva headline extract, the aluminum price series' venue, and the trading scripts are not public at the pinned commit. Timing alignment of `Sent_t` to the traded return is `underspecified`. Signal is therefore **partially underspecified**, not fully reproducible.

### Negative evidence

Recorded from the same pinned v2 unless noted.

1. **The effect is regime-conditional and mostly absent.** In the low-volatility bin (66 months, the largest regime) **all strategies land in 0.29–0.54** — i.e. sentiment adds no distinguishable value for the majority of the sample; the headline 1.04 rests on **28 high-volatility months**.
2. **The regime partition is ex-post.** Bin cut-points are computed from the *observed full-sample* volatility range (20%/50% of 1.13%–37.47%), so "high volatility ⇒ sentiment works" is conditioned on information only available after the sample ends; the bin boundaries are not a pre-registered, point-in-time filter.
3. **Forward-looking text is a null.** Sentiment built only from forecasts/guidance yields **Sharpe −0.01** (0.20 inside Price Movement) — the paper's own EMH-consistent result; any deployment that pools forecast and event text without separating them is contradicted by the source.
4. **Several topics are negative, not merely weak:** Supply Disruption **−0.07**, Geopolitical **−0.26**, Trade Policy **−0.25**, Company News **−0.06** (global, Figure 4B) — naive sentiment on the most "obviously bullish/bearish" supply-and-policy text loses money in this sample.
5. **Two of three news sources are near-useless:** Dow Jones 0.18 / +32% and China News Service 0.15 / +22% over the whole period vs Reuters 0.80 / +433%. The alpha is substantially a **Reuters-coverage artifact**, which is a single-vendor dependency, not a generic "news sentiment" effect.
6. **Multiple-testing / selection in the topic filter:** the published "Best Combo" (Sharpe 1.00) is the maximum of **4,094 subsets evaluated on the same sample it is reported on**, with no deflated-Sharpe, holdout, or correction of any kind; the improvement claim (+23.6%) is therefore a selection statistic, not out-of-sample evidence.
7. **Multiple-testing in the forecaster:** a **2,400-combination** hyperparameter grid over 80 configurations, then "the best-performing model for each window size is retrained on the full dataset". Walk-forward prediction generation does not remove configuration-selection bias measured on the full sample.
8. **Hit rates are barely above chance:** overall average **0.556**, best cell **0.577**, and the no-sentiment baseline is only 0.005–0.02 behind the sentiment variants on hit rate — directional edge is thin even before costs.
9. **Return cells are frequently negative:** TFT average **−42.9%**, GRU **−30.8%** at 3 months, ConvLSTM average **−4.5%** despite the best hit rate — directional accuracy and realized return are decoupled in the source's own Table 3.
10. **Three hand-picked trade anecdotes carry the narrative:** all highlighted sentiment-vs-no-sentiment gaps fall in **2020–2022** (a uniquely volatile window), which is consistent with the regime-conditional reading rather than a durable edge.
11. **No cost model exists in the source**, and the price series has an unresolved venue identity — so no figure above can be treated as net or as tradable.
12. **Look-ahead exposure is untested, not cleared:** sentiment for month t is computed from month t's headlines and multiplied by "the aluminum return realized for each period" without an explicit one-month lag statement.
13. **Publication-bias / provenance risk:** sole-source preprint, no journal-ref, venue contradiction inside one document, and the linked repository (pinned `0c43c26…`) contains **no** aluminum or trading code, so the trading leg cannot be audited.

None of the above was contradicted by any other reviewed source; absence of an external replication is not evidence that no negative result exists.

## Falsification plan

Tests and thresholds below that are not printed in the source are labelled `research-proposed` (design choices) or `research-defined falsification threshold` (accept/reject cutoffs). Data assumed: point-in-time headline stream with publication timestamps + an unambiguous tradable aluminum price series (SHFE or LME, chosen explicitly) + published fee schedule.

- **F1 — Timing / look-ahead audit (`research-proposed`; `research-defined falsification threshold`):** rebuild `Sent_t` strictly from headlines with publication time ≤ month-t close, then trade **month t+1** (not month t). **Fail if** the Reuters sentiment-only full-period Sharpe drops below **0.30** (monthly-vs-annualized convention pinned first and held fixed) or the long-short monthly mean return's Newey–West t-statistic falls below **2.0** — either result ⇒ the published effect is a same-month alignment artifact.
- **F2 — Cost stress (`research-proposed`; threshold `research-defined`):** apply a round-trip cost ladder of 0 / 5 / 10 / 20 bps per side plus, if a futures/perp venue is used, the observed funding schedule. **Fail if** net Sharpe ≤ 0 at 10 bps/side, or if net Sharpe at 20 bps/side is < 50% of the gross figure.
- **F3 — Pre-registered regime filter (`research-proposed`; threshold `research-defined`):** replace the ex-post 8.40%/19.30% cut-points with cut-points computed only from data available at each rebalance (trailing 36-month volatility quantiles). **Fail if** the high-volatility-bin Sharpe advantage over the low-volatility bin shrinks by ≥ 50% versus the published 1.04 vs 0.29–0.54 spread.
- **F4 — Topic-combo selection control (`research-proposed`; threshold `research-defined`):** evaluate the published 8-topic "Best Combo" on a held-out period never used in the 4,094-subset search, and report Deflated Sharpe over all 4,094 tried. **Fail if** the held-out Sharpe is ≤ the all-topics benchmark (0.81-equivalent) or deflated Sharpe < 1.0 ⇒ the +23.6% filter gain is rejected as selection luck.
- **F5 — Source-replication test (`research-proposed`; threshold `research-defined`):** run the identical sign-of-monthly-mean rule on Dow Jones-only and China-News-Service-only streams (published 0.18 / 0.15). **Fail if** non-Reuters streams stay below Sharpe 0.30 while Reuters alone passes F1 ⇒ the effect is reclassified as vendor-specific (single-source dependency), not a market-wide sentiment mechanism.
- **F6 — Event-vs-forecast ablation (`research-proposed`; threshold `research-defined`):** split headlines by occurred/forward-looking before aggregation. **Fail if** the occurred-only arm does not beat the forward-looking arm out-of-sample by ≥ 0.20 Sharpe units ⇒ the information-diffusion mechanism is not supported even if a combined arm profits.
- **F7 — Placebo (`research-proposed`; threshold `research-defined`):** date-shuffle headline months (or rotate the sentiment series by ±1..±6 months) 1,000× and recompute full-period Sharpe. **Fail if** the true Sharpe's |z| < 2.0 against the placebo distribution, or if ≥ 5% of shuffles beat it.
- **F8 — Baseline comparison (`research-proposed`):** compare against (a) always-long aluminum, (b) sign of trailing 1-month aluminum return, (c) the tabular-only LSTM arm. **Fail if** sentiment does not beat (a) and (b) on a risk-adjusted basis after F2 costs ⇒ sentiment is redundant with beta/momentum.
- **F9 — Cross-commodity generalization (`research-proposed`):** replicate on copper/zinc with the same frozen pipeline. **Fail if** ≥ 2 of 3 metals are null ⇒ treat as a one-off single-asset result, `unproven` for the family.
- **Failure action (`research-defined`):** any of F1, F2, F4 failing ⇒ record remains research-only and is marked falsified at the mechanism level in any future update; F5–F9 failures ⇒ narrow the claim (vendor- or asset-specific) rather than discard wholesale.

## Crypto portability

**`unproven`.**

- The source contains **zero** crypto evidence: one base-metal price series, monthly, Wind/Factiva data. Nothing in v2 tests digital assets, so porting is a hypothesis, not a finding.
- Mechanism-level plausibility is real but weaker than it looks: crypto has 24/7 news flow (monthly buckets still work), fragmented English-only-plus-Korean/Chinese social streams, and far faster price absorption than a thinly covered metal — the paper's own EMH result (forward-looking text ≈ Sharpe −0.01) suggests crypto's faster absorption could kill the event leg too.
- Concrete porting risks to resolve before any crypto test (`research-proposed`): venue choice (spot vs USDT-perp vs coin-margined) changes the P&L identity because the source's "signal × price return" ignores funding entirely; funding accrues 8h× and can flip the sign of a monthly carry; 24/7 candle boundaries vs UTC-month cutoffs change `Sent_t` membership; exchange fragmentation means a Reuters headline hits CEX books within seconds; borrow/liquidation on the short leg is venue-specific and unmodeled in the source; wash-traded spot prints can distort month-boundary prices.
- Label stays `unproven` — not `adapted`, because not even a crypto-adapted variant has been specified or tested by the source, and not `direct`, which the README forbids for non-crypto evidence.
