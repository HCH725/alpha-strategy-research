## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The authors' stated rationale has four links. (1) Well-documented style factors earn positive premia over long horizons, but their **short-horizon performance is strongly cyclical**, with persistent underperformance lasting years (the value drawdown of the 2010s is their example, citing Israel et al. 2021). (2) Rather than *predict* factor turns - which the paper explicitly rejects, citing Asness (2016) "the siren song of factor timing" and Bender et al. (2018) - the paper performs **regime identification**: an unsupervised clustering of each factor's historical periods into homogeneous sub-clusters, followed by an online inference step that relies on the persistence assumption, so that "the purpose of regime-based strategies [is] not to predict regime shifts or future market movements, but to identify when a regime shift has occurred, and then benefit from the persistence of equilibrium returns and volatilities" (quoting Nystrup et al. 2015). (3) Each factor is scored on its **active return** (factor index return minus market index return), which removes the common market component and lowers cross-factor correlation - the source reports the highest correlation of factor active returns as 54% between value and size and -52% between value and growth, while correlations of factor returns in excess of the risk-free rate all exceed 85% (footnote 7). (4) The six factor-specific regime inferences are merged by a **Black-Litterman model** that takes six relative views (long factor / short market) into a mean-variance optimisation with long-only, fully-invested constraints over the seven indices, with confidence levels chosen to hit a target tracking error of 1% to 4% against an equally weighted benchmark.

Component roles as the source assigns them:

```text
Regime:            two-state sparse jump model (SJM) on 17 enumerated features
                   of each factor's active returns plus market-environment features
Primary signal:    online-inferred state at end of day T -> expected active return
                   for that state (historical average active return in that regime),
                   capped at +/-5% per annum; position applied on day T+2
Synthesis:         Black-Litterman six relative views (factor vs market), posterior
                   expected returns into long-only fully-invested mean-variance
Risk / sizing:     tracking-error target 1%-4% vs the quarterly-rebalanced
                   equally weighted benchmark; risk aversion delta = 2.5;
                   EWMA covariance with halflife 126 trading days
Benchmark:         equally weighted allocation across all seven indices,
                   rebalanced quarterly (turnover 3.8% annualised, Exhibit 9)
```

### Research interpretation

The falsifiable hypothesis is **regime-conditional persistence of factor active returns**: if a factor's active-return series is well described by two persistent states, and a penalty-regularised clustering can detect state changes quickly enough to survive a one-day execution delay, then conditionally holding the factor in its detected bull state and de-emphasising it in its detected bear state should raise the information ratio of a long-only multi-factor book relative to an unconditional equally weighted book, after a 5 bp-per-side cost. The mechanism is a **conditional risk-premium / crowding-persistence channel**, not a new premium: the source itself stresses that regime analysis is descriptive rather than predictive, so any edge must come from the *durability* of the identified states, and it disappears if states flip faster than the T-to-T+2 execution lag.

Note that the traded artefact is a long-only allocation across pre-constructed indices; the single-factor long-short book exists **only** as an in-process evaluator of the regime labels and, per Section 3.2, "is intended solely for evaluating the inferred regimes and not for practical trading". Ablation of each component (regime model, feature set, Black-Litterman synthesis, tracking-error level) is therefore required before attributing any part of the reported uplift to the regime signal itself.

## Signal

Everything in this section marked `source-reported` is stated in arXiv:2410.14841v1; anything not stated by the source is explicitly labelled `research-proposed`.

- **Formation timestamp (`source-reported`):** daily, U.S. equity session. The feature vector `x_T` is computed at the end of day T from data available at that time; the SJM state `s_T` is inferred then; the portfolio is rebalanced **at the end of day T+1**, so the inferred regime affects positions from **day T+2** (Section 3.1.3). Market-environment inputs (VIX, 2-year yield, 10-year-minus-2-year slope) come from FRED and are stated to be daily, real-time, without delay.
- **Lookback (`source-reported`):** all windows in trading days, from Exhibit 3. Factor-specific features computed on factor active returns: EWMA active return at effective windows 8, 21, 63; RSI at 8, 21, 63; stochastic oscillator %K at 8, 21, 63; MACD at (8,21) and (21,63); downside deviation log-transformed at 21; active market beta at 21. Market-environment features: EWMA market return 21; VIX as log-difference then EWMA 21; 2-year yield difference then EWMA 21; 10y-minus-2y slope difference then EWMA 21. Transformations apply in sequence. SJM training uses an **expanding window of minimum 8 years and maximum 12 years**, refit **monthly**, with a **6-year validation window rolling forward every 6 months**; hyperparameters are applied for the following 6 months (Section 3.2).
- **Regime model (`source-reported`):** two-state statistical jump model solved as `min over states and centroids of sum 1/2||x_t - theta_{s_t}||^2 + lambda * sum 1{s_{t-1} != s_t}`, extended to the sparse jump model with an l1 budget `kappa` on feature weights (Section 3.1, Eq. 1). Example fitting in Exhibit 4 / Exhibit 5 uses "typical hyperparameters" **lambda = 50.0** and **kappa2 = 9.5**; the production hyperparameters are the output of the six-monthly validation selection and are **not printed in the source** (`data gap`). Bull versus bear labelling is by cumulative active return within each state across training periods.
- **Feature count (`source-reported`, with a source discrepancy):** Exhibit 3 enumerates 13 factor-specific features plus 4 market-environment features = **17**, and Exhibit 5 prints exactly 17 feature rows; the body text in Section 1 says "We input around twenty features into a two-state sparse jump model for each factor index". The printed enumeration and the printed weights both give 17; the discrepancy is recorded here rather than silently reconciled.
- **Expected-return mapping (`source-reported`):** the inferred regime is converted to an expected active return by taking the historical average active return across all training periods under that same regime (Section 3.2); this is stated to be "not intended to be an accurate active return forecast but rather a convenient way to represent the inferred regimes".
- **Long entry / short entry, hypothetical single-factor evaluator (`source-reported`, and contested - see frontmatter):** when expected active return exceeds **+5% per annum**, go long 100% of the factor and short 100% of the market; when it is below **-5%**, short 100% of the factor and long 100% of the market; inside [-5%, +5%] invest in the relative portfolio with linear weighting. The same paragraph then says "the position size proportional to the expected factor active return, capped at +/-5%", while Appendix B says "We cap the estimated expected active return at +/-5% per annum to mitigate the impact of extreme values". The two readings differ by a factor of 20 in position size and are **not reconciled by the source**; both must be implemented in any reproduction.
- **Exit / holding period (`source-reported`):** there is no separate exit rule. The portfolio is re-derived at each monthly SJM refit and each time the online inference updates, with the T-to-T+2 delay; the source reports annualised **one-way turnover of 237.2%, 395.5%, 522.5% and 627.5%** for tracking-error targets 1%, 2%, 3% and 4% respectively, and 3.8% for the equally weighted benchmark (Exhibit 9 Panel A), which implies continuous daily-to-weekly position changes rather than a discrete holding period.
- **Position sizing, final portfolio (`source-reported`):** long-only, 100% fully invested across the seven indices; no shorting and no cash. Mean-variance optimisation with long-only and fully-invested weight constraints on the Black-Litterman posterior expected returns; risk aversion `delta = 2.5` ("world average risk tolerance", He and Litterman 1999); covariance estimated by EWMA with halflife 126 trading days; view confidence chosen so realised tracking error against the benchmark lands at 1%, 2%, 3% or 4%.
- **Benchmark (`source-reported`):** equally weighted allocation across the same seven indices, rebalanced quarterly.
- **Hyperparameter search space (`data gap`):** the source states time-series cross-validation with the highest validation Sharpe selected every six months, tuned per factor individually, but **never prints the number of hyperparameter combinations searched**, the grids for `lambda` and `kappa`, or the selected values. The multiple-testing burden behind the reported test-period numbers is therefore not quantifiable from the source.
- **Specification status:** the signal is **reconstructable at the level of features, windows, timing and optimiser**, but **underspecified** at the level of the SJM hyperparameter grid, the selected hyperparameters, the exact linear-weighting map inside [-5%, +5%], and the position-sizing semantics noted above.

### Negative evidence

1. **Only two of six factor alphas are significant in the source's own Exhibit 1:** momentum t = 2.27 and quality t = 2.17 clear a two-sided 5% bar; value t = 0.35, size t = 0.15, growth t = 0.31 and low volatility t = 1.21 do not. The strategy is therefore conditioning a large part of its book on factors the source cannot show have a premium.
2. **The uplift over the static benchmark is small in absolute terms:** information ratio versus the market improves from 0.05 (EW) to at best 0.44, active return from 0.09% to 1.97% per year, and excess-return Sharpe from 0.54 to 0.65.
3. **Drawdown is barely improved:** absolute max drawdown moves from -54.9% (market) and -52.9% (EW) to -50.5% at best, a 4.4 percentage-point difference; the source attributes this to staying 100% invested and concedes that adding a market-level absolute view "could potentially achieve further risk mitigation", i.e. the reported design does not deliver downside protection.
4. **No statistical significance is reported for the strategy itself.** The only t-statistic in the whole paper is in Exhibit 1 for factor alphas. There is no t-stat, p-value, confidence interval, bootstrap, Newey-West/HAC statistic, deflated Sharpe or probability-of-backtest-overfitting measure anywhere in the pinned text (census: `p-value` 0, `bootstrap` 0, `deflated` 0, `t-stat` 1).
5. **The multiple-testing burden is unknowable.** Hyperparameters are selected every six months by maximising validation Sharpe, per factor, over an unstated grid, for roughly 18 years of test period; the number of trials N is never printed, so no deflated-Sharpe style correction can be computed from the source.
6. **Hyperparameter re-selection appears to continue inside the reported test span (research interpretation, not a source statement).** Section 3.2 describes an expanding 8-12 year training window, a 6-year validation window "rolls forward every six months", monthly refits, and hyperparameters "applying these values for the next six months", while "testing period begins in 2007" and the reported test window runs to 2024. The source never states that tuning stops at 2007; under the procedure as described, the 2007-2024 headline numbers are produced with hyperparameters re-selected on trailing 6-year windows that lie inside the reported test span. This must be resolved before the 0.54 to 0.65 Sharpe gap can be read as a clean out-of-sample result.
7. **Caption ambiguity on costs for the regime-quality evaluator:** the Exhibit 6 note says the strategy return is "before accounting for transaction costs" while also saying a 5 bp cost is applied, so the 0.16-0.39 Sharpe range used to judge regime quality cannot be classified as gross or net.
8. **Position-sizing semantics are internally contradictory:** the main text simultaneously asserts 100% position and a +/-5% position cap for the same rule (contradiction 1 in frontmatter). A factor-of-20 sizing ambiguity sits directly under every Exhibit 6 number.
9. **The source itself reports real-time regime instability:** "the number of regime shifts in real-time inference remains higher - usually twice as frequent - than in the in-sample training"; online signals that flip quickly "are unlikely to remain effective given the time discrepancy between T and T+2"; and k-means is criticised precisely for "an unrealistically large number of regime shifts".
10. **The source rates its own regime detection weakest where it matters defensively:** "For the quality factor, which has particularly low volatility and max drawdown due to its defensive nature, we found the regime identification to be the least effective", and quality shows only 0.64 shifts per year against 3.66 for momentum, i.e. the signal barely fires on the one factor designed to protect in drawdowns.
11. **Market-environment features are effectively discarded:** Exhibit 5 gives the four macro features weights of 1.17%, 0.96%, 0.00% and 0.00%; the source concedes this "may underestimate the influence of the market environment", leaving the model close to a pure price-and-technical construction on active returns.
12. **Regime analysis is explicitly not prediction,** so the entire edge rests on the persistence assumption; the source quotes the framing directly and cites Asness (2016) and Bender et al. (2018) on the difficulty of factor timing.
13. **Costs are thin and one-sided:** only 5 bp per side. Slippage, spread, market impact, participation, latency, borrow, funding and capacity are absent from the pinned text. At 627.5% one-way turnover, doubling the cost assumption to 10 bp per side removes about a further 0.31% per year of return.
14. **Turnover is high for a "passive-like" allocation:** 522.5% to 627.5% one-way at the tracking-error targets the source highlights, versus 3.8% for the benchmark.
15. **Index methodology changes inside the sample** (Appendix A footnotes 15-19: five of six factor indices were different indices before 1994/6 to 2002/6), which weakens the claim that a single stable factor exposure was timed across 1993-2024.
16. **Single sample, single vendor, single country:** Bloomberg daily total returns for seven U.S. indices, 1993-2024, one index provider, no second vendor, no second market, no period holdout beyond the stated 2007 start.
17. **Feature-count discrepancy:** the text says "around twenty features" while Exhibit 3 and Exhibit 5 both give 17.
18. **Not independently reproduced; single-author-lab provenance with no code release for the strategy itself.** The only linked repository is a general jump-model library, not the strategy pipeline; there is no data-availability statement and no seed (deterministic optimisation, `seed` census 0).
19. **Downstream adoption risk:** none of this has been run in our own stack; presence of this record is research capture only.

## Falsification plan

Every threshold below is a `research-defined falsification threshold` chosen by the Scout, not by the source. Each gate has an explicit Action on failure. Failure of any gate means the hypothesis is materially weakened and must not be promoted; it does not license retuning.

- **F1 - Printed-value reproduction.** Threshold: recomputing Exhibit 9 Sharpe as excess return / excess risk and Exhibit 8 active returns from the source's own inputs reproduces every printed value to within 0.01 (Sharpe) and 0.02 percentage points (active return). Action: failure means the record's numbers are reclassified as unverified and the record is withdrawn from the intake pool.
- **F2 - Feature-specification gate.** Threshold: an independent reimplementation recovers exactly the 17 Exhibit 5 feature weights to within 1 percentage point of weight and reproduces the bull/bear centroids to within 0.5 percentage points. Action: failure means the signal is `underspecified` and cannot proceed; request the feature list and grids from the source authors.
- **F3 - Position-sizing resolution gate.** Threshold: implementing both readings of the contested sizing rule (position capped at +/-5% versus 100% position with the expected return capped at +/-5%) must preserve both headline conclusions - all six single-factor Sharpes positive, and information ratio versus EW at or above 0.40 - under at least one consistent reading, with the two readings reported side by side. Action: if only one reading produces the published numbers, that reading is adopted as the source's intent and the other is recorded as refuted; if neither reproduces, F1 fails.
- **F4 - Cost ladder.** Threshold: re-running with 5, 10, 20 and 50 bp per side, applied to the printed one-way turnover, keeps net Sharpe at or above the equally weighted benchmark's 0.54 at 20 bp. Action: failure means the reported edge is a cost artefact; reject for implementation.
- **F5 - Hyperparameter-freeze gate.** Threshold: freezing the SJM hyperparameters at their values selected for the first six months of the test period and never re-selecting them through 2024 still delivers net Sharpe at or above 0.60 and information ratio versus EW at or above 0.40. Action: failure means the uplift is attributable to adaptive in-span selection rather than to the regime signal; reject the timing claim and retain only the descriptive regime result.
- **F6 - Multiple-testing gate.** Threshold: the source's stated hyperparameter grid must be enumerable (N trials reported) and the selected configuration must reach deflated Sharpe Ratio at or above 0.95 at that N. Action: if N cannot be recovered, the gate is marked **currently failing (data gap)** and no Sharpe claim may be used downstream.
- **F7 - Statistical-significance gate.** Threshold: HAC/Newey-West t-statistic of the dynamic-minus-EW active return series over 2007-2024 at or above 2.0, with the lag rule stated. Action: below 2.0, treat the 0.11 Sharpe uplift as statistically indistinguishable from zero.
- **F8 - Regime ablation and placebo gate.** Threshold: the SJM configuration must beat (a) the same pipeline with jump penalty lambda = 0 (k-means), (b) the same pipeline with states randomly reassigned with matched shift counts, and (c) a static EW allocation, by at least 0.05 net Sharpe each, and must beat a simple 12-month momentum-on-active-return regime proxy. Action: failure means the sparse jump model contributes nothing beyond clustering genericity; reject the mechanism.
- **F9 - Execution-delay stability gate.** Threshold: applying the inferred regime with 0, 1, 2 and 3 days of delay changes net Sharpe by no more than 0.10 across the full range. Action: failure means the edge decays faster than the T-to-T+2 convention the source assumes, i.e. it is an execution artifact.
- **F10 - Cost-and-capacity ladder with impact.** Threshold: net Sharpe at or above 0.60 under 20 bp per side and under a square-root market-impact model evaluated at 1x, 10x and 100x a `research-proposed` reference book of 100 million USD of the seven ETFs, with a `research-proposed` participation cap of 10% of 20-day traded volume. Action: failure means the strategy is capacity-constrained below its claimed implementability.
- **F11 - Benchmark and horizon gate.** Threshold: over a frozen forward window beginning 2026-10-01 and running at least 12 months, the dynamic allocation must beat both the market index and the quarterly EW benchmark on excess return, with net Sharpe at or above 0.54. Action: failure means the 2007-2024 result does not extend; the record stays research-only permanently.
- **F12 - Universe and vendor replication gate.** Threshold: reproducing the design on a second index provider's equivalent factor series and on a non-U.S. market (for example MSCI World style indices or STOXX factors) must give information ratio versus EW at or above 0.30 in at least two of three additional markets. Action: failure means the result is specific to one vendor's index construction and one country.
- **No-retuning rule.** Once any gate above is evaluated, the following are frozen and may not be altered to rescue a failure: the seven-index universe and their historical index substitutions; the 8/21/63 trading-day windows and the 21-day risk/market windows; the 17-feature vector and the transformation order; K = 2 states; the monthly refit cadence; the 8-12 year expanding training window and the 6-year validation window; the six-month selection cadence; risk aversion delta = 2.5; EWMA halflife 126 days; the quarterly EW benchmark; the 1% to 4% tracking-error targets; the +/-5% expected-return cap; the T-to-T+2 execution delay; the 5 bp base cost and every rung of every ladder above; the 2007-2024 test window and the 2026-10-01 forward window; and all thresholds in F1-F12.

## Crypto portability

`adapted` - economic viability `unproven`.

The mechanism is instrument-agnostic only in the abstract: identify persistent states of an asset's *active* return versus a reference, then tilt toward it while the state persists. Nothing in the pinned source demonstrates this in crypto; the paper contains no occurrence of crypto, bitcoin, ethereum, perpetual, funding, Binance or USDT, so this is a **ported hypothesis, not crypto empirical evidence**, and `direct` is not permitted.

Portability risks:

- **No equivalent instrument set.** The source's universe is six liquid, index-backed smart-beta ETFs with billions in AUM and 15 bp expense ratios. Crypto has no comparable long-only factor-index ETF complex with comparable depth; the closest proxies are thin sector/thematic baskets or bespoke factor sorts computed in-house, which reintroduces index-construction risk that the source outsources to MSCI/Russell.
- **24/7 sessions versus trading-day windows.** Every window (8, 21, 63, 126, 252-day-equivalent) and the T-to-T+2 delay assumes U.S. equity sessions. Crypto trades continuously, so "day T" boundaries, candle boundaries and weekend gaps must be redefined; the source gives no convention.
- **Funding and carry.** A long-only crypto basket on spot carries no funding, but any factor tilt expressed on perpetuals pays or receives funding every 1-8 hours, an unmodelled carry term absent from the source's 5 bp model.
- **Borrow and shorting.** The hypothetical evaluator shorts a factor basket; crypto shorting goes through perpetuals or borrow markets with utilisation-dependent rates and liquidation/ADL mechanics the source does not model.
- **Mark / index price and venue fragmentation.** Crypto index definitions, constituent thresholds and rebalance rules vary by vendor and change frequently; point-in-time index membership and survivorship (delistings, exchange removals) are materially harder than for MSCI equity indices.
- **Macro feature transfer.** Four of the seventeen features are VIX, 2-year yield, 10y-2y slope and market return. VIX and the Treasury curve have no direct crypto analogue; substituting them changes the feature vector and therefore the model, which the no-retuning rule would forbid.
- **Liquidity and capacity.** 627.5% one-way turnover on U.S. large-cap ETFs at 5 bp is optimistic for anything beyond the largest crypto pairs, where 5 bp does not cover spread plus impact on a comparable turnover.

## Limitations

- `underspecified`: the SJM hyperparameter grid, the number of combinations searched, and the selected values are never printed; the linear weighting map inside [-5%, +5%] is not given; the gross/net status of Exhibit 6 is unresolved; the position-sizing cap is internally contradictory.
- `data gap`: no data vintage, revision policy, missing-data policy, index backfill treatment, second vendor, seed, code release for the strategy, or data-availability statement. `data gap`: no slippage, spread, market impact, participation, latency, borrow, funding, capacity or fill model anywhere in the pinned text; absence was established by a whole-document census over the complete 23-page extraction, not inferred from a missing section.
- `not independently reproduced`: every performance number is a third-party claim.
- The headline comparison is a **long-only, fully-invested, seven-asset allocation** whose best reported Sharpe (0.65) is only 0.11 above a quarterly-rebalanced equally weighted benchmark, with no significance test attached to that difference.
- The regime evaluator and the traded portfolio are different objects; the single-factor long-short Sharpes are explicitly labelled by the source as "hypothetical" and "not for practical trading".
- Selection procedure: six-monthly maximisation of validation Sharpe, per factor, over an unstated grid, with no multiple-testing correction and (under the procedure as written) re-selection continuing inside the reported test span. This is the single largest threat to the reported test-period numbers.
- Index definitional changes before 1994/6, 1994/6, 1997/12, 1998/12 and 2002/6 mean the "factor" being timed is not the same index throughout the sample.
- Single market, single vendor, single period boundary (2007), no holdout after 2024-06-28, no walk-forward report, no second-implementation check.
- Source quality: arXiv preprint, no journal reference, no peer-review statement, no repository of the strategy itself; the linked repository is a general-purpose jump-model library pinned for identity only and not executed.
- Publication-bias and narrative risk: the paper is careful about the factor-timing literature's failures, but a single positive allocation result over one 17-year window should be treated as hypothesis-generating.
