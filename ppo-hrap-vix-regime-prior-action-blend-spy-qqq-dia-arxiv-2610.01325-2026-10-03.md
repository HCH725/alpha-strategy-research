## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The paper frames daily ETF allocation as a discounted MDP (Section 3.1) and argues that reward-only policies fail in two ways: a profit-only policy "can converge to behavior that is close to remaining fully long on an upward-drifting equity index", while an aggressively risk-penalized reward "can become excessively defensive and sacrifice most upside" (Section 1). The stated design answer separates two questions - "which direction the portfolio should lean, and how much risk should be taken under the current market regime" (Section 1): trend signals (`ret20`, `SMA5/SMA20 - 1`) define a desired directional exposure, and the VIX z-score "controls drawdown sensitivity and exposure reduction under stress" (Section 1), with VIX explicitly treated "as a risk-sizing signal rather than as a tradable asset" (Section 3.2). The executed weight is the blend `w_t = (1 - rho) a_t + rho w*_t` (Equation 16), where `a_t` is the raw PPO action in `[-1, 1]` and `w*_t` is the regime target of Table 1; the source describes rho as "a behavioral prior: the actor can still adapt through `a_t`, but execution is biased toward a regime-consistent weight", with `rho = 0` reducing to raw PPO and `rho = 1` making the system "a deterministic regime rule" (Section 3.3). Training reward is the four-term decomposition of Equations 17 and 18 - portfolio log return minus a VIX-scaled drawdown-increase penalty, minus a target-exposure deviation penalty, minus a turnover penalty - whose dynamic coefficient is `lambda_rrc(t) = max{0, lambda_base (1 + alpha clip(z_vix, -3, 3))}` (Equation 19). The source's own framing of what it contributes is "a hybrid control-plus-RL trading policy rather than as a purely learned reward innovation" and it warns that "the reported gains should be interpreted as the joint effect of the prior and the learned correction" (Section 2).

### Research interpretation

The falsifiable mechanism this record captures is **stress-conditioned exposure reduction in a single beta instrument, delivered by a coarse deterministic prior that a learned policy only refines**, with the open question the source never answers - whether the learned correction earns anything beyond the prior:

```text
Regime (source-reported): deterministic target exposure w* from trend and stress.
        bullish = ret20 > 0 and (SMA5/SMA20 - 1) > 0; bearish = ret20 < 0 and
        (SMA5/SMA20 - 1) < 0; stress = VIX z-score above 0.80; crisis = above 1.80
        (source code uses strict >; the manuscript writes "at least"). Table 1:
        calm bull 1.0, stressed bull 0.5, neutral 0.3, neutral-negative 0.1,
        calm bear 0.0, stressed bear -0.1, crisis bear -0.8.
Primary signal (source-reported): PPO actor a_t in [-1, 1] over a 13-dimensional
        state = 9 market features (log return, close/SMA50 - 1, RSI(14),
        Bollinger bandwidth 4*sigma20/SMA20, VIX z(252), 5- and 20-day returns,
        SMA5/SMA20 - 1, 5-day VIX change) + 4 portfolio features (cash ratio,
        previous weight, unrealized PnL, drawdown).
Confirmation / blend (source-reported): w_t = clip((1 - 0.6) a_t + 0.6 w*_t, -1, 1),
        rho = 0.6 chosen on the 2018-2019 validation window (Table 8, Section 4.5).
Risk / exit (source-reported): there is no exit rule; exposure is continuous in
        [-1, 1] with cash as the defensive asset. Drawdown control is a training
        reward term (Equations 18-20), not a trade trigger.
Cost (source-reported): 0.1% proportional transaction cost charged at each
        reallocation (Section 4.1, Table 3); the shipped environment implements
        it as previous_NAV * 0.001 * |w_t - w_{t-1}| deducted before the return.
Research-proposed (NOT source-reported): any threshold, stop, sizing rule or
        benchmark used to test this hypothesis ourselves, including the gates in
        the Falsification plan.
```

Under the source's reading the mechanism is a variance/ruin reducer rather than a return premium: de-risking when the VIX is above its trailing mean should cut the deep drawdowns of a buy-and-hold book at some cost in upside, and the PPO's job is only to stop the prior from being applied mechanically. The competing reading is that the whole result is the deterministic rule plus a favorable test window, and that PPO contributes noise - a reading the source's own Section 2 caveat invites ("the reported gains should be interpreted as the joint effect of the prior and the learned correction") but never tests, because no `rho = 1` or `rho = 0` test result is reported. This record therefore treats the regime table and the blend as source-reported, treats the attribution of any gain to reinforcement learning as `unproven`, and does not assume each component contributes (ablation is required, see Falsification plan).

## Signal

All rules below are source-reported unless explicitly labeled `research-proposed` or `research-defined`.

- **Formation timestamp (source-reported):** features are computed "from information available before the trading action" (Section 3.2) and are functions of closes up to and including day t; the shipped environment then applies the resulting weight to `close[t+1] / close[t] - 1` (`env/trading_env.py`, `asset_return = (next_close / current_close) - 1.0`), so the return attribution is next-bar close-to-close and the position is set at the day-t close. ## Execution assumptions

Source-reported: the simulator charges `previous_NAV * 0.001 * |w_t - w_{t-1}|` at every reallocation and applies the new weight to the next close-to-close return, so reported returns and Sharpe are net of that single proportional cost (Section 4.1, Table 3; confirmed line by line in `env/trading_env.py`); positions are bounded to `[-1, 1]`, so there is no leverage beyond 1x long or 1x short; the defensive asset is cash; risk parity rebalances every 21 days and CPPI uses floor `F = 0.8 NAV_0` with multiplier `m = 3` (Table 4); all methods share the same cost rate.

Source-stated omissions: Section 5 concedes only that "turnover is high" and that "cross-asset evidence on QQQ and DIA is still single-run"; the manuscript names no spread, slippage, impact, commission, borrow, latency, fill or capacity assumption anywhere (census above).

Not stated in source, therefore `data gap` rather than assumed zero: order type and whether orders are market or limit, fill and partial-fill model, whether the day-t close is actually reachable as an execution price, slippage past the close, bid-ask spread, commissions, borrowing the ETF for the short leg, margin and interest on collateral, interest paid on the cash sleeve (the environment implicitly earns 0 percent on cash because `portfolio_simple_return = target_weight * asset_return`), latency, capacity and participation limits, and the treatment of corporate actions and dividends (the crawl uses `auto_adjust=False`, so the shipped prices are unadjusted closes and dividends are not separately modelled - code-level observation, `data gap` in the manuscript). ## Crypto portability

`adapted`; the sign of the effect in crypto is `unproven`.

The mechanism - a coarse stress-conditioned exposure target blended with a learned correction in a single instrument plus cash - is universe-agnostic in form, so the design ports mechanically. What does not port is every input the source relies on: (a) the VIX is an equity-implied vol index with 252-session history; a crypto port needs a substitute stress variable, and the candidates (trailing realized-volatility z-score, implied-volatility proxy, funding-based stress, BTC dominance or drawdown-based risk-off flags) are `research-proposed` and untested; (b) the paper's calendar, `252`-day annualization and the 2010-2022 splits must be recut for 24/7 sessions, which changes every indicator window, the vol targets and the annualization; (c) spot versus perpetual changes the payoff: a perpetual position carries funding, mark-versus-index divergence, liquidation and leverage rules that are `not stated in source` and not modelled, and `funding` 0 and `liquidation` 0 occurrences in the paper; (d) venue fragmentation, listing churn and stablecoin/quote-currency effects have no analogue in a single ETF plus cash, and the defensive asset would become a stablecoin whose yield is not zero, invalidating the F3 convention; (e) shorting in crypto needs margin or a borrow market with its own fee schedule, while the source models no borrow at all; (f) the data path (`yfinance` daily bars) would be replaced by exchange APIs with tick alignment, missing-candle and adjustment conventions that the source leaves unspecified. Crypto portability is not authorization to trade.
