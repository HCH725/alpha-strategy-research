## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The source's stated rationale (§1, §7): equity-premium prediction is usually done with **mean regression**, which assumes a homoscedastic, symmetric error. The authors argue this is the wrong target because the predictor–return relationship is **fan-shaped** (Figure 2 plots `svar` and `log(dfy)` against the excess return and shows clearly widening dispersion), so an OLS-style conditional mean produces **mis-calibrated prediction intervals** — in Figure 1 the OLS 80% out-of-sample interval covers about **90%** of realised excess returns while the quantile-regression interval covers approximately its nominal 80%. Modelling the **full conditional distribution** at nine quantiles therefore carries information that the conditional mean discards (§1, §6.3).

Two design choices carry the method (§2.2, §4):

1. **Grouped-Quantile Lasso (`gQ-Lasso`)** — estimate `K` quantiles of the *same* response jointly with a **group-lasso penalty on each predictor's coefficient vector across quantiles** (eq. 4): `L_λ(β) = (1/n) Σ_k Σ_i ρ_{τ_k}(y_i − x_iᵀβ_k) + λ Σ_j ‖β^j‖₂`. The penalty forces a predictor to be **either in or out at every quantile at once**, which the source motivates purely by *interpretability* and by the "**crossing quantiles**" pathology: separate per-quantile Lasso fits (`rq-Lasso`) produced **over 30% crossing predictions** out-of-sample in the authors' experiments (§1, Figure 6).
2. **Huber-approximated quantile loss + augmented data + groupwise majorization descent** (eq. 5-6, §4), warm starts and the strong rule for the λ path — a computational device, not an economic one.

**Stated empirical mechanism findings (§1, §6.2):** predictors show a **heteroscedastic (fan-shaped)** relation to the premium; `svar` (stock variance) is **negative at low quantiles and positive at high quantiles**, which the source contrasts with the uniformly positive relation reported in Guo (2006); `avgcor`, `dfr`, `fbm`, `infl` are the strongest predictors agreed across all methods, while `dtoy`, `e/p`, `skvw`, `svar` are picked up by quantile regressions but by **none** of the three mean regressions; `b/m` is strong under OLS and Lasso but unselected by Huber-Lasso and by most quantile models; `log(svar)` is ignored by all mean regressions but selected by the quantile models. The source also reports that **higher-quantile predictions are generally its best-performing quantiles** (§1).

### Research interpretation

Falsifiable hypothesis in normalized form: **the conditional *distribution* of the one-month S&P 500 excess return is predictable from public macro/valuation predictors, while its conditional *mean* is not** — because the mapping from predictors to returns is heteroscedastic, a model that fits the whole distribution (joint quantile paths with one shared variable set) should beat both the historical-mean benchmark and mean-regression fits on distributional metrics (interval coverage, quantile `R²`) and, secondarily, on **sign** (direction) accuracy, which is the only part that could be traded. Component roles:

```text
Regime (source, ex-post, not a rule): predictability is reported to be weak over the
    full 1965-2021 window and "modest" over windows that exclude the 1973-75 oil
    shock, the 2008-09 GFC and the 2020 COVID episode (§1). The source never turns
    this into an ex-ante regime filter.
Primary signal (source): sign of the gQ-Lasso median (tau = 0.5) forecast of the
    next month's S&P 500 excess return  ->  P(excess return > 0) >= 0.5 iff the
    median forecast is positive (§6.3 definition of PES)
Distributional layer (source): nine jointly fitted quantile paths tau = 0.1..0.9
    ->  prediction intervals, coverage, tail quantiles; a source-reported *outcome
    variable* of the forecast, not a trading instruction
Execution layer (research-proposed, absent from the source): monthly long/flat in a
    broad equity vehicle, entered on the sign of the median forecast, no shorting,
    charged a per-side cost ladder
```

The hypothesised friction is **estimation/measurement**: homoscedastic mean models are misspecified for a fat-tailed, heteroscedastic monthly return, so their intervals and signs are unreliable. That is a *forecast-quality* mechanism, **not** a documented economic source of returns; no behavioural, risk-premium or structural channel for a positive expected excess return is claimed by the source. No claim is made that every component contributes alpha; ablation of the quantile layer versus the sign layer is required (see Falsification plan).

## Signal

All items below are **source-reported** unless explicitly labeled `research-proposed` / `research-defined` / `underspecified`.

- **Formation timestamp:** the response is the **calendar-month** excess return of the S&P 500. **All predictors are lagged by one month** so that the fitted model is month-ahead (§6.1). The forecast for month `t+1` is produced by refitting on **all data through month `t`** (expanding window, §6.3): "we use all available data up to time stamp T to estimate the model and predict the excess return at time T+1, then re-estimate ... using data up to T+1 and predict T+2, and keep it going till the end of the prediction window." Availability convention beyond the one-month lag (publication dates of slow-moving predictors such as `b/m`, `e/p`, `dtoat`, `dtoy`, `ntis`, `ogap`): **`underspecified`** — the source discusses no release lag, revision or point-in-time rule.
- **Lookback:** initial estimation window **December 1926 – December 1964 = 457 monthly observations** (*our count*, implied by N = 1,141 and an OOS window starting Jan 1965); window then expands by one month per step to a maximum of 1,141. Endpoints inclusive. Warm-up: none beyond the initial 457 months.
- **Estimation protocol (part of the signal):** `K = 9` quantiles `τ ∈ {0.1, 0.2, …, 0.9}` estimated **jointly** with the group penalty (§6.2); **λ selected by 10-fold cross-validation for every penalised method** (§6.2); Huberisation parameter `γ` of eq. (5): **numeric value not stated** → `underspecified`; warm start + strong rule along the λ path (§4); implementation via CRAN `rqPen`.
- **Design matrix:** 24 predictors (`avgcor, b/m, d/e, d/p, d/y, dfr, dfy, dtoat, dtoy, e/p, fbm, infl, ltr, lty, lzrt, ntis, ogap, rdsp, skvw, svar, tail, tbl, tms, wtexas`; Table 2), one-month lag, `log(svar)`, `log(dfy)`, `log(rdsp)` (§6.1).
- **Long entry / short entry:** **the source specifies no trading rule at all** — no position, no instrument, no short leg, no threshold. Its only decision-relevant statistic is the **percentage error of sign prediction (PES)** of the median forecast: `PES = (1/n) Σ I[sign(Y_i) ≠ sign(Ŷ_{i,τ=0.5})]` for quantile fits, with the mean forecast substituted for mean regressions (§6.3). Turning that into a position — long equity next month when `Ŷ_{τ=0.5} > 0`, otherwise hold cash — is `research-proposed`.
- **Exit / holding period:** `research-proposed` = one month (the forecast horizon), refreshed at each monthly refit; the source states no holding, stop, take-profit, re-entry or overlap rule → `underspecified`.
- **Parameters:** `τ` grid `{0.1,…,0.9}`; group penalty λ by 10-fold CV; log transforms on `svar`/`dfy`/`rdsp`; missing-data screen "drop predictors with >20% missing"; sub-windows `{1965-1972, 1976-2007, 2010-2019}` — all **source-specified**. The source describes these as standard choices and discloses no tuning on the evaluation sample, but the **sub-window selection rule is ex-post** (recession-episode exclusion) → see Negative evidence.
- **Benchmark definitions (part of the signal):** in-sample `R²` vs full-sample mean; out-of-sample `R²` vs the **historical mean up to t−1** (eq. 10), positive = beats the historic average; for quantiles the mean/benchmark is replaced by the historic τ-quantile and squared error by the check loss (eq. 9-10 modifications); `R²_mean` for quantile fits is computed from the **equal-weighted average across the nine quantile forecasts** (§6.3).
- **`underspecified` items:** (a) no trading rule, position, instrument or benchmark portfolio anywhere in the source; (b) `γ` value; (c) publication/point-in-time lags of predictors; (d) treatment of the 57-year OOS window's sub-sample selection as a rule; (e) no stated random seed, fold assignment or code for the empirical section; (f) PES is printed as a **proportion in Table 3 and as a percent in Table 4** with no unit row — an easy misreading across tables. The signal is **reconstructable for the model and the forecast**, but **not for any executable trade**.

2. **Baseline hit-rate test (F2, `research-defined`):** compare the median-sign hit rate against (a) the unconditional positive-month share and (b) an always-invested control. Fail if gQ-Lasso does not exceed the unconditional share by at least **2 percentage points** over 1965-2021 (`research-defined`). *This test is designed to be able to kill the timing claim outright, since the source reports no such baseline.*
3. **Mean-regression horse race (F3, `research-defined`):** re-estimate all six methods on identical folds. 4. **Pre-declared regime rule (F4, `research-defined` + `research-proposed`):** replace the ex-post sub-windows with an **ex-ante NBER-recession calendar** (or a published leading-index rule) applied without look-ahead. ## Crypto portability

**unproven.** The source contains **zero** crypto evidence — a single US cash index, monthly calendar bars, S&P 500 total return versus a T-bill-style risk-free rate, and 24 macro/valuation predictors (dividend ratios, default and term spreads, output gap, net equity expansion, distance-to-Dow-high) that have **no crypto counterpart**.
