## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

Path-dependent drawdown-depth mean-reversion with a fixed-horizon exit, long side only. The program's construction is self-describing: the 50-bar highest high defines a trailing peak; drawdown depth (percent under that peak) is the sole state variable; its own 50-bar mean and population standard deviation form the gate — a bar whose depth sits at least one sigma below (deeper than) its trailing mean arms the entry. The exit is purely positional time: 35 bars after the entry signal bar, the whole book closes. The page prose claims only the generic intuition (buying extreme drawdowns, tune parameters per instrument, keep risk control in live trading) — this record adopts none of it. The embedded backtest header (`Futures_Binance BTC_USDT`, `1d`, 2019-12-23→2024-11-28) is a source fact, not this record's venue. No return, win-rate, or trade-count figure from any source is carried into the derived specification.

### Research interpretation

Trailing-peak drawdown z-gate with a signal-counted 35-bar unwind, long-flat. Unlike the pool's price-level z-score cousins (which standardize log-price or close against their own trailing mean — a level object), this rule never looks at any price level: it standardizes the *distance below the trailing peak* against that distance's own trailing distribution — a path-dependent object that widens after sharp selloffs and compresses in grinds. Unlike the pool's oscillator-plus-time-exit cousins (RSI2-gated 28-bar, CMO9-gated 5-bar — first-moment reversal objects), the gate here is second-order in drawdown space and the cadence is a slow 35-bar hold, so entries arrive only when depth is statistically extreme relative to its own recent regime, and exits cut by the clock with no stop leg. The failure mode is plain: depth that stays extreme re-arms immediately after each 35-bar close (back-to-back full holds through a continuing slide), and the degenerate constant tape arms by arithmetic (`0 <= 0`, disclosed below) — both disclosed, not upgraded.
