## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The authors' claim is a **mechanism** claim about *why* time-series factor momentum earns returns, not a new premium of its own. Their chain is: (i) **factor momentum** is the time-series strategy that buys factors with positive recent returns and shorts those with negative recent returns, and Ehsani and Linnainmaa (2022) show it subsumes stock momentum and other pervasive momentum strategies; (ii) factors are meant to capture systematic risk, so their own past returns should not predict them - the puzzle; (iii) the proposed explanation is **positive-feedback speculative demand**: performance-chasing across styles (Barberis and Shleifer, 2003) plus positive-feedback trading on an asset's own past return (De Long et al., 1990a) plus flow-driven continuation (Lou, 2012); (iv) **leverage makes speculative capital elastic** - gains raise collateral values and borrowing capacity, so recently winning factors attract newly borrowed money without requiring sales elsewhere, while losses force deleveraging sales of recent losers; (v) **limits of arbitrage** let the mispricing persist at the factor level because correcting it requires bearing systematic risk (Kozak, Nagel and Santosh, 2018); (vi) therefore **margin-debt growth**, the aggregate debit balance in customers' securities margin accounts, is a *demand* quantity that measures the leverage-financed component of that speculative demand, and it should forecast factor momentum's profitability.

Three hypotheses are stated verbatim in Section 2:

- **Hypothesis 1.** Factor momentum is stronger following periods of rapid growth in margin-financed speculative positions.
- **Hypothesis 2.** The relation varies with the look-back horizon of the momentum signal: positive and strongest for nearest-horizon FMOM(1,1), declining at the intermediate FMOM(10,2), and non-positive at the far end FMOM(12,11).
- **Hypothesis 3.** The relation is stronger among factors that are more costly to arbitrage, as captured by higher idiosyncratic volatility.

Supporting claims the source makes: the amplification is not explained by risk, uncertainty or credit supply; it is specific to customers' own borrowing rather than to available cash or hedge-fund leverage; it holds out of sample against a historical-mean benchmark; and after the underlying anomalies are published, factor return **levels** decay while factor **momentum** does not. These are the authors' claims, not our findings.

### Research interpretation

- **Regime / conditioning role:** the conditioner is **lagged quarterly log growth of aggregate brokerage-customer margin debt**, standardized over 1964Q2-2024Q3, entered one quarter before the return quarter. This is a *macro speculative-leverage regime* flag, not a risk-model input. One standard deviation equals **11.85 percentage points** of quarterly margin-debt growth (Table 2 Panel B).
- **Primary signal:** time-series (per-factor) momentum on the factor's own prior one-month return - long the factors with a positive signal, short those with a negative signal, equal-weighted within each leg, rebalanced each month.
- **Sharpening restrictions the source itself establishes:** use only the **nearest horizon** (prior one month), because the intermediate horizon is spanned by stock momentum and the far horizon is not durable; and expect the amplification to concentrate in **high-idiosyncratic-volatility** (hard-to-arbitrage) factors.
- **Falsifiable form:** across a fixed universe of characteristic-sorted equity factor portfolios, the average monthly return (and FFC6 alpha) of nearest-horizon time-series factor momentum in quarter `t` is increasing in standardized lagged quarterly margin-debt growth, after controlling for the prior-quarter market excess return, the prior-year market excess return excluding the most recent quarter, and contemporaneous FFC6 factors; the coefficient is monotonically smaller as the look-back horizon lengthens; the coefficient is larger in the above-median-idiosyncratic-volatility half of the factor cross-section; and a placebo using the *following* quarter's margin growth is zero.
- **Candidate economic channel to test:** leverage-financed performance chasing with slow unwinding - a quarter of rapid borrowing builds positions that persist past the flow, so the most recent month's factor returns are the ones still being pushed by fresh borrowed money.
- **Alternative explanations the source tests and (it argues) rejects:** risk and uncertainty (VIX, realized volatility, Jurado-Ludvigson-Ng and Ludvigson-Ma-Ng uncertainty, Baker-Bloom-Davis EPU), credit supply (federal funds rate, SLOOS standards, bank credit and C&I loan growth), funding/intermediary constraints (TED spread, He-Kelly-Manela intermediary capital, Adrian-Etula-Muir broker-dealer leverage), available cash (free credit balances), mechanical timing (non-overlapping dependent-variable months), and arbitrageur rather than speculator leverage (Z.1 hedge-fund prime-brokerage borrowing).
- **Explicit caution:** this is a **conditional-return relation**, not a trading rule. The source itself states that the forecasts are *not implementable in real time* because the Federal Reserve releases the Z.1 data with a delay, and that its purpose is *not to claim a tradable strategy*. Any operationalization below is `research-proposed`.

## Signal

All items below are **source-reported** unless marked `research-proposed` or `underspecified`.

- **Formation timestamp:** each month, each factor's signal is its own return over the prior one calendar month. The source does not state a clock, a timezone, or whether formation uses the last trading day's close or the next session's first print - **underspecified**.
- **Lookback:** FMOM(1,1) = the prior one-month factor return; FMOM(10,2) = the average factor return from two to ten months ago; FMOM(12,11) = the average factor return from eleven to twelve months ago (Section 3.1). The three look-back signals are all first available in July 1964, which is why Table 3 Panel A begins then. Warm-up, endpoint inclusivity and minimum-history rules for factors entering mid-sample are **underspecified**.
- **Long entry:** "long the factors whose look-back return signal is positive" (Table 3 caption); equivalently "long factors with a positive prior one-month return" (Table 4 caption).
- **Short entry:** "short those with a negative signal, equal-weighted within each leg" (Table 3 caption).
- **Ties / zero signal:** not addressed by the source - **underspecified** (a factor with exactly zero prior-month return is neither defined as long nor excluded).
- **Portfolio construction:** equal weighting across factors inside each leg; the two legs are never stated to be dollar-neutral, sized 100/100, or levered - **underspecified**. The number of factors per leg is never printed - **data gap**.
- **Holding period / rebalance:** monthly; Table 4 caption says the strategy "is equal-weighted and rebalanced each month". No stop, no take-profit, no partial exit, no overlapping-position rule. Re-entry is automatic at each monthly rebalance.
- **Predictor timing (the conditioning leg):** Equation (1) is `FMOM(1,1)_t = beta_0 + beta_1 * z(delta log MD)_{t-1} + gamma' X_{t-1} + eps_t`, estimated at **quarterly** frequency where the dependent variable is the **average monthly** FMOM(1,1) return inside the return quarter and the margin-debt growth regressor is dated one quarter earlier. `X` contains the prior-quarter market excess return and the market excess return summed over quarters t-4 through t-2. One unit of the standardized predictor equals 11.85 pp of quarterly margin-debt growth. Column (3) of every main table additionally loads contemporaneous FFC6 factors (Mktrf, SMB, HML, RMW, CMA, UMD).
- **Why quarterly:** monthly FINRA margin-debt growth has first-order autocorrelation about 0.25 (implied correlation about 0.06 two months ahead, near zero three months ahead), so a borrowing episode runs its course within roughly a quarter, and quarterly aggregation filters settlement-basis measurement noise in the monthly data (Section 4.2.1 and footnote 7).

- **Order timing, price, order type, fill model:** **underspecified** - not stated anywhere in the source (`order type`, `limit order`, `market order`, `execution` all 0).

### Negative evidence

1. **There is no cost model of any kind.** In the pinned 117,317-byte text layer, `transaction cost`, `transaction`, `slippage`, `commission`, `bid-ask`, `fees`, `backtest`, `execution`, `market impact`, `latency`, `order type`, `limit order`, `market order`, `maker`, `taker`, `gross` and `net of` all occur **0** times; `fee` occurs 10 times - 8 inside `feedback`, 1 inside the line-broken `feed-`/`back`, and 1 as the genuine phrase `annualized management fee` in Section 4.2.5 - and `turnover` occurs exactly once, inside footnote 16, never as this strategy's own turnover; the single `participation` hit means `retail participation in financial markets`, and every `capacity` and `cost`/`costs` hit is an arbitrage-cost, borrowing-cost, funding-cost or leverage-capacity phrase rather than an execution field. Every printed return, alpha and coefficient is gross of all trading frictions, and the source never even uses the words "gross" or "net". Net-of-cost performance is a `data gap`, never a zero.
2. **Turnover is never reported**, even though the strategy re-forms two legs across up to 201 long-short factor portfolios every month for 726 months. Cost drag cannot be bounded from the source.
3. **The meta-strategy inherits the cost and borrow profile of roughly 100 long plus 100 short stock portfolios** (the constituent weighting used inside each OSAP factor is `not stated in source` for this paper), and the source explicitly declines to model this: Section 4.2.4 argues that shorting a losing factor "means reversing a long-short portfolio rather than borrowing shares", which is true only at the factor-index level - an implementer must still short the underlying stocks, and borrow availability, locate rules and borrow fees are absent.
4. **The authors themselves state that the forecasts are not tradable.** Footnote 4: "the Federal Reserve publishes the Z.1 data with a delay, and thus these forecasts are not implementable in real time. The purpose of the tests is to establish the robustness and the economic magnitude of the relation ... not to claim a tradable strategy in this paper." Section 4.2.5 repeats it. No release-lag length or point-in-time vintage rule is given, so even the research-side timing of the predictor is **underspecified**.
5. **The relation is completely absent in the early decades.** Table 5 column (3) reports **-0.058 (t = -0.53)** over 1964Q3-1997Q2, while factor momentum itself was just as strong then (Table 3 Panel B). The source concedes in the Conclusion that it does "not identify what changed".
6. **Out-of-sample performance before July 1997 is negative.** Table 9 prints R2_OS of -1.14% (margin only) and -6.61% (with market controls) with insignificant Clark-West statistics of 0.20 and 0.33 and CER of 0.09% and -0.42%. Every positive out-of-sample number comes from a window that starts in 1997 or 2010 - the same window in which the in-sample relation exists.
7. **The far and intermediate horizons do not support the amplification.** Table 6 Panel A gives 0.084 (t = 1.19) at FMOM(10,2) and -0.018 (t = -0.13) at FMOM(12,11), and the near-minus-far difference is only 0.322 with t = 1.73 (significant at 10% only). The full-sample horizon-monotonicity evidence is therefore weak; the strong version exists only post-1997 (0.589, t = 2.17).
8. **The nearest-horizon FFC6 alpha itself is not uniform across subperiods**: 0.51 (t = 4.90) before July 1997 and 0.64 (t = 3.66) after, with the *conditioning* effect zero in the first and 0.492 in the second - so the tradeable object and its conditioner are supported in different windows.
9. **The headline is an in-sample regression coefficient, not a strategy return.** The source never reports a conditional long/short portfolio that sizes positions by the margin-debt signal, never reports its turnover, never reports its Sharpe, never reports a drawdown. `Sharpe ratio` occurs exactly **once** in the whole document and only inside a citation to Barroso and Santa-Clara (2015); `annualiz` occurs **3** times, all describing the CER column and a management-fee metaphor; `drawdown`, `cagr`, `win rate` occur **0** times.
10. **No multiplicity control anywhere.** `bonferroni`, `benjamini`, `holm`, `fdr`, `multiple test`, `multiplicity`, `deflated`, `p-hacking` and `data snoop` all occur **0** times, across a family that includes 3 horizons x 3 margin series x 6 windows/start dates x 6 risk controls x 6 credit controls x 2 idiosyncratic-volatility halves x 5 placebo rows x 2 hedge-fund variants plus the publication panel of Table 8. `Newey-West` occurs 9 times and `cluster` 4 times - inference is heteroskedasticity/autocorrelation-robust but not selection-adjusted.
11. **No structural-break test.** `Hansen`, `Andrews`, `Quandt` and `structural break` all occur **0** times; the pre-1997/post-1997 split is asserted rather than tested, and the alternative start-year estimates (0.385, 0.425, 0.530) are explicitly described by the authors as nested windows that share the recent decades and therefore show only "insensitivity to the precise break point".
12. **No code, no seed, no replication package.** `github`, `python`, `matlab`, `stata`, `software`, `source code` and `seed` all occur **0** times. The inputs (OSAP factor returns, FRED/Z.1, FINRA) are public, so a from-scratch reconstruction is possible, but the authors publish nothing with the paper. The only method disclosure is that Claude (Anthropic) was used to write the analysis code.
13. **The hedge-fund-leverage comparison is underpowered and the authors say so.** Table 13 uses **47 quarters**, and Section 4.5 states that "in forty-seven quarters the two cannot be separated" and that hedge-fund leverage growth is close to redundant given the market control (0.85 correlation with the same-quarter market return).
14. **The free-credit placebo for margin accounts starts only in 2010Q3** (N = 58), so one of the two placebos that supports the "borrowing, not cash" claim runs on 58 quarters rather than 110.
15. **Publication-effect evidence is a single specification family.** The claim that factor momentum survives publication rests on columns (3)-(5) of Table 8; the momentum interaction in column (3) alone prints a significant post-publication decay of -0.214 (t = -2.0), and the source attributes that to omitted level controls rather than reporting it as a competing result.
16. **The "only the change in the TED spread forecasts factor momentum" sentence in Section 4.3.2 is loose**: Table 11 Panel B also flags C&I loan growth at -0.174 with t = -1.86 (10% level), and the same paragraph acknowledges it immediately. Recorded as a **research-observed presentational looseness**, not as a source contradiction, so `contested` stays `false`.

## Falsification plan

Every threshold below is a **research-defined falsification threshold**; every rule marked *research-proposed* is a Scout operationalization, not a source-reported choice. All gates are pre-registered before any data is touched.

- **F1 - Printed-value reproduction.** Rebuild the 201-factor OSAP panel (excluding the 11 momentum-category factors) and re-run Table 3 and Table 4. *Fail:* the reproduced FMOM(1,1) full-sample mean monthly return lies outside 0.60 +/- 0.05 pp, or its FFC6 alpha lies outside 0.54 +/- 0.05 pp, or the Table 4 Panel A column (3) coefficient lies outside 0.304 +/- 0.06 with its t outside [2.6, 4.1]. *Action:* stop and reconcile the reconstruction with the source before any further work.
- **F2 - Real-time vintage gate (targets negative evidence 4).** Re-estimate Equation (1) using only the Z.1 vintage that was actually published by the formation date, with a *research-proposed* release lag taken from the official release calendar. *Fail:* the 1997Q3-2024Q4 coefficient falls below 0.20 with |t| < 1.96, or changes sign, once the lag is applied. *Action:* record the relation as non-implementable and downgrade every downstream gate to mechanism-only evidence.
- **F3 - Net-of-cost gate.** Apply a *research-proposed* per-side cost ladder of 0 / 5 / 10 / 20 / 50 bp plus a *research-proposed* one-session delay to an estimated turnover derived from the monthly re-forming of both legs of up to 201 factor portfolios. *Fail:* at 10 bp per side the conditional strategy's net monthly return is <= 0, or its net Sharpe falls below 0.50 times its gross Sharpe. *Action:* mark cost-infeasible and stop.
- **F4 - Borrow and short-leg gate.** Attempt the short leg across the constituents of the sign-negative factor set. *Fail:* more than 10% of required short notional is unavailable at a borrow fee above 50 bp annualised, *research-defined*. *Action:* re-run as a long-only underweight implementation and treat any surviving spread as a different hypothesis.
- **F5 - Horizon-monotonicity gate (Hypothesis 2).** *Fail:* on refreshed data the 1997Q3-2024Q4 near-minus-far difference is <= 0 or has |t| < 1.96 (source baseline 0.589, t = 2.17), or FMOM(10,2) / FMOM(12,11) coefficients become positive and significant. *Action:* reject the horizon restriction and treat the nearest-horizon choice as window-selected.

- **F8 - Publication-survival gate.** Re-estimate Table 8 columns (4) and (5) with refreshed OSAP publication dates. *Fail:* the `D x Post-Pub` interaction becomes significantly negative (t <= -1.96) once the level controls and month fixed effects are present (source baseline 0.096 and 0.094, both insignificant). *Action:* conclude factor momentum itself decays after publication and weaken the limits-of-arbitrage story.
- **F9 - Alternative-explanation battery gate.** Re-run the Table 10 and Table 11 Panel B specifications jointly. *Fail:* any added control or the joint risk-and-uncertainty block reduces the margin coefficient by more than 50% (from 0.492 to below 0.246) while the added control is itself significant at |t| >= 1.96. *Action:* reclassify margin-debt growth as a risk or credit proxy rather than speculative demand.
- **F10 - Placebo gate.** *Fail:* the next-quarter timing placebo (source baseline -0.121, t = -0.74) or either free-credit placebo (source baselines -0.085, t = -0.56 and 0.043, t = 0.31) becomes significant at |t| >= 1.96. *Action:* reject the borrowing-specific interpretation.
- **F11 - Whose-leverage gate.** Extend Table 13 beyond 2013Q2 for at least 12 additional quarters, *research-defined*. *Fail:* customer margin growth falls below 0.20 with |t| < 1.96 once hedge-fund prime-brokerage leverage growth and the market controls are present. *Action:* the "customers' own borrowing" attribution fails.
- **F12 - Multiplicity gate.** Apply Benjamini-Hochberg at q < 0.10 across the specification family enumerated in negative-evidence item 10, plus a deflated-Sharpe adjustment for the number of horizons, margin series, windows and controls tried. *Fail:* the baseline post-1997 coefficient does not survive correction. *Action:* reclassify the result as selection-contaminated.

## Crypto portability

**adapted - performance unproven.**

The pinned source contains zero occurrences of `crypto`, `bitcoin`, `ethereum`, `perpetual`, `futures`, `etf`, `derivative` and `spot` (`options` occurs once and only in "option-based volatility index"). There is no crypto evidence of any kind in this record.

- **What ports mechanically:** the abstract structure - *condition a time-series momentum signal on the growth rate of an aggregate, demand-side measure of leveraged position building*. The identity of the conditioner is universe-agnostic; crypto candidates would be exchange-level or aggregate open-interest growth, futures-margin growth on a venue, or a composite leverage index, all of which would be *research-proposed* substitutes.
- **What does not port:** (i) the source's entire appeal is that the Fed Z.1 margin-debt series is an **official, eight-decade, demand-side** quantity with a regulator-defined boundary; crypto has no equivalent uniformly-defined, regulator-published aggregate margin series, and venue metrics are vendor-defined, patchy and revision-prone; (ii) the identification rests on **quarterly** growth measuring one complete borrowing episode - crypto leverage data arrives tick-level, so the economic horizon would have to be re-derived rather than copied; (iii) the 201 long-short characteristic-sorted factor portfolios have no crypto analogue (crypto "factors" are thin, often single-asset, and few have long continuous histories); (iv) shorting mechanics differ fundamentally (perpetual-futures synthetic shorts with funding payments, ADL and liquidation versus equity borrow and locate); (v) 24/7 sessions break the calendar month and quarter boundaries used for both signal and predictor; (vi) listing/delisting survivorship in crypto factor inputs is severe and untreated in the source.
- **Boundary:** portability here is a hypothesis about mechanism only. Nothing in this record is crypto empirical evidence, and nothing here authorizes crypto trading.
