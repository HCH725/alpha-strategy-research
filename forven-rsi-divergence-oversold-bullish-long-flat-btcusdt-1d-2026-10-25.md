## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The source claims only what its code prints: hold long exposure entered when a trailing-10-bar window prints a bullish trough divergence (second-half price minimum undercuts the first-half price minimum while the second-half RSI minimum holds above the first-half RSI minimum) with the current RSI-14 under 30, stand back to flat when the RSI-14 prints above 70. The divergence test and the oversold gate are a conjunction on entry; the overbought level alone exits. No stop, no target, no trailing, no time exit, no sizing, no cost basis ships anywhere — this record adopts no source-reported performance (see Evidence).

### Research interpretation

Oversold-divergence snap-back catcher, long-only, edge-ish entry with a level exit, zero-stop. Entries are selective by construction: chop that never pushes RSI under 30 never arms no matter how the window troughs align, and a clean lower-low without RSI non-confirmation fires nothing. Verified this cycle on an 800-bar synthetic tape (pinned seed 99): 3 entries against 63 exits — the exit leg is level-dominant (any bar with RSI over 70 exits, even without any divergence) and fires an order of magnitude more often, usually from flat (absorbed as no-ops by the position pin). The failure mode is plain: a V-shaped reversal whose RSI never printed under 30 is watched from flat, and a divergence that confirms one bar after RSI has already reclaimed 30 is missed until the next qualifying window (the entry reads the current bar's RSI alone — trough-bar RSI never substitutes for it).

## Signal

```text
rsi_t            = WilderRSI14(close)_t
win_t            = close_{t-9..t} split at half=5: first=iloc[:5], second=iloc[5:]
price_LL_t       = (min(second) < min(first))                        // strict
rsi_HL_t         = (RSI_at_second_min_bar > RSI_at_first_min_bar)    // strict
div_t            = price_LL_t & rsi_HL_t
entry_t          = div_t & (rsi_t < 30)                              // strict
exit_t           = (rsi_t > 70)                                      // strict level
```

## Limitations

- Entry sparsity by construction: the divergence-plus-oversold conjunction is rare — verified 3 entries per 800 synthetic bars against 63 exits. Live `1d` tape may print long flat stretches punctuated by brief longs; exits firing from flat are absorbed as no-ops, disclosed.
- Completed-bar exit latency: a bar whose RSI prints 75 triggers the exit on that same completed bar, with the full-unit exit at that bar's effective close under the declared derived fill model. There is no intrabar stop order to front-run that close, disclosed.
- Late-entry profile: a reversal whose RSI never printed under 30 is missed entirely, and a divergence confirming one bar after RSI reclaims 30 is missed until the next qualifying window — the entry never chases a held divergence stance without the current-bar zone, disclosed.
- Zone whipsaw: RSI oscillating around 30 can print entry bars with no trough-continuation requirement, and chop around 70 can exit a still-valid snap-back — the zones are levels, not hysteresis bands, disclosed.
- No risk leg: no stop loss, take profit, ROI rung, trailing-stop order, or time limit exists in source — recorded as explicitly disabled; adverse excursions have no capital guardrail besides the overbought-level exit.
- Downtrend blindness: declines are watched from flat with no short participation — the zero-`short` posture is a stance choice with a return cost in sustained downtrends, disclosed.
- Single-market scope: derived pin is BTCUSDT spot `1d` only; portability to other symbols/timeframes is untested and claimed nowhere.
- Research overlay dependence: fills, sizing, and costs follow the derived pins and the stated economics identity — not the source engine's own semantics, and not the engine summary labels fenced above.
- No trailing, no breakeven, no short leg, no cooldown — all explicitly absent, never to be added by repair.
