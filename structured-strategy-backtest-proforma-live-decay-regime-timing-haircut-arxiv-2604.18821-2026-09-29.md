## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The source's claim is an evaluation mechanism, not a return premium. Marketed pro-forma (backtested) track records are constructed before any dollar is traded and are conditioned on a favourable run; the source argues they "predominantly reflect the common factor regime present before launch rather than strategy-specific skill". Once live performance is measured against a leave-one-out (LOO) bucket-average peer benchmark built from the same bucket's other strategies, the residual cross-sectional association between pro-forma and live performance falls from 0.137 to 0.034 (75 per cent attenuation), and against an independently constructed Bloomberg total-return benchmark it falls to 0.025 (81 per cent attenuation); the source reads the agreement of two benchmarks built from disjoint information sets as evidence that the absorbed component is the shared factor environment. Two channels are then named: regime timing at launch, where strategies launched after an unusually hot bucket-factor year embed an environment unlikely to persist, and launch-cohort density as a crowding proxy, which the source finds has no level effect on decay but does amplify the pro-forma signal at six months only.

### Research interpretation

Falsifiable restatement of the mechanism: (a) if marketed backtests mainly encode a common factor regime, then the slope of pro-forma volatility-adjusted return on benchmark-relative live return must collapse toward zero once an exogenous common-factor benchmark is applied, while remaining large on raw returns; (b) if launch-time regime selection drives the residual, then a pre-launch bucket-factor extremity measure computed from information available only before launch must positively predict subsequent benchmark-relative decay, with a monotone dose-response across extremity quintiles; (c) if crowding instead drives it, a launch-density proxy must predict decay directly. The source's own evidence supports (a) and (b) and rejects (c) as a level effect. The missing link the source does not close is that neither result is demonstrated on a tradable instrument, on costs, or on any crypto universe; the mechanism is a selection-and-mean-reversion story about how backtests are marketed, and its economic value depends entirely on an allocator acting on the discount.

## Signal

The source specifies a due-diligence discount rule, not a trade. No entry, exit, holding period, order type or position-sizing rule for any security is given anywhere in the pinned text; a tradable implementation would be `research-proposed` and is not asserted here.

Source-reported rule components:

- Formation timestamp: evaluation is performed at, or before, strategy launch; the benchmark mapping is "applied uniformly across all ten institutions and fixed before launch, precluding strategic benchmark selection"; volatility normalisation is explicitly in-window so that "this choice sidesteps look-ahead bias in the pro-forma period". Timezone and clock convention for the daily return series are `not stated in source`.
- Lookback: 12 months of pre-launch bucket returns for regime extremity; horizon-matched 6-month and 12-month windows either side of launch for pro-forma and live performance; the pro-forma history additionally supplies an "early" control computed over the pro-forma period excluding the final 12 months, plus pro-forma volatility and track-record age.
- Normalisation: every return is rescaled to a common 10 per cent annualised volatility target (Equation 1), so all performance quantities are printed in volatility-adjusted percentage points p.a. and are proportional to the realised Sharpe ratio over the window (source states r_adj = SR x 0.10).
- Regime extremity (Equation 12): the 12-month pre-launch annualised LOO bucket average minus the within-bucket full-sample mean, in raw percentage points; positive means a "hot" regime for that risk premium.
- Haircut rule (Equation 20): expected 12-month live return = lambda0 (approx 0) + lambda1 (approx 0.137) x pro-forma 12-month volatility-adjusted return - gamma (approx 5) x regime-extremity z-score. Source-reported caveats: lambda1 is the raw cross-sectional slope from Table 4 Row 1, and gamma "is a rounded calibration chosen to reproduce the four-percentage-point cold-minus-hot quintile spread" rather than an estimate. Worked example printed in the source: an FX carry strategy with pro-forma +12 percentage points p.a. and a bucket z-score of +1.5 maps to 0 + 0.137 x 12 - 5 x 1.5 = -5.86 percentage points p.a., which the source contrasts with the ad-hoc 50 per cent haircut "commonly applied in practice".
- Failure classifier (Equation 21, Table 9): five outcomes evaluated over the first 12 live months - Negative return (live Sharpe below zero), Performance decay (worst quartile of pro-forma-to-live decay within objective group), Severe drawdown (worst quartile of live maximum drawdown), Tail loss (worst quartile of live CVaR95), and Multiple failures (at least two of the four elemental criteria). Thresholds are re-estimated on the training fold only; validation is a time-split design trained on strategies launched before the median launch year and tested on later-launch cohorts.
- Exclusion filter: strategies in the top 10 per cent of predicted decay risk are excluded or heavily haircut. In the Return-Seeking group this cell reports base rate 25.6 per cent, AUC 0.813, top-decile capture 29.2 per cent, top-decile rate 76.5 per cent, lift 3.0x.
- Sizing advice is qualitative only: "position sizes calibrated on pro-forma volatility and downside statistics are therefore likely too aggressive"; no numeric sizing rule is printed.
- Underspecified by the source: the standardising denominator behind the Equation 20 z-score; any bucket-level factor definition beyond the LOO average; any capacity, turnover, participation or cost constraint; any rule for the roughly 40 per cent of the sample with convex payoff profiles; and any treatment of the Hedging/Defensive group, for which the source explicitly says a state-dependent payoff evaluation "would be required for that judgment, and is left for future work".

### Negative evidence

1. Every return in the dataset is gross of transaction costs, and the source states costs and fees would reduce live performance; no fee, spread, slippage, impact or turnover model exists anywhere in the pinned text, so all decay figures understate net decay (`data gap`).
2. The strategy-level data are proprietary, licensed and non-redistributable; code is available only on reasonable request, so exact replication is not possible from public inputs (`data gap`).
3. The sample is positively selected on the institutional go/no-go decision because never-launched strategies are unobserved; the source states its decay estimates are therefore a lower bound on the gap that would prevail in an unselected universe, and the size of that bound is unknown.
4. Peer benchmarking attenuates but does not eliminate the pro-forma signal: LOO 0.034 (p<0.01) and Bloomberg 0.025 (p<0.05) remain statistically significant, and the source concedes "a smaller residual component persists after benchmark adjustment".
5. The out-of-sample failure classifier does not predict the headline outcome at all: Return-Seeking negative return AUC 0.477 with top-decile capture 7.1 per cent and lift 0.7x, i.e. worse than random ranking, and Carry/Short-Convexity negative return AUC 0.551 with lift 1.3x.
6. The crowding channel fails its own test: log launch density is statistically indistinguishable from zero at both horizons in all three specifications, so the "individual-strategy capacity-erosion prediction of the crowding literature is not supported in this sample".
7. The density interaction is significant only at six months and not at twelve, a horizon asymmetry the source interprets as short-lived capacity erosion but does not test further.
8. The bootstrap significance claim rests on a simplified univariate regression with the fixed effects and controls dropped (observed coefficient -0.801) precisely to make resampling tractable, while the headline estimate is -0.896 under the full specification.
9. Section 4 asserts p<0.001 for a block-size-robust bootstrap while Appendix Table C.1 prints 0.0040 and <0.002 from 500 replications, so the stated significance level is not attainable from the cited design.
10. The levels-versus-decay coefficient identity fails at both horizons (differences 1.033 and 1.038 instead of 1), so one of the two printed coefficient pairs is wrong; lambda1 in the operative haircut rule is the very coefficient affected.
11. Table 3's commodities twelve-month decay cell (-4.6) cannot be produced from its own printed pro-forma (6.5) and live (2.1) cells.
12. Roughly 47 per cent of the sample is Carry/Short-Convexity and 17 per cent Hedging/Defensive; the source states unconditional volatility-adjusted return is the wrong criterion for both, declines to judge whether hedging strategies deliver their insurance function, and warns that the smaller raw decay in Hedging/Defensive "should not be over-interpreted as evidence of superior portability".
13. Part of the raw decay is mechanical by construction because the pro-forma window is a high-water-mark selection; the bootstrap addresses magnitude but only within the block sizes and specification chosen.
14. The LOO peer benchmark includes every peer with an observed return on that date "regardless of whether they are in their pro-forma or live period", so the benchmark is partly composed of simulated backtest returns rather than tradable live returns; the source reports this as a design choice and does not bound the contamination.
15. Main-table standard errors are clustered at strategy level; the natural cluster unit is the ten institutions, and the wild cluster bootstrap in Appendix E is reported for only four coefficients.
16. No multiple-testing control exists: `benjamini`, `fdr` and `multiple testing` each occur zero times across nine body tables and six appendix tables full of starred coefficients.
17. No walk-forward evaluation, no pre-registration and no held-out institution; time-split out-of-sample validation is used only for the failure classifier, not for the decay or regime coefficients.
18. The operative rule's coefficients are not estimated for their stated purpose: lambda1 is the raw slope that the source itself shows is about 81 per cent common factor, and gamma is explicitly a rounded calibration rather than an estimate.
19. Results are not universal across asset classes: rates shows +0.3 percentage points of decay at six months and credit -0.1 (not significant), so "nearly every asset class exhibits negative mean performance decay" holds only with qualifications.
20. The author is employed by the firm that supplied the dataset; the source discloses this and states the firm did not participate in design, analysis or drafting, but the dependency remains.
21. The sample ends in 2025 and contains no crypto, no perpetual futures, no retail product and no post-2025 launch cohort, so external validity beyond institutional structured strategies is untested.

All thresholds below are `research-defined falsification thresholds` set by the Scout; none is printed by the source. Every gate carries a failure action, and no gate may be rescued by retuning.

Action on failure: treat the record as transcription-unreliable and stop.
- F2 - Coefficient-identity gate. Under identical samples, fixed effects, controls and clustering, verify beta_levels - beta_decay = 1 within 0.005 at both horizons. Current printed values fail (1.033 and 1.038). Action: obtain an author erratum or the underlying regression output before using lambda1 = 0.137 in any rule; if unavailable, mark the haircut rule unusable for calibration.
- F3 - Independent-benchmark attenuation gate (core mechanism). Re-estimate the pro-forma slope with a common-factor benchmark constructed from information disjoint from both the strategy panel and Bloomberg, for example a static style-factor or investable-index benchmark fixed before launch. Fail if beta_adjusted / beta_raw > 0.50, because the source's claim is that the absorbed share is the majority. Action on failure: reject the common-factor-regime mechanism as stated and downgrade the record to a descriptive decay fact with no attribution.
- F4 - Regime-extremity portability gate. Leave-one-institution-out estimation of the Table 6 Specification (iii) coefficient across the ten institutions. Fail if the coefficient changes sign in more than two of ten folds or if the pooled estimate falls below 0.20 percentage points per percentage point of extremity. Action on failure: drop regime extremity from Equation 20 and report the haircut as a pure slope discount with no regime term.
- F5 - Prospective gate. Freeze Equation 20 as printed and apply it to strategies launched after 2025 with no coefficient revision. Fail if the mean absolute prediction error exceeds 2 percentage points p.a. at twelve months, or if the realised cross-sectional correlation between predicted and realised live return is non-positive. Action on failure: treat the rule as non-prospective and block any adoption discussion.
- F6 - Cost gate (research-proposed). Attach a manager-fee, redemption and switching-cost ladder of 0, 25, 50 and 100 basis points round trip to the allocator action implied by the rule. Fail if the net improvement in realised portfolio outcome from acting on the haircut is non-positive at any rung the allocator actually faces. Action on failure: record the rule as cost-uneconomic and stop.
- F7 - Placebo gate. Repeat the Appendix D design with 9,999 draws instead of 999. Fail if the share of strategies rejecting at p<0.05 exceeds 10 per cent, which would indicate within-strategy launch cherry-picking and break the source's channel attribution. Action on failure: retract the regime-versus-idiosyncratic channel split and re-open the within-strategy selection explanation.
- F8 - Multiplicity gate. Apply Benjamini-Hochberg at q < 0.10 across the full family of starred coefficients in Tables 3 through 9 and Appendices C, E, F, H and I. Fail if the Table 6 Specification (iii) regime-extremity coefficient does not survive. Action on failure: mark every regime-channel coefficient as exploratory and unusable for the rule.

Action on failure (already triggered): reject any capacity, crowding or deployment-scale claim built on this record, and do not re-test it on the same data.

## Crypto portability

`unproven`. The source contains no crypto instrument, no perpetual futures, no spot venue, no funding rate and no 24/7 session; the universe is ten anonymised institutions' multi-asset strategies over 2009-2025, and `crypto` does not appear in the pinned text as a market. Nothing in the source demonstrates the mechanism in crypto markets, so the label cannot be `direct`.

What would have to change for a research-proposed port: bucket factors would need to be redefined for crypto cohorts (for example funding-carry, basis, trend or cross-sectional momentum buckets) because the seven institutional buckets (Carry, Hedging, Momentum, Multi Premia, Factor, Value, Liquidity) are not portable as printed; the 12-month pre-launch lookback interacts with crypto's faster regime cycles; 24/7 timestamps and the absence of a settlement calendar change the LOO daily benchmark construction; perp funding, mark and index conventions, liquidation, leverage and venue fragmentation all sit outside anything the source models; and the pro-forma-versus-live distinction depends on a marketing practice (commercially distributed backtested track records) whose crypto analogue is unverified. Crypto portability is not authorization to trade.

## Limitations

- `not independently reproduced`: every performance, regression and classifier figure is source-reported; only arithmetic identities among printed cells were checked.
- `data gap`: proprietary non-redistributable strategy-level data; code on request only; no public artifact; PDF bytes and page count; timezone; any cost, fee, spread, borrow, funding, impact or capacity quantity; the standardising denominator behind the Equation 20 z-score.
- `underspecified`: the bucket factor beyond the LOO average; treatment of the roughly 40 per cent convex-payoff strategies; any state-dependent evaluation for Hedging/Defensive strategies; any numeric position-sizing rule; the exact family of hypotheses over which significance stars are computed.
- `unproven`: any crypto application, any application to single strategies rather than a population, and any claim that acting on the haircut rule improves realised allocator outcomes.
- Identification limits stated by the source: positive selection on launch, mechanical mean reversion inside a high-water-mark window, ten institution clusters, and a peer benchmark that mixes pro-forma and live peer returns.
- Publication-bias and dependency limits: single-author preprint under review at a journal, no citations reported, author employed by the data supplier, no external funding, no pre-registration.
- Scope limits: institutional structured strategies only; no retail, no crypto, no post-2025 evidence; decay measured over only six and twelve months after launch while mean live track records are 5.6 years, so long-horizon persistence is `data gap`.
- Incremental-write note: this record is written because the source identity, the leave-one-out peer-benchmark attenuation design and the regime-conditional haircut rule are absent from this repository, not because the underlying question (do backtests overstate live performance?) is new.
