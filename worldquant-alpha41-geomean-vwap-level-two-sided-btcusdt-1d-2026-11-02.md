## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

Coarse mean-reversion/momentum building blocks. The paper frames its universe coarsely: `one can think of alpha signals as based on mean-reversion or momentum`, where `a mean-reversion alpha has a sign opposite to the return on which it is based`. Alpha#41 is a single-print level block: the bar's high-low geometric mean against its volume-weighted average price. The paper prints the formula and the delay-1 next-day class and nothing else about its behaviour. The paper ships no stop, target, or risk leg of any kind.

### Research interpretation

Bar-extreme anchor versus where volume actually traded, two-sided overall. Write the score as `score[t] = sqrt(high[t] * low[t]) - vwap[t]`. The minuend is the geometric mean of the day's extremes — the bar's midpoint in log-price space, pulled toward the low on wide-range days. The subtrahend is where the day's volume concentrated. When the extreme-anchor prints above the volume anchor (`score > 0`), the day's range stretched higher than its traded centre and the book goes long the next open; when it prints below (`score < 0`), the range sagged beneath its traded centre and the book goes short. Unlike sibling worldquant-alpha54 (a single-print level that is short-or-flat by arithmetic, delay-0 same-close — a one-sided object with no long leg), this rule is genuinely two-sided: both legs fire from the same subtraction with no disabled side. Unlike sibling worldquant-alpha101 (the intraday body-to-range ratio `(close - open) / (high - low + 0.001)` — a body object normalized by the range), this rule never reads open or close and never normalizes: it anchors on the extremes and the volume centre only. Unlike sibling worldquant-alpha53 (a 9-bar change of a close-location ratio — a change object), this rule has no change, no lookback, and no cross-bar memory of any kind. Unlike siblings worldquant-alpha6/worldquant-alpha22 (two-series correlations — statistic objects), this rule correlates nothing. The failure mode is plain: the live window flips 1,225 entries / 1,224 exits across 2,607 post-warmup bars (a flip roughly every other bar) with no guardrail and no cost awareness — disclosed, not upgraded. Score magnitude is ignored (single-unit book), so the quote-currency scale of the difference never sizes anything.

- Direction mapping (source-grounded, fenced in Negative evidence 1): `score[t] > 0` → long signal; `score[t] < 0` → short signal; `score[t] == 0` → flat signal (fires only on the exact print `sqrt(high*low) == vwap` — zero such bars across the full 2,612-bar live window, disclosed); non-finite → NaN → flat signal by record rule, never interpolated. - Exit: the first opposite-side or flat signal reverses-to or flattens the full unit at the next-bar open — flip-or-flatten is the sole exit path by construction (a long holding into a short signal reverses across the next open, counting one exit plus one entry; into a flat signal it closes flat). - Direction: two-sided overall (extreme-anchor-above-volume-centre longs; below-centre shorts; exact-tie or undefined prints flat).
- Position rule (predeclared researcher pin): single-unit long-or-short-or-flat; an already-positioned book treats same-side signal bars as no-ops (no pyramiding, no averaging, no scaling by score magnitude); flat-regime bars while already flat are no-ops. Re-entry re-arms on the next signal bar after a flat exit (no cooldown specified — explicitly none, not invented). Pre-first-signal bars (before the adopted warmup completes) and any NaN-score bars are flat by record rule.
