## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

Coarse mean-reversion/momentum building blocks. The paper frames its universe coarsely: `one can think of alpha signals as based on mean-reversion or momentum`, where `a mean-reversion alpha has a sign opposite to the return on which it is based`. Alpha#26 is a negated volume–high rank-trajectory agreement block: it rank-transforms the trailing 5-bar volume path and the trailing 5-bar high path (distribution-free, level-blind), correlates the two rank trajectories over 5 bars, takes the trailing 3-bar maximum of that agreement, and negates it — so the strongest recent volume-confirmed high prints score most negative. The paper ships no behavioural, structural, or risk-premium mechanism beyond this measurement construction, and no stop, target, or risk leg of any kind.

### Research interpretation

Rank-trajectory volume-confirmation fade, two-sided, threshold-free, continuous-score. When the trailing rank-trajectories of volume and highs agree (volume swells as highs climb through their 5-bar ranges), the negated envelope scores negative and the book shorts the confirmation; when they disagree (highs climb on thinning volume, or volume churns against soft highs), the score turns positive and the book longs the divergence. Unlike sibling worldquant-alpha6 (a raw 10-bar open-vs-volume level correlation with no rank transform, no envelope, and a different series pair) this rule never correlates raw levels — both inputs pass through the 5-bar time-series rank first, so absolute price and volume scales never enter the gate. Unlike sibling worldquant-alpha7 (an `adv20 < volume` gate selecting between a 60-bar time-rank contrarian leg and a `-1` short default) this rule has no volume-average gate, no fixed leg, and no sign multiplier — its only object is the correlation envelope itself. Unlike sibling worldquant-alpha12 (a 1-bar `sign(volume-change) × (−price-change)` product with no window and no rank) this rule's memory is 11 bars deep and every comparison is rank-space, never raw differences. Unlike sibling worldquant-alpha35 (a triple `ts_rank` product that can never print negative — single-sided by construction) this rule's negated correlation spans both signs and is genuinely two-sided. Unlike pool Bollinger/band records (mean-plus/minus-dispersion envelopes on closes) there is no mean, no dispersion, and no band — the envelope here is a trailing maximum of agreement, not of price. The failure mode is plain: a steadfastly confirmed trend pins the book short against it with no scaling and no guardrail, while flat-volume or flat-high stretches print zero-variance `0.0` correlations that collapse the envelope toward flat — disclosed, not upgraded.

## Signal

Exact rule as pinned (any other window, series, transform, envelope, sign, fill, frame, book, or tie treatment is a different, unpinned rule):

- Inputs (paper A.2): `high[t]`, `volume[t]` on completed `1d` bars — the klines print as-is (base-asset volume, no quote conversion, no normalization). No `open`, no `close`, no `low`, no vwap/adv/returns/cap/sector, no cross-sectional rank, no second asset or timeframe is read by any order-gating line.
- Rank trajectories (paper A.1, pinned impl lines 48–68 and 154–156): `rv[t] = ts_rank(volume, 5)[t]` (rank of today's volume within its trailing 5-bar window, average-tie, normalized by `n`), `rh[t] = ts_rank(high, 5)[t]` (same for highs). Both undefined before 5 consecutive finite bars exist, by the `_rolling` all-finite-block convention. Scale-free by construction: only within-window ordinals enter, never levels.
- Agreement envelope (paper A.1, pinned impl lines 112–140): `corr[t] = correlation(rv, rh, 5)[t]` (trailing 5-bar Pearson correlation of the two rank trajectories; all-finite 5-block required, else NaN; a defined all-constant window prints `0.0` per the pinned WorldQuant convention, fenced in Negative evidence 5), `score[t] = -ts_max(corr, 3)[t]` (negated trailing 3-bar maximum of agreement). Output range is exactly `[-1, 1]`; both signs are attainable on real prints (agreement maxima are positive whenever any of the three trailing correlations is positive; negative whenever all three are negative). Undefined (NaN) while any required window is incomplete — flat by the warmup rule, never traded.
- Direction mapping (source-grounded, fenced in Negative evidence 1): `score[t] > 0` → long signal; `score[t] < 0` → short signal; `score[t] == 0` → flat signal (fires when the trailing agreement maximum prints exactly zero, e.g. all three trailing correlations at the zero-variance `0.0`). The entry fills at the next bar's open, one single unit, only if the book does not already carry that side (see position rule).
- Exit: the first opposite signal reverses the full unit in one step (close plus opposite entry at the same next-bar-open fill); a zero print flattens the full unit at the next open — reversal-or-flatten is the sole exit path by construction. Consecutive same-side bars extend one continuous hold, never re-enter. No stop-loss, no take-profit, no trailing, no time exit (the paper ships no risk leg — carried over exactly, disclosed in Provenance adaptation (4), never upgraded into a source claim).
- Direction: two-sided (short leg fires on volume-confirmed high agreement; long leg fires on volume–high trajectory divergence). Shorts are native to USDT-M margin (no uptick rule, no borrow desk).
- Position rule (predeclared researcher pin): single-unit; an already-long book treats further long-signal bars as no-ops (no pyramiding, no averaging), and symmetrically for shorts; flat-regime bars while already flat are no-ops. Re-entry re-arms on the next opposite or flat-exit signal only (no cooldown specified — explicitly none, not invented). Pre-first-signal and any NaN-score bars are flat by record rule.
