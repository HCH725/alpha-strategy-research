## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

- The paper's framing claim is that **financial valuation is a distinct class of symbolic-discovery problems** with three defining properties: *multiple valid perspectives* (earnings-based, cash-flow-based and growth-based valuation "can simultaneously provide equally valid but distinct explanations of asset prices"), *non-stationary environments* (the relationship between signals and outcomes varies by regime and sector), and *noisy, continuous feedback* (candidates are scored by error magnitude, not binary correctness).
- **Disentangled discovery:** five specialist LLM agents, one per valuation philosophy - the paper names them **earnings, cash flow, asset, growth and quality** (Appendix D) - each proposes executable symbolic expressions over accounting variables that it retrieves itself from an accounting database through a semantic-retrieval tool, gets structured error feedback when retrieval or execution fails, and fits free scalar coefficients with **differential evolution** (Storn and Price 1997). Fitness is `MAPE(f(x), y)` against the look-ahead price (Equation 3).
- **Hierarchical-level reasoning:** a meta-coordinator agent, conditioned on context `C(i,t) = (s(i), r(t))` = company **sector** and market **regime**, selects a subset of the specialist agents and computes their weights by **SLSQP constrained optimisation** (Kraft 1988); the aggregated valuation is the weighted sum of the specialist forecasts (Equation 4), scored again by MAPE (Equation 5). Regimes follow the practitioner convention of Pagan and Sossounov (2003): **bull = index up at least 20% from its most recent trough, bear = index down at least 20% from its most recent peak**, computed from **Yahoo Finance** index prices.
- **Statistical performance memory:** instead of pass/fail, each agent stores per-expression summaries across seven categories (Table 1) - Accuracy (mean/median MAPE), Stability (standard deviation, IQR, coefficient of variation), Tail Risk (p90/p95 error, worst-decile mean), Complexity (variable + operator count, prediction coverage), Bias (mean signed percentage error), Ranking (Spearman rank correlation), Calibration (signed pct error by quartiles of actual price) - and distils these into learnings `Z^(a)` that are injected into the next generation prompt.
- **Stated payoff:** "state-of-the-art performance on the valuation task compared to classical finance methods, financial large language models, and SR approaches, while simultaneously producing interpretable equations", plus released artefacts (equations, distilled learnings, context weights).

### Research interpretation

- Hypothesised mechanism in falsifiable terms: **disentangling the search into perspective-constrained equation families, then re-weighting those families by sector and regime, reduces search variance and avoids the perspective collapse that a single global expression suffers in a non-stationary cross-section - so the ensemble's out-of-sample price forecasts should be both more accurate and better ordered cross-sectionally than (a) any single equation family, (b) an equal-weight ensemble of the same families, (c) generic symbolic regression, and (d) a naive price-persistence forecast.** Component roles: *perspective decomposition* = diversity prior over the hypothesis space; *meta-coordinator* = regime/sector conditioning; *statistical memory* = robustness selection under noisy feedback.
- A second, weaker reading is also consistent with the printed evidence: the target is a **price level**, and the discovered expressions are adapted textbook multiples (Table 4 maps each agent's output to Gordon Growth, Discounted Cash Flow, Book Multiple, Relative Valuation and Residual Income forms). Under this reading the mechanism is **fundamental anchoring plus context-weighted averaging**, i.e. a structured value model, not a new risk premium. Nothing in the pinned text claims a premium, a portfolio, or a return.
- **The tradable extension - converting the forecast into a cross-sectional expected-return signal and holding a long/short book - is entirely `research-proposed`; the source never performs it.** See Signal and Falsification plan.
- Competing explanations the source does not exclude: level/trend fitting (a persistent target rewards matching scale, not predicting change); a weak baseline set (no price-persistence, no linear factor model, no tuned gradient-boosted baseline on identical features); undefined MAPE scale; search-loop exposure to the held-out test quarters (never stated); backbone-specific behaviour (one 4B model for every agent); single-run reporting with no seeds.
- This is a **ported hypothesis**: all evidence is quarterly equity cash-market evidence across five listed universes. See Crypto portability.

## Signal

Everything below is `source-reported` unless explicitly labelled `research-proposed` or `data gap`.

- **Formation timestamp:** quarterly, at the time each company's **original quarterly filing** enters the accounting database `X_t`; the paper states it uses "data from original quarterly filings only, preventing look-ahead bias from subsequent restatements". The target `y_{i,t+1}` is **"the stock price at the end of the fiscal quarter immediately following each filing period"**, and "the forecasting resolution is quarterly, as fundamental analysis is typically used for long-term stock investment". Publication lag between filing and database availability, and the exact as-of convention for macro inputs, are `data gap`.
- **Lookback / variable selection:** explanatory variables are **not specified a priori**; each agent retrieves candidate variable names and descriptions from a semantic index and pulls the corresponding series. No lookback window, warm-up rule or feature list is printed for the main run - `data gap`.
- **Discovered equations (Table 4, S&P 500 dataset, coefficients as printed):**
  - Earnings agent: `NI_ps * (1 + 0.01 * g_EPS) * PE * 0.88` (closest literature form: Gordon Growth `EPS/(r-g)`)
  - Cashflow agent: `FCF_ps * (EV/EBITDA) * (100/88.38)` (Discounted Cash Flow)
  - Asset agent: `BVPS * (1 + 1.01 * R&D/Assets) * (1 - 13829.10 * R&D/Assets) * (PB - 7.24e-4) / 1.34` (Book Multiple)
  - Growth agent: `(P/FCF)_mkt * (FCF_ps + 0.29 * (P/FCF)/(P/FCF)_mkt) * (1 - 14.80 * (FCF_ps < 1.08)) * 0.01` (Relative Valuation, `Metric * Multiple`)
  - Quality agent: `NI_ps * P/FCF * (1 + log(1 + ROE/100) * 0.87)` (Residual Income)
  Equivalent per-market expressions for Russell 2000, STOXX 600, FTSE Asia ex Japan and CSI 300 are printed in Tables 6-9 (Appendix C). Coefficient values are fitted by differential evolution, so they are **tuned on the training data by the source**, not fixed a priori.
- **Aggregation:** `y_hat = w_C(i,t)^T * y_hat_vector` with `w` produced by the meta-coordinator per (sector, regime) cell via SLSQP (Equation 4); the coordinator may select a subset of agents. The weight heatmaps are shown in Figure 4 but **no numeric weight is printed** - `data gap`.
- **Entry / exit / holding period / re-entry / sizing:** **none specified by the source** - it defines no trading rule, no portfolio, no position sizing and no rebalance cadence (0 occurrences of `portfolio`, `backtest`, `Sharpe`, `rebalance`, `long short`). A tradable version is therefore `research-proposed`: *rank the cross-section each quarter by `expected return = predicted next-quarter price / current price - 1`, long the top decile, short the bottom decile, hold one quarter, rebalance at the quarter close after the filing set is complete.*
- **Parameters printed:** five perspectives; context = (sector, bull/bear); `Qwen3-4B-Thinking` for all agents; temperature **0.7** for agents and **0.3** for memory components; context window **32,768** tokens; max generation **16,384** tokens; served with **vLLM on NVIDIA RTX A5000** GPUs, "takes ~10 mins per agent on each processing unit"; coefficient fitting by differential evolution; weight fitting by SLSQP; evaluation metric MAPE. **Number of search iterations, population sizes, random seeds, number of repeats, baseline hyper-parameters and the search-loop train/test boundary are all `data gap`** - Algorithm 1 and Algorithm 2 both say only `for all iterations do` over generic `targets y_{i,t+1}`.
- **Overall reconstruction status:** the architecture, the printed equations and the main hyper-parameters are reproducible in outline, but the iteration budget, seeds, variable universe, weight matrix, baseline tuning and the split each optimisation step sees are missing, so the signal is **underspecified** and must not be presented as reproducible evidence.

### Negative evidence

1. **No naive or persistence baseline.** For a price-level target the trivial forecast "next quarter's price = this quarter's price" is the natural comparator; the baseline list (Section 4, five categories) contains none, so the economic content of a 0.43-0.51 MAPE is unanchored.
2. **No economic evaluation whatsoever.** `portfolio`, `backtest`, `Sharpe`, `turnover`, `rebalance`, `long short` and `cost` all return zero occurrences; the source itself frames the exercise as valuation forecasting, so no return, drawdown, capacity or tradability evidence exists.
3. **MAPE scale is never defined.** Values run from 0.4343 to 2.1244; the paper only says "We evaluate the models on Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE)". If read as decimals these are 43%-212% average absolute percentage errors on a one-quarter price target, which would be very large; if read as percentages they would be implausibly small. The scale is `underspecified`, and no percentage/decimal statement appears.
4. **The headline improvement figure does not reconcile** - `19.65%` versus a closest natural definition of 19.5570% (contradiction 1).
5. **Significance table does not match the results table** - Table 5 entries are not the Table 2 differences (contradiction 3), so the inferential layer and the headline layer rest on different aggregations, neither of which is documented.
6. **The two ablation tables disagree with each other** on three Russell 2000 cells (contradiction 2), and the final row of the Avg Delta column silently changes definition (contradiction 4).
7. **No multiple-comparison control.** `Benjamini`, `Bonferroni`, `FDR` and `false discovery` all return zero occurrences while Table 5 prints 60 significance stars across 12 baselines x 5 markets.
8. **No exact p-values, no confidence intervals, no dispersion of any kind.** Every printed cell is a single number; `seed` has zero occurrences, the number of runs is unstated, and the iteration budget of the search loop is unstated.
9. **Search-loop exposure to the held-out quarters is not excluded.** Algorithm 1 and Algorithm 2 optimise against generic `targets y_{i,t+1}` without naming the split, so it cannot be verified that equation search, differential-evolution coefficient fitting and SLSQP weight fitting see only the training 80%.
10. **Regime labels are not shown to be as-of-date.** The +/-20% bull/bear rule is computed from Yahoo index prices; the paper does not state the evaluation date convention, so a look-ahead regime label cannot be excluded.
11. **The disentanglement claim is not uniform.** On FTSE APAC the equal-weight multi-perspective variant (0.6442) is *worse* than the single-perspective agent (0.6095), so perspective diversity helps in 4 of 5 markets, not all 5 (source's own wording acknowledges "four of the five markets").
12. **Agent-selection instability is severe.** `M.P (Best Single Agent)` collapses to 1.9173/1.9170 on Russell 2000 - roughly 3.4x mufasa's 0.5718 - which the source explains as needing "an agent-selection rule that is not available in advance".
13. **Complex models frequently lose to a simple ratio.** On All Data sector-median P/E (0.6652) beats **14 of the other 16 baselines** (only Random Forest 0.6579 and PySR 0.6351 are better), and on STOXX 600 P/E (0.6209) beats **15 of the other 16** (only EV/EBIT 0.6099 is better); the source itself notes that traditional ratios are "competitive across most markets" while FCFF/FCFE fail on noisy growth and discount inputs. The "state-of-the-art" framing therefore rests on beating a set that excludes persistence, a tuned gradient-boosted model and a regularised linear factor model.
14. **Single backbone for every component.** All agents and both memory/coordination prompts use Qwen3-4B-Thinking; no alternative backbone, size or provider is tested, so the result may be backbone-specific - `unproven`.
15. **No released code or data.** Zero URLs in the pinned text; the "released artefacts" are printed tables and heatmaps inside the PDF, and the panel itself is Bloomberg/FactSet proprietary.
16. **Universe construction is only partly documented.** Only the S&P 500 panel states entry/exit dates for survivorship; the other four panels state no constituent rule, no liquidity filter and no delisting handling.
17. **Unequal and non-overlapping sample windows are pooled.** S&P 500 ends 2023 Q2 while three panels run to 2025 and CSI 300 stops at 2021; the All Data column averages them anyway (research-verified as a plain mean), so it is an average over different eras, not a pooled estimator.
18. **Cross-reference and caption integrity problems** - Appendix G points at "Table 7" for a figure-captioned object (contradiction 5), a small but real signal of limited editorial review.
19. **Publication status is unstated:** no Comments field, no journal reference, no peer-review statement - venue-based quality priors must not be applied.
20. **Appendix H's own limitations are narrow:** it discusses only baseline modelling assumptions (constant-growth Gordon model) and manually designed numbers of perspectives/contexts. It does **not** address costs, tradability, leakage, the missing naive baseline, or the MAPE scale.

## Crypto portability

**adapted** (performance in crypto: `unproven`).

- **Why not `direct`:** every empirical result is listed-equity cash-market evidence at quarterly resolution built on corporate filings; the pinned text contains no crypto instrument, no perpetual, no funding term and no 24/7 session handling.
- **What plausibly ports:** the *method* is instrument-agnostic - perspective-constrained equation search, sector/regime context weighting, statistical-performance memory and MAPE/rank evaluation need only a periodic panel of a target price and periodic "fundamental" features. On crypto the analogous features would be token-supply, unlock, treasury, revenue and on-chain metrics evaluated against a periodic price.
- **What does not port as stated:** (i) there are **no quarterly filings**, no GAAP-equivalent accounting, no sector-median multiples and no audited FCF/BVPS for most tokens, so all five printed perspectives lose their inputs; (ii) the target convention "price at the end of the fiscal quarter immediately following the filing" has no analogue - a 24/7 market with continuous repricing and no fiscal calendar changes the horizon definition; (iii) universe churn and delisting are extreme and the source states constituent rules only for the S&P 500; (iv) crypto adds funding, mark/index price, liquidation, venue fragmentation and borrow constraints that the source never models (all zero occurrences); (v) there is **no cost anchor to inherit** - the source has no cost model at all, so F5 must be built from scratch.
- **Crypto-specific risks to state explicitly:** spot vs perpetual basis and funding carry; mark/index price vs last price at the ranking date; 24/7 candle boundaries versus quarterly cut-offs; listing/delisting survivorship; single-venue vs multi-venue liquidity fragmentation; stablecoin quote-currency effects; custody and withdrawal constraints; thin liquidity in small-cap tokens where a decile-long/short book would concentrate.
