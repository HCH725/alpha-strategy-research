## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The authors' stated story (Section 1, Section 4.3.1, Section 4.4, Section 5): a stock's one-month-ahead excess return is not adequately described by its conditional mean alone, so a single Heteroscedastic Neural Network (Nix and Weigend, 1994) trained by Quasi-MLE under a Gaussian likelihood estimates **both** the conditional mean `g*(z)` and the conditional variance `h*(z)` of the same return, and a Deep Ensemble (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017) of 10 seeds averages them. Sorting **first** on predicted conditional volatility and **within** that control on predicted expected return (the `double sort`, methodology attributed to Bali et al. 2016) balances the volatility of the long leg against the volatility of the short leg, which single sorting on predicted mean alone does not -- because, the authors argue, predicted mean and predicted volatility are **negatively related in the cross-section**. Source-reported evidence offered for that mechanism: (i) Table 9 monthly cross-sectional slopes of predicted mean on predicted vol have mean `-3.31` to `-4.09` percentage points with mean t-statistics `-13.10` to `-23.67` and mean R2 `4.56%` to `11.51%`; (ii) Table 10 Pearson correlations between predicted mean and seven volatility measures average `-0.14` to `-0.30` over the out-of-sample months with printed p-values `0.00` to `0.05`; (iii) the sign holds for the three **historical, non-predicted** volatility measures (`ivol_capm_252d`, `ivol_ff3_21d`, `rvol_21d`) as well as for GARCH, GJR-GARCH and a linear counterpart, which the source offers as proof that the negative relation is not an artefact of its own estimator; (iv) Table 8 shows the double-sorted books carry roughly half the maximum drawdown and roughly one third the extreme month of the single-sorted books at the same time as higher Sharpe.

### Research interpretation

Falsifiable restatement: **conditioning a cross-sectional long-short sort on a predicted second moment raises the Sharpe ratio not by raising expected return but by equalising the conditional volatilities of the two legs, and the whole effect is downstream of a stable negative cross-sectional correlation between predicted first and second moments.** Component roles (`research interpretation`):

```text
Regime / conditioning: none -- the claim is unconditional over 2001-2020 and is not
                       gated by any macro, volatility or liquidity regime
Primary signal:        predicted one-month-ahead excess return  mu_hat(i,t+1)
                       from network g*, five depths NN1..NN5
Volatility control:    predicted conditional volatility sigma_hat(i,t+1) from network h*
                       (source's own forecast), used as the primary / controlling sort key
Sorting variants:      single sort on mu_hat; independent double sort on (mu_hat, sigma_hat);
                       dependent double sort on (mu_hat | sigma_hat) -- three constructs,
                       two weightings (equal, market-equity value)
Baseline / control:    single sort on mu_hat alone (the source's own control), plus six
                       alternative volatility measures in Table 7 including three
                       historical, non-predicted ones (ivol_capm_252d, ivol_ff3_21d,
                       rvol_21d) -- an in-source ablation of the volatility control
Confirmation:           FF5+MOM time-series alphas, information ratios, maximum drawdown,
                       worst month, and turnover (Table 8)
Risk / exit:           none specified by the source -- monthly re-formation only; this is a
                       signal-and-sort claim and risk management is not part of the hypothesis
```

Competing explanations that must be tested rather than assumed away: (i) the equal-weighted book sits in a universe with a **5 million USD market-cap floor**, so the headline `~3.0` Sharpe may be an **illiquidity / micro-cap premium** that a value-weighted, borrowable, participation-capped book cannot harvest -- the value-weighted maximum is only **1.44**; (ii) **turnover of 198% to 285% per month** with **no cost model at all** means the reported returns are gross by construction and any ranking that survives must be shown net at plausible one-way costs; (iii) the negative mean-vol correlation could be **partly mechanical** because both predictions come from the same 153-characteristic vector and the same likelihood -- the source's Table 10 columns built on historical volatility measures are the relevant check, and their correlations are the weakest in the table (`-0.14` to `-0.22` with p up to `0.05`); (iv) the headline is the **maximum over a 5 x 3 x 2 grid**, so model/sort selection could be doing part of the work; (v) the expected-return forecast itself has out-of-sample R2 of only **0.12% to 0.40%**, so a Sharpe near 3 rests on a very weak mean signal amplified by a wide cross-sectional spread.

## Crypto portability

**adapted** (performance unproven).

The mechanism is a **cross-sectional second-moment control inside an equity long-short sort**. Nothing in the pinned text addresses crypto: `crypto` 0, `bitcoin` 0, `ethereum` 0, `perpetual` 0, `futures` 0 as an asset class, `etf` 0. The statistical object ports mechanically -- one could train the same Heteroscedastic Neural Network on crypto cross-sectional features and sort on `(mu_hat, sigma_hat)` -- but the following do not port and are `research-proposed` if anyone tries:

- the **153 firm characteristics are CRSP/Compustat accounting fields** and have **no crypto analogue**; a crypto port would need an entirely different feature set (funding, open interest, basis, on-chain flows), which is a new hypothesis rather than a port;
- **no listed short-able universe with a borrow market**: crypto shorting runs through perpetual futures with **funding**, **mark/index price**, **liquidation** and **ADL** mechanics that this source never models;
- **24/7 sessions and candle boundaries** break the monthly US-trading-calendar alignment the rolling 7/3/1 scheme assumes;
- **venue fragmentation and survivorship** are far more severe: delistings, stablecoin quote-currency effects and thin-asset turnover are unaddressed;
- **turnover of 200%+ per month** collides directly with taker fees on perpetuals, where round-trip cost is routinely several basis points per side.

Marking is `adapted` because the mechanism is portable in form and `unproven` in crypto performance; it is **not** `direct`, since the source demonstrates no crypto evidence.
