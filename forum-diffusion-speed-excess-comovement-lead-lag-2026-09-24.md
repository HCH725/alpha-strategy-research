## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

- The paper's framework is **information consumption**: retail investors process information and re-transmit it through posts and replies inside stock-specific subforums of Guba (China) and StockTwits (U.S.); "co-investors" — users active in the subforums of *both* stocks of a pair — create observable cross-stock diffusion pathways. Because attention is bounded (Egeth & Kahneman 1975; Hirshleifer & Teoh 2003, as cited), investors concentrate on subforums of fundamentally similar stocks, so cross-subforum interaction transmits information between related stocks and raises the part of return correlation not explained by common fundamentals (**excess comovement**, measured as pairwise Pearson correlation of FF5 residual returns, eqs. 1–2).
- The **institutional channel** follows Cohen & Frazzini (2008) as cited: common institutional ownership (institutions holding ≥ a threshold in both stocks of a pair) embeds information in ownership networks and produces simultaneous portfolio reallocation, co-moving prices.
- **Dominant-group asymmetry (H3a/H3b):** per Jones et al. (2024) as cited, Chinese retail accounts for 84.5% of trading volume (§1; §2.1.1 also prints ~80% of daily volume with ~20% of market cap) while U.S. institutions hold 68.3% of market cap (§1; §2.1.1 prints 67%); market-microstructure theory (Mann & O'Hara 1996, as cited) implies the dominant group exerts disproportionate price influence → retail-driven `Flow` should dominate in China, ownership-driven `Inst` should dominate in the U.S.
- **Mechanism claim:** investors' *trading behavior* mediates the effect — diffusion raises the correlation of buy-sell imbalance (BSI) between pair members, which in turn raises excess comovement (§4.4, Table 9; methods drawn from Kumar & Lee 2006 and Jiang/Gong/Safiullah/Chen et al. as cited).
- **Lead-lag claim (§4.5):** stocks with faster information diffusion attract more informed investors and react to new information sooner (Holden & Subrahmanyam 1992; Pantzalis & Wang 2017; Brennan et al. 1993, all as cited), so excess comovement among **fast**-diffusion pairs should *precede* comovement among **slow**-diffusion pairs; lagged diffusion also retains multi-quarter predictive power for pair-level comovement, with persistence aligned to each market's dominant investor type.
- Source's own practical implication (§5, verbatim in substance): Chinese retail investors "may benefit from developing trading strategies that incorporate insights derived from discussions on stock forums," and U.S. institutions should monitor ownership-network information to mitigate synchronized downside risk. The paper **does not construct, backtest, or evaluate any trading strategy.**

### Research interpretation

- **Falsifiable core hypothesis:** pairwise *consumption-side attention linkage* (observed co-participation of the same users in two stock forums) is a slowly-persisting state variable that (i) raises FF5-residual return correlation between the two stocks within a quarter, and (ii) exhibits a **diffusion-speed lead-lag**: the comovement of fast-diffusion pairs at t−1 forecasts comovement of slow-diffusion pairs at t (monthly), and lagged quarterly `Flow`/`Inst` forecast pair-level comovement up to four quarters ahead.
- **Alpha hypothesis (our extrapolation, NOT source-reported):** if slow-diffusion stocks genuinely adjust to information with a delay, then conditional on a movement in a *fast-diffusion linked leader*, the *slow-diffusion follower* should drift in the same direction over a subsequent days-to-weeks window — a pairwise information-diffusion lead-lag trade. The source tests this only in **correlation space**, never in return space; whether any of it survives costs is completely open.
- **Risk channel vs alpha channel (must stay separated):** the same state variable is directly usable as a **risk/diversification input** (raise pairwise correlation → reduce effective breadth of a two-stock or portfolio book) without any return prediction being true. Both readings are consistent with the printed evidence; only a return-space test can separate them.
- **Component roles (hybrid reading, all roles research-interpreted):** *Regime/state:* market's dominant investor type (retail-dominant China vs institution-dominant U.S.). *Primary signal:* pairwise retail `Flow` (forum co-investor posts + replies + triggered replies). *Secondary signal:* pairwise common-ownership `Inst`. *Outcome/trigger:* leader-follower ordering by diffusion speed (fast group → slow group). *Risk layer:* none exists in the source; any stop, sizing, or horizon is research-proposed.
- **Competing explanations the source does not fully exclude:** common attention/news shocks at the pair level (it controls contemporaneous `News` and `Analyst` counts, but not their interaction with speed); industry/style overlap (controls `Sameind`, decile-similarity dummies, plus an industry-exclusion robustness block); reverse causality (comovement attracts forum attention — addressed with app-launch quasi-experiments, PSM and IV, not eliminated); platform-selection effects (Guba/StockTwits users are not the whole retail population — the source's own stated limitation).

## Signal

**Source-reported construction** (reconstructable as a measurement/prediction pipeline, NOT as a trade rule)

- **Pairwise retail diffusion `Flow_ij,t` (eq. 3, §3.2.2):** identify co-investors = users who posted/replied in *both* subforums i and j during quarter t; `Flow_ij,t = Σ_{n=1..N_ij} (Pon_i,t + Pon_j,t + Ren_i,t + Ren_j,t)` where `Pon` = posts by co-investor n on each forum and `Ren` = replies *triggered by* those posts/replies; `N_ij` = number of co-investors active in both forums. Robustness variant: drop posts with fewer than 3 replies (§4.3.6).
- **Pairwise institutional diffusion `Inst_ij,t` (eq. 4, §3.2.3):** sum of ownership proportions (shares held / outstanding, in percent) across all institutions holding **both** stocks in quarter t (Cohen & Frazzini construction); robustness thresholds `Inst(1%)`, `Inst(3%)` = only institutions holding more than 1% / 3% of *both* stocks (§4.3.2, Appendix A).
- **Outcome `Cor_ij,t^FF5` (eqs. 1–2):** per stock and trading day, residualize `r_id − r_f` on MKT/SMB/HML/RMW/CMA (FF5), then take the within-quarter Pearson correlation of the two residual series (`D_t` trading days in quarter t); robustness outcomes `CorFF3`, `CorCAPM` (§4.3.1, Appendix A).
- **Baseline regressions (eqs. 7/8/9, §3.4):** `Cor = β0 + β1 Flow + β2 Control + ε_i,t + ε_j,t + ε_ij,t` (eq. 7), the `Inst` analogue (eq. 8), and the joint model (eq. 9). Controls (10): `News` (count of news articles mentioning both stocks), `Analyst` (count of analysts covering both), `Sameind`, `Samelocation` (same HQ city), and same-decile dummies `Similarprice/Similarsize/Similarroa/Similarlever/Similarvol/Similarmom`. **Fixed effects: stock i × quarter t and stock j × quarter t** (§3.4 prose and every table note) to absorb time-varying pair-common factors; standard errors clustered at the stock-pair level; `Flow` and `Inst` are **standardized** before comparison (Table 3 note). Table 3 note also says "the individual and time fixed effects are included."
- **Endogeneity layer (§4.2):** (i) quasi-natural experiments — Guba mobile-app launch (China, Nov 2012, pre/post 2012Q3→2012Q4) and StockTwits mobile-app launch (U.S., Oct 2010, pre/post 2010Q3→2010Q4), with placebo years China 2015/2018/2021 and U.S. 2013/2016/2019; (ii) PSM — quartiles of `Flow`/`Inst`, 1:1 nearest neighbor without replacement, caliper 0.01, logit on baseline controls; (iii) IV — `Age` (average registration length of co-investors) for China retail, `PostUpdate` (StockTwits 2013-09-06 feature update dummy, =1 from 2013Q4 per Appendix A) for U.S. retail, `Dmerger` (financial-institution merger quarter dummy) for `Inst` in both markets.
- **Mechanism layer (§4.4, Table 9):** add pair-level Pearson correlation of daily BSI for retail (`COR^Retl_BSI`) and institutional (`COR^Inst_BSI`) investors as mediators; BSI built from buy/sell transactions with institutional trades defined by a 20,000-dollar threshold (Lee & Radhakrishna, as cited in §3.2.4).
- **Lead-lag test (§4.5.1, Table 10/11):** *monthly* data 2010–2022; compute within-month correlation of daily excess returns for all pairs; sort pairs into **five groups (quintiles) by the level of monthly retail information diffusion**, fast → slow; per group take the **diffusion-weighted average correlation** (weights = each pair's share of the group's total information diffusion); compare fast vs slow with a non-parametric test (Table 10); then estimate eq. (10): `COR_slow(t) = c0 + c1 · COR_fast(t−1) + ε` (Table 11).
- **Prediction test (§4.5.2, Table 12):** eq. (11): `Cor_ij,t = ρ0 + Σ_{n=1..4} ρ_n Flow_ij,t−n + Σ_{n=1..4} ρ_n Inst_ij,t−n + Σ_{n=1..4} ρ_n Control_ij,t−n + ε`, within-quarter realized correlation of daily excess returns, 4 quarterly lags of everything, stock i × quarter and stock j × quarter FE, SEs clustered at stock-pair level. Printed observation counts fall monotonically as lags are added (China 165,337,435 → 128,171,735; U.S. 96,013,039 → 75,453,431), i.e. lagged models are estimated on shrinking samples rather than a balanced panel.
- **Parameters fixed by the source:** quintile (5) speed buckets; 4 quarterly lags; institutional thresholds 0 (baseline), 1%, 3%; low-interaction cut at 3 replies; stock-level forum-volume filter at 10% of cross-forum average; IPO-quarter exclusion; 1:1 PSM caliper 0.01; institutional trade threshold 20,000 dollars; FF5 baseline (FF3/CAPM robustness); placebo years as listed.
- **Explicitly absent (underspecified by the source):** there is **no entry trigger, no exit, no direction, no position sizing, no holding period, no rebalance cadence, no stop, no universe liquidity filter, no signal-to-execution timestamp, no fill/fee/slippage model, and no P&L of any kind** anywhere in v1. The source's dependent variable is a *correlation*, so even the sign of a trade implied by its lead-lag result is not specified. **The entire trading layer is research-proposed below and must not be read as source-reported.**

**Research-proposed tradable operationalization** (entirely ours; none of it is in the source)

- **Rule A — correlation-space (closest to the source, still research-proposed):** use published `Flow`/`Inst` lags to forecast next-quarter pair comovement; increase pairwise concentration risk limits when predicted comovement rises, reduce when it falls. Falsified if forecast pair comovement carries no out-of-sample R² beyond lagged realized correlation. This is a *risk* rule, not alpha.
- **Rule B — return-space alpha test (pure research hypothesis):** monthly, form all eligible pairs; classify each pair's stocks by relative diffusion speed using lagged pairwise `Flow` (market-appropriate: `Flow` for China, `Inst` for the U.S., per the source's own dominance result); when the **fast** member's lagged (e.g. 1-month) FF5-residual return is extreme-positive/negative, go **long/short the slow member** next month with a research-proposed one-month holding period and equal-risk sizing across pairs. **Everything in this paragraph — threshold, direction mapping, horizon, sizing — is `research-proposed`;** the source provides no such rule and no evidence that its correlation lead-lag translates into return lead-lag.
- **Why we stop here:** operationalizing further (thresholds, stops, portfolio construction) before a return-space replication exists would manufacture precision the primary source does not have; the falsification plan below tests the hypothesis before any parameter tuning.

### Negative evidence

1. **No trading evidence whatsoever:** the source never converts its lead-lag into returns, P&L, Sharpe, drawdown, turnover or win rate — the alpha claim for any strategy built on this is entirely untested (our reading of full v1).
2. **No cost, fill, borrow, capacity or leverage model exists** (full-text scan, Provenance item (e)); everything cost-related is data gap, never zero.
3. **The tradable object is a correlation, not a return:** `Cor_slow(t)` regressed on `COR_fast(t−1)` does not identify the sign or magnitude of any return flow; a high future pairwise correlation is equally consistent with "don't trade these together" as with any directional trade.
4. **U.S. lead-lag result is weak on its own terms:** Table 10 fast−slow significant only at 10%; Table 11 U.S. coefficient 0.082 significant only at 10%; the U.S. quintile ordering is non-monotone (Table 10 Panel B), so "faster diffusion → higher comovement" fails to order the interior buckets in the institution-dominated market.
5. **China's headline prediction model explains 3.4% of variance** (Table 12 adj. R² 0.034–0.035) and Table 11 China R² = 0.105; the economically large `Flow` t-stats coexist with near-zero explanatory power for pair-level comovement.
6. **Mediation weakens the U.S. fit:** adding the BSI mediators drops U.S. adjusted R² from 0.393 to 0.235/0.269 (Table 9 Panel B) — printed, not reconciled by the source.
7. **No out-of-sample discipline:** there is no train/test split, no walk-forward design, no frozen forecast evaluation; all "prediction" uses in-sample lag structures over the same 2010–2022 window, and the model/lag choice (4 lags, quintiles, thresholds) was not pre-registered.
8. **No multiplicity control:** the printed grid spans 6 baseline columns, 8 quasi-experiment columns, 5 PSM/IV columns, 6+ robustness column sets across 7 blocks, 10 mechanism columns, 5+5 speed-portfolio rows, 2 lead-lag rows and 8 prediction columns — with zero adjustment for multiple testing (our count of printed columns, not a re-estimation).
9. **Endogeneity is mitigated, not eliminated:** the paper itself flags that comovement may *attract* retail attention (§4.2); IV validity rests on debatable exclusion restrictions (co-investor registration `Age`; a StockTwits feature-update dummy; financial-institution mergers), and `PostUpdate` onset is printed both as 2013-09-06 and "from 2013Q4."
10. **Conclusion overclaim:** §5's "confirming the causal direction" rests on a two-variable lagged regression of group-average correlations (Table 11) with no instrument, no design-based identification, and no reported observation count for that table.
11. **Pair-sample construction is unreconstructable from the text** (≈2/3 of possible pairs absent without a stated rule — Required data), so the very composition of the fast/slow quintiles cannot be independently rebuilt.
12. **Point-in-time risk for the U.S. institutional channel:** 13F's ~45-day publication lag is never addressed, so `Inst` may embed look-ahead when used within the same quarter; the source's own persistent U.S. `Inst` result (Table 12 cols 5–8) is the one most exposed to this.
13. **Platform representativeness (source's own stated limitations, §5):** `Flow` uses post/reply *volume* without textual content analysis, and only Guba/StockTwits are covered — Reddit, X and other platforms are excluded; U.S. retail's measured predictive power already collapses to a single quarter (Table 12 cols 5–8), consistent with StockTwits being a thin proxy.
14. **Platform-confound direction:** the quasi-experiments identify *platform adoption* effects on measured `Flow` significance, which is also evidence that the measurement is sensitive to platform design changes (app launches, feature updates) rather than to a stable investor-behavior parameter.
15. **Level asymmetry across markets:** U.S. pair residual correlations average ≈0.14–0.19 while China's average ≈0.005–0.019 (Table 10) — the same coefficient scale is not economically comparable across panels, and `Flow`/`Inst` are standardized (Table 3 note), so cross-market magnitudes must not be compared directly.
16. **Sample ends 2022** and covers no explicit stress-window breakdown of the predictive layer (the mechanism/robustness blocks are full-sample); regime stability of the lead-lag is untested.

## Crypto portability

**unproven.**

- The mechanism's two inputs do not exist as such in crypto: there is **no FF5 (or any standardized multi-factor) residual framework** for tokens, and **no forum co-investor graph with per-asset subforums that dominates price discovery** the way Guba does for Chinese A-shares; StockTwits-equivalent coverage of crypto is diffuse across X/Telegram/Discord/Reddit with no comparable two-asset subforum structure.
- The institutional channel is also absent: no 13F-style common-ownership disclosure for tokens; the closest analogues are wallet-cluster co-holdings and fund/portfolio disclosures — **a different material data dependency, hence `unproven`, not `adapted`**.
- Structural portability risks: 24/7 sessions destroy the "quarterly/monthly comovement window" convention; venue fragmentation makes pairwise residual correlation venue-dependent; perp funding, mark/index manipulation, listing churn and thin breadth (a 4,500-name cross-section does not exist for liquid tokens) all break the design; short legs face borrow/liquidation rules the source never models.
