## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

A two-candle Piercing Pattern (long black candle, white candle opening below the prior close but closing above the prior body's midpoint) is presented as a short-term bullish reversal setup inside an existing downtrend; the Dark Cloud Cover mirror (long white candle, black candle opening above the prior close but closing below the prior body's midpoint) is presented as a bearish reversal setup inside an existing uptrend. The displacement of the second open shows residual old-trend pressure; the midpoint penetration shows new-trend counter-pressure taking over intraday. Textbook confirmation (further strength/weakness within 1–3 days) is recommended. These are hypotheses, not demonstrated profits.

### Research interpretation

This derivative buys a *completed* piercing duo and holds until a *completed* dark-cloud duo. It is neither a moving-average crossover nor a single-candle wick trade, and it uses no volume, volatility, or multi-timeframe input. Because the textbook confirmation wait and all protective stops are disabled as unmodelable on the pinned close-simulation primitive, entries may fire on patterns that never confirm, and a continued decline after entry may leave the long position exposed indefinitely; the only risk control is the opposite-duo close exit.

## Signal

Let `O[t], H[t], L[t], C[t]` be a **fully completed** UTC daily OHLC bar with positive finite `C[t]` and `H[t]>=max(O[t],C[t])>=min(O[t],C[t])>=L[t]>0`. For any bar `b`, `body(b)=|C-O|`, `range(b)=H-L`, `mid(b)=(O+C)/2`. Any duo bar with `range<=0` forces both predicates false (no division by zero). `SMA20[t]` is the exact arithmetic mean of closes `C[t-19..t]` (no recursive seed; identical inputs always give identical output).

1. **Entry predicate `P[t]` (piercing)** on completed duo `(t-1, t)`: `C[t-1]<O[t-1]` AND `body(t-1)/range(t-1)>=0.55` AND `C[t-1]<SMA20[t-1]` AND `C[t]>O[t]` AND `body(t)/range(t)>=0.55` AND `O[t]<C[t-1]` AND `C[t]>mid(t-1)`.
2. **Exit predicate `D[t]` (dark cloud)** on completed duo `(t-1, t)`: `C[t-1]>O[t-1]` AND `body(t-1)/range(t-1)>=0.55` AND `C[t-1]>SMA20[t-1]` AND `C[t]<O[t]` AND `body(t)/range(t)>=0.55` AND `O[t]>C[t-1]` AND `C[t]<mid(t-1)`.
3. **Researcher pins, not source numbers:** `0.55` quantifies the source's "fairly large bodies" (adopted for consistency with the admitted sibling body-ratio convention; no parameter search performed); strict midpoint/open comparisons follow the source's own boundary ruling ("A close below/above the midpoint might qualify as a reversal, but would not be considered as bullish/bearish"); `SMA20` quantifies "existing downtrend/uptrend … for the short term"; no minimum gap size is required beyond the strict opening displacement (the source requires only the direction of the displacement versus the previous close). A doji first candle fails its strict color comparison, so zero-body first candles can never trigger; this is arithmetic, not a special case. All comparisons are exact IEEE-754 double comparisons on the computed values — deterministic for identical inputs; near-boundary float dust is deterministic, not random, and both sides are covered in the falsification plan.
4. `P[t]` and `D[t]` are **mutually exclusive by construction**: they require opposite strict colors of the shared first candle (`C[t-1]<O[t-1]` vs `C[t-1]>O[t-1]`); a doji first candle satisfies neither. Fuzz-verified over 20,000 seeded random duos with zero joint triggers. On a valid completed bar, process **EXIT before ENTRY** as a standing rule (moot under exclusivity, binding if inputs ever change).
5. **Warmup:** the first **20** fully completed, contiguous, validated bars (indices 0–19) are warmup-only and cannot trigger orders. The first decidable bar is `t=20`: its `SMA20[19]` consumes exactly closes 0–19. Warmup bars are used **only** to build the SMA window (legitimate indicator-state warmup); no pattern decision is evaluated before `t=20`, and no pre-warmup signal is carried forward — every signal is a pure function of a completed duo evaluated at `t>=20`.
6. Direction and state: only `FLAT` or `LONG(0.001 BTC)`, initially FLAT. If LONG and `D[t]`, issue one full-quantity SELL at that completed close. Else if FLAT and `P[t]`, issue one BUY of 0.001 BTC at that completed close. Otherwise hold. Never short; no pyramiding, scaling, averaging, BO/SO, DCA, reverse orders, hedges, or simultaneous position/executor exposure. An entry predicate while LONG is a no-op; an exit while FLAT is a no-op. Re-entry is allowed only on a **future completed bar** with a freshly evaluated `P` after SELL fill confirmation — same-bar re-entry is impossible by predicate exclusivity and is additionally prohibited. No cooldown, no pending limit orders. At most one outstanding MARKET action per bar, resolved within that bar's simulation (see Execution assumptions).
7. No stop-loss, take-profit, trailing stop, maximum holding period, forced end-of-sample liquidation, equity-based sizing, leverage, margin, funding payment, or session/date restriction. These are **explicitly disabled researcher decisions** (the textbook confirmation wait and any intrabar stop/target need fill primitives the pinned close-simulation path does not offer), not asserted source defaults. Unclosed positions at the predetermined research end remain marked open at the last close and must not be represented as realized PnL.

A missing/failed fill or position-book mismatch **aborts the run** rather than inventing an order, cash balance, or missing price. Such gaps can prolong unprotected exposure. This rule is strictly causal: an early bar is never retroactively voided by a later gap.

- The textbook confirmation wait, any stop/target/trailing construction, and live order semantics are not reproduced; entries/exits happen at the simulated duo-completion close, which changes events versus any textbook trade management.
- Exact source revision, source-native fill clock, original sizing/cost settings and profitability are unknown. The simulated completed-close fill is a **backtest convention**, not a live execution guarantee.
- Signals are sparse (7 duo events per ~1000 daily bars in the availability check); the SMA20 trend gate lags fast reversals. Near-boundary float comparisons are exact and deterministic but platform-float-dependent at the dust level.
- The modeled 0.1%-per-side research cost, zero modeled slippage, fixed base size and absence of futures funding do not prove actual Binance trade economics. A long position can remain exposed during data outages. No Qlib parity, survivor status or trading approval follows from HB_READY.
