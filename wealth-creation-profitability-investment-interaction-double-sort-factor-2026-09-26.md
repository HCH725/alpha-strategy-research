## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

- The paper starts from the dividend-discount identity `M_t = Σ E_t(D_{t+i}) / (1+r)^i`, rewrites it in residual-income form and divides by book equity to obtain its **Equation (4)**: `M_t/B_t = 1 + Σ E_t[(ROE_{t+i} − r)(1 + I_{t+i−1})] / (1+r)^i`. The source's claim: **future investment raises firm value when ROE exceeds the (cost-of-equity/expected-return) discount rate `r`**, so the sign of the investment–return relation is *conditional on profitability*. High profitability + heavy investment = "wealth creation" (higher expected return at constant valuation); low profitability + heavy investment = value-destroying investment, which should carry a return discount. This is labelled the **wealth creation (WC) effect** (§The WC Effect, Theoretical Framework; Appendix A.1 summarized in main text).
- Operationally the source claims the **product of current profitability and investment** carries incremental cross-sectional return predictability beyond the two main effects, holding valuation (book-to-market) constant, and that it also **predicts the future interaction of the same variables up to five years ahead** (Abstract; §Empirical Predictions; §Conclusion).
- The source's practitioner framing (§Introduction, §Conclusion): factor implementations that load mechanically on a negative investment main effect (the CMA/asset-growth side) "screen out" profitable compounders; screening **jointly** on profitability and investment rather than on either dimension alone is presented as an implementable, accounting-data-only return source that complements existing style exposures.

### Research interpretation

- **Hypothesized mechanism (falsifiable form):** a *conditional investment anomaly* — cross-sectional expected returns load positively on `profitability × investment` after controlling for book-to-market, size-related characteristics, momentum and reversal. Two candidate channels that must be told apart in testing: (a) a **behavioural/implementation channel** — quantitative pipelines penalize high investment unconditionally, so profitable high-investors are systematically under-held; (b) a **mechanical/NPV channel** — the interaction is genuinely priced because the present-value identity implies it. The source's own alternative-explanation tests address expected-growth, profitability–investment comovement and dispersion stories, not the behavioural channel directly (§Discussion of Alternative Explanations; Appendix A.4 summarized in main text).
- **Component roles of the hybrid:** *Signal*: annual 3×3 double sort on profitability and investment (NYSE 30/70 breakpoints), value-weighted. *Tradeable construction*: WC factor = `½(HH + LL) − ½(HL + LH)` (source Equation 8), i.e. long the two "aligned" cells, short the two "mixed" cells. *Risk/cost overlay (not alpha)*: static effective half-spread cost model and a Novy-Marx–Velikov capacity cap (§Transaction Costs and Capacity, Note 10). No regime filter, no stop, no timing rule exists in the source — any such addition would be `research-proposed`.
- The record does **not** assume the LL leg contributes: the source's own decomposition shows the alpha comes overwhelmingly from **HH long / LH short** (Table 5), so a long-HH/short-LH variant is a *source-stated practical reduction*, not a Scout invention (§Table 5 discussion: "can be largely captured by overweighting HH firms and underweighting LH firms").

## Signal

All items below are **source-reported** unless explicitly labelled `research-proposed` / `underspecified` / `data gap`.

- **Signal formation timestamp:** portfolios are formed **at the end of June each year t** using **annual accounting data for the fiscal year ending in calendar year t−1** (§Data, standard Fama–French 1993 procedure). Exact trading date/within-June execution day, order time and timezone: `underspecified` (the source states June formation only).
- **Lookback / warm-up:** accounting lookback = prior fiscal year (annual sorts) for the portfolio analysis; quarterly regressions use accounting data from the fiscal quarter ending **at least four months before** the return month (§Data, following Hou et al. 2020). Cross-sectional regression explanatory variables are **trimmed at the 1st and 99th percentiles** (§Data). Warm-up: quarterly accounting coverage starts Jan 1976 for the regression sample; portfolio sample starts July 1963. Endpoint inclusion conventions: `underspecified`.
- **Sorting rule (primary, asset-based specification):** independent sorts each year in June along two dimensions — **profitability** = cash-based operating profits / lagged total assets, where cash operating profits = REVT − COGS − XSGA + XRD (0 if missing) − ΔRECT − ΔINVT + Δ(DRC + DRLT) + ΔAP (all balance-sheet changes annual, set to 0 if missing) — and **investment** = annual change in total assets / prior-year total assets. Each dimension is split into **three groups using the 30th and 70th percentiles of the NYSE distribution**, giving **nine intersected portfolios** (§Data; §Evidence from Portfolios).
- **Alternative (equity-based) specification:** profitability = ROE = IB / one-year-lagged book equity (book equity per the SE/CE/PSTK/TXDITC hierarchy, positive book equity required); investment = annual change in book equity / prior-year book equity (§Data). Reported as robustness (Table 3 Panel B, Table 4 Panel B, Table 5 Panel B).
- **Long entry:** buy the equally weighted average of the **HH** (high profitability, high investment) and **LL** (low profitability, low investment) cells, **one dollar total** (half a dollar each — Note 5 states each leg is scaled by ½ so the strategy is $1 long and $1 short).
- **Short entry:** short the equally weighted average of the **HL** and **LH** cells, one dollar total (source Equation 8; Table 4 Notes).
- **Portfolio weighting:** **value-weighted** (Table 3 Notes: "Portfolio returns are value-weighted"; §Conclusion: "value-weighted WC factor").
- **Holding period / rebalance cadence:** annual formation each June (i.e. a 12-month holding window implied by the stated Fama–French 1993 procedure) with **monthly return measurement**; the exact exit/re-entry convention and treatment of a stock that drops out mid-year are `underspecified`.
- **Parameters (all source-fixed, none tuned by the Scout):** 3 groups per dimension; NYSE 30/70 breakpoints; 3×3 = 9 cells; `½/½` leg scaling; annual June rebalance; value weights; asset-based variables primary, equity-based variables robustness.
- **Continuous-signal variant:** the source notes (§Transaction Costs and Capacity) that "broader implementations — for example, using the continuous interaction signal to form wider portfolios — would spread trades across a larger fraction of the market", but **does not specify** that construction (no ranking rule, no breadth, no weight function) → `underspecified`, any operationalization would be `research-proposed`.
- **Cross-sectional regression signal (tests, not a trade rule):** Fama–MacBeth regressions of monthly returns on profitability, investment, their interaction, book-to-market and standard controls (accruals, short-term reversal `Ret1`, medium-term momentum `Ret2,13`, five-year rolling CAPM beta) using quarterly data lagged ≥4 months (§Data; §The WC Effect in Stock Returns). Not itself tradable as specified.
- **Overall reproducibility judgement:** the *portfolio* construction is reconstructable from the source (variables, breakpoints, timing, weights, leg scaling all stated). Missing for exact reconstruction: exact execution day, mid-year handling of dropping names, universe liquidity/price filters beyond the SIC-6 exclusion, microcap treatment inside the sorts, and the provider of the benchmark factor series → the record is **reconstructable-with-gaps**, not fully reproducible.

## Execution assumptions

### Source-reported

- **Signal-to-order timing:** June annual formation; monthly rebalancing of returns measured thereafter. Exact execution date, same-bar/next-bar treatment, market vs. limit orders: `underspecified`.
- **Turnover definition (Note 10):** annual one-way turnover = **one half of the sum of absolute changes in value weights across consecutive June rebalancing dates**. Measured turnover: **WC ≈ 70% annual one-way (both specifications)** vs. HML 38%, RMW 48%, CMA 63%, momentum 332% (§Transaction Costs and Capacity).
- **Cost model (Note 10, quoted in substance):** monthly trading cost = **1/12 × Σ over the four portfolio legs of (leg turnover × round-trip effective spread)**, where round-trip effective spread = twice the half-spread from the 19/31/57 bps NYSE size grid, weighted by each leg's share in the factor. Result: **asset-based FF5 alpha gross 25.4 bps/month → 5.1 bps/month estimated costs → net 20.3 bps/month**; **q5 alpha net of costs 25.5 bps/month** (§Transaction Costs and Capacity).
- **Break-even (Note 10):** `c = 12·s / Σ TO_ℓ` where `s` = gross monthly alpha, `TO_ℓ` = annual one-way turnover of leg ℓ; reported **break-even one-way cost = 109 bps** for the five-factor alpha, which the source describes as "well above the effective half-spreads of even the smallest stocks in the portfolio".
- **Capacity (Note 10, Novy-Marx & Velikov 2016 §5.2):** `D = (α × s_net × ME) / (ε × TO)` with **ε = 6.53** price elasticity of supply; reported **≈ $4.1 billion AUM at which price impact fully erodes the net alpha**, binding on the **short side**; the factor spans **≈1,820 stocks**; **long-leg (HH only) capacity ≈ $29 billion** with average HH market cap **$6.3 billion per firm**. Comparison capacities quoted from Novy-Marx & Velikov (2016, Table 7): investment factor $7.4B, asset growth $5.6B, value $20.7B, momentum $5.2B.
- **Explicitly omitted / absent from the source:** no commission model, no slippage model beyond the static half-spread grid, no market-impact model inside the return series (impact appears only in the capacity formula), no borrow/stock-loan fee, no short-availability or borrow-constraint treatment, no margin/leverage model, no fill/partial-fill model, no latency assumption, no participation/ADV cap in the return computation, no funding term. Each is `not stated in source` → `data gap`, **never to be read as zero**.

### Research interpretation (Scout-labelled)

- Any live implementation would need an execution date rule, a borrow-feasibility screen and a contemporaneous spread/impact estimate; none of these are supplied by the source, so **every "net" number above is a source-modeled cost estimate, not an executable net-of-frictions result** (`research-proposed` if later operationalized).
- The fixed 2007–2016 spread grid applied to a 1963–2024 sample is a source design choice (Note 10) that this record flags as a **cost-model vintage mismatch** — a falsifiable concern, not an error claim.

### Negative evidence

- **Decade-scale dormancy inside the sample:** the 60-month rolling FF5 alpha **"hovers near zero" during the 2010s** and is **"not always statistically significant"** (§Evolution, Figure 1) — roughly a decade in which the factor would have looked dead to an investor.
- **Microcap dependence cuts both ways:** the asset-based interaction is **insignificant among microcaps alone (t = 1.07, Table 2 column 3)**, yet microcaps are exactly where a value-weighted 3×3 sort's cheap liquidity sits; excluding microcaps *strengthens* the effect (3.75, t = 2.63), so the result is not an illiquidity artefact but neither is it demonstrated in the small-cap tail where costs are highest.
- **Value-group fragility:** in the high book-to-market group the HH cell alpha is **13 bps with t = 1.02 (insignificant)** and the high–low investment spread among profitable firms is only **24 bps (t = 1.65)** (Table A7 discussion) — the "conditional investment" story is far weaker inside value stocks than the headline.
- **The long leg is half-empty:** LL's FF5 alpha is **−0.16 (t = −1.81)** in Panel A and **−0.015** in Panel B (Table 5), and the equity-based **short basket alpha is insignificant (−0.116, t = −1.56)** — one leg of each side carries little or no alpha in one of the two specifications, so the "long HH+LL, short HL+LH" construction is not symmetric in its evidence.
- **Cost/capacity reality check:** even on the source's own model, **capacity ≈ $4.1B with the short side binding**, and the entire net result rests on a **fixed 2007–2016 half-spread grid (19/31/57 bps) applied to 1963–2024** (Note 10); no commissions, no stock-loan fees, no impact inside the return series, no fill model. Sizing above $4.1B, or short-leg borrow costs, are unmodeled.
- **In-sample optimization:** the tangency Sharpe improvements (Table 7, Figure 2) use **sample moments** (Note 9) over the same 1963–2024 window the factor was evaluated on — no holdout, no walk-forward, no out-of-sample window anywhere in the paper.
- **Conflict of interest (§Disclosure Statement):** Applied Finance commercializes variations of these approaches; two authors are partners and the third consults for that firm.
- **Adverse independent literature already in this repository:** `published-equity-anomaly-zoo-large-cap-post-2005-luck-adjusted-null-arxiv-2607.06502-2026-09-23.md` (Chen & Welch, *What Useful Alphas?*) reports a **7 bp median** and a **luck-adjusted null** for published anomalies in the investable large-cap universe **post-2005**, with post-publication decay of roughly half — exactly the subsample in which this factor's rolling alpha is weakest; `reviving-anomalies-expected-net-return-double-sort-1n-implementable-2026-09-23.md` shows that when **scale-dependent price impact** is subtracted from published anomaly signals, net performance degrades sharply. Neither paper tests the WC factor specifically — they are contrary *class-level* evidence, not a refutation of this source.
- **No multiplicity control:** the paper reports CAPM/FF3/FF5/FF6/q5 × asset/equity × portfolio/regression/tangency specifications without a multiple-testing adjustment; the near-threshold cells (t = 1.97, t = 1.02, t = 1.65) sit inside that garden of forking paths.
- **Publication-selection concern:** an interaction term that "works" in the same 61-year sample used to motivate it, in a journal article whose authors have a commercial interest, should be treated as hypothesis-generating until out-of-sample replication.

## Falsification plan

Every threshold below is **`research-defined` (Scout-chosen)**, and every operational rule not stated by the source is **`research-proposed`**. None of these tests has been run.

- **F1 — Honest cost ladder (`research-proposed` execution, `research-defined` threshold):** rebuild the factor point-in-time and charge 0/5/10/20/30 bps one-way plus a 20%-of-ADV participation cap and a 100 bps/yr stock-loan fee on the short leg. **Fail** if net FF5 alpha ≤ 0 at 10 bps one-way, or if the net alpha at 20 bps falls below **50%** of the reported 25.4 bps gross.
- **F2 — Point-in-time/look-ahead audit (`research-defined`):** recompute all June sorts using only data publicly available by the formation date (filing lags respected, not just the 4-month quarterly rule). **Fail** if the FF5 alpha shrinks by more than **50%** or loses significance at t < 2.0.
- **F3 — Regime split (`research-defined`):** 1963–1990 / 1991–2005 / 2006–2024 subperiod FF5 alphas. **Fail** if the alpha is ≤ 0 in **≥ 2 of 3** subperiods, or if the post-2005 subperiod net alpha ≤ 0 (this directly confronts the Chen–Welch class-level decay evidence held in this repository).
- **F4 — Microcap/liquidity transport (`research-defined`):** repeat F1 on the "excluding microcaps" universe (the source's own stronger specification) and on an NYSE-only universe. **Fail** if the interaction or the long-short spread is insignificant (t < 2.0) in **both**.
- **F5 — Turnover verification (`research-defined`):** compute realized annual one-way turnover from the rebuilt value weights. **Fail** if realized turnover exceeds the reported **≈70%** by more than **30% relative** (i.e. > ≈91%), since alpha scales linearly against the cost model through this input.
- **F6 — Leg ablation (`research-defined`):** compare full WC vs. HH-long-only vs. LH-short-only vs. LL/HL-only baskets. **Fail** the *factor* claim if HH+LH alone explains **≥ 90%** of the FF5 alpha (the record's interpretation is that the source already points this way), and fail the *long-short* claim if the short leg cannot be borrowed for the tested names.
- **F7 — Placebo (`research-defined`):** 1,000 draws of shuffled profitability labels within NYSE size deciles, re-running the 3×3 sort each time. **Fail** if the observed WC spread does not exceed the **95th percentile** of the placebo distribution.
- **F8 — Definition robustness (`research-defined`):** asset-based vs. equity-based vs. at least one alternative profitability (e.g. ROA) and investment (e.g. capex+Δinventory scaling) definition. **Fail** if the interaction sign flips or becomes insignificant (t < 1.96) in more than **one** variant.
- **F9 — Competing-signal control (`research-proposed`):** re-run the cross-sectional regression with value, momentum, size, accruals and an expected-investment factor jointly included. **Fail** if the interaction t-stat falls below **1.96** or the coefficient shrinks by > **50%**.
- **F10 — Multiple-testing control (`research-defined`):** Benjamini–Hochberg at q < 0.10 over the full reported specification grid (models × panels × portfolio/regression/tangency). **Fail** if the WC interaction does not survive.
- **F11 — Frozen forward window (`research-defined`):** extend the sample past 2024 with data that accrues after a frozen cutoff, evaluating monthly. **Fail** if the trailing **5-year** net FF5 alpha ≤ 0 or the interaction coefficient is insignificant at 5%.
- **F12 — Cost-vintage sensitivity (`research-proposed`):** replace the fixed 2007–2016 half-spread grid with a rolling contemporaneous spread estimate. **Fail** if net alpha at the reported turnover falls below **10 bps/month** under the contemporaneous grid.
- **Action on failure:** any failed test downgrades the record's `confidence` and blocks promotion beyond `research-only`; F1/F2/F3 failures are treated as mechanism-level refutations, not parameter-tuning invitations (no unconstrained retuning is allowed to rescue the claim).

## Crypto portability

**unproven.**

- The mechanism and every empirical number come from **US listed equities with audited accounting statements**; crypto issuers publish no Compustat-equivalent profitability/investment accounting on a comparable basis, so the primary signal (cash operating profitability over lagged assets × asset growth) **has no direct data analogue** in crypto spot or perpetual markets.
- Structural porting would require a `research-proposed` redefinition (e.g. exchange/protocol revenue × treasury or token supply growth), which changes the mechanism's data dependency and is therefore not evidence for the original claim.
- Crypto-specific frictions are entirely absent from the source: **funding, mark/index price, liquidation, 24/7 sessions, venue fragmentation, custody/withdrawal risk, borrow/short availability on tokens, and stablecoin/quote-currency effects** — none appear anywhere in the paper, including its cost model.
- The short leg (a real constraint even in US equities, where capacity binds at ≈$4.1B on the short side) would face token-borrow availability and fee structures the source never models.
