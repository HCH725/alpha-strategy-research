## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

QuantaAlpha's core contribution is a controlled evolutionary factor-mining workflow: it represents an end-to-end research run as a trajectory of hypothesis generation, factor expression, code implementation, and backtest evaluation; targeted mutation revises weak trajectory steps, while crossover recombines useful sub-trajectories from higher-reward parents. During factor generation it applies hypothesis/expression/code semantic-consistency checks and controls factor complexity and redundancy. The method is a research-and-discovery process; a selected factor plus an explicitly defined portfolio process is needed before it becomes a trading strategy.

### Research interpretation

The source-reviewed Wiki record associates this family with an institutional price-volume correlation and intraday accumulation factor. It records the expression:

```text
RANK(TS_CORR(DELTA($close,1)/$close,
             DELTA($volume,1)/$volume,20)
     * TS_MEAN(($close-$open)/$close,5))
```

This can be interpreted as combining a rolling 20-observation correlation between close-price and volume changes with a five-observation mean intraday return term, then ranking cross-sectionally. The formula is retained from that Wiki provenance. The paper-level mechanism does not make the legacy `signal_rule_version: 1` parameters sufficient to prove it was the implemented expression for these survivor runs.

## Signal

The source paper's general factor-generation setup uses daily open, high, low, close, volume, and VWAP features to predict next-day returns, with target `y_t = P_(t+2)^close / P_(t+1)^close - 1`, and applies cross-sectional rank normalization to features and labels. Its portfolio evaluation uses a TopkDropout construction. The Wiki record's reported candidate factor is the expression above; it is not a self-contained single-symbol entry/exit rule.

Unavailable or not established for the legacy runs: exact factor code/hash, warmup and missing-value behavior, source price alignment, threshold/entry mapping from ranked scores, direction and conflict handling, per-cohort rebalance schedule, and any source-to-Qlib event mapping. These are not inferred from the survivor label or score.

## Required data

- For the paper's reported factor-mining experiments: daily equity OHLCV and VWAP features, cross-sectional universe membership and labels, with the stated next-day-return target and cross-sectional preprocessing.
- For the Wiki-recorded candidate expression: time series of close, open, and volume sufficient for the stated 20- and 5-observation operations, plus a cross-sectional universe for ranking.
- Paper experimental universes include CSI 300, CSI 500, and S&P 500 transfer evaluations. They are not the crypto symbols in the historical survivor evidence.
- Exact legacy Qlib source data, point-in-time universe, signal arrays, and execution inputs are not established by the available baselines.

## Execution assumptions

- The paper's portfolio process is TopkDropout: each trading day rank stocks by predicted scores, liquidate the five lowest-scoring current holdings, and replace them with the highest-ranked candidates to maintain 50 equal-weight holdings. Execution uses the next-day open; a 9.5% price-limit threshold is specified. Reported transaction costs are 0.05% buy and 0.15% sell (commission plus stamp duty), or 0.20% round-trip.
- This is cross-sectional portfolio construction, not one independent pair trade. Its next-day-open rebalance and portfolio membership cannot be represented by current single-pair candle execution without changing the strategy.
- The legacy baseline DCA fields are historic execution parameters only; they do not establish that the source paper used DCA or that those settings are source-native.
- Source-to-run fill timing, sizing, fees, and funding are not proven equivalent. No missing rule is supplied here.

## Evidence

### Source-reported

The v3 abstract and main-results discussion agree that, on CSI 300, the GPT-5.2 QuantaAlpha factor-pool evaluation reports IC `0.0472`, ARR `4.68%`, and MDD `11.8%`; the paper also reports four-year cumulative excess returns of about `40.28%` and `19.1%` on CSI 500 and S&P 500 transfer evaluations. The authors describe roughly 150 validated factors evaluated through the same downstream LightGBM model, so these are factor-pool/framework results—not results for this one Wiki-associated factor or the legacy crypto survivor runs. They were not independently reproduced here.
