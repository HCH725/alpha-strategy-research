# Self-Consistent Adjoint Policy Iteration for Constrained Dynamic Portfolio Choice: Shifted-Adjoint Cancellation, Factorized HJB Verification, and Multi-Asset QP Decoding

## Provenance

- **Primary Source:** Jeonggyu Huh, Yeoneung Kim, and Seungwon Jeong (Sungkyunkwan University - SKKU, Machine Learning in Financial Engineering Lab), *"Self-Consistent Adjoint Policy Iteration for Constrained Dynamic Portfolio Choice"*, arXiv preprint `arXiv:2608.17808v1 [q-fin.CP, q-fin.PM, math.OC]`, submitted August 18, 2026. DOI: [10.48550/arXiv.2608.17808](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2608.17808). Stable URL: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17808](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17808).
- **Primary Subject Areas:** Computational Finance (`q-fin.CP`), Portfolio Management (`q-fin.PM`), Optimization and Control (`math.OC`).
- **Research Scope:** In quantitative portfolio management, asset returns exhibit time-varying predictability driven by macroeconomic and market factors (e.g., interest rate spreads, valuation ratios, momentum). Optimal multi-period continuous-time allocation under predictable returns requires solving Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equations or Pontryagin Maximum Principle (PMP) backward systems. However, real-world institutional portfolios operate under strict convex constraints (no-short sales, leverage limits, and asset-specific concentration caps) across large asset universes ($K \ge 50$). High-dimensional state-space PDE solvers break down completely due to the curse of dimensionality ($5.21 \times 10^{10}$ grid points for 5 factors and 50 assets on a modest 41-node grid). While deep reinforcement learning and neural policy networks can handle high dimensions, standard policy gradient methods suffer from high variance, lack convergence certificates, and fail to respect active constraint boundaries. Huh, Kim, and Jeong develop a simulation-based policy iteration method that bridges stochastic maximum principle adjoints with HJB dynamic programming. They prove that re-evaluating a fixed-latent Open-Loop Backpropagation Through Time (OL-BPTT) adjoint field under the deployed policy achieves exact shifted-adjoint cancellation, provably bounding the Hamiltonian-gradient error by the policy-improvement residual and enabling explicit quadratic programming (QP) recovery of large-scale constrained asset allocations.

## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

1. **Hedging Demand under Predictable Returns vs. Myopic Allocation:** Under Merton (1971) constant investment opportunities, optimal allocations are purely myopic and policy independent. When returns are predictable (driven by mean-reverting Ornstein-Uhlenbeck factor processes), optimal portfolio choice decomposes into myopic demand plus intertemporal hedging demand. The hedging demand depends non-linearly on the continuation value of the investor's current wealth and factor state.
2. **Shifted-Adjoint Cancellation:** In standard PMP implementations, the second-order adjoint $P^u$ (the Hessian of the costate) typically diverges from the true value function Hessian $\Gamma^u = \mathrm{D}_{ss}^2 V^u$. Huh, Kim, and Jeong discover that under a shifted input formulation, the second-adjoint discrepancy $(P^u - \Gamma^u)$ is multiplied exclusively by the diffusion displacement $(\sigma(a) - \sigma(u))$ generated by the trial action $a$. At the current policy $a = u(t, s)$, this second-adjoint discrepancy cancels completely.
3. **Residual-Scaled Discrepancy Decay:** On a regular future tube, the discrepancy between the adjoint-based Hamiltonian gradient and the true HJB Hamiltonian gradient decays proportionally to the policy-improvement residual $\mathfrak{r}_\mathcal{D}(u)$. Consequently, any fixed point of the HJB policy-improvement operator is also a fixed point of the population adjoint operator.
4. **Homothetic Decoupling under CRRA Utility:** For Constant Relative Risk Aversion (CRRA) preferences, wealth homogeneity separates the wealth coordinate from the factor dynamics. The represented Hamiltonian reduces to a low-dimensional factor hedging field, allowing the multi-asset action block to be decoded via a fast, deterministic, convex quadratic program (QP).
5. **Active-Boundary Recovery via Pre-Projection Averaging:** Projecting actions onto box constraints during intermediate interpolation creates non-smooth "staircase" artifacts along switching boundaries. Averaging the unconstrained pre-projection adjoint field across simulation rollouts and executing projection onto the constraint polytope only after spatial interpolation preserves the exact geometry of active constraints.

### Research interpretation

The falsifiable thesis is that **in multi-asset dynamic portfolio choice with predictable return factors and convex trading bounds, re-evaluating the low-dimensional adjoint hedging field under the current deployed policy achieves significant policy RMSE and certainty-equivalent improvements ($>35\%$) over matched-budget pooled refinement, while pre-projection spatial averaging reconstructs active constraint boundaries with near-zero error ($<0.01\%$)**:
- Re-evaluation succeeds because changing the portfolio alters the pathwise distribution of wealth and the realized investment opportunities, generating new policy-dependent hedging information that repeated sampling from an outdated policy cannot supply.
- For static Merton benchmarks with constant parameters, re-evaluation offers zero benefit (acting as a negative control), confirming that the algorithmic value of iteration is strictly tied to time-varying return predictability.

## Signal

### 1. Market Dynamics and State Equations

Let $S_t = (X_t, Z_t) \in \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}^{d_z}$ denote the state vector, where $X_t > 0$ is the investor's wealth and $Z_t$ is a $d_z$-dimensional vector of predictable factor processes.
Wealth and factor dynamics evolve under filtered probability space $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, (\mathcal{F}_t)_{t \in [0,T]}, \mathbb{P})$:
$$dX_t^u = X_t^u \left[ (r + u_t^\top \mu(Z_t)) dt + u_t^\top \Sigma^{1/2} dB_t^R \right]$$
$$dZ_t = K_Z (\bar{Z} - Z_t) dt + \Sigma_Z^{1/2} dB_t^Z$$
where $B_t = (B_t^R, B_t^Z)$ is a standard Brownian motion with instantaneous cross-correlation $d\langle B^R, B^Z \rangle_t = \rho dt$.
The action $u_t = u(t, Z_t) \in \mathcal{K} \subset \mathbb{R}^K$ represents portfolio weights in $K$ risky assets, subject to closed convex constraints $\mathcal{K}$ (e.g., $u_i \in [u_{\min, i}, u_{\max, i}]$).

### 2. Investor Objective & Homothetic Reduction

The investor maximizes terminal CRRA utility:
$$\sup_{u \in \mathcal{U}} \mathbb{E}\left[ \frac{(X_T^u)^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma} \right], \quad \gamma > 0, \gamma \neq 1$$
By homotheticity, the value function factors as $V^u(t, x, z) = \frac{x^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma} F^u(t, z)$, where $F^u(t, z)$ is the reduced factor continuation value with terminal condition $F^u(T, z) = 1$.
The exact HJB policy-improvement operator maximizes the normalized quadratic Hamiltonian:
$$\mathcal{T}(u)(t, z) = \arg\max_{a \in \mathcal{K}} \left\{ a^\top \mu(z) - \frac{\gamma}{2} a^\top \Sigma a + \frac{1}{F^u(t, z)} a^\top \Sigma^{1/2} \rho \Sigma_Z^{1/2} \nabla_z F^u(t, z) \right\}$$
The first two terms represent myopic mean-variance allocation; the third term represents intertemporal hedging demand driven by the factor gradient $\nabla_z F^u$.

### 3. Simulation-Based Adjoint Policy Iteration (OL-BPTT)

The algorithm executes the following sequence:
1. **Rollout Simulation:** Simulate $M$ trajectories of factor states $Z_t$ and adjoint costates under current feedback $u^k$ using Girsanov-tilted sampling (shifting factor drift by $\chi C^\top u$ with $\chi = 1-\gamma$).
2. **Reverse Adjoint Acquisition (OL-BPTT):** Compute the normalized factor adjoint field $\hat{r}(t, z) \approx \frac{\nabla_z F^{u^k}(t, z)}{F^{u^k}(t, z)}$ in a single backward pass through the trajectory bank.
3. **Structured Sieve Representation:** Fit the adjoint field across time knots using dense non-linear sieve features (e.g., fixed tanh basis with ridge regression).
4. **Explicit QP Action Decoding:** At any live decision state $(t, z)$, solve the $K$-dimensional convex quadratic program:
   $$a^*(t, z) = \arg\min_{a \in \mathcal{K}} \left\{ \frac{1}{2} a^\top (\gamma \Sigma) a - a^\top \left( \mu(z) + \Sigma^{1/2} \rho \Sigma_Z^{1/2} \hat{r}(t, z) \right) \right\}$$
5. **Damped Policy Update:** Form the updated policy using conservative interpolation:
   $$u^{k+1}(t, z) = (1 - \beta) u^k(t, z) + \beta a^*(t, z), \quad \beta \in (0, 1] \text{ (typically } \beta = 0.5\text{)}$$
   Pre-projection spatial averaging is enforced across simulation knots before applying the metric projection $\Pi_\mathcal{K}$.

## Required data

- **Asset Universe:** Multi-asset baskets of equities, bonds, commodities, or liquid crypto tokens ($K = 50$ assets in the benchmark).
- **Predictive Factors ($d_z \in \{1, 3, 5\}$):** Macroeconomic and market state indicators (e.g., dividend yield, default spread, term spread, short rate, momentum factors) modeled as mean-reverting diffusion processes.
- **Microstructure & Factor Inputs:**
  - Asset excess drift affine mapping $\mu(z) = \mu_0 + \mu_1 z$.
  - Asset covariance matrix $\Sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times K}$ (positive definite).
  - Factor mean-reversion matrix $K_Z$, long-run mean $\bar{Z}$, and factor volatility $\Sigma_Z$.
  - Factor-asset Brownian correlation matrix $\rho \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times d_z}$.
- **Timeframe & Resolution:** Investment horizon $T = 1.0$ year; time discretization $N_t = 64\text{--}128$ steps; daily to multi-day decision frequency.

## Execution assumptions

- **Action Bounds:** Heterogeneous asset-wise box constraints $u_i \in [0, u_{\max, i}]$ (no short-selling, with diversified individual caps).
- **Rebalancing Mechanics:** Continuous or discrete multi-period state feedback rebalancing evaluated without market impact in the core formulation.
- **Sampling Law:** Girsanov-tilted Monte Carlo simulation with antithetic path pairs ($M = 65,536\text{--}131,072$ paths).
- **Anchor Design-Point Mixture:** $50\text{--}50$ mixture of transient on-policy paths and broad stationary factor samples (scaled to $1.8$ standard deviations) to ensure robust boundary identification.

## Evidence

### Source-reported

All quantitative results below are directly cited from Huh, Kim, and Jeong (arXiv:2608.17808v1, August 18, 2026), evaluated across rigorous numerical benchmarks:

1. **Merton Negative Control (100 Assets, No Shorting):**
   - In the unconstrained or metric-projected Merton benchmark with constant coefficients, the policy-improvement operator is policy independent.
   - Concentrating the computational budget on a single policy update yields policy RMSE **$6.00 \times 10^{-4}$**.
   - Splitting the identical budget across current-policy iterations increases policy RMSE to **$1.62 \times 10^{-3}$**, demonstrating that iteration provides zero benefit without return predictability.
2. **Predictable-Return Mechanism (Scalar Ornstein-Uhlenbeck Predictor):**
   - Starting from the myopic policy, initial policy RMSE is **$5.99 \times 10^{-2}$**.
   - A single policy update reduces RMSE to **$2.67 \times 10^{-3}$**.
   - Re-evaluating the normalized adjoint field under the updated rollout policy achieves RMSE **$6.58 \times 10^{-4}$** (a **$75.4\%$ error reduction** over one-shot).
   - In contrast, concentrating the matched budget (8 operator evaluations) at the initial policy leaves RMSE stagnant at **$2.68 \times 10^{-3}$**, proving that re-evaluation acquires genuinely new policy-dependent hedging information.
3. **Active-Set Boundary Reconstruction (Table 3 & Figure 2):**
   - In the constrained one-factor benchmark with $u_t \in [-2.5, 2.5]$, averaging the rollout policy directly produces an active-set boundary error of **$4.85\%$**.
   - Averaging the pre-projection adjoint field and executing projection onto constraints only after spatial interpolation collapses the active-set boundary error to **$0.007\%$** at $N_y = 81$ grid points.
4. **Large-Scale Constrained Benchmark (3 Factors, 50 Assets, Heterogeneous Bounds):**
   - Evaluated under the $50\text{--}50$ on-policy/broad design mixture with tilted OL-BPTT:
   - *Policy RMSE Reduction:* Current-policy re-evaluation achieves on-policy RMSE of **$2.775 \times 10^{-3}$**, compared to **$4.453 \times 10^{-3}$** for matched-budget pooled refinement—a **$37.69\%$ reduction** (winning in all 3 random seeds). Relative to one-shot OL-BPTT ($4.550 \times 10^{-3}$), the reduction is **$39.01\%$**.
   - *Certainty Equivalent Gap:* Log certainty-equivalent gap drops from **$1.72 \times 10^{-4}$** (pooled initial) to **$6.99 \times 10^{-5}$** (current-policy re-evaluation), winning in all 3 seeds.
   - *Design Mixture Impact:* Increasing the broad stationary fraction from $1/6$ to $1/2$ reduces broad RMSE from $7.958 \times 10^{-3}$ to $4.708 \times 10^{-3}$ (**$41\%$ reduction**) and tail-only RMSE from $1.084 \times 10^{-2}$ to $6.113 \times 10^{-3}$ (**$44\%$ reduction**).
5. **Theorem-Matched Occupation-Measure Relative-Error Audit:**
   - Over 180 audited path banks across 4 population iterates, the largest per-bank $95\%$ upper confidence endpoint of the relative error ratio $\widehat{\kappa}_{\text{occ}}$ is **$0.073936$**, sitting nearly an order of magnitude below the theoretical half-step convergence threshold ($\kappa < 0.75$).

### Negative evidence

- **Representation-Limited Sieve Saturation:** When factor dynamics are observed through non-linear coordinate transforms, fitting affine or low-order quadratic sieves saturates at an irreducible representation error; additional policy iterations cannot repair structural feature misspecification (Table 5).
- **Richardson Extrapolation Inefficiency in High Dimensions:** While Richardson time-extrapolation reduces discretization bias from $1.30 \times 10^{-4}$ to $2.36 \times 10^{-5}$ in low-dimensional grids, it degrades efficiency in 50-asset multifactor settings where regression noise dominates time discretization error. Standard single-grid $N_t = 64$ OL-BPTT is strictly superior.
- **Tail-Only Error Tradeoff:** While current-policy re-evaluation dominates on-policy and broad evaluation metrics, matched pooled initial refinement achieved lower error strictly in the extreme tail domain ($> 2.2$ standard deviations), as current-policy rollouts naturally concentrate probability mass on the induced ergodic path.

## Falsification plan

1. **Stationary Factor Predictability Null Test:** Set the factor-return predictive coupling $\mu_1 = 0$ while retaining factor volatility. If the algorithm continues to trigger re-evaluations that alter portfolio weights beyond Monte Carlo sampling variance ($\Delta u > 1.0\%$), the policy-iteration mechanism is falsified as capturing spurious statistical noise.
2. **Execution Latency & Discrete Rebalance Stress:** Implement execution delay $\tau \in [1, 5]$ days between factor state observation and multi-asset QP execution. If latency degrades certainty-equivalent wealth below a static buy-and-hold benchmark, the continuous-time feedback assumption is falsified for implementation.
3. **Severe Non-Gaussian Jump Innovations:** Subject factor processes $Z_t$ to Merton compound Poisson jumps ($\lambda_{\text{jump}} = 2/\text{year}$, jump size $\sigma_J = 3\sigma_Z$). If jump-induced factor gaps push empirical relative error $\widehat{\kappa}_{\text{occ}}$ above the $0.75$ threshold, the diffusion-based shifted-adjoint cancellation is falsified.
4. **Rejection Threshold:** Falsify and reject the policy if on-policy RMSE fails to beat matched pooled initial refinement by at least $15\%$ across 5 independent simulation seeds, or if QP solver status fails to converge within $100$ ms.

## Crypto portability

- **Portability Classification:** `adapted` / `unproven`.
- **Crypto Multi-Asset Baskets:** Highly relevant for managing cross-sectional portfolios of 20–50 crypto perpetual tokens under heterogeneous position limits and leverage caps.
- **Factor Predictability in Crypto:** Common crypto predictive factors include cross-sectional funding rate spreads, perpetual basis drift, stablecoin supply growth, and Bitcoin dominance momentum.
- **Constraint Handling in Crypto Perp Desks:** The explicit QP decoding step perfectly accommodates margin constraints, exchange position limits, and collateral concentration caps without training separate constraint-penalty networks.
- **Non-Stationary Crypto Volatility Regimes:** Crypto factor volatility $\Sigma_Z$ undergoes extreme structural shifts; assuming constant Ornstein-Uhlenbeck parameters over a 1-year horizon without online regime switching (e.g., Markov switching OU) will cause severe model misspecification.

## Limitations

- **Diffusion Continuity Assumption:** The theoretical shifted-adjoint cancellation and HJB consistency bounds assume continuous Itô diffusions; discrete price jumps and gap openings violate the vector Feynman–Kac representation.
- **Known Factor Model Structure:** The derivation assumes the investor knows or has accurately pre-estimated factor drift $\mu(z)$ and covariance matrices $(\Sigma, \Sigma_Z, \rho)$; parameter estimation risk is not modeled.
- **Frictionless Market Execution:** Transaction costs, bid-ask spread crossing, and price impact are omitted from the core QP objective.
- **Computational Overhead of Simulation Sieve:** Generating $65,536$ Girsanov-tilted trajectories at each outer policy iteration requires dedicated GPU/multicore hardware, making sub-minute intraday rebalancing computationally challenging.
