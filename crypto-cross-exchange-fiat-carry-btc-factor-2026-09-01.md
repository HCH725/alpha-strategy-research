# Crypto Cross-Exchange Fiat Carry BTC Factor (CarryBtc)

## Provenance

- **Primary source:** Nicola Borri and Kirill Shakhnov, "The Cross-Section of Cryptocurrency Returns", *The Review of Asset Pricing Studies*, Volume 12, Issue 3 (September 2022), Pages 667–705.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1093/rapstu/raac007
- **Sample period:** May 26, 2015 to May 25, 2021.
- **Empirical universe:** Baseline sample of up to 68 fiat-to-bitcoin currency pairs traded across 29 reputable cryptocurrency exchanges (from an initial universe of 258 pairs), accounting for withdrawal fees, trading fees, and bid-ask spreads.
- **Factor construction:** Cross-sectional sorting of fiat-crypto pairs on price level / cross-exchange basis into long-short carry portfolios (`CarryBtc`), mirroring currency carry trade architectures in traditional international finance.

## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

Borri and Shakhnov (2022) investigate why Bitcoin prices persistently differ across exchanges and fiat currency pairs (e.g. USD, EUR, KRW, JPY, GBP). While existing literature often attributes price dispersion entirely to frictions or market segmentation, the authors demonstrate an asset-pricing **risk-based explanation**:

1. **Systematic Downside Vulnerability:** Bitcoin pairs trading at higher relative prices are systematically riskier because they depreciate significantly more during market downturns, liquidity contractions, and periods of depressed investor sentiment.
2. **Compensation for Systematic Factor Exposure:** The excess returns generated by the `CarryBtc` long-short strategy represent compensation for bearing three distinct systematic cryptocurrency market risk factors:
   - **Bitcoin Liquidity Risk:** Exposure to market-wide drying up of liquidity across secondary venues.
   - **Momentum Risk:** Co-movement with broad market trend reversals.
   - **Counterparty / Exchange Aggregate Risk:** Concentration in exchanges with higher withdrawal frictions or regulatory capital controls.
3. **Pervasive Cross-Sectional Pricing:** The `CarryBtc` factor is priced in both the time series and the cross-section of cryptocurrency portfolio returns, explaining return variations that standard market beta cannot account for.

### Research interpretation

The hypothesized mechanism is a **segmented-market currency carry trade with liquidity/tail-risk premium**:
1. **Capital Mobility Frictions:** Fiat banking rails, national capital controls (e.g. South Korean FX limits causing the Kimchi premium), and exchange-specific withdrawal delays prevent frictionless instant spatial arbitrage.
2. **Carry Trade Parallel:** Analogous to FX carry trades (where high-interest currencies carry downside crash risk), high-priced fiat-crypto pairs offer apparent price discrepancies that cannot be instantly closed without bearing holding-period inventory and basis risk.
3. **Factor Extraction:** Sorting fiat-BTC pairs across venues creates a persistent cross-sectional carry portfolio that captures the risk premium paid to participants who provide cross-venue liquidity during stressed market regimes.

## Signal

Normalized source-consistent portfolio construction rule:

1. **Universe Filtering:**
   - At each daily formation timestamp $t$, identify eligible fiat-to-BTC pairs across reputable exchanges with active trading history, verifiable order books, and transparent fee schedules (baseline up to 68 pairs across 29 venues).
2. **Cross-Exchange Basis / Price Normalization:**
   - For each pair $i$ on exchange $k$, convert local fiat quote price $P_{i,k,t}$ to USD using spot foreign exchange rates $S_{i,t}$:
     $$p_{i,k,t}^{\text{USD}} = \frac{P_{i,k,t}}{S_{i,t}}$$
   - Compute relative price deviation (carry rank metric) against the cross-exchange median or reference benchmark $P_{t}^{\text{ref}}$:
     $$\Delta p_{i,k,t} = \frac{p_{i,k,t}^{\text{USD}} - P_{t}^{\text{ref}}}{P_{t}^{\text{ref}}}$$
3. **Portfolio Sorting & Weighting (`CarryBtc`):**
   - Rank pairs into portfolios based on $\Delta p_{i,k,t}$.
   - **Long Leg:** Low-price / discounted fiat-BTC pairs.
   - **Short Leg:** High-price / premium fiat-BTC pairs.
4. **Rebalancing Cadence:** Daily rebalancing net of modeled withdrawal fees and bid-ask spread crossing costs.
5. **Specification Status:** **Partially specified**. The cross-sectional sort and factor pricing are fully specified in the source; intraday execution routing, fiat currency hedging legs, and dynamic withdrawal scheduling are underspecified.

## Required data

- **Crypto Price Data:** Daily and tick-level OHLCV, top-of-book bid/ask quotes, and volume for BTC traded across multiple fiat currencies (USD, EUR, KRW, JPY, GBP, CAD, AUD, etc.) across 20+ global exchanges.
- **FX Spot Rates:** Point-in-time daily WM/Reuters or central bank exchange rates for converting local fiat pairs to USD.
- **Fee Schedules:** Venue-specific deposit, trading (maker/taker), and crypto/fiat withdrawal fees.
- **Exchange Lineage & Metadata:** Operating status, jurisdiction, regulatory capital control indicators, and banking rail status.

## Execution assumptions

- **Execution Frequency:** Daily at fixed UTC close.
- **Transaction Costs & Fees:** Explicit deduction of exchange trading fees, bid-ask spreads, and fiat/crypto transfer and withdrawal costs.
- **Hedging / Delta Neutrality:** Spot-only execution requires holding fiat balances or synthetic hedging via USD/fiat FX forwards; perpetual futures can provide delta hedging where available.
- **Capital Fragmentation:** Capital must be pre-positioned across multiple exchange accounts to avoid transfer latency.
- **Capacity:** Constrained by smaller regional exchange fiat books and banking withdrawal limits.

## Evidence

### Source-reported

Borri and Shakhnov (*The Review of Asset Pricing Studies*, 2022) report:
- **Net Daily Excess Return:** The long/short cross-strategy generates an average net excess return of **31 basis points per day** (accounting for transaction costs, withdrawal fees, and bid/ask spreads).
- **Daily Sharpe Ratio:** The strategy delivers a daily (non-annualized) Sharpe ratio of **13%** for the broad cross-strategy and **26%** for the `CarryBtc` portfolio.
- **Benchmark Comparison:** Over the same sample period, Bitcoin buy-and-hold generated a daily Sharpe ratio of ~6%, and the US equity market excess return generated a daily Sharpe ratio of ~4%.
- **Risk Attribution:** The mean return on single-exchange benchmarks (e.g. BtcKraken) is approximately half that of `CarryBtc`, while exhibiting higher volatility, leading to a lower Sharpe ratio (~9% for BtcKraken vs ~26% for `CarryBtc`).
- **Downside Risk Sensitivity:** High-price pairs exhibit significantly larger drawdown and depreciation during market-wide crashes, confirming the risk-compensation thesis.

*All figures above are source-reported from Borri & Shakhnov (2022) and have not been independently reproduced.*

### Negative evidence

- **Banking Rail & Regulatory Risk:** Significant portion of the price premium historically concentrated in venues with capital controls (e.g. South Korean KRW exchanges) or banking access friction. Arbitrageurs face non-negligible legal, regulatory, or account-freezing risks that cannot be captured in econometric models.
- **Post-2021 Market Maturation:** The expansion of institutional prime brokers (FalconX, Hidden Road), OTC desks, and USDT/USDC cross-venue settlement has materially compressed fiat price discrepancies across major global venues.
- **Fiat FX Conversion Costs:** Rebalancing fiat legs across jurisdictions involves currency conversion spreads and banking transfer delays that can erode net returns during low-volatility regimes.

## Falsification plan

The `CarryBtc` factor hypothesis should be rejected or classified as historical-only if:

1. **Modern Sample Replication (2021–2026):** In an out-of-sample test from 2021 to 2026, the net return of the `CarryBtc` long-short portfolio falls to zero or negative after factoring in modern institutional fee tiers and banking friction.
2. **Risk Factor Subsumption:** If the `CarryBtc` alpha is fully explained by simple momentum or market beta in a multi-factor Fama-MacBeth regression, disproving the independent currency carry risk premium.
3. **Execution Feasibility:** If simulated execution accounting for real-world fiat withdrawal latency (1–3 business days) and banking AML/KYC daily transfer caps eliminates profitable portfolio rebalancing.

## Crypto portability

**Direct**, as the study was designed and conducted on cryptocurrency spot exchange markets across fiat pairs.

Portability considerations:
- **Perpetual Futures Adaptation (Adapted / Unproven):** While the original study uses spot fiat pairs, a synthetic equivalent exists across crypto perpetuals with different margin currencies (e.g. USD vs USDT vs USDC perps) or cross-exchange perpetual funding spreads.
- **Stablecoin Pairs (Adapted):** Generalizes to cross-exchange USDT/USDC premium spreads (e.g. Binance vs OKX vs Coinbase).

## Limitations

- **Source-reported only:** All statistics trace to Borri & Shakhnov (2022); no independent reproduction in our internal pipeline.
- **Operational Complexity:** Requires active multi-jurisdictional fiat accounts, banking rails, and multi-venue capital allocation.
- **Regulatory / Capital Control Barriers:** High premiums frequently coincide with jurisdictions where foreign capital repatriation is legally restricted.
- **Sample Period (2015–2021):** Covers an earlier phase of crypto market structure before widespread institutional clearing and stablecoin dominance.
