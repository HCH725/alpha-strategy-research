## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The paper starts from Glosten and Milgrom: market makers post a bid and an offer, earn the half-spread, and pay adverse selection to whoever knows more (PDF p.1, section 2, reference [20] "the bid-ask spread as compensation for adverse selection"). A fill therefore splits into (i) the captured half-spread relative to the mid at fill time and (ii) the post-trade drift of the mid. The author states that on CME depth-10 futures - the companion paper [18], whose order sits at the front of the remaining queue - the two legs nearly offset: capture `+0.654` bp against drift `-0.661` bp, with net values indistinguishable from zero at each of thirteen roots (section 1, section 2); the Figure 1 caption adds that the net they imply is `-0.007` bp, indistinguishable from zero at the precision published. On the three crypto perpetual venues studied here, with the quoter simulated at the **tail of the queue**, the adverse leg exceeds the captured spread on every venue (abstract, section 5, Table 3). The mechanism the source claims is Glosten-Milgrom rather than a spread-width claim: adverse selection over-consumes the touch instead of offsetting it, so the queue-tail touch quoter's entry markout is negative on all three venues before any fee (abstract, section 1). On the venue comparison the source offers only a *majority reading across weightings* - that Hyperliquid earns a **wider** effective spread (a tick-size and depth property) rather than facing less toxic flow - and labels it as rung-dependent and "not a measured decomposition" (section 8). The paper also reports external corroboration from Albers et al. [5] (SSRN 7162966, cited, not read here), which measured the same estimand on *observed* Hyperliquid maker fills: an average maker fill of about `-0.48` bp at five seconds before rebates and `+0.32` bp for its queue-priority cohort (section 2, section 4, section 14 threat 8). All of the above is **source-reported**.

### Research interpretation

- **Hypothesized mechanism (falsifiable form):** liquidity provision / adverse selection - a passive quote posted at the best bid or offer with **no time priority** captures less half-spread than the adverse drift it absorbs, so the pre-fee entry markout is negative. Testable implication: reversing the queue assumption (front-of-queue) or adding an exit leg must be capable of flipping the sign; if it does, the finding is a queue-position artefact rather than a venue property.
- **Component roles:** `Regime: none (fixed quoting rule across every snapshot where both sides exist)`. `Signal: none - this is a cost measurement, not a predictive signal`. `Risk / exit: not present - the measurement is entry-side only`. `Cost layer: venue maker fee schedule, used only as a reference subtraction, plus a rebate/break-even sweep`.
- **What this record is *not*:** it is not an alpha signal, not a portfolio construction, and not evidence that any strategy is profitable. The economic content is a *cost* claim about standing quotes. A tradable reading of it (skip the touch, quote deeper, or demand a rebate) is our inference and is labeled `research-proposed` in the sections below.
- **Missing link stated:** the measurement never observes real maker fills and never models the exit, so a negative entry markout is *not* by itself a demonstration that the position loses money - the source says exactly this in sections 1, 5 and 14, and we adopt the qualification rather than upgrading the result.

## Signal

There is no predictive signal. The tested object is a fixed, fully specified **quoting rule plus decomposition rule** (source-specified throughout):

- **Formation timestamp:** on each venue depth snapshot where both best bid and best ask are present, a simulated maker posts 1 unit at the best bid and 1 unit at the best ask. Re-quotes occur at every snapshot but never more often than every `100 ms`, so the effective interval is `max(100 ms, venue snapshot cadence)` - about 100 ms on Bybit and about 541 ms median on Hyperliquid (section 4; Binance cadence is not printed in the shipped panels and is `data gap`).
- **Queue position and fill:** the resting order joins the back of the queue behind all size visible at that price and fills only when the aggressor on the opposite side prints at the quoted price with trade size greater than the queue ahead; fill quantity is the overflow above the queue, capped by the resting amount (section 4). Orders posted at the final snapshot persist to the end of the tape; there is no cancel (section 4).
- **Reference mid:** read by a staircase rule - the last snapshot strictly *before* the time in question, ties broken on receive timestamp, no interpolation (section 4).
- **Decomposition (Proposition 1):** with `s` the maker side (`+1` long after an aggressor sell), `m0` the mid at fill, `mtau` the mid `tau` later, `f` the fill price: `capture = s*(m0-f)/m0`, `adverse = s*(mtau-m0)/m0`, `net = s*(mtau-f)/m0`. Horizons `tau` in `{10 s, 60 s}`, headline `10 s`. Direction is taken from the exchange's aggressor flag and inverted for the maker; Section 6 audits sign inference with Lee-Ready and the tick rule. Reported quantities are in basis points, **pre-fee** (sections 4, 5).
- **Parameters:** re-quote floor `100 ms`, horizons `10 s / 60 s`, snapshot-derived fill rule - all fixed by the source, none tuned per venue (section 4). Run gaps of `100, 250, 500, 1000, 2000, 5000, 10000 ms` and depth levels are described in sections 4, 10 and Table 2.
- **Exit / holding period:** **none** - "one-sided: no exit and no exit cost" (abstract; sections 1 and 5). No exit trigger, no stop, no take-profit, no rebalance cadence exists in the source.
- **Position sizing / portfolio:** submitted size is fixed at one unit of the asset on each side, so the headline carries no sizing rule at all; the source notes that "one unit of one asset is not comparable to one unit of another", making its size-weighting true within a coin but not a value-weighted mean across coins (section 5, Table 4 note). The only pool-level variants are fill-count versus coin weighting (section 5) and the depth/rung panels of sections 10-11, which are robustness exercises rather than a sizing model.
- **Research-proposed operationalization (NOT source-specified):** any deployment that skips the touch, quotes at a depth rung, imposes a rebate threshold, or builds an inventory-aware arm follows the source's own levers (sections 10, 11) but our thresholds for adopting any of them (F5-F7 below) are `research-defined falsification thresholds`, not source numbers.
- **Reconstruction verdict:** the *measurement* rule is exact enough to rebuild from the paper plus the shipped fill-rule reference implementation. The *venue data path* (raw archives, Binance chain, snapshot cadence metadata) is not fully reconstructable from the paper -> `underspecified` for independent end-to-end reproduction.

## Execution assumptions

- **Order type:** limit orders posted at the best bid and best ask (source wording: "a simulated maker posts one unit at the best bid and one at the best ask on every snapshot, and only when both sides exist"; `limit order` appears 6 times in the paper). No market orders, no IOC/FOK logic, no cancel.
- **Fill model:** source-specified **simulated** rule (tail of queue, aggressor size must exceed visible queue ahead, overflow fill, 100 ms re-quote floor). This is a simulator, not observed fills - the source states plainly that the entire dataset is simulated fills from a queue simulator that is "never validated against an observed maker fill on any venue" and that "no shipped code produces the fills" (section 14 threat 8).
- **Latency / signal-to-order delay:** `latency` occurs twice in the pinned PDF and **both occurrences are descriptions of cited work** (the Barone/Lillo "Good, the Bad, and Latency" title and a description of prior execution research), not a latency model of this experiment -> latency cost `data gap`.
- **Slippage / spread / impact / capacity:** `slippage 0`, `commission 0`, `market impact 2` (both occurrences belong to cited work - one in a sentence about parent-order market impact, one inside a printed reference title), `capacity 0`, `turnover 0` occurrences. All of slippage, market impact, capacity and quoted-spread cost are `data gap` / not modeled - stated as a gap, not inferred.
- **Fees:** every headline number is **pre-fee**. Charging the published maker rates moves them to `-2.8387 bp` (Bybit), `-2.3912 bp` (Binance), `-1.9575 bp` (Hyperliquid), and the rebate that would restore zero is `0.8387`, `0.3912`, `0.4575 bp` respectively (section 5).
- **Funding / leverage / margin / shorting / borrow:** not modeled anywhere (`funding` appears once, as an exclusion; `leverage 0`, `margin 0`, `borrow 0`).
- **Exit and inventory:** none; section 11's quoting arm carries an inventory rule but its planned `300 s` unwind is *not* priced into the entry markout (section 11).
- **Partial fills / failures:** fill quantity is the overflow above the queue ahead, capped by the amount still resting, so realised fill size varies between zero and one coin (sections 4 and 5); an order posted at the final snapshot has no subsequent snapshot to cancel it and persists to the end of the tape, and the rule carries no cancel and no adverse-selection rejection logic (section 4). Any share of snapshots on which the rule cannot fill is `not stated in source`.
- **What the source assumes vs what we assume:** everything above is source-specified. We add no latency, no fee, no slippage and no fill assumption of our own; our only additions are the labeled thresholds in the falsification plan.

**Research-defined falsification thresholds (all thresholds below are ours, `research-defined`, not source numbers):**

- **F1 - Front-of-queue arm.** Re-run the identical decomposition with the quoter first in line at both touch prices on the same shipped panels. *Failure of our reading* (i.e., the "queue-tail kills quoting" claim collapses): pooled 10 s net `>= 0` with a two-way clustered interval excluding zero on the positive side at `>= 2 of 3` venues. *Action if failed:* reclassify the record as a queue-position artefact and drop any cost-based quote decision built on it.
