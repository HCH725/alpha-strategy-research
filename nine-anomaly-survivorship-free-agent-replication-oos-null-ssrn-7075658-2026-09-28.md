## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

- The source asks one question per paper: **"does the documented effect reproduce on clean data, and if it does, is it worth trading?"** (companion post, section "How the agent works"), while the pinned PDF phrases the same verdict as **"does the documented effect reproduce on clean, survivorship-free data?"** (section 1), with a second layer asking where the research agent itself goes wrong and what catches that error before a wrong answer ships.
- The stated empirical result (abstract, section 8): **"Three of nine papers show a statistically significant full-sample alpha; only Bali, Cakici and Whitelaw (2011) clears t > 3; on paper-faithful builds, none survives out-of-sample."** The source states plainly that "The fidelity discipline is the methodology result; the null on anomalies on clean data is the empirical result."
- The stated mechanisms behind the null (section 7, "Cross-paper observations"):
  1. **Out-of-sample decay is the rule** - the three statistically real full-sample effects all faded to insignificance over the fixed 2023-25 window, which the source reads as mirroring McLean and Pontiff (2016), "though we measure decay against a common out-of-sample date, not from each paper's publication year".
  2. **Nothing beat passive in 2023-25** - "The out-of-sample regime, a narrow mega-cap rally, was hostile to every value, low-beta, lottery, and short-leg strategy and kind to passive cap-weighted beta. The benchmark, not the signals, dominated."
  3. **Equal-weighting and micro-caps inflate anomalies** - "value-weight and screen for tradeability or you are measuring frictions, not edge", following Hou, Xue and Zhang (2020).
  4. **Verifying the build against the source paper is a gate, not an option** - the Zhu pipeline was "built from notes while the paper was paywalled, it diverged from the paper's design (a corner-sort rather than the specified reversal-within-quintile), and on a faithful rebuild it inverts to -0.95" (abstract).
  5. **Look-ahead bias is quantifiable and large** - "Geertsema-Lu puts a number on it: ~3.5 Sharpe points out-of-sample of pure illusion from one price field. Point-in-time as-traded data flips the sign of the result, not just its size."
- Per-paper economic mechanisms as the source states them (section 5): RSI bull-momentum continuation on index members; reversal *conditional on* 52-week-high proximity (the proximity is the screen, the reversal is the signal within it); lottery/low-MAX preference (stocks with a recent extreme single-day gain subsequently underperform); low-beta risk-adjusted dominance; deep-beaten-down value with a solvency overlay; a momentum signal that **reverses in down-states** of the market; persistence of consecutive top/bottom-decile membership; same-calendar-month return persistence year over year; and a low-price effect that exists only under retroactively split-adjusted prices.
- The source refuses to upgrade any of this into a trading recommendation: "This is the scientific question; it is not a trading recommendation" (section 1).

### Research interpretation

- **Hypothesis in falsifiable form (Scout framing):** on survivorship-free, point-in-time US equity data with value weighting, a micro-cap/tradeability screen, a pre-registered 2023-25 holdout and a `t > 3` multiple-testing hurdle, **a randomly chosen published equity anomaly has a low probability of showing a tradeable out-of-sample alpha**, and the residual survivors are gated by microstructure (turnover, shortability, capacity) rather than by signal.
- **Component roles (a programme, not one rule):**
  - Regime/benchmark: SPY buy-and-hold as the OOS hurdle, plus a single fixed OOS break at 2023-01.
  - Screens: point-in-time universe construction (index members, top-500 by market cap, or broad cross-section with a `$5` price floor and one-year listing minimum), a trailing dollar-volume floor and a stale-price guard.
  - Signals: the nine anomaly rules normalised in the Signal section.
  - Risk/verification layer: two-pass fidelity review (code fidelity, execution fidelity), data-integrity scan, as-traded-price discipline, IS/OOS lock, independent-engine re-run.
  - Statistical layer: CAPM alpha with t-statistics, the Harvey-Liu-Zhu `t > 3` hurdle, and an explicit family-wise statement that **no out-of-sample result is claimed significant** at 36 months.
- **Critical boundary:** five of the nine verdicts are "Reproduces (caveated)" and three are "Does not reproduce" / one "Untestable". A portfolio implementation of any of the nine is **`research-proposed` by this Scout**; the source itself states that the study "is not a trading recommendation" and that "on paper-faithful builds, none survives out-of-sample".
- **Friction assumed by the mechanisms (stated, not measured):** a broad cross-section of small caps, monthly or daily re-ranking, and short legs in four of the nine books. The source prices these only through a flat `5 bps/side` baseline plus per-strategy turnover-keyed stress (see Execution assumptions).

## Signal

All items below are **source-reported** unless explicitly marked `research-proposed`, `research-defined` or `data gap`. The nine builds follow one shared protocol (section 2, section 9b) and then differ per paper.

**Shared protocol (source-reported):** universe construction is **annual and point-in-time**; delisted names are retained so a strategy "is only ever allowed to hold names that were actually investable on the formation date"; **data gaps are logged and skipped, never substituted** ("substitution silently reintroduces survivorship bias"); level-based signals are sorted on **as-traded**, never retroactively split-adjusted, prices; broad-universe runs apply a `$5` price floor and a one-year listing minimum plus the liquidity/stale-price tradeability screen; long/short books are **gross-1.0 sleeves** (50% long + 50% short, total exposure 1x capital).

1. **RSI trend-following - Hill (2019), source section 5.1.** Formation: monthly, on **point-in-time S&P 500 membership**. Rule: hold an **equal-weight basket of every S&P 500 member whose RSI(14) pushed above 70 within the prior ~6 months** ("bull momentum"), **rebalanced monthly**. The source notes the paper itself "deliberately builds no portfolio" and reports trade-level statistics instead (2.11 profit-to-loss ratio, ~58% hit rate, Jul 1998 - Jun 2018). The exact RSI convention (Wilder smoothing period, price field), the precise meaning of "prior ~6 months" (any touch versus the latest reading) and the exit rule when a name stops qualifying are **not stated in source** -> `data gap` / `underspecified`.
2. **52-week-high reversal - Zhu, Sun & Stivers (2021), source section 5.2.** `PTH = current price / 52-week high`. Rule: **both legs inside the low-PTH quintile**, sort on **one-month raw return**, **long the recent losers and short the recent winners**, **value-weighted**, with the **52-week high measured the month before**. The source is explicit that the first build was wrong: a **blended corner-sort** (long names *near* their highs that had pulled back, short names far from theirs that had popped) that was **equal-weighted**, which produced the apparent survivor (OOS Sharpe 1.95). Quintile breakpoint convention, formation-to-execution lag and the exact 52-week window endpoints are **not stated in source** -> `data gap`.
3. **Lottery / MAX - Bali, Cakici & Whitelaw (2011), source section 5.3.** `MAX` = "a stock's single largest daily return over the prior month" (section 4). Rule: each month sort the **broad cross-section** by that value and hold the **low-MAX** names, **value-weighted** (the paper's own convention), rebalanced monthly, after the point-in-time liquidity and stale-price screen.
4. **Low-beta / BAB - Frazzini & Pedersen (2014), source section 5.4.** Rule: **sort on OLS market beta; hold the lowest-beta names inside a rolling top-500 by market cap**. The source declares every deviation from the paper: **long-only sleeve, OLS beta instead of the paper's `sigma x rho` decomposition, no Vasicek shrinkage, a decile sort instead of the median split, equal-weighting instead of rank-weighting, and no leverage**. The levered construction was **degenerate on this data: 153 of 276 months contain near-zero-beta names that lever the long leg toward near-infinite exposure**. Beta estimation window is **not stated in source** -> `data gap`.
5. **Falling knives - Arendarski (2012), source section 5.5.** Rule: screen for names **down 50% or more versus SPY over the prior 500 trading days**, then within that set favour the financially healthiest by an **independent solvency overlay**; hold **equal-weight**, rebalanced monthly. The paper's headline variant uses **Altman Z >= 2.2** (+1,722% versus SPY's +8% over 2001-2011; the best debt/equity variant returned +901%). The companion post adds that the paper specifies a **`$0.50` price floor**, which the agent initially replaced with `$5` before the dossier-card fix. The exact solvency variable and cut-off used in the rebuild are **not stated in the pinned PDF** -> `data gap`.
6. **Market-state momentum - Rodon Comas (2025), source section 5.6.** Rule: **rank on intermediate-horizon momentum**; in **up-states** hold winners-minus-losers, in **down-states** flip to losers-minus-winners, using **overlapping cohorts**; market-neutral. The deployable reported form is the **top-500** build. The momentum horizon, the down-state definition and the state filter are **not stated in source** -> `data gap` (the mechanism is therefore `underspecified` for independent reconstruction).
7. **Persistent momentum - Chen (2016), source section 5.7.** Rule: identify names that were **top-decile in both of two consecutive formation windows** (persistent winners) and **bottom-decile in both** (persistent losers); hold **winners-minus-losers**, market-neutral, top-500. The source **declares two deviations**: it proxies the paper's "top-decile in two consecutive windows" with **a count rule (>= 4 of N months, N not stated)** and it **omits the paper's one-month skip** between formation and holding. The paper's central conditioning variable, **analyst-coverage dispersion (IBES), is not in the source's data**, so the conditional claim is untested; the source names **standardised unexpected volume (SUV)** as the open substitution path and prints the paper's own Table 8 values: persistent-winners x SUV **+0.34%/mo, t = 4.5**; persistent-losers x SUV **-0.76%/mo, t = -5.9**.
8. **Same-month seasonality - Heston & Sadka (2008), source section 5.8.** Rule: each month **rank stocks by their average historical return in that same calendar month over a long lookback** (the paper-faithful variant uses **20 years**) and hold a **long/short spread** on that ranking; market-neutral. Original sample 1965-2002, NYSE/AMEX only. On a **1998 data start the 20-year variant has full 20-observation depth only from ~2015**, with earlier formations using >= 10 observations; a **5-year lookback variant was also run but is explicitly "not the paper's signal"**.
9. **Price-level look-ahead - Geertsema & Lu (2019), source section 5.9.** Not a strategy but a paired test: run the **same low-minus-high-price long/short sort twice**, once on **as-traded prices (correct)** and once on **retroactively split-adjusted prices (look-ahead)**, and compare. The source classifies it as "a methodological demonstration, not a tradeable strategy".

- **Position sizing:** value-weighted where the paper's primary specification is value-weighted (Bali, Zhu), equal-weighted where the paper specifies it (Hill, Arendarski) or for the declared BAB derivative; long/short books are gross-1.0 sleeves. No leverage, no volatility targeting and no stop rule is present in any build -> any such rule downstream is **`research-proposed`**.
- **Holding / rebalance cadence:** monthly for all nine (section 5 headings and the tradeability screen description); the companion post records that an early 500-name **daily**-rebalanced run generated orders "in the millions" before rebalancing was gated to **month-rolls and active-set changes, cutting it to about 20,000 over 26 years**.
- **Not present in the source:** entry triggers beyond the monthly sort, stops, take-profits, re-entry rules, cash management, transaction-level fill assumptions and any capacity number in dollars.

## Required data

- **Instrument / universe:** US common stocks. Three universe families, all point-in-time and refreshed annually (section 2, section 9b): **point-in-time S&P 500 members** for Hill (membership reconstructed by replaying the index add/remove event log into `[start, end)` intervals and snapshotting annually, with spot checks Tesla first eligible 2021, Meta 2014, Nvidia 2002, and delisted names retained in their membership years - Enron through 2001, Lehman through 2008, AMR through 2003); **rolling top-500 by market cap** for the momentum and low-beta papers; **broad cross-section ~3,000-3,400 names/month** with a `$5` price floor and a one-year listing minimum for breadth-dependent signals.
- **Market type / venue:** US listed cash equities; no derivatives, no crypto, no non-US market.
- **Timeframe:** **daily bars** over **1998-2025** (the source works from daily prices and a daily equity curve); portfolio formation and rebalance **monthly**; evaluation windows **in-sample 2000-22** and **out-of-sample 2023-25 (36 months)**.
- **Fields:** daily split- and dividend-adjusted closes **and** as-traded closes (both required - the look-ahead demonstration depends on their difference); shares outstanding / market cap for the top-500 screen; daily returns for `MAX` and beta; quarterly **point-in-time** fundamentals for the solvency overlay; point-in-time S&P 500 membership; a trailing dollar-volume series for the tradeability floor; the risk-free rate (period-appropriate, **~1.7%/yr in-sample, ~4.7%/yr out-of-sample**); a market excess-return series for the CAPM regressions.
- **Fields named as required but absent from the source's data:** **IBES analyst-coverage dispersion** (the Chen conditioning variable) is explicitly "not in our data"; **borrow rates, locate availability and short-sellability flags** are not listed as data inputs even though four books are long/short -> `data gap`.
- **Point-in-time:** survivorship-free by construction because delisted names are retained; the source audited this - "eventually-delisted names make up **43-88%** of held positions in the older years" - and booked losses on every name that delisted while held, which moved the Rodon broad variant's out-of-sample Sharpe only **~1.87 -> ~1.84**.
- **Missing data:** "data gaps are logged and skipped, never substituted"; an early run that hit a hard no-data rejection for a name delisted on the last trading day of 1999 was fixed by **pre-filtering the universe to names with real price history** (companion post).
- **Timestamp / timezone:** not stated in source -> `data gap`. Daily bar boundary, closing-price convention and the monthly formation cut-off are never printed.
- **Cost/fee fields:** a flat `5 bps/side` baseline plus per-strategy turnover and a break-even per-side cost (see Execution assumptions).

## Execution assumptions

Determined from a **Methods-level read of sections 1, 2, 3, 5.1-5.9, 7, 8, 9a, 9b and 9c of the pinned PDF**, plus a whole-document term scan of the extracted 48,842-character text, cross-checked against the Quantpedia companion post sections "Tradeability and costs" and "How the agent works".

- **Cost treatment (source-reported, explicit):** the consolidated scorecard is headed **"All returns net of 5 bps/side."** Section 2: "Paper-comparison results use a flat 5 bps per side. This is conservative for large-caps and optimistic for micro-caps, so every candidate is additionally stress-tested at realistic costs keyed to its turnover and universe liquidity (**commission + half-spread + borrow for short legs**), and we report each strategy's break-even cost." Section 9b repeats the same cost model. Every headline number in this record is therefore **net of a flat 5 bps/side**, not gross.
- **Cost-term scan of the pinned PDF (Scout-recomputed):** `5 bps/side` baseline stated in the scorecard caption, section 2 and section 9b; `break-even` defined as "the per-side cost that drives the out-of-sample return to zero"; `commission` appears in the cost-stress decomposition and in the companion-post statement that the broad market-state variant is "commission-impractical at realistic size"; `borrow` appears only as a short-leg cost component and as the Rodon "harsh borrow assumption"; `turnover` is reported for all eight scorecard rows with `annual one-way traded notional` as the definition; `capacity` appears in capacity notes and in the Rodon "near-zero realistic capacity" statement; `market impact` and `slippage` **do not appear as modelled terms** - the cost build is commission + half-spread + borrow, so market impact and slippage are **`data gap`, never zero**. `latency`, `fill model`, `order type`, `partial fills`, `participation` and `signal-to-order delay` return **zero occurrences** -> **`data gap`, never zero**.
- **Break-even costs (Table B, source-reported):** RSI trend **~1,300 bps**; Lottery (MAX) **~60 bps** (estimated, marked `†`); Low-beta (BAB) **~200 bps** (estimated, `†`); Market-state momentum **~8 bps**; Persistent momentum **~10 bps**; Seasonality **~35 bps** (estimated, `†`); **Falling knives and 52-week-high reversal are marked `‡` = "the strategy loses money before any costs"**.
- **Turnover (Table B, annual one-way traded notional):** RSI trend **~100%**; Lottery (MAX) **1,200%†**; Low-beta (BAB) **350%†**; Falling knives **1,000%†**; Market-state momentum **~790%**; **52-week-high reversal 2,200%†**; Persistent momentum **~1,050%**; Seasonality **1,800%†**. The `†` mark means "estimated for the reconstructed papers" - turnover is computed exactly only where per-name holdings exist (Zhu, Chen, Rodon) or derived from trade counts (Hill).
- **Order type / fill model / signal-to-order delay:** **not stated in source** -> `data gap`. Whether the monthly sort executes at the close, at the next open or at an average price is never printed.
- **Sharpe convention (section 2, section 9b):** **annualised arithmetic**; long-only Sharpes **in excess of cash**; long/short Sharpes are **zero-cost spreads with no cash deduction**; a flat low risk-free rate "would overstate every Sharpe in the current regime by ~0.3-0.5".
- **Drawdown convention:** MaxDD is peak-to-trough of the **daily** equity curve where one was persisted (SPY, Hill, Zhu, Chen, Rodon); five papers retain a **monthly-resampled** MaxDD marked `ᵐ` (Bali, Frazzini, Arendarski, Heston-Sadka, Geertsema-Lu) which "understates the true draw-down ~1.5–2.6×" and must be read as a lower bound; the source states "Sharpe and CAGR are resolution-robust and unaffected".
- **Factor decomposition:** single-factor **CAPM** versus the market (SPY), alpha annualised with its t-statistic, for the cross-paper scorecard; the source states a multi-factor decomposition of surviving candidates is "the natural next refinement".

## Crypto portability

**Verdict: `unproven`.**

- The evidence is entirely **US listed cash equities, 1998-2025, point-in-time survivorship-free**, with a single out-of-sample regime (2023-25). The source performs **no crypto test of any kind**, so every port below is a **ported hypothesis, not crypto empirical evidence**.
- **What could port conceptually (`research-proposed` adaptation):** the *method* is venue-agnostic - survivorship-free point-in-time universes, as-traded rather than retroactively adjusted prices, value weighting plus a liquidity/stale-price screen, a pre-registered holdout, a `t > 3`-style multiplicity hurdle, turnover-keyed cost stress, and a build-versus-source fidelity review. That method is directly reusable on crypto cross-sections.
- **What cuts against a direct port of the *signals*:**
  - **Universe and survivorship:** the top-500-by-market-cap and PIT S&P 500 screens have no analogue; crypto listing/delisting churn is far faster, and delisting (including forced delistings and rug events) is a first-order return component rather than a tail nuisance.
  - **Price fields:** crypto has no stock splits in the corporate-action sense, so the Geertsema-Lu look-ahead mechanism does **not** transfer literally; the equivalent hazard is index/mark-price construction, adjusted-versus-unadjusted vendor series and stablecoin-quote-currency effects.
  - **Session structure:** 24/7 trading removes the closing-auction anchor that the monthly equity formation implicitly uses; UTC month boundaries and funding intervals create artificial cut-offs.
  - **Short legs:** equity locates are replaced by perp borrow/funding and by venue-specific hard-to-borrow constraints; four of the nine books are long/short, so this is decisive.
  - **Benchmark:** there is no SPY-equivalent passive hurdle with a 1.43 Sharpe; the appropriate benchmark (BTC, an equal-weight index, or cash) must be declared `research-proposed`.
  - **Costs:** the flat 5 bps/side equity baseline is not a crypto cost model; taker/maker fees, spread regimes and slippage on small caps differ by an order of magnitude.
- **Conclusion:** portability is `unproven`. A crypto test would be a new study applying the *method* above, not a re-labelling of these results.
