## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

Classic Golden Cross / Death Cross trend riding (strategy README: `Classic trend-following strategy using Simple Moving Average crossovers. The Golden Cross (fast SMA crosses above slow SMA) signals bullish trends, while the Death Cross (fast crosses below slow) signals bearish trends`; `Exit: Opposite signal closes previous position`; Risk Warning: `This strategy works best in trending markets. During sideways consolidation, expect multiple false signals and drawdowns`). The slow 200-bar leg is the memory of roughly the last two quarterly regimes on daily bars; the fast 50-bar leg crossing it means the medium trend just overpowered the long regime — the system joins the new side and holds until the opposite cross. There is deliberately no stop, no target, no trailing order leg, no time exit, no `strategy.close` leg, and no pyramiding add anywhere — the opposite entry is the entire exit path, by design gap rather than by hidden leg.

### Research interpretation

Always-in-tendency dual-average reversal with the slowest admission clock in the Eterna sibling family. Two pinned subtleties carry the economics, stated plainly. First, signals are EDGES, not states: `ta.crossover` fires once on the cross bar and then goes quiet — a book that stays correctly positioned through a multi-month trend fires nothing further (pyramiding-0 parity would no-op a re-fire anyway), so the system rides rather than compounds; a state machine keyed on `fastSMA > slowSMA` would re-signal every trend bar. Second, there is NO flat state past seeding: with no `strategy.close` leg anywhere, the book exits a side only by reversing into the other — whipsaw cost concentrates in ranging markets as full round-trip pairs (the README's own warning), while trending markets pay exactly one entry per regime turn. The bet is on 50-bar momentum persisting against the 200-bar regime at frozen `50/200` geometry with no filter, no gate, and no risk leg — not on any volume print, RSI confirm, trailing stop, or retuned pair.
