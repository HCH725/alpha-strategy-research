## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The source's claim is a **conditional asset-pricing claim, not an unconditional premium and not a proposed trading rule**: it tests two stated hypotheses and reports that the first fails on average while the second survives.

- **H1 (Section 2.1, ICAPM baseline).** "Social unrest beta (beta_RSUI) is negatively associated with firms' cross-sectional expected returns." The stated channel is Merton (1973) intertemporal hedging: unrest is an adverse state that depresses investment opportunities, so a stock that performs well when unrest rises hedges that state and investors accept a lower expected return on it, while stocks that fall when unrest rises must be compensated.
- **H2 (Section 2.2, salience condition).** "The negative association between beta_RSUI and expected returns (H1) is absent before the U.S. unrest of 2020-2021 and present after it." The stated reason H1 can fail unconditionally is that investors must first recognize unrest as a priced state variable; salience theory (Bordalo, Gennaioli and Shleifer, 2013) and the learning literature (Pagano, Wagner and Zechner, 2023; Bolton and Kacperczyk, 2021) supply the timing story.
- **Measurement.** beta_RSUI is the slope on the Reported Social Unrest Index (RSUI; Barrett et al., 2022) in a rolling 36-month six-factor time-series regression of the stock's excess return on the return-like log-transformed RSUI plus the five Fama-French factors (Equation 2), estimated over months `t-36` to `t-1` so the loadings used for month `t` share no observation with the return being explained.
- **Transition episode.** The two candidate transition months are June 2020 (BLM protests) and January 2021 (Capitol attack), which the source notes are the only two months flagged by the major-event indicator distributed with the RSUI for the United States over 1990-2024; the shaded band in Figure 1 runs 25 May 2020 to 6 January 2021.

### Research interpretation

Falsifiable restatement (research interpretation, not a source claim): **a dollar-neutral monthly long-short book that is long the lowest-unrest-beta decile and short the highest-unrest-beta decile of S&P 500 constituents, equal-weighted, begun after the January 2021 boundary, should deliver a negative H-L spread (that is, positive return on the low-minus-high book) of roughly 1% per month that survives Fama-French five-factor controls, survives transaction costs, persists out of sample beyond August 2024, and is not explained by pandemic reversal, salience overreaction or sector rotation.** Roles: **beta_RSUI** = the signal (a characteristic-like exposure estimate); **the 36-month rolling estimation window** = lookback and the anti-look-ahead device; **monthly decile re-formation** = portfolio construction; **the January 2021 boundary** = regime gate, fixed by the historical calendar rather than chosen from returns. Every one of the operational choices needed to turn this into a rule - entry price, order type, sizing, cost model, the decision to trade the boundary at all - is **research-proposed**, because the source explicitly states "we propose neither a traded factor nor an addition to the five-factor model" (Section 6.4).

## Signal

- **Formation timestamp (source-reported).** Stocks are sorted each month `t` into deciles on beta_RSUI, where the loading is estimated from returns over months `t-36` to `t-1`, so the sort is known before month `t` begins (Table 6 and Table 8 notes). Returns are evaluated in month `t` (the contemporaneous return, which the source argues is a one-month-ahead test because the loadings end at `t-1`), in month `t+1`, and cumulatively over `[t+1,t+3]` and `[t+1,t+6]`. The forward windows skip one month between the end of the estimation window and the start of the return window. Timezone, session convention and the exact signal-to-order delay are **not stated in source** (data gap).
- **Lookback (source-reported).** Rolling 36-month window; first window January 1990 to December 1992, first loadings assigned to January 1993 (Section 3.1). Minimum 36 consecutive months of return history required per firm. RSUI enters in logs and is rescaled affinely to the scale of the market excess return (Section 3.2, footnote 1).
- **Long entry (research-proposed for trading; source-reported as a test).** The source's test longs decile 10 and shorts decile 1 to form the H-L spread (Table 6, Table 8). The direction implied by H1 after the break is the opposite: the tradeable reading is **long decile 1 (lowest beta_RSUI), short decile 10 (highest beta_RSUI)**, equal-weighted, from the January 2021 boundary. This direction is **research-proposed**, not source-reported.
- **Short entry.** Decile 10 under the research-proposed direction. The source never discusses shortability, locate, borrow or dividend reimbursement (`short sale` 0, `borrow` 0, `shorting` 0 in the pinned text layer) - **data gap**.
- **Exit / holding period / re-entry (source-reported test horizons).** Monthly re-formation with evaluation at one month, three months and six months; cumulative returns are computed within each sample period so formation months whose return window would cross a period boundary are dropped, giving `N - 2h + 1` monthly cross-sections for horizon `h` in a period of `N` months. No stop, no take-profit, no time-based exit and no re-entry rule exist in the source; **absence is source-reported, not a Scout choice**.
- **Parameters (source-reported).** 36-month beta window; decile sorts (10 groups) for the headline, quintiles for the two-way sorts; three Fama-French sample boundaries (1993-2010, 2011-2020, 2021-2024) fixed by the protest literature and the event calendar; Bai-Perron break search allowing up to two breaks with a minimum segment length of 36 months, run at the one-, three- and six-month horizons; Newey-West standard errors with three lags throughout; GRS test on the ten sorted portfolios.
- **Parameters NOT printed in source (underspecified / data gap).** The risk-free rate or excess-return deflator; the exact CRSP return construction (dividend inclusion, delisting returns); the index-constituents membership rule beyond "constituents at any point between January 1990 and August 2024"; whether sorts use NYSE breakpoints; the treatment of ties in the decile sort; and any fitted model hyperparameters.
- **Reproducibility verdict.** The rule is **partially specified**: exposure estimation, sort construction, horizons, boundaries and inference are reconstructible; the data assembly choices, the excess-return deflator, the universe hygiene and any executable order handling are not.

### Negative evidence

1. **The unconditional hypothesis is rejected in the source's own headline.** Over January 1993 to August 2024 the equal-weighted H-L spread is 0.12% per month (t = 0.62) at month `t` and -0.12% over six months (t = -0.11): exposure to unrest carried no average premium for three decades.
2. **The post-break result rests on 44 months.** Section 6.1 states 44 months exist in 2021-2024, of which 43 are usable with a one-month skip, 39 for three-month returns and 33 for six-month returns; Table 9 reports only 38-39 cross-sections for the one-month FM horizon.
3. **The effect vanishes under value weighting.** Post-break value-weighted spreads are +0.42, +0.47, +2.57 and +2.39 with |t| never above 1.21 (Table 6 Panel B); Section 5.5 states plainly that "the largest firms do not share in the effect".
4. **The effect is confined to the smallest constituents.** The six-month spread is -10.53% in the smallest size quintile and +1.69% (t = 0.37) in the largest (Table 11 Panel A), and is not monotone in size.
5. **The effect comes from one decile.** Deciles 1 to 9 show no trend in month `t` while decile 10 alone earns -0.05%; Section 5.3 concedes "the result is a statement about the extreme decile and should not be read as a linear price of risk".
6. **Controls erode the coefficient.** In the fullest specification the post-break slope is -0.0041 (t = -1.55), -0.0135 (t = -1.95) and -0.0269 (t = -1.70): significant at no better than the 10% level (Table 9).
7. **The six-month t-statistics are overstated and the source says so.** Three Newey-West lags do not cover five months of overlap; Section 6.1 names the -4.58 on the six-month alpha as "the clearest case".
8. **The Harvey-Liu-Zhu 3.0 hurdle is not cleared.** Section 6.4 states the post-break t-statistics with valid standard errors lie between -2.3 and -2.7 without controls and -1.6 to -2.0 with controls, drawn from an analysis over four horizons, two weightings and three periods.
9. **The pre-break positive spread does not survive controls.** In 2011-2020 the raw spread is +0.55%/month (t = 2.18), but the FM slope collapses to 0.0004-0.0005 once Fama-French loadings are included (Table 9); the source takes "the conservative reading" that it is not robust.
10. **The GRS test does not support the result.** For 2021-2024 at month `t+1` F = 0.68 (p = 0.733) equal-weighted, and the source says the non-rejection "reflects low power... we do not count it in favor of H1".
11. **Explanatory power is negligible.** Even after the break beta_RSUI accounts for less than 1% of the variation in six-month returns (Table 13: Owen value 0.007684 on a total R2 of 0.026443).
12. **The pandemic-reversal confound is explicitly not ruled out.** Section 6.3: the tests include "no control for industry or for direct exposure to the pandemic", and the authors "have not re-estimated the betas on windows that exclude 2020".
13. **Overreaction versus risk premium cannot be separated.** Section 6.3 states that 44 months "cannot discriminate between a premium and a slow correction", and that both accounts predict a negative post-break spread.
14. **The sector confound is only partly addressed.** The sector subsample covers two of ten GICS sectors and the regressions contain no industry effects (Sections 5.5 and 6.3).
15. **No transaction-cost, turnover or execution model of any kind.** Every friction substring returns 0; `gross` 0, so no gross/net label exists anywhere; the reported spreads are friction-free artifacts of a decile sort.
16. **No Sharpe ratio, no win rate and no drawdown are reported for any portfolio.** `sharpe ratio` 0, `win rate` 0; the paper reports only spreads, alphas, coefficients and t-statistics.
17. **No multiple-testing correction is applied.** `benjamini` 0, `bonferroni` 0, `multiplicity` 0, `deflated` 0; Section 6.4 raises the topic and declines to clear the bar.
18. **No out-of-sample, walk-forward or live window.** `walk-forward` 0; the sample ends August 2024 and nothing after that date exists in the pinned source.
19. **Universe hygiene is unstated.** `survivorship` 0, `delisting` 0, `point-in-time` 0, `share code` 0: the panel is the retrospective union of S&P 500 membership over 1990-2024 from a commercial index vendor, with no statement about when membership was known or how leavers were handled.

21. **The second Bai-Perron break is unstable and disowned.** It falls in July 2007, October 2007 or May 2017 depending on the horizon, has "no counterpart in the history of U.S. unrest", and the source states "we do not interpret it".
22. **Break evidence at the one-month horizon is weak.** The BIC improves only from -3153.60 to -3154.71 and the CUSUM does not reject (p = 0.147) for month `t+1` returns.
