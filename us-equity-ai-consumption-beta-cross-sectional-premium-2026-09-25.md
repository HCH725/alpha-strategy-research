## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The paper builds three objects in sequence (Sections 1-3). **(1) The AI Factor** is the first principal component of the *standardized weekly log growth* of three realized consumption series - total tokens, usage dollars and distinct active users - with PC weights **0.665, 0.559, 0.496** explaining **56.5%** of their joint variance (Section 2.1, Appendix Table IA.2; the weekly decomposition is Monday-indexed per IA.2). Growth rather than levels is used because "asset returns should respond to the unexpected component of AI consumption ... rather than to the mechanical upward trend of a diffusing technology". **(2) Firm-level AI beta** `beta^AI_{i,t}` is the slope from a **rolling 13-week weekly regression** of stock `i`'s log excess return on the AI factor, controlling for the log excess market return, over the window ending at formation week `t`, requiring **at least 9 non-missing weeks** and re-estimated every week (Section 3.1, eq. 1); the source states the exposure "is free of look-ahead" because it "uses only returns through the formation week". **(3) The AI Premium** is whatever that exposure earns in the cross-section: portfolios are sorted each week into quintiles on current `beta^AI` and rebalanced weekly (Section 3.1).

The source's stated economic rationale is the **transition-risk / creative-destruction channel** (Section 1, citing Pastor and Veronesi 2009, Aghion and Howitt 1992, Acemoglu and Zilibotti 1997, Acemoglu and Restrepo 2019/2022): AI reallocates rents across firms, sectors and tasks; when that reallocation is systematic and imperfectly diversifiable, risk-averse investors require higher expected returns for holding the firms most exposed to it. On this reading the premium is a **risk price, not an inefficiency**. The source stresses two caveats in the same paragraph: the sample "covers an early, fast-moving phase of AI diffusion, so the estimates should be interpreted as the current price of AI exposure, rather than a long-run estimate", and OpenRouter "likely overrepresents sophisticated and developer users".

**Where the premium lives (Section 3.3.3, Table 7; Section 3.3.2, Table 6).** It loads on the *intensive, frontier-oriented* margin - closed-source models, paid/core accounts, seasoned users, long prompts - and not on the casual or extensive margin; and it is a developed-market phenomenon: **17.9 bp/week in MSCI developed markets vs 5.0 bp/week (t = 0.94) in emerging markets**, with China A-shares negative and insignificant under both factor definitions (Appendix Table IA.14).

**Rival explanations the source itself tests (Section 3.3.1, Table 5; Appendix IA.7, IA.8, IA.9, IA.13; Section 3.3.4, Table 8).** The first-pass beta regression is re-estimated controlling separately for the FF10 high-technology industry return, a value-weighted semiconductor portfolio, an equal-weighted AI/robotics ETF basket, FF30 industry-demeaned exposure, Google Trends "Artificial intelligence" growth, and SEC-filing AI-mention portfolios; plus a hand-collected calendar of 19 frontier and 28 non-frontier model releases is used for an event study and for a sample-exclusion test. The source concludes the premium prices exposure to *realized AI consumption*, not tech-sector beta, attention, disclosure narrative, or a handful of news weeks.

### Research interpretation

- **Primary hypothesis, falsifiable:** over the Jan 2024 - Apr 2026 US equity cross-section, a weekly-sorted long-short on the trailing 13-week co-movement slope with an aggregate LLM-consumption growth factor earns a positive average return that survives FF5+momentum adjustment, i.e. **the market prices a systematic AI-transition exposure**. Mechanism class: **compensation for systematic technology-transition risk** (a risk-price claim), not an informational edge.
- **Boundary hypothesis, falsifiable:** the premium is concentrated where listed firms and investors sit closest to the AI frontier (DM, closed-source, paid, seasoned, long-prompt margins) and should vanish or reverse in EM/China and on the casual/open-weight margin. The source's own China cells are consistent with a *sign reversal* (negative, insignificant), which is a stronger statement than mere absence.
- **Attribution hypothesis, falsifiable:** the effect is *not* a relabeling of tech-sector beta, semiconductor beta, AI-theme ETF beta, Google-Trends attention, or firms' own AI disclosure text; and it is *not* solely the realization of AI news surprises. These are the source's four explicit ablations (Tables 5, 8, IA.7, IA.8, IA.9, IA.13).
- **Component-role note for this hybrid:** *factor* = aggregate realized AI-consumption growth PC (macro shock); *signal* = firm-level 13-week regression slope on that factor (exposure, not direct forecast); *sort* = weekly quintile breakpoints; *risk/exit logic* = none (the source has no exit, stop or sizing rule beyond value weighting). Do not assume each component contributes alpha; ablations are required.
- This record must not be read as a profitable-strategy claim: the source reports no transaction costs, no turnover, no Sharpe and no drawdown for the long-short book, and explicitly frames the estimates as a *current* price of exposure.

## Signal

All items below are **source-reported** unless marked `research-proposed` / `research-defined`.

- **Formation timestamp and tradability.** Portfolios are "formed by sorting all firms **at the beginning of each week** into quintiles by their current AI exposure", with "excess return ... the average **post-formation-week** portfolio return" (Section 3.1, Table 3 caption). The AI factor is a **Monday-indexed weekly** series (Appendix IA.2). The **publication/availability lag of the weekly OpenRouter aggregates at formation time is not stated anywhere in v2** -> `data gap`; so is the timezone of the consumption clock (OpenRouter requests are server-timestamped but no clock convention is printed) -> `data gap`.
- **Lookback.** Rolling window `W_t` = **13 weeks ending at formation week t**, minimum **9** non-missing weekly observations (Section 3.1, eq. 1). Exposure is re-estimated **every week**.
- **Sort / breakpoints.** Five quintiles per formation week; baseline breakpoints from the **full all-stock eligible universe** ("all-stock breakpoints"), with an **NYSE-breakpoint** variant and a **decile** variant (Appendix IA.12). Each quintile holds about **680 stocks on average** (Section 3.1; Table 2 prints 679/678/678/678/679).
- **Entry (portfolio, not order).** Long the top `beta^AI` quintile, short the bottom quintile - the source defines `H-L` as "the spread between the High and Low AI-beta quintiles" (Table 3 caption) and describes it as "a value-weighted **long-short strategy**" (Abstract, Section 1). **No statement of dollar neutrality, gross exposure, net exposure or position limits exists anywhere in v2** -> `underspecified`.
- **Position sizing.** Baseline **value-weighted by formation-week market capitalization**; equal-weighted variants reported (Table 3 Panels C-D); international quintile returns are equal-weighted across firms and then equal-weighted across local markets (Table 6 note). Individual-name weight caps, lot rules and sector caps are **not stated** -> `data gap`.
- **Holding period / rebalance.** **Weekly** rebalance of the whole sort (Table 3 caption); no separate holding-period, re-entry, stop, take-profit or time-exit rule exists -> the trading layer beyond the weekly re-sort is absent.
- **Parameters (all fixed by the source).** Estimation window 13 weeks; minimum 9 observations; 5 quintiles (10 in IA.12); breakpoints all-stock / NYSE; weighting value / equal; factor = PC1 of three standardized weekly log-growth series with weights 0.665 / 0.559 / 0.496; sub-factor splits (closed vs open, core vs new, seasoned vs non-seasoned, long vs short prompt) use **predetermined** group definitions requiring >= 13 weeks tenure, >= 8 prior active weeks, >= USD 10 prior usage, >= 20 prior requests, or the prior-13-week rolling 75th percentile of prompt length (Section 3.3.3); Fama-MacBeth characteristics are cross-sectionally **winsorized at the 1st/99th percentiles and standardized to unit variance** each week (Table 4 note).
- **Known timing weaknesses inside the signal (source-acknowledged).** The **PC weights and the z-standardization of the three input series are estimated on the full sample**, so the factor itself contains full-sample information; the source says the alternative equal-weighted aggregator "partly addresses the forward-looking concern that principal-component weights are estimated from the full sample, **although it does not eliminate full-sample information**" (Appendix Table IA.10 note). The exposure regression is look-ahead-free; **the factor construction is not**.
- **Reconstruction status.** The beta regression, the sort, the weighting and the rebalance are exactly specified. **Not reconstructable from the public record:** the OpenRouter panel itself (licensed, non-redistributable), the availability timing of the weekly aggregates, the definition of "excess" in Table 3 (the caption does not name a risk-free series; the Fama-French weekly RF is only referenced in the ETF-basket note of IA.13), and the t-statistic convention for Tables 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 (no Newey-West/HAC statement in those captions - NW is stated only for Table 8, "three lags", and IA.5, "four-week lags") -> `underspecified`.

## Required data

- **Instrument / universe (US baseline, Section 2.2).** CRSP common stocks listed on **NYSE, AMEX and Nasdaq** matched to Compustat; baseline filters are **formation-week market capitalization >= USD 1 million and CRSP price >= USD 5**; the eligible cross-section averages roughly 3,390 names per week (our count from Table 2's ~680 x 5).
- **Venue / market type:** US listed equity cash market (spot); data vendors **CRSP, Compustat, IBES, Kenneth French's library, Google Trends, SEC full-text filing archive**; the alternative-data core is the **licensed proprietary OpenRouter user-model-day panel**.
- **Timeframe:** weekly, January 2024 through April 2026 (stated on every empirical table). Window length is **not printed as N**; our count of Mondays in 2024-01-01..2026-04-30 is **122** weekly formation weeks (`research-defined` count, not a source number).
- **International / China universes (Section 2.2).** Compustat Global common stocks split into **MSCI developed and emerging** (same size and price filters in USD, returns in local currency, JKP-style daily winsorization using same-month CRSP cutoffs before weekly compounding, Table 6 note); **Datastream China A-share** panel with a China-specific AI factor built from mainland-China OpenRouter consumption.
- **Fields.** OpenRouter: requests, prompt and completion tokens, dollar cost, model identifier and version, user tenure/intensity cohorts, prompt-content category tags, `finish_reason == tool_calls` (agentic flag), cache-read and reasoning token shares - aggregated weekly into tokens / dollars / distinct users. - **Point-in-time / availability.** Exposure uses only returns through the formation week (source claim); group splits are "predetermined using information available before the portfolio-formation week" (Section 3.3.3); the model-release calendar is hand-collected from official model cards, lab blog posts and developer announcements (Section 2.2, Appendix IA.16) - **collection date and any look-back protocol for that calendar are not stated** -> `data gap`. - **Missing data.** Minimum 9 non-missing weeks per rolling regression; samples "vary by split according to non-missing rolling-beta availability" (IA.5 caption); no imputation rule for missing returns is printed -> `data gap`.
- **Cost / fee / spread needs:** **nothing is observed, modelled or omitted-by-statement** - no fees, spread, slippage, impact, borrow, financing, participation, latency or capacity input exists -> `data gap`.

### Negative evidence

1. **No cost model of any kind.** The 64.1 bp/week headline is gross of every fee, spread, slippage, impact, borrow and financing term; the word-boundary scan above finds none in v2. With a weekly full re-sort of ~3,390 names, cost drag is unknown, not zero.
2. **Very short, single-regime sample.** Jan 2024 - Apr 2026 (~122 weeks, our count) overlaps an unusual AI-driven equity regime; the source itself warns the estimates are "the current price of AI exposure, rather than a long-run estimate" and calls it "our short sample" (Section 3.3.1).
3. **Full-sample information in the factor.** PC weights and input standardization are estimated on the full Jan 2024 - Apr 2026 window; the source concedes in the Table IA.10 note that the alternative construction "does not eliminate full-sample information". The "free of look-ahead" claim covers only the exposure regression's return window.
4. **Large momentum imbalance in the sort.** Table 2's only large characteristic gap is momentum (0.019 -> 0.140, spread 0.121, **t 6.80**) - bigger than the size gap - so the raw spread is not characteristic-neutral; FF5+Mom alpha survives (55.9 bp) but the sort itself is entangled with momentum, and the high quintile is also lower-volatility (t -5.87) and lower-analyst-dispersion (t -4.55).
5. **The premium is not portable outside developed markets.** EM spread 5.0 bp (t 0.94) insignificant; **China A-shares negative and insignificant under both the baseline and the China-specific factor, in both weightings** (Table IA.14: -0.159 t -0.61, -0.072 t -0.37, -0.241 t -1.03, -0.300 t -1.59).
6. **The agentic sub-claim is unsupported by its own direct sort.** Table 11's "0.3 to 0.5 percent per week" are imprecise point estimates, and Appendix IA.11's direct agentic sort is **31.0 bp (t 0.76), insignificant**, while the *non-agentic* sort (44.6 bp, t 2.15) is the one that loads.
7. **Event-window pre-drift.** Figure 2 documents "pre-drift before the release" for frontier launches, so part of the event effect accrues before the announced date - a point-in-time hazard for any news-timed implementation.
8. **Significance is week-sensitive.** Removing the release weeks drops the spread to **0.395 (t 1.96)** and **0.456 (t 1.77)**, i.e. below conventional significance.
9. **The low quintile earns nothing.** Low-quintile excess return is **-0.002 %/wk (t -0.01)** while High is 0.639 - the entire spread is the high quintile in this sample, so the evidence does not support a long-only tilt or a "low-AI-beta is cheap" reading.
10. **Mechanism instability across sub-universes.** SaaS incumbents load insignificantly over the full sample (0.035, t 0.27) and significantly *negatively* in the last quarter (-1.353, t -2.69); the cross-sectional story therefore coexists with sharply different sub-universe behavior.
11. **No reported risk/turnover/capacity statistics.** No Sharpe, drawdown, turnover, ADV or participation figure appears anywhere; Figure IA.4's rolling volatility is a plot, not a summary statistic, and the book's implementable size (with a USD 1 million market-cap floor) is unknown.
12. **Not independently reproducible.** The OpenRouter panel is licensed and non-redistributable, there is no code or replication package, and no data/code-availability statement exists in v2.
13. **No multiplicity control.** The pinned v2 contains **0** occurrences of "Benjamini", "Bonferroni", "family-wise", "multiple testing", "false discovery" or "deflated" while printing 11 main and 19 appendix tables of parallel sorts, splits, controls and windows; inference is per-table only.
14. **Source-quality / publication status.** Single unrefereed v2 preprint, no journal reference, no peer review stated; the alternative-data provider is also the data licensor, and the authors note OpenRouter "likely overrepresents sophisticated and developer users".
15. **Inference convention underspecified for the baseline.** Table 3, 4, 5, 6 and 7 captions do not state the t-statistic convention (Newey-West is stated only for Table 8, three lags, and IA.5, four-week lags), so serial-correlation handling behind the headline `t = 2.84` cannot be verified from the source -> `underspecified`.

## Falsification plan

All thresholds below are **research-defined** and all operational choices not printed by the source are **research-proposed**. Each test names data, sample/regime, metric, decision rule and the action on failure.

- **F1 - Point-in-time factor reconstruction** (`research-proposed` rule, `research-defined` threshold). Rebuild the AI factor with **expanding-window PC weights and expanding-window standardization** (only data available at each formation week), then re-run the Table 3 Panel A sort. Threshold: `H-L` >= **0.30 %/wk** with Newey-West (>= 4 lags) `t >= 2.0`. **Action on failure:** the premium is partly full-sample-standardization leakage; the record's claim downgrades to "unverified under point-in-time construction".
- **F2 - Signal-availability lag** (`research-proposed`). Re-run the sort with the AI factor **lagged one week** and with exposure formed on returns through `t-1`. Threshold: `H-L` >= **0.45 %/wk** (>= 70% of the printed 0.641). **Action on failure:** the result depends on same-week factor availability that is not documented; treat as a timing artefact.
- **F3 - Cost ladder with borrow** (`research-proposed` cost lines, `research-defined` thresholds). Re-book the weekly H-L at **0 / 5 / 10 / 20 / 30 bp per side**, plus **50 bp per year stock-loan fee on the short leg** and a mid-to-ask paid-spread variant, with participation capped at **20% ADV**. Threshold: net `H-L` > 0 at 10 bp and >= **0.20 %/wk** at 20 bp, with no sign flip below 30 bp. **Action on failure:** no investable claim; the record stays a risk-pricing finding.
- **F4 - Turnover, borrowability and capacity audit** (`research-defined`). Measure one-way turnover of the weekly re-sort and the share of short-leg names that are borrowable and above USD 100 million market cap. Threshold: median weekly one-way turnover <= **25%**, >= **95%** of short-leg weight borrowable, and >= **90%** of short-leg weight above USD 100 million. **Action on failure:** capacity and short feasibility are unknown; withdraw any implementation reading.
- **F5 - Factor-shuffle placebo** (`research-defined`). 1,000 **circular-shift** draws of the weekly AI-factor series (preserving its autocorrelation and marginal distribution), each re-running the full beta-and-sort pipeline. Threshold: observed `H-L` must exceed the **95th percentile of |placebo H-L|** and placebo means must sit within +/- 0.10 %/wk of zero. **Action on failure:** the spread is a property of the 2024-2026 equity path, not of the factor.
- **F6 - Joint competing-explanation horse race** (`research-proposed`). Put **all** controls - FF10 HiTec, semiconductor portfolio, AI-ETF basket, Google Trends AI growth, SEC AI-mention H-L, and past-12-month momentum - into a **single** first-pass beta regression (the source tests them one at a time). Threshold: `H-L` >= **0.30 %/wk** with `t >= 2.0`. **Action on failure:** the "AI consumption" reading is a relabeling of tech/attention/momentum exposure.
- **F7 - Characteristic-neutral double sort** (`research-defined`). 5x5 portfolios on **momentum quartile x AI-beta quartile**, within FF30 industries, value-weighted, all-stock breakpoints. Threshold: within each momentum quartile the top-minus-bottom AI-beta spread averages >= **0.20 %/wk** and is positive in >= 4 of 5 quartiles. **Action on failure:** the premium is carried by the momentum imbalance documented in Table 2.
- **F8 - Frozen out-of-sample forward test** (`research-defined`). Freeze window 13, min-obs 9, breakpoints, weighting and **PC weights/standardization estimated only through April 2026**, then run May 2026 forward for >= 52 weeks. Threshold: positive `H-L` in >= **3 of 4 quarters** and cumulative >= **+1.0%**. **Action on failure:** early-sample regime artefact; close the hypothesis.
- **F9 - Release-event placebo** (`research-defined`). 1,000 placebo calendars of 19 dates matched on weekday, month and adjacent-volatility; compare the frontier `(0,+5)` raw CAAR 1.933% and the FF5+Mom 1.071% against the placebo distribution, and require pre-event day -5..-1 CAAR to be **insignificant**. **Action on failure:** the event content is calendar noise and/or pre-drift leakage; drop the news channel from the mechanism.
- **F10 - Second data provider / factor stability** (`research-defined`). Rebuild the tokens/dollars/users aggregates from a second independent LLM-usage source (or a public aggregate index). Threshold: weekly factor growth correlation >= **0.7** with the OpenRouter PC and the same-signed `H-L` with `t >= 2.0`. **Action on failure:** the factor is provider-specific; the mechanism cannot be operationalized outside one vendor's panel.
- **F11 - Multiplicity audit** (`research-defined`). Apply Benjamini-Hochberg at `q = 0.10` across the printed grid of baseline, split, control, region and window tests, plus a deflated-Sharpe >= **0.95** requirement for the H-L series after the number of reported variants is counted. **Action on failure:** the evidentiary weight is inflated by uncorrected multiplicity; record the premium as exploratory.

## Crypto portability

**unproven.**

The *statistical form* - aggregate usage-growth factor, rolling co-movement beta, weekly cross-sectional quintile long-short - is market-agnostic and could in principle be pointed at crypto. The **empirical content is not**: every reported number comes from **US CRSP common stocks** (plus MSCI DM/EM and China A-shares) over **Jan 2024 - Apr 2026**, with Fama-French factor adjustments, a USD 1 million / USD 5 price universe floor, and a factor built from LLM API consumption attributed to *firms*, which no crypto asset exhibits.

Crypto-specific portability risks: there is **no listed-firm AI-exposure analogue** - the natural ports (AI-infrastructure tokens, GPU-depin baskets, agent-token revenue) are project-level, far smaller, and their "returns" mix token supply inflation with price; **24/7 sessions** break the Monday-indexed weekly factor clock and the "beginning of the week" formation convention; **perpetual funding, mark/index price, liquidation, borrow and venue fragmentation** are entirely absent from a source cost model that already models no equity costs; **survivorship and listing churn** are far more severe than a CRSP common-stock filter; and the source's own boundary result - **negative/inscentive-free in China and insignificant in EM** - is direct evidence that the premium does not automatically transport outside the market where the technology is priced. Any crypto version is a **ported hypothesis**, not crypto evidence.

## Limitations

- `underspecified`: t-statistic convention for Tables 3-7; the definition of "excess" in Table 3; dollar neutrality / net exposure of the H-L; availability and timezone of the weekly OpenRouter aggregates; price basis for entry; dividend convention; model-release calendar collection protocol; any exit, stop or sizing rule.
- `data gap`: transaction fees, spread, slippage, market impact, participation, latency, borrow/locate, financing, leverage/margin, turnover, capacity, fill model, delisting/failure handling, and the risk-free rate used in the headline excess returns.
- `not independently reproduced`: every number in this record is source-reported from a single unrefereed v2 preprint read on 2026-09-25; the underlying panel cannot be obtained.
- `unproven`: transport beyond the Jan 2024 - Apr 2026 US regime; transport to EM/China (source reports negative/insignificant); transport to crypto; a long-run size of the premium; any net-of-cost profitability.
- **Look-ahead residue:** PC weights and input standardization are full-sample (source-acknowledged in the Table IA.10 note), so the "free of look-ahead" statement applies only to the exposure regression.
- **Single provider, provider-as-licensor**, with an acknowledged developer-user skew; no second-source validation exists.
- **No multiplicity control** across 30 printed tables of parallel tests; no Sharpe/drawdown/turnover reporting for the traded spread; baseline inference convention unstated.
- **Publication-bias / source-quality:** preprint only, no journal reference, no peer review stated; the headline is a two-decimal basis-point claim over ~122 weeks.
- **Incremental-write check:** this record captures a new family (macro alternative-data consumption co-movement priced in equity cross-sections) with new falsification and negative-evidence content; it does not duplicate the repository's text/news/filings-based equity exposure records or its crypto usage-growth record.
