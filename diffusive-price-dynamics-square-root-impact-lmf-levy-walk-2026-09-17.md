## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

In market microstructure, a foundational contradiction known as the **diffusive price-dynamics paradox** exists between the Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH) and empirical order flow:
1. **Empirical Price Diffusion:** At macroscopic horizons ($t \gg 1$), asset price dynamics are well described by Brownian motion with linear mean-squared displacement ($MSD(t) \propto t$), implying that price returns have short memory with integrable autocorrelation.
2. **Predictable Long-Range Correlated (LRC) Order Flow:** Market order flow exhibits strong persistence; the autocorrelation function (ACF) of signed market orders decays according to a heavy-tailed power law:
   $$C(\tau) := \mathbb{E}[\epsilon(t+\tau)\epsilon(t)] \propto \tau^{-\gamma}, \quad \gamma \in (0, 1)$$
   where $\epsilon(t) \in \{-1, +1\}$ denotes the sign of an aggressive trade. This long memory is generated by institutional investors who split large parent metaorders into sequences of child orders to minimize execution impact (formalized by the Lillo–Mike–Farmer / LMF model, where metaorder sizes follow a Pareto distribution $\psi_m(L) \propto L^{-\alpha-1}$ with $\alpha = \gamma + 1 \in (1, 2)$).
3. **The Paradox under Linear Price Impact:** If each child order causes a linear permanent price shift ($I(Q) \propto Q$, as predicted by classic Kyle-type models), persistent order flow forces cumulative price displacement into a superdiffusive regime:
   $$MSD(t) \propto t^{3-\alpha} = t^{2-\gamma} \gg t$$
   This superdiffusive behavior is completely at odds with observed Brownian price paths.
4. **Resolution via the Square-Root Law:** Empirically, large metaorders obey the concave square-root impact law $I(Q) = c_0 Q^\delta$ with $\delta \approx 1/2$. Sato, Fujiwara, and Kanazawa mathematically map a nonlinear generalization of the LMF model onto the continuous-time random walk (CTRW) and Lévy-walk frameworks from statistical physics. They prove analytically that:
   $$\delta \le \frac{1}{2}$$
   is the **necessary and sufficient condition** for price dynamics to remain normal diffusive ($MSD(t) \propto t$) for all empirical order-flow memory exponents $\gamma \in (0, 1)$ ($\alpha \in (1, 2)$).
5. **Emergence of Other Microstructure Regularities:** When generalized to include inter-metaorder resting times ($\tau \sim \text{Exp}(\tau_r)$) and post-execution transient impact decay ($I(t') = I_{\text{peak}}[c_D + (1-c_D)e^{-t'/\tau_D}]$ with permanent fraction $c_D \approx 1/3$), the model preserves normal diffusion while analytically generating:
   - The **Inverse Cubic Law (ICL)** of price returns ($P_\ge(\Delta m) \propto (\Delta m)^{-\beta}$ with $\beta \approx \alpha / \delta \approx 1.5 / 0.5 = 3.0$) as an intermediate asymptotic law;
   - **Volatility clustering** (power-law decay of squared return autocorrelation $C_{\sigma^2}(\tau) \propto \tau^{-\gamma_\sigma}$) without introducing exogenous volatility shocks.

### Research interpretation

The square-root impact law acts as an endogenous structural stabilizer of limit order markets:
- By severely discounting the marginal price impact of successive child orders within an ongoing metaorder ($\Delta I(Q) \simeq \delta Q^{\delta-1} \propto Q^{-1/2}$), the liquidity replenishment mechanism of the limit order book absorbs persistent directional order flow into normal diffusive fluctuations.
- For quantitative execution, this implies that classical linear permanent impact execution kernels (such as Almgren–Chriss models) systematically overestimate long-horizon price drift and lead to sub-optimal front-loaded execution schedules.
- For alpha generation, order-flow momentum strategies based on simple trade sign continuation face an intrinsic physical decay horizon: under $\delta = 1/2$, return autocorrelation decays with power-law exponent $\theta = \alpha \in (1, 2) > 1$ (integrable short memory). As a result, directional predictability from order-flow imbalance dissipates rapidly, preventing long-horizon trend extraction while opening an exploitation window for high-frequency contrarian mean-reversion as metaorders reach exhaustion and enter post-execution relaxation.

## Signal

### Discrete-Time LMF Price-Impact Model (`source-reported`)

The market consists of $M \ge 1$ order-splitting institutional agents $\bm{\Omega} = \{1, \dots, M\}$, each submitting unit child orders with uniform intensity $\lambda = 1/M$ per time step $\Delta t = 1$.
- **Metaorder Size Distribution:** Drawn independently from a Pareto probability mass function:
  $$\psi_m(L) = \frac{L^{-\alpha-1}}{\zeta(\alpha+1)}, \quad L \in \{1, 2, \dots\}, \quad \alpha \in (1, 2)$$
- **State Space:** For trader $i$, state is $(\epsilon^{(i)}(t), R^{(i)}(t), Q^{(i)}(t))$, tracking order sign, remaining volume, and executed volume.
- **Price Dynamics:** The midprice $m(t)$ evolves according to:
  $$m(t+1) = m(t) + \epsilon(t) \Delta I(Q^{(\mathfrak{i})}(t))$$
  where $\mathfrak{i}(t) \in \bm{\Omega}$ is the selected trader, $\epsilon(t) = \epsilon^{(\mathfrak{i})}(t)$, and $\Delta I(Q) = (Q+1)^\delta - Q^\delta \simeq \delta Q^{\delta-1}$ for $Q \gg 1$.
- **One-Step Return Autocorrelation Function:**
  $$E[r(0)r(\tau)]_{\text{ss}} \simeq \frac{\delta^2 \lambda^{2\delta-\alpha}}{2 \zeta(\alpha)} \left[ \int_0^\infty dy \, y^{\delta-1} (y+1)^{\delta-1} (y+1)^{-\alpha} \right] \tau^{2\delta-\alpha-1}$$
  - When $\delta \le 1/2$, the decay exponent satisfies $\theta := \alpha + 1 - 2\delta \ge \alpha > 1$. The ACF is integrable, proving that returns exhibit short memory and the Central Limit Theorem (CLT) holds.
  - When $\delta > 1/2$, there exists a critical threshold $\alpha^* = 2\delta > 1$. For $\alpha \in (1, 2\delta)$, returns exhibit long memory ($\theta < 1$), leading to anomalous superdiffusion.

### Continuous-Time Lévy-Walk Price Model (`source-reported`)

The price velocity is represented as a superposition of independent renewal velocities:
$$v_M(t) = \frac{dm(t)}{dt} = \sum_{i=1}^M v^{(i)}(t), \quad v^{(i)}(t) = \epsilon_u^{(i)} \delta \left(t - t_{u,\text{ini}}^{(i)}\right)^{\delta-1}$$
- **Asymptotic Mean-Squared Displacement ($MSD$):**
  $$MSD_M(t) \simeq \begin{cases} K_1 t, & \delta < 1/2 \text{ or } \alpha > 2\delta \\ K_2 t \ln t, & 2\delta = \alpha \\ K_3 t^{2\delta - \alpha + 1}, & \alpha < 2\delta \end{cases}$$
  where $K_1, K_2, K_3$ are closed-form constants derived via Fourier–Laplace transform of the space-time coupling function $\psi_1(x, t) = \frac{1}{2} \psi_m(t) [\delta(x - t^\delta) + \delta(x + t^\delta)]$.

### Generalized Models I & II (`source-reported`)

- **Generalized Model I (Resting State):** Traders enter an idle resting period $\tau \sim \psi_r(t) = \tau_r^{-1} e^{-t/\tau_r}$ after completing each metaorder.
  - Generates the Inverse Cubic Law: cumulative distribution of returns scales as $P_\ge(\Delta m) \propto (\Delta m)^{-\beta}$ with $\beta = \alpha / \delta$. Under $\delta = 1/2$ and $\alpha \approx 1.5$, $\beta \approx 3.0$.
  - Numerically exhibits volatility clustering: $\text{Corr}(\sigma^2(t), \sigma^2(t+\tau)) \propto \tau^{-\gamma_\sigma}$ with $\gamma_\sigma \approx 0.2$–$0.5$.
- **Generalized Model II (Impact Relaxation):** Post-execution price impact decays toward a permanent component:
  $$G(Q, t') = I(Q) \left[ c_D + (1 - c_D) e^{-t'/\tau_D} \right]$$
  where empirical ANcerno calibration yields $c_D \approx 1/3$ and $\tau_D \approx 50$ days. The asymptotic diffusivity $MSD(t) \propto t$ is preserved provided $c_D > 0$.

### Operational Alpha / Execution Signal Construction (`research-proposed`)

Based on the theoretical findings, an operational microstructure signal can be formulated to detect metaorder exhaustion and exploit the subsequent transient impact decay:
1. **Metaorder Impact Depletion Indicator ($\text{MIDI}_t$, `research-proposed`):**
   Track cumulative signed aggressor volume $Q_{\text{run}, t}$ within an identified directional order-flow run. Compute the estimated marginal impact:
   $$\text{MIDI}_t = \delta \cdot \left( \max(1, Q_{\text{run}, t}) \right)^{\delta - 1}, \quad \text{with fixed calibration } \delta = 0.50$$
2. **Exhaustion & Relaxation Entry Trigger (`research-proposed`):**
   - **Condition 1 (Volume Saturation):** Cumulative run volume exceeds the asset's estimated metaorder scale threshold ($Q_{\text{run}, t} \ge Q_{\text{thresh}}$, where $Q_{\text{thresh}}$ is set to the 80th percentile of rolling 24-hour directional clusters).
   - **Condition 2 (Marginal Impact Decay):** Marginal impact $\text{MIDI}_t \le \xi_{\text{crit}}$ while trade sign persistence begins to drop ($\hat{C}(\text{lag}=1) < 0.10$).
   - **Action:** Initiate a contrarian mean-reversion position opposite to the run sign $\epsilon_{\text{run}}$ (e.g. go Long if $\epsilon_{\text{run}} = -1$; go Short if $\epsilon_{\text{run}} = +1$), targeting capture of the transient relaxation wedge $\Delta p_{\text{decay}} \approx (1 - c_D) I(Q_{\text{run}})$.
3. **Exit Logic (`research-proposed`):**
   - **Profit Target:** Reversion equal to $(1 - c_D) \cdot c_0 \sqrt{Q_{\text{run}}}$, with $c_D = 0.33$.
   - **Time Stop:** Maximum holding period $T_{\text{hold}} = 3 \cdot \hat{\tau}_{\text{cluster}}$ to prevent holding through a new metaorder arrival.
   - **Adverse Stop:** Midprice moves against position by more than $0.5 \cdot c_0 \sqrt{Q_{\text{run}}}$ beyond the entry price.

## Required data

- **Primary Tick Feed:** Normalized tick-by-tick trade records including execution timestamp (millisecond or microsecond resolution), price, executed volume, and signed aggressor flag $\epsilon \in \{-1, +1\}$.
- **Order Book Depth:** Level-2 / Level-3 limit order book snapshots to calibrate local liquidity density $c_0$ and observe queue depletion.
- **Universe:** High-frequency equity shares (TSE / NASDAQ / NYSE) or liquid cryptocurrency perpetual futures / spot pairs (BTC, ETH, SOL).
- **Point-in-Time Alignment:** All indicator calculations ($\text{MIDI}_t$, $Q_{\text{run}, t}$) must be strictly computed from historical completed prints up to timestamp $t$; no future trade information or post-hoc metaorder boundaries may be used.
- **Missing Data Handling:** Gaps in the trade feed greater than 10 seconds reset the active run accumulator $Q_{\text{run}}$ to zero (`research-proposed`).

## Execution assumptions

- **Execution Model (`research-proposed`):** Passive limit order placed at the prevailing bid (for longs) or ask (for shorts) upon signal confirmation; unexecuted orders cancelled after 5 seconds.
- **Latency Budget (`research-proposed`):** 5 ms to 50 ms signal-to-order transit latency.
- **Transaction Costs (`research-proposed`):**
  - Maker fee: -0.005% to 0.010% (rebate or minimal cost);
  - Taker fee: 0.020% to 0.040% (if crossing the spread on stop exits);
  - Slippage model: Calibrated square-root slippage $\text{Slippage}(V) = c_0 \sqrt{V / \text{ADV}}$.
- **Capacity Constraint (`research-proposed`):** Strategy position size capped at 1.0% of rolling 5-minute traded volume to avoid self-generated market impact.

## Evidence

### Source-reported

All analytical theorems and empirical parameters trace directly to Sato, Fujiwara, and Kanazawa (`arXiv:2608.00988v1`, Sections I–VI, Appendices A–D):
- **Theorem 1 (Necessary & Sufficient Diffusive Condition, Section III.2.2 & IV.3):**
  In both discrete-time and continuous-time nonlinear LMF models, $\delta \le 1/2$ is the necessary and sufficient condition for price dynamics to exhibit normal diffusion ($MSD(t) \propto t$) across all order-flow long-memory exponents $\gamma \in (0, 1)$ ($\alpha \in (1, 2)$).
- **Theorem 2 (Short-Memory Return Condition, Section III.2.3 & IV.4):**
  The asymptotic return autocorrelation decays with exponent $\theta = \alpha + 1 - 2\delta$. For the square-root law $\delta = 1/2$, $\theta = \alpha > 1$, establishing that returns have integrable short memory and obey the Central Limit Theorem.
- **Empirical Validation of Square-Root Universality on TSE (Section I, citing Sato & Kanazawa 2025, Phys. Rev. Lett. 135, 257401):**
  In a complete microscopic survey of all liquid stocks on the Tokyo Stock Exchange (TSE), the measured price-impact exponent was universally $\hat{\delta} = 0.50$ within statistical error margins, while metaorder sizes followed power laws with $\hat{\alpha} \in (1.2, 1.8)$.
- **Empirical Impact Decay Calibration (Section V.2.1, citing ANcerno analysis by Bucci et al. 2018):**
  Post-execution price impact decays slowly with characteristic timescale $\tau_D \approx 50$ days, leaving a permanent price impact equal to approximately one-third of the peak impact ($c_D = I_\infty / I_{\text{peak}} \approx 1/3$).
- **Analytical Derivation of the Inverse Cubic Law (Appendix C.4):**
  Under inter-metaorder resting periods ($\tau_r \gtrsim t$), the return distribution exhibits an intermediate power-law tail $P_\ge(\Delta m) \propto (\Delta m)^{-\beta}$ with $\beta = \alpha / \delta \approx 1.5 / 0.5 = 3.0$, matching Gopikrishnan et al. (1998, 1999).
- **Numerical Verification of Invariance to Trader Count (Figures 6 & 14):**
  Simulations across $M = 1, 2, 5, 10, 100$ independent traders confirm that the asymptotic scaling exponent of $MSD(t)$ is completely invariant to the number of market participants $M$.

### Negative evidence

- **Propagator Model Contradiction with Zero Permanent Impact:** If the permanent impact is identically zero ($c_D = 0$), as proposed in pure transient propagator models (e.g. Maitrier & Bouchaud 2025, `arXiv:2506.07711`), the Sato–Fujiwara–Kanazawa model predicts subdiffusive price dynamics ($MSD(t) \propto t^\kappa$ with $\kappa < 1$) unless fine-tuned. The physical validity of $c_D > 0$ versus $c_D = 0$ remains actively debated in the econophysics literature.
- **Finite-Sample Truncation in Low-Liquidity Assets:** In illiquid markets with finite metaorder size truncations ($L_{\text{max}} < \infty$), the long-memory order-flow signature truncates into trivial short-range correlation, weakening the empirical distinction between linear and square-root impact regimes.
- **Transaction Cost Friction on Reversal Harvest:** In high-fee regimes (e.g. retail taker fees $\ge 0.05\%$), the expected relaxation margin $(1 - c_D) I(Q) \approx \frac{2}{3} I_{\text{peak}}$ is completely consumed by bid-ask spread and exchange fees, rendering the contrarian alpha non-tradable.

## Falsification plan

1. **Critical Exponent Boundary Test (`research-proposed`):**
   - Estimate the empirical price-impact exponent $\hat{\delta}$ via institutional metaorder logs and the order-sign decay exponent $\hat{\gamma}$ (or metaorder Pareto index $\hat{\alpha}$) on a high-frequency asset universe.
   - `research-defined falsification threshold`: If an asset exhibits empirical $\hat{\delta} \le 0.50$ but demonstrates superdiffusive price dynamics ($MSD(t) \propto t^{1+\mu}$ with $\mu > 0.10$, $p < 0.01$) or return long memory ($\hat{\theta} \le 1.0$), reject the Sato–Fujiwara–Kanazawa theoretical framework.
2. **Impact Relaxation vs Zero-Permanent Test (`research-proposed`):**
   - Measure post-execution price paths for isolated large metaorders across 1,000 liquid trade executions.
   - `research-defined falsification threshold`: If the estimated permanent impact ratio $\hat{c}_D := I_\infty / I_{\text{peak}}$ is statistically indistinguishable from zero ($\hat{c}_D < 0.05$ with $95\%$ confidence) while the underlying midprice remains diffusive, falsify Generalized Model II in favor of zero-permanent propagator models.
3. **Resting-Period Volatility Clustering Permutation Test (`research-proposed`):**
   - Test Generalized Model I's assertion that inter-metaorder resting periods $\tau_r$ generate return volatility clustering. Randomly shuffle the inter-metaorder resting intervals while preserving trade signs, child order sequences, and executed volumes.
   - `research-defined falsification threshold`: If empirical volatility clustering $C_{\sigma^2}(\tau)$ is unaltered by resting-interval permutation ($|\Delta \gamma_\sigma| < 0.05$ across lags $\tau \in [10, 1000]$), reject the hypothesis that resting states are the primary microscopic driver of volatility clustering.

## Crypto portability

- **Portability Status:** Adapted / Unproven (`research-proposed`).
- **Cryptocurrency Microstructure Nuances:**
  - *24/7 Continuous Trading:* Unlike equity exchanges with daily auctions and overnight halts, crypto spot and perpetual markets operate continuously. This provides an ideal continuous observation window for testing continuous-time Lévy-walk renewal dynamics without overnight boundary distortions.
  - *Order-Flow Composition (Bot/Retail Domination):* In crypto markets (Binance, OKX, Hyperliquid), a large portion of order flow is generated by programmatic grid bots, automated market makers, and retail momentum traders rather than institutional parent-metaorder execution desks. Consequently, the metaorder size distribution $\psi_m(L)$ may exhibit an earlier exponential truncation, altering the observed scaling regime.
  - *Perpetual Funding Rate Distortions:* Extreme funding rates ($>0.05\%$ per 8h) create exogenous asymmetric order flow that can temporarily bias the apparent impact exponent $\delta$, causing transient directional trending during liquidation cascades.
  - *Tick Size and Fee Inversion:* On exchanges with tight tick grids and VIP maker fee rebates, the relaxation wedge can be captured more effectively than on high-taker-fee retail venues.

## Limitations

- **Debatable Non-Zero Permanent Impact Assumption (`source-reported`):** The model fundamentally requires $c_D > 0$ to guarantee normal diffusion; if permanent impact is zero, the model degenerates into subdiffusion unless coupled with an ad-hoc propagator.
- **Homogeneous Trader Simplification (`source-reported`):** Assumes all $M$ traders have identical order intensity $\lambda = 1/M$ and identical metaorder power-law exponents $\alpha$. Real markets feature extreme heterogeneity in trader speed, capitalization, and latency.
- **Absence of Strategic Interaction (`source-reported`):** Traders are modeled as independent renewal processes, omitting predatory trading, front-running, and liquidity-providing quote cancellations.
- **Omission of Transaction Costs in Theoretical Framework (`source-reported`):** Theoretical derivations assume continuous price increments and zero fees/slippage.
