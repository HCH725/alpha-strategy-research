## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The authors' thesis is that anomaly returns start accruing *before* the accounting information that defines the anomaly is publicly released, because the signal itself is predictable from already-public quarterly data, and market participants under-react to that persistence (§I, §III.A). They frame it as a Samuelson (1965) "properly anticipated prices" test: if Q3 public information properly anticipates the Q4 signal, pre-release prices should not drift — they find they do (§III.A, Figure 1, Table II). The paper explicitly tests three candidate mechanisms (§III.D.3):

1. **Rational inattention / costly information processing (Caplin & Dean 2015):** splitting the 28 anomalies by martingale F1 score (mean 0.70; hard = F1 < 0.70 → 10 hard, 18 easy), pre-release drift concentrates in the hard-to-predict group. Perfect foresight, three months early: early period hard **368 bps** vs easy **233 bps**; recent period hard **263 bps** vs easy **4 bps** (Table X Panel A). Under the martingale model the recent-period three-month-early returns are hard **−1 bp**, easy **−30 bps** (Table X Panel B). Robust to median/tercile F1 cuts, persistence cuts, and two accounting-complexity scores (§III.D.3, Internet Appendix G, Table AIX, Table AX, Figures A8–A12).
2. **Career concerns / investment horizon constraints:** with one-quarter-ahead returns competed away, drift has migrated earlier in event time. In the recent period, a **two-quarter-ahead (Q2) martingale** earns economically meaningful pre-release returns — **approximately 2% for both easy and hard groups over the six months pre-release**, entirely inside the **−120 to −60 trading-day** window rather than the final 60 days (§III.D.3, Figure 12).
3. **Short-sale constraints are *not* the primary driver:** repeating the event-time analysis after excluding the lowest institutional-ownership decile (Nagel 2005 proxy) leaves the pre-release drift intact for medium/high-IO stocks under both perfect foresight and martingale specifications (§III.D.3, Figure 13). The authors conclude short-sale frictions may amplify magnitudes at the constrained tail but do not generate the drift.

The paper also argues the findings are inconsistent with data-mining and pure-risk explanations of anomalies, because returns line up with *when the information arrives* and are largest for stocks whose inclusion was genuinely new (false negatives) (§III.D.1, §IV).

### Research interpretation

- **Hypothesis in falsifiable form:** public, persistent-but-under-anticipated accounting data creates a *pre-release* drift; a portfolio formed on a predicted Q4 anomaly ranking 60 trading days before the annual information release date earns a positive long-short return that survives realistic costs.
- **Component roles (hybrid structure as normalized):**
  - **Event / regime anchor:** annual *information release date* = earliest date at which **all** inputs needed for the anomaly are public, taken from Compustat Snapshot effective dates (latest item wins for composite measures) (§II.B, footnote 14).
  - **Primary signal:** martingale carry-forward `E[x_Q4 | x_Q1..x_Q3] = x_Q3` — use the Q3 year-to-date anomaly decile rank as the predicted Q4 annual rank (Eq. 2, §III.C).
  - **Prediction alternatives (also source-specified):** AR Signals and AR Rankings (Eq. 1, rolling training window to avoid look-ahead), a LightGBM classifier with a fixed ex-ante feature list (§III.C, footnote 19–21), and a persistence-based benchmark that keeps a stock in its Q3 leg with probability equal to that anomaly's Q3→Q4 persistence (§III.C, footnote 18).
  - **Entry:** form the predicted top/bottom-decile long-short portfolio **60 trading days (three months) before** the release date (§III.A); shorter horizons (−1, −3 days, −1 week, −2 weeks, −1 month, −2 months) are also reported (Table VII).
  - **Exit / continuation:** hold across the release date; the paper's combined construction rebalances on the actual signal after release and holds three further months (248 bps over the six-month window, Table VII footnote 26).
  - **Risk management:** **none is specified by the source** — no stop, no volatility targeting, no position limits, no beta neutralization beyond the long-short decile construction. This absence is recorded, not repaired.
- **Do not assume every component is alpha:** the source's own error decomposition shows the *false-positive* leg loses (−60 bps over three months pre-release) and the *false-negative* leg — the part the model could not predict — earns the most (297 bps) (Table VIII). The predictable-and-correct leg earns 127 bps. Perfect foresight (171 bps) is a non-tradable ceiling, not a strategy.

## Signal

All items are `source-reported` unless explicitly marked `research-proposed`, `underspecified`, or `data gap`.

- **Formation timestamp / availability:** the prediction input is the **Q3 year-to-date anomaly signal** (or Q2 for the two-quarter-ahead variant), computed from that quarter's financial statements; the target event is the annual *information release date* defined by Compustat Snapshot `Effective Date` — the date Compustat recorded the new fundamental value as public (§II.A, Internet Appendix B). Snapshot granularity caveat stated by the source: **weekly production before December 2008, at least daily thereafter**; earliest effective date 1986-12-14, which is why the sample starts in 1990 (Internet Appendix B). Timezone/session: US equity trading days (CRSP daily); exact intraday convention is `not stated in source`.
- **Lookback / sampling:** quarterly year-to-date construction of each anomaly (not year-over-year; §II.C — the authors state year-over-year gave lower predictive power with unchanged inferences, §III preamble); rolling/expanding training windows for AR and ML models using data up to year *t* only, to prevent look-ahead (§III.C); event windows are **60 trading days** before and after the release date for the main tables, **120 trading days** before for the Q2/six-month analysis (Figure 9, Figure 12). Endpoints inclusive as reported in compound event-time returns; warm-up: sample starts 1990 to allow multi-year anomaly inputs (§II.A).
- **Long entry / short entry:** predicted top decile → long leg, predicted bottom decile → short leg of each of the **28 anomalies**; the *Average anomaly* portfolio combines all 28 (§II.C, §III.A). Ranking is within the eligible December-FYE cross-section.
- **Prediction rules (parameters, source-fixed):**
  - *Martingale* (headline): Q3 decile rank carries forward as the Q4 prediction — `E[x₄,t | x₁,t…x₃,t] = x₃,t` (Eq. 2).
  - *AR Signals / AR Rankings*: `x₄,t = δ + φ₁x₃,t + φ₂x₄,t₋₁ + w₄,t` (Eq. 1), estimated in a rolling window on data through year *t*.
  - *Machine learning*: **LightGBM classifier**, features fixed ex ante = Q3 anomaly rank, Q2 anomaly rank, Q2×Q3 rank interaction, squared Q3 and Q2 ranks, log total assets, Q3-rank × log-assets interaction, Q3−Q2 rank difference, industry; no cross-validation or hyperparameter search, defaults retained, class weights 1 (class 0) / 2 (class 1); XGBoost tried with nearly identical predictions but less efficiency (§III.C, footnotes 19–21); class-weight grid and value-weighting variants in Internet Appendix D (Table AIV, Tables AI–AIII).
  - *Persistence benchmark*: keep Q3 leg membership with probability = that anomaly's Q3→Q4 persistence, entering stocks otherwise with probability `(1−p)/9` so the leg stays 10% (§III.C, footnote 18).
- **Exit / holding period:** main event-time tables measure compound returns over the stated pre-release windows; the implementable construction holds from entry (−60 trading days) **through** the release date and, in the combined strategy, rebalances on the realized signal and holds three more months (Table VII footnote 26: 70 + 178 = 248 bps). Re-entry: annual event, once per firm-year (overlapping firm events coexist). **Portfolio turnover, average holding period, and the treatment of overlapping event windows are `not stated in source` (`data gap`).**
- **Weighting:** F1 scores are equal-weighted in the headline tables with value-weighted robustness (Internet Appendix D); **whether the reported event-time *returns* are equal- or value-weighted is `not stated in source` (`data gap`)** — Figures 13 and Table X refer to "long-short hedge returns" without a weighting statement.
- **Fully specified vs underspecified:** the prediction rules, horizons, decile construction, event-date definition and sample are reconstructable. `underspecified`: return weighting, turnover/overlap mechanics, delisting and missing-data handling, universe/liquidity screens, order and fill conventions, and any cost assumption (see Execution assumptions).

## Execution assumptions

- **Signal-to-order timing:** entry is defined at **60 trading days before the annual information release date** (event-time convention). The source does not specify order type, same-day versus next-day execution, fill price, latency, or partial-fill handling — **`not stated in source`**.
- **Fees / spread / slippage / impact / capacity:** **no model, no rate, and no net/gross statement anywhere in the pinned version** (main text, Tables I–X, Internet Appendices A–G) → **`data gap` / `not stated in source`**. Reported returns must therefore be treated as *unverified as to gross-vs-net*.
- **Borrow / shorting:** the strategy is a two-sided decile long-short. Borrow availability and borrow fees are **not modeled**; the paper uses institutional ownership only to *test whether short-sale constraints explain the drift* (Figure 13), not to establish implementability → `not stated in source`.
- **Leverage / margin:** not stated in source.
- **Research-proposed execution assumptions (clearly labeled, not from the source):** a `research-proposed` cost ladder of **10 / 20 / 40 bp one-way** applied to estimated turnover; a `research-proposed` feasibility gate requiring the Q3 statement's Snapshot effective date to precede the entry date (see Falsification plan item 2); a `research-proposed` liquidity screen excluding the bottom decile by dollar volume before sizing the short leg. ### Negative evidence

- **The implementable edge is gone in the paper's own recent sample:** martingale three-month-early = **−19 bps**, ML = **−7 bps** for 2007–2023 (Table IX Panel A); even the one-month-early window is +7 / +12 bps before any cost. The full-sample +70 bps is carried by 1990–2006 (**198 bps**, Table IX). Source's own reading: "returns … have been arbitraged away in recent years" (§III.D.2).
- **Prediction errors are where the money is:** false positives lose **−60 bps** full-sample and **−138 bps** in the recent period over three months (Tables VIII, IX Panel B), while false negatives — stocks the model cannot predict — earn **297 bps** full sample / **268 bps** recent (Tables VIII, IX). A tradable implementation captures 127 bps (correct leg) net of a −60 bp FP drag, not 297.
- **Perfect foresight is not tradable:** the 171 bps / 7.10% headline for one-quarter-early trading assumes knowledge of the future signal (Table II); it bounds, but cannot be implemented.
- **No cost, spread, slippage, borrow, fill or net/gross treatment anywhere in the pinned document** → the 70 bps / 2.80% headline is unverified as to net-of-cost (`data gap`). With decile long-short turnover twice a year plus stock-selection churn, 2.80% annualized has very little headroom against even a 10–20 bp one-way drag (`research-proposed` observation, not a source claim).
- **Version drift on the same source identity:** the August 2024 version (directly read, SHA-256 `740d43fa…1df8`) headlines **3.65% annualized (ML)** with a **1990–2019** sample and 1990–2004/2005–2005-style splits (1990–2004 vs 2005–2019); the January 2025 revision abstract headlines **2.80%**; the April 2026 pin headlines **2.80% (martingale)** with a **1990–2023** sample. Extending the sample by four years roughly halved the headline and flipped the recent-period prediction-model returns negative. Any citation of this work must state which version it uses (`data gap` if unspecified).
- **Selection/multiple-testing exposure:** 28 anomalies × 4 models + benchmark × 7 horizons × 2 periods × easy/hard splits, all on one sample with no holdout and no deflated-Sharpe / family-wise correction reported (`data gap`); the easy/hard cutoff at F1 = 0.70 is the sample mean (§III.D.3), i.e. an in-sample cut, though robustness to median/tercile cuts is reported (Internet Appendix G).

## Falsification plan

All thresholds and acceptance rules below are **`research-defined falsification thresholds` (Scout-authored, not from the source)**; all operational choices not stated by the source are **`research-proposed`**.

1. **Point-in-time replication, then genuine out-of-sample.** Rebuild the 28-anomaly panel 1990–2023 with Compustat Snapshot effective dates, reproduce Table VII within ±10 bps at the three-month horizon (research-defined tolerance), then evaluate 2024-01 → 2026-12 untouched. **Fail rule:** if the martingale three-month-early compound return is ≤ 0 on the 2024+ holdout, the "still tradable" claim is rejected (the paper's own 2007–2023 result already suggests failure).
2. **Data-availability feasibility gate (`research-proposed`).** For every firm-year, require `Q3 effective date ≤ entry date (release date − 60 trading days)`. If fewer than **90%** of events pass (research-defined), re-anchor entry to `max(−60 td, Q3 effective date + 1 trading day)` and re-measure; **fail implementability** if the re-anchored return is ≤ 0 after costs.
3. **Cost stress (`research-proposed` rates).** Report turnover of the −60-day → +60-day construction, then charge **10 / 20 / 40 bp one-way**. **Fail rule:** if net three-month-early return ≤ 0 at **20 bp** (research-defined), reject any net-of-cost claim; the record's 2.80% gross headline has <100 bp/yr of headroom.
4. **Placebo / shuffled-signal test.** Replace the martingale ranking with 1,000 independently shuffled cross-sectional pseudo-rankings (same decile sizes, same event dates). **Fail rule:** the real martingale three-month-early return must exceed the **95th percentile** of the placebo distribution (research-defined); otherwise the result is event-window arithmetic, not signal.
5. **Ablation of the hybrid.** Compare persistence benchmark (0.50 F1) → AR → LightGBM → martingale at identical horizons, and hard-vs-easy groups at the source's F1 = 0.70 cut plus median/tercile cuts. **Fail rule:** if the martingale does not beat the persistence benchmark by **> 0 bps** at three months in the holdout (research-defined), the "prediction" layer adds nothing over mechanical persistence.
6. **Borrow / short-leg implementability (`research-proposed`).** Re-run excluding the lowest institutional-ownership decile *and* any name without a documented borrow source, with the short leg charged a borrow fee ladder of 0/50/100 bp per year. **Fail rule:** return ≤ 0 at 50 bp/yr → reject as short-leg-infeasible.
7. **Sub-period stability.** Require non-negative three-month-early martingale returns in **each** of 2007–2015 and 2016–2023 (research-defined). Current Table IX evidence fails this on the combined 2007–2023 window (−19 bps), which would classify the effect as historical/regime-dependent rather than live.
8. **Multiple-testing discipline.** Compute a deflated Sharpe ratio with trial count = 28 anomalies × 4 models × 7 horizons (research-defined K). **Fail rule:** DSR < 0.95 → treat the full-sample result as selection-contaminated.
9. **Version-consistency re-check.** Before any downstream use, re-read the published JFE version if/when it appears; **fail rule:** if the three-month-early headline differs from the pinned 70 bps by **> 25% relative** (research-defined), this record must be updated or superseded before citing any number.
10. **Cross-market portability is out of scope for this source** — no non-US replication is claimed; a claim of generality requires a separate primary source.

## Crypto portability

**unproven**

- The mechanism is anchored to a data regime crypto does not have: discrete, audited **quarterly/annual GAAP statements** with point-in-time line-item release dating (Compustat Snapshot) and 28 pre-defined accounting anomalies. No token, chain, or venue publishes this; protocol treasuries, unlock schedules and exchange proof-of-reserves are *different* signals requiring their own primary sources before any analogy is evidence.

- Nothing in the source is crypto evidence: universe is US equities, weekly-to-daily Snapshot dating, T-bill-free event-time long-short with no funding line. **This record must not be cited as support for a crypto strategy.**
