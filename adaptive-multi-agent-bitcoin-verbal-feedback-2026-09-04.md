## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The paper hypothesizes that Bitcoin's price dynamics are driven primarily by non-fundamental forces—regulatory announcements, influencer statements, speculative fear/greed cycles—making static regression or neural-network models trained solely on historical data insufficient. LLMs can synthesize heterogeneous inputs (technical indicators, news sentiment, market regime) and reason over them in context. The proposed verbal feedback loop allows the system to adapt without retraining: a Reflect agent critiques past trading decisions in natural language, and these critiques are injected into future prompts, adjusting indicator priorities, sentiment weights, and allocation logic.

### Research interpretation

The hypothesized alpha mechanism is **in-context adaptive weight reallocation via natural-language self-critique**. Specifically:

1. **Regime-conditioned signal mixing:** Multiple specialized agents (Quantitative, Sentiment, Decision) generate independent signals. A weighting function combines them, with weights modulated by the verbal feedback from the Reflect agent.
2. **In-context learning as parameter adjustment:** Rather than fine-tuning model weights, the system injects textual critiques into the prompt context, exploiting the LLM's in-context learning ability to adjust decision policies at inference time.
3. **Regime decomposition:** The system separates bullish, bearish, and sideways regimes and applies different agent weight profiles to each.

The core falsifiable claim: verbal self-critique injected into LLM prompts produces statistically significant outperformance over the same system without feedback, across market regimes, after transaction costs.

## Evidence

### Source-reported

The paper backtests on Bitcoin price data from July 2024 to April 2025 (~10 months):

- **Quantitative agent:** Over 30% higher returns in bullish phases; 15% overall gains versus buy-and-hold baseline.
- **Sentiment agent:** Turned sideways markets from a small loss into a gain of over 100%.
- **Weekly feedback:** Improved total performance by 31% and reduced bearish losses by 10%.
- All results are source-reported and have not been independently reproduced.
- The paper states these are backtested results; no live trading or paper trading is reported.
- The paper does not report Sharpe ratio, maximum drawdown, or other risk-adjusted metrics in the abstract. Full metrics may be in tables not accessible during this scout run.

## Falsification plan

1. **Out-of-sample test:** Extend the backtest to at least 3 years covering multiple BTC cycles (bull, bear, sideways). If the verbal-feedback advantage disappears out-of-sample, the mechanism is likely overfit.
2. **Ablation on feedback:** Run the same system with (a) no feedback, (b) random feedback, (c) fixed feedback. The verbal-feedback mechanism should outperform (a) and (b) but may not outperform (c) if the content of the critique is not informative.
3. **Cost sensitivity:** Re-run with realistic taker fees (0.04–0.05%), spread, and slippage. If net Sharpe collapses, the source-reported alpha is not tradeable.
4. **Regime decomposition:** Test whether the regime-specific weighting actually improves over a single global weight. If regime classification adds no value, the regime component is decorative.
5. **LLM backbone substitution:** Replace DeepSeek-r1 with other LLMs (GPT-4, Claude, open-source). If performance is backbone-dependent, the mechanism may be an artifact of a specific model's in-context learning quirks.
6. **Alternative universe:** Test on ETH or a basket of top-10 crypto assets. If the mechanism is general, it should generalize beyond BTC.
7. **Failure threshold:** If the verbal-feedback variant does not achieve statistically significant outperformance (t-test, p < 0.05) over the no-feedback baseline across at least 2 non-overlapping 6-month windows, the mechanism is not validated.
