## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The source's mechanism is **correlation neglect** applied to the U.S. corporate earnings calendar (Sections 1, 3.1, 4):

- Fiscal quarters are aligned to calendar quarters (Table 1 Panel A: 86.0% of Compustat fiscal quarters end on the last day of Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec, 1971-2023), while announcement lags are heterogeneous. Among on-cycle announcements, **44.7% fall in the first month of a quarter, 47.7% in the second month, 7.6% in the third** (Table 1 Panel B, total 800,939 announcements, 1971-2023).
- The source names the **first month of a quarter a "newsy" month** (first fresh aggregate earnings news about the prior fiscal quarter) and the **second month a "repetitive" month** (announcements pertain to the same fiscal quarter, so aggregate "news" is predictably repetitive); the third month contains substantially fewer on-cycle announcements, so repetition is muted and those months are excluded from the regression design.
- **Model (Section 4):** a three-period economy (newsy → repetitive → next newsy), risk-neutral investors, zero risk-free rate, uniform prior over the productivity probability `p`, Laplace's rule of succession for the price. With probability `p_x` the second-period signal is a literal repeat of the first. A **rational** investor earns `Cov(R1,R2)=0` and `E[R3R2]=0`; an investor with **correlation neglect** double-counts the repeated signal, so `E[R2R1] > 0` (Equation 4) and `E[R3R2] < 0` (Equation 5), and both effects increase in `p_x` and are stronger when the two signals are similar (Equations 6-7). Appendix B derives the same pricing rule from retrieved-context memory (Howard and Kahana 2002; Wachter and Kahana 2024): repeated items encoded in different temporal contexts accumulate as if independent.
- **Stated economic channel:** investors overreact to predictably repetitive aggregate earnings news in the second month of a quarter (continuation arm), and the overreaction is corrected when the next quarter's fresh earnings arrive (reversal arm). Put compactly by the source: *"a newsy month's return positively predicts the next repetitive month's return, which then negatively predicts the next newsy month's return."*
- The source explicitly separates this from **Guo (2025) "parameter compression"** (sum of the past four newsy-month returns): controlling for that predictor (Table 3), both mechanisms remain significant, and the source states parameter compression accounts for "roughly half" of the continuation arm and only "a trace" of the reversal arm.
- The source additionally rules out, or argues against, several alternatives in Section 6: strategic timing of announcements (6.1), time-varying risk (6.2), and different clienteles/diffusion in newsy vs. repetitive months (6.3). It concedes one version of the clientele story "is in fact correlation neglect."
- Non-trading supporting tests the source reports as mechanism evidence: stronger pattern when announced ROE in the two months is more similar (Table 5), weaker pattern when the predictor month's return is driven by non-fundamental shocks (Table 6), belief revisions in IBES long-term-growth forecasts showing same-direction continuation and opposite-direction correction (Table 7), the two arms co-moving across years (Table 8), absence of the pattern in the first week of newsy months and no concentration on macroeconomic announcement days (Figure 4b, Section 5.4), and the same pattern in industry excess returns with stronger effects in industries with more constituents plus fake-industry placebos (Tables 9-10).

### Research interpretation

- **Hypothesized tradable mechanism:** a calendar-anchored behavioral mispricing at the **aggregate equity market** level — overreaction to repeated aggregate earnings news in month 2 of a quarter, reversed in month 1 of the next quarter — producing **sign-alternating first-order autocorrelation of monthly market excess returns conditioned on month-of-quarter**, with zero average market exposure. This is a falsifiable market-timing hypothesis, not a cross-sectional factor.
- The two arms are best treated as **one hybrid signal** (regime = calendar month class; primary signal = deviation of the most recent newsy/repetitive month's excess return from its expanding mean, sign-flipped depending on the target month class; risk/overlay = volatility targeting with a leverage cap). The source does not report an ablation of the arms inside a live portfolio, so it is an open question whether the reversal arm alone or both arms together carry the economic value after costs ⇒ research question, not a source claim.
- The mechanism depends on a **corporate earnings announcement calendar**, so the hypothesis is about institutional market structure, not about price series per se. Whether the same calendar structure exists in any other asset class (including crypto) is untested by the source.
- **Not established by the source:** causal identification (the paper is observational plus a theoretical model), the size of any tradable net-of-cost return, capacity, and post-2024 generalization.

## Signal

All parameters below are **source-reported** unless explicitly labeled otherwise.

- **Formation timestamp:** monthly, formed at the **previous month-end (`t-1`)** and applied to target month `t`. The source's Table 8 note: the weight "is proportional to the previous-month-end predictor values `s_{t-1}`" and "the market weight is then scaled so that the strategy has a historical volatility of 5% **as of `t-1`**." Section 3.3 confirms the forecast uses "information up to month `t-1`." **Timezone, closing-bell convention, and exact timestamp of the month-end observation are `data gap` / `not stated in source`** (a US-equity month-end close is implied by CRSP monthly data but never stated).
- **Calendar labeling (source-reported, Section 3.1-3.2):** `newsy` months = Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct; `repetitive` months = Feb/May/Aug/Nov; third months (Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec) are excluded from regressions and get **zero weight** in the strategy. `nr(t)` = the most recent month strictly before `t` that is newsy or repetitive (source's example: `t`=February ⇒ `nr(t)`=January; `t`=April ⇒ `nr(t)`=February).
- **Predictor (Section 3.3 and Table 4 note, re-read in layout mode):** `z_{t-1} ≡ mkt_{nr(t)} − mkt̄_{t-1}` for a **repetitive** target `t`, and `z_{t-1} ≡ −(mkt_{nr(t)} − mkt̄_{t-1})` for a **newsy** target `t`, where `mkt_{nr(t)}` is that month's market excess return and **`mkt̄_{t-1}` is the expanding-window mean of past market excess returns up to month `t-1`** (defined verbatim in Section 3.3). Forecast for month `t`: `mkt̂_t = β̂_{t-1} z_{t-1} + mkt̄_{t-1}`, with `β̂_{t-1}` and `α̂_{t-1}` from an expanding-window OLS of historical market excess returns on historical predictor values and a constant using data up to `t-1`.
  - *Research interpretation of an ambiguous notation:* the Table 8 strategy note re-prints the predictor as `mkt_{nr(t)} − mkt_{t-1}` without restating the "expanding window mean" definition. A literal "last month's return" reading would make the predictor **identically zero for every repetitive month** (because for a repetitive `t`, `nr(t) = t−1`), so the centered reading above is the only coherent one. This reading is **research interpretation**, not a verbatim source statement.
- **Long entry:** target month is newsy and `z_{t-1}` predicts a positive month ⇒ long the aggregate market; target month is repetitive and `z_{t-1}` ⇒ long when the signed predictor is positive. **Short entry:** the symmetric negative case (the source states the portfolio "takes long or short positions in the aggregate market for a given month").
- **Position sizing (Table 8 note, source-reported):** weight **proportional** to `s_{t-1}`; then **scaled so the strategy has a historical volatility of 5% as of `t-1`**; then **truncated at `+3` and `−3`**. The proportionality constant, the exact volatility estimator (window, estimator type) and the normalization that makes the weight "proportional" are **`underspecified`** in this source — the source says the construction is "based on the method that Guo and Wachter (2025) develop" (SSRN 5808182, *Forecast-Agnostic Portfolios*), which is a different paper this Scout did not open.
- **Exit / holding period / rebalance:** **one-month holding, monthly rebalance** (per-month weight applied to each month's market return; the strategy is described month by month). **No stop-loss, no take-profit, no time-based exit beyond the monthly re-weighting is specified**; any such rule would be `research-proposed`.
- **Flat period:** third month of each quarter ⇒ weight 0 (source-reported: "and 0 otherwise"). The source also describes the strategy as "beta-neutral" in Section 3.2 with "a zero equity position on average" — this refers to the average exposure being approximately zero because the predictor is demeaned; it is **not** a same-month long/short hedge.
- **Signals/parameters that are NOT in the source and must not be attributed to it:** any `|z|` entry threshold, minimum-|z| filter, earnings-similarity gate as a tradable filter (Table 5 is an analysis, not a trading rule), non-fundamental-shock gate as a tradable filter (Table 6 likewise), transaction-cost-based entry veto, position limits other than the ±3 truncation, stop rules, portfolio construction across the industry leg, and any crypto port rule — all **`research-proposed`**.
- **Reproducibility status of the rule:** the sign structure, calendar classes, volatility target, leverage truncation and expanding-window estimation are exact enough to reconstruct the research backtest; the weight normalization and full no-look-ahead implementation are **`underspecified`** (delegated to a paper not included here) and the execution convention is **`data gap`**.

### Negative evidence

1. **No transaction-cost model exists anywhere in the source** (Methods-level read plus full-text word scan of the 66-page pinned PDF). Commission, fees, spread, slippage, impact, participation, capacity, borrow, financing, leverage, margin, latency, fill and turnover are all `data gap` — **never zero** — so the reported in-sample Sharpe "around 0.4" and all Table 4/8 results cannot be read as net-of-cost evidence.
2. **The strategy's turnover is never reported and never defined**, even though the design implies a full monthly re-weighting of a ±3-truncated market position. Cost sensitivity is therefore unmeasured.
3. **No printed performance table for the strategy:** mean return, volatility, Sharpe, hit rate and maximum drawdown of the time-series portfolio are absent from the source; Figure 4a/4b bars carry no printed numbers ⇒ `data gap`.
4. **Effect size decays over the sample:** difference coefficient −0.686 (1926-1973) → −0.341 (1974-2024) → −0.291 (post-WWII); continuation 0.358 → 0.149 → 0.129. The second-half and post-WWII magnitudes are roughly half the first-half ones.
5. **The reversal arm is not significant internationally:** Table E2 column 1 gives −0.046 (t=−1.15); only the difference (and the continuation arm) are significant across the 24 developed markets.
6. **The industry reversal arm is not significant in the first half:** Table 9 Panel B −0.067 (t=−1.36).
7. **The pattern is concentrated in one cell:** Table 5's effect lives only in the most-similar earnings decile (**N=42 months**), and the source itself states this comparison "has limited power."
8. **The effect disappears when the predictor month is driven by non-fundamental shocks:** Table 6 with control gives **+0.021 (t=0.08)** in the high half — i.e. the signal quality is conditional on an unobservable-in-advance classification.
9. **Mechanisms are not cleanly separated:** the source states Guo (2025) parameter compression "accounts for roughly half" of the continuation arm and implies only "a trace" of the reversal; both are estimated in the same sample (Table 3), so attribution is partly joint.
10. **No out-of-sample strategy return series is reported.** Table 4 evaluates forecasts, not realized portfolio returns; the only strategy Sharpe stated (~0.4) is explicitly **in-sample**.
11. **Economically implausible forecasts are part of the result:** the OOS forecast implies the market is expected to underperform cash in **24% of months**, a feature the source defends rather than resolves; Campbell and Thompson (2008), cited by the source, warn such negative expanding-window forecasts are often noise.
12. **Absolute explanatory power is small:** OOS R² of 2.75% (reversal) to 6.95% (combined) leaves **>93% of monthly variance unexplained**; the Goyal-Welch critique the source itself invokes applies.
13. **No multiplicity correction anywhere:** Tables 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, E2 plus Figures 3-5 are run without any Bonferroni/FDR control, and several sub-results are marginal (Table 3 Guo-predictor reversal t=−1.87; Table 10 Panel A1 low-group t=−1.67; Table 9 first-half reversal t=−1.36).
14. **Standard-error convention is inconsistent across tables:** White (Tables 2, 3, 5, 6, 8), Newey-West (Table 7), month-clustered (Tables 9, 10, E2). No HAC correction is reported for the headline Table 2/3 monthly return regressions ⇒ the headline t-statistics may be optimistic if residual autocorrelation/heteroskedasticity is not fully captured by White SEs.
15. **Label look-back:** newsy/repetitive labels are inferred from a 1971-2023 accounting sample and applied to return data starting in **1926**; the source acknowledges this and argues stability, but no point-in-time labeling test is run.
16. **No code, no data, no replication package:** all availability fields are `not stated in source`; the data are licensed vendors, so independent reproduction is expensive by construction.
17. **Not peer reviewed** (NBER working paper cover statement) and no journal publication is indicated on the landing page (no `journal_ref`-equivalent, no Other Versions block).
18. **Sample ends in 2024** while the paper is dated September 2026 ⇒ no evidence for 2025-2026, a period the record cannot speak to.
19. **Leverage to ±3 with no margin/financing/borrow model:** the design can be 3× long or 3× short the market, and the short leg's borrow/financing cost is entirely unmodeled.
20. **Execution convention unstated:** no statement of order type, month-end close vs next open, latency, partial fills, or failure handling ⇒ realized returns could differ materially from the monthly return series used.
21. **Alternative explanations are argued, not eliminated:** Section 6 rebuts strategic announcement timing, time-varying risk and clientele/diffusion stories by reasoning and by one expected-return-frequency plot, not by a horse race with priced risk factors or by placebo calendar anchors.
22. **International implementation caveats conceded by the source:** Australia operates on a semi-annual reporting cycle (the source keeps the quarterly recipe anyway "for simplicity"), and pre-1974 international results "should be interpreted with caution."
23. **No capacity/liquidity analysis:** no participation rate, no ADV constraint, no AUM sensitivity, no market-impact estimate.

## Falsification plan

All thresholds and cutoffs below are **`research-defined falsification threshold`** (chosen by this Scout, not by the source). All operational rules not printed in the source are **`research-proposed`**.

- **F1 — Frozen forward replication (primary test).** Data: monthly US market excess returns for 2025-01 through 2026-12, never used by the source. Rule: extend the expanding-window OLS and the signed predictor exactly as specified, no re-tuning. Metric: out-of-sample R² (both arms) and the difference coefficient. **Fail if** OOS R² ≤ 0 over the extension **or** the difference coefficient ≥ 0. **Action:** report the hypothesis as not generalizing and stop treating it as a candidate.
- **F2 — Cost ladder (decisive for tradability).** First measure one-way monthly turnover of the TSP from absolute weight changes (`research-proposed`). Then apply 0/1/5/10/20/30 bp one-way plus a separate commission schedule. **Fail if** the strategy's Sharpe falls by more than 50% at 10 bp one-way, or if the sign of mean monthly return flips at or below 10 bp.
- **F3 — Turnover and capacity audit.** Compute average/median one-way monthly turnover, then cap positions at 10% (and 20%) of the daily volume of the chosen index instrument. **Fail if** the ±3 weight cannot be achieved within the 10% participation cap, or if required turnover exceeds 500% per year of the instrument's traded volume.
- **F4 — Decisive mechanism ablation.** Run the three-way comparison of Table 3 on a held-out window: (i) correlation-neglect predictor alone, (ii) Guo-2025 sum-of-four-newsy-months predictor alone, (iii) both. **Fail if** after adding (ii), the correlation-neglect difference coefficient has |t| < 1.96 or changes sign.
- **F5 — Calendar placebo / circular shift.** Re-anchor the newsy/repetitive calendar by k ∈ {1..11} months, preserving the 4-4-4 structure, 1000 re-anchored draws (and as a second null, circularly shift the monthly return series). **Fail if** the true anchor's |difference| is not above the 95th percentile of the null distribution.
- **F6 — Earnings-similarity dose-response.** Reproduce Table 5's decile monotonicity on refreshed data (1971 → present). **Fail if** the most-similar decile coefficient is not more negative than the least-similar decile with a difference significant at |t| ≥ 1.96, i.e. the source's own mechanism signature fails.
- **F7 — Within-quarter timing placebo.** Recreate the daily decomposition: the pattern must be absent in the first week of newsy months and must not be stronger on macroeconomic announcement days. **Fail if** the first week of the newsy month carries a significant (|t| ≥ 1.96) portion of the effect, or if macro-announcement days carry more of it than non-announcement days.
- **F8 — Risk-based competing-explanation horse race (`research-proposed`).** Add standard monthly risk controls (market variance, downside semivariance, macro predictors) to the Table 2/3 regressions. **Fail if** the difference coefficient shrinks by more than 50% and becomes insignificant (|t| < 1.96).
- **F9 — Multiplicity control.** Apply Benjamini-Hochberg at q < 0.10 across the full battery of headline tests (Table 2 panels A-D, Table 3, Table 5, Table 6, Table 9 panels A-D, Table 10 A1/A2, Table E2 panels A-D). **Fail if** the headline difference coefficients do not survive.
- **F10 — Subperiod stability.** Require the difference coefficient to be negative and significant (|t| ≥ 1.96) in at least 3 of 4 subperiods: 1926-1973, 1974-2024, 1990-2024, 2010-2024. **Fail otherwise.** (Note the source already documents magnitude decay — see Negative evidence 4.)
- **F11 — International transport.** Replicate Table E2 per country with each country's own announcement calendar. **Fail if** the reversal arm is insignificant in more than 75% of the 24 markets, or if the pooled reversal arm remains insignificant as in the source's own column 1.
- **F12 — Fake-industry placebo integrity.** Rerun the Table 10 Panel B randomizations with ≥1000 draws. **Fail if** the genuine-industry coefficient does not fall beyond the 95th percentile of the null.
- **F13 — Reproducibility gate.** Either obtain the authors' code/replication data or rebuild from CRSP/Compustat/IBES. **Fail (as "not reproducible") if** Table 2's three coefficients cannot be reproduced within ±0.05 and Table 4's OOS R² within ±0.5 percentage points.
- **F14 — Post-2024 sample hygiene.** Any extension must keep 2025+ fully untouched during specification. **Fail if** any threshold, window, or calendar rule is revised after observing 2025-2026 data.
- **F15 — Crypto port gate (`research-proposed`, only if a port is ever attempted).** Require Newey-West |t| ≥ 1.96 on the difference coefficient **and** a positive net-of-10 bp Sharpe ≥ 0.30 over ≥ 3 years of crypto data. **Fail otherwise** and keep portability at `unproven`.

## Crypto portability

**unproven.**

- The source contains **zero crypto evidence**: no crypto instrument, no exchange, no perpetual/futures contract, no funding, no 24/7 session, no on-chain data.
- The mechanism is anchored to a **corporate earnings announcement calendar** (aligned fiscal quarters + heterogeneous filing lags). Crypto has no equivalent scheduled aggregate cash-flow reporting cycle, so the newsy/repetitive month labels have **no defined counterpart**; mapping them onto a proxy (e.g., quarterly results of crypto-exposed listed companies, or exchange "quarterly" events) would be an entirely new hypothesis and is **`research-proposed`**, not a port.
- Market structure differences that would have to be re-derived: 24/7 sessions vs monthly candles with exchange-specific boundaries; funding and mark/index price; leverage and liquidation mechanics (the source's ±3 weight has no margin model even for equities); venue fragmentation and per-venue seasonality; borrow availability for any short leg; spot vs perpetual differences; custody/withdrawal risk; stablecoin/quote-currency effects; and the fact that the source's monthly horizon, expanding-window estimation and 5% vol target were never tested outside US equities.
- Because neither the mechanism nor the signal has been demonstrated in crypto by the source, portability is **`unproven`**; this is not authorization to trade anything.

## Limitations

- `not independently reproduced` — no number in this record has been reproduced by us.
- `data gap` — transaction costs, fees, spread, slippage, impact, participation, capacity, borrow, financing, leverage, margin, latency, fill model, failure handling, turnover, and gross-vs-net labeling: the source models **none** of them (verified by Methods-level read plus full-text scan), and none may be read as zero.
- `data gap` — strategy-level performance (mean, volatility, Sharpe, drawdown, hit rate) is never printed; the only Sharpe ("around 0.4") is in-sample and derived from a regression t-statistic.
- `underspecified` — the strategy's weight normalization, volatility estimator window/type, and full no-look-ahead construction are delegated to Guo and Wachter (2025), SSRN 5808182, which is a different document not opened here.
- `underspecified` — execution convention (order type, month-end close vs next open, latency, partial fills) is never stated; timezone and closing-bell convention for the month-end observation are never stated.
- `underspecified` — Figure 3's `mean = 0.31` label does not state whether its scope includes the continuation/reversal bars.
- `not stated in source` — code availability, data availability, replication package, journal publication status beyond the NBER working-paper cover, and the per-award breakdown of the acknowledged funding.
- Identification is observational: the theoretical model is consistent with the data, but the paper does not run a causal design, a random-anchor placebo for the calendar, or a priced-risk horse race (Section 6 argues rather than tests).
- Sample is US-equity centric (1926-2024) with an earnings-calendar label inferred from 1971-2023; the international panel's reversal arm is insignificant and pre-1974 international results are flagged by the source itself.
- Publication-bias and multiplicity concerns apply: many tables and figures, no multiplicity correction, mixed standard-error conventions, and marginal sub-results.
- Capacity is unaddressed; the ±3 leverage and monthly full re-weighting make the strategy's implementability at scale unknown.
- The record is a research capture of a public working paper. It is not evidence that the pattern survives after costs, survives 2025-2026, or exists outside US equities.
