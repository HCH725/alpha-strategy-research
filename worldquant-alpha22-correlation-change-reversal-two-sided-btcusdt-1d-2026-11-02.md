## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

Coarse mean-reversion/momentum building blocks. The paper frames its universe coarsely: `one can think of alpha signals as based on mean-reversion or momentum`, where `a mean-reversion alpha has a sign opposite to the return on which it is based`. Alpha#22 is a negated agreement-change block: the inner `correlation(high, volume, 5)` measures how tightly highs and volume move together over five days, and the outer negation fades the 5-bar change of that agreement — when high–volume co-movement strengthens over five bars the book shorts, when it weakens the book longs. The paper prints no behavioural mechanism for #22 itself and ships no stop, target, or risk leg of any kind.

### Research interpretation

Agreement-momentum fade, two-sided overall. The inner value (`c[t] = corr(high[t-4..t], volume[t-4..t])`) is a trailing 5-bar Pearson level: near `+1` when rising highs print on rising volume and falling highs on falling volume, near `-1` for the inverse pattern, `0.0` for no linear relation — including the pinned zero-variance convention (a flat 5-bar high or volume window prints `0.0`, never NaN). The outer score (`score[t] = -(c[t] - c[t-5])`) fades the 5-bar drift of that agreement: co-movement that built up over five bars fires short, co-movement that decayed fires long. Unlike sibling worldquant-alpha6 (a negated 10-bar open-vs-volume level correlation — a level object on a different price series with no change leg), this rule never trades the correlation level itself, only its 5-bar change. Unlike sibling worldquant-alpha26 (which rank-transforms both trailing paths before correlating and then takes a trailing 3-bar maximum envelope — a rank-space agreement level with memory), this rule correlates raw levels with no rank transform, no envelope, and memory limited to the two 5-bar endpoints. Unlike sibling worldquant-alpha12 (a 1-bar volume-sign × price-change product with no window and no correlation), this rule's memory is 10 bars deep and every comparison is a windowed statistic, never a single-bar product. The failure mode is plain: a steadily rising high–volume agreement pins the book short with no scaling and no guardrail, while correlation estimates on five bars swing bar to bar with no smoothing — disclosed, not upgraded. Live-window behaviour (2,612 completed 1d bars 2019-09-08→2026-11-01) prints 1,281 long-signal against 1,301 short-signal bars with zero exact-zero scores — the book is essentially always sided, and every one of the 696 state transitions is an atomic reversal (695) plus a single first entry, disclosed as a structural property, not tuned.

## Signal

Exact rule as pinned (any other series, window, operator, fill, frame, book, or tie treatment is a different, unpinned rule):

- Inputs (paper A.2): `high[t]`, `volume[t]`, `close[t]` on completed `1d` bars only. `high`/`volume` feed the correlation legs; `close` feeds only the 20-bar definedness gate. No `open`, no vwap/adv/returns/market-cap/sector, no cross-sectional rank, no second asset or timeframe is read by any order-gating line.
- Agreement value (paper Appendix A, pinned impl lines 120–141, 332–333): `c[t] = correlation(high, volume, 5)[t]` — trailing 5-bar Pearson over completed bars; NaN unless all five prints of both legs are finite (impl all-finite block rule); exactly `0.0` when either 5-bar leg has zero variance (pinned impl convention, never redefined to NaN).
- 5-bar agreement-change score (paper Appendix A, pinned impl lines 74–82, 332–333): `score[t] = -(c[t] - c[t-5]) × rank(stddev(close, 20))[t]`, where the rank factor is the implementation-exact single-asset constant `1.0` whenever the trailing 20-bar sample standard deviation (`ddof=1`) of closes is finite, and NaN before the 20th completed bar or over any non-finite 20-bar close window (per `_rank_1d`/`stddev` code). Undefined (NaN) before the 20th completed bar or whenever either correlation endpoint is NaN — flat by record rule, never interpolated.
- Direction mapping (source-grounded, fenced in Negative evidence 1): `score[t] > 0` → long signal; `score[t] < 0` → short signal; `score[t] == 0` → flat signal (fires only when the 5-bar agreement change prints exactly zero — absent across the full 2,612-bar live window, disclosed). The entry fills at the next bar's open, one single unit, only if the book does not already carry that side (see position rule).
- Exit: the first opposite signal reverses the full unit in one step (close plus opposite entry at the next-bar open); a zero print flattens the full unit at the next-bar open — reversal-or-flatten is the sole exit path by construction. No stop-loss, no take-profit, no trailing, no time exit (the paper ships no risk leg — carried over exactly, disclosed in Provenance adaptation (4), never upgraded into a source claim).
- Direction: two-sided overall (agreement-buildup shorts; agreement-decay longs).
- Position rule (predeclared researcher pin): single-unit; an already-long book treats further long-signal bars as no-ops (no pyramiding, no averaging), and symmetrically for shorts; flat-regime bars while already flat are no-ops. Re-entry re-arms on the next opposite or flat-exit signal only (no cooldown specified — explicitly none, not invented). Pre-first-signal, pre-warmup, and any NaN-score bars are flat by record rule.

## Limitations
