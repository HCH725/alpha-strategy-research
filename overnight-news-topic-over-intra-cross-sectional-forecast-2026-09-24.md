## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

- The authors state that over 30 years nearly all U.S. market gains were earned overnight while average intraday returns were negative or flat, and that *"a large part of this effect can be explained through features of intraday and overnight news"* (Abstract; §1).
- Proposed channel (§4, §6 Conclusion): **firm-level news exposures are persistent** (Table 2 persistence ρ), and **the market's response to the same news topic has opposite signs in the two sessions** — news content arriving intraday vs overnight is positively correlated across sessions, while the response coefficients `β^i` and `β^o` are generally negatively correlated (Figure 5). Combining past exposure with the market's past response is claimed to explain *"a large extent"* of over-intra outperformance.
- Competing mechanisms the paper explicitly tests and does **not** absorb into its own effect: firm characteristics (§5.2), annual overnight/intraday momentum (§5.3), and dealer inventory management (§5.4, Tables 8–9). It also contrasts, without testing, retail attention (Berkman et al. 2012), capital-constrained arbitrage (Bogousslavsky 2021), European-session uncertainty resolution (Bondarenko–Muravyev 2023) and Lou–Polk–Skouras (2019) anomaly timing.
- **The authors' own viability statement (§1, verbatim):** *"Because of the extreme turnover required to trade the over-intra effect, our findings fall short of being a viable trading strategy."* They position the result as useful for **institutional entry/exit timing and for market makers pre-positioning inventory**, not as a stand-alone strategy.

### Research interpretation

- **Hypothesized mechanism (falsifiable form):** information produced outside a stock's continuous trading session, and the session in which firm-specific information is digested, leaves a **persistent, session-specific repricing pattern** that is cross-sectionally predictable from *which topics* a firm's recent news loads on and *how the market has historically repriced* those topics in each session. This is an **underreaction / attention-and-session-liquidity channel**, not a risk premium: the forecast is built from public text only, with no cash-flow, valuation, or risk-factor variable entering the topic exposure vector.
- **Hybrid structure and the role of each component (source-reported roles unless noted):**
  - *Signal construction:* branching-LDA topic exposures `z̄^{i/o}` over `n=4` years → lasso cross-sectional forecast of next-year session-specific returns (Eqs. 5–7).
  - *Selection rule:* top **25** names by overnight forecast, bottom **25** names by intraday forecast, **fixed for the whole following year**.
  - *Session split (the "regime" component):* the long leg is held **only overnight** (close → next open) and the short candidate leg **only intraday** (open → close). The over-intra thesis *depends* on this split; a close-to-close implementation would destroy the claim.
  - *Risk / exit / sizing:* **no stop, no volatility target, no exit rule, no position-size rule beyond equal weighting appears in the source** → `data gap`. Risk management is entirely absent from the source design.
- Do not assume each component contributes alpha: the paper's own ablations (§5.1, Eqs. 9–15) show that **differentiating the response `β^i` vs `β^o` matters more than differentiating the content `z̄^i` vs `z̄^o`** (Table 6 columns (a)/(c) vs (b)/(d)), i.e. the content component is partially redundant. A component ablation is required before crediting both.
- **This record is a ported empirical hypothesis, not crypto evidence**, and it inherits the source's own "not a viable trading strategy" caveat.

## Signal

Everything in this section is **source-reported** unless explicitly prefixed `research-proposed` / `research-inference`.

- **Formation timestamp:** end of calendar year `t`. Estimation uses annual data through year `t`; the forecast is applied to year `t+1` (§4.1, Figure 2). Selections are made **annually** and **remain fixed throughout the subsequent year** (Table 3 caption). Intraday window = 9:30–16:00 on day `s`; overnight window = 16:00 on day `s−1` → 9:30 on day `s`; each period is identified by its **ending** day; weekend/holiday articles count as overnight news for the next trading day (§2).
- **Lookback:** cumulative topic exposures `z̄^{i/o}_{j,t} = z_{t−n+1} + … + z_t` with baseline **`n = 4` years**; results also shown for `n = 1` (Eqs. 5; Tables 4, 7). News archive starts 1996 → estimation of (6) starts from year-2000 returns → **first tradable selection at end-2000, first evaluated year 2001** (§4.1 footnote 4). Endpoints: years are whole calendar years, summed within year, summed across the window; **warm-up of 5 years (4 exposure years + 1 return year) is explicit in the source**.
- **Estimation (Eq. 6):** per session `p ∈ {i,o}`, cross-sectional-by-year regression of annual return `r^p_{j,t}` on **lagged** exposures `z̄^p_{j,t−1}` plus controls, estimated by **lasso** (only the `β` vector penalized; intercepts `α_t`, `α^i_t` and control coefficients `γ_t` never penalized), penalty chosen by **five-fold cross-validation minimizing mean-squared error** (not trading performance). Lasso retains **20–140 topics per year** (Online Appendix Figure A2, `data gap` — appendix not in v1 HTML). **Controls:** size, book-to-market, investment, profitability, **intraday momentum**, **overnight momentum**, intraday volatility, overnight volatility, all measured in year `t−1`, **cross-sectionally demeaned within each year** (§2, §4.1).
- **Forecast (Eq. 7):** `f^p_{j,t} = α_t + I(p=i)·α^i_t + β^p_t · z̄^p_{j,t}` — exposures as of **`t` (not `t−1`)**, **controls deliberately dropped** so only news flow drives the pick (§4.1, Figure 2 caption: "we do not use returns or topic exposures from year t+1").
- **Long entry:** `LS^o_t` = the **25 companies with the highest** overnight forecasts `f^o_{j,t}`; held **only overnight, every day of year `t+1`**, **equally weighted**, earning their overnight (close→open) return (§4.1, Table 3 caption).
- **Short entry:** `SS^i_t` = the **25 companies with the lowest** intraday forecasts `f^i_{j,t}`; held **only intraday, every day of year `t+1`**, equally weighted. **Source footnote 5 states the figure plots a LONG position in `SS^i` and that "one would want to take a short position in the portfolio to profit from these forecasts"** — i.e. the executable short leg is described but **no short-execution rule (locate, hard-to-borrow, recall, intraday reborrow) is specified → `underspecified`.**
- **Exit:** instantaneous per session — the overnight leg is flat 09:30–16:00, the intraday leg is flat 16:00–09:30. No stop, no take-profit, no time exit beyond the annual re-selection (§4.1).
- **Holding period:** **1 session per leg, re-entered the next session, 250+ times per year, with the 25-name roster unchanged for ~12 months.** `research-inference:` the overnight leg must therefore be **bought at the close and sold at the next open every single day**, implying **on the order of 100% one-way turnover per day within each leg**; the source never prints a turnover number — it only says *"extreme turnover"* (§1) — so this figure is **our inference from the holding rule, not a source-reported number**.
- **Re-entry / ties / simultaneous signals:** tie-breaking among equal forecasts, treatment of names that leave the index mid-year, and treatment of names with no news in a session are **not specified in v1 → `underspecified`.**
- **Parameters and their source:** `K = 200` topics (chosen because it "worked well" in the authors' 2020 paper; 100- and 500-topic versions "not very sensitive", §3 — **fixed from prior work, not tuned here**); vocabulary = tokens appearing ≥25 times in ≥25 documents → **~44,000 tokens**; articles dropped if they mention 0 or >3 S&P 500 names or have <25 words → **~2.4 million articles retained, ~two-thirds overnight**; stemming, stopword removal, numbers mapped to `__NUM__`/`__MIL__`/`__BIL__`, **unigrams only** (§2). **Top-25 / bottom-25 counts and the equal weighting are stated by the source; no other threshold exists.**
- **Reproducibility verdict:** the estimation/selection logic is **reconstructable in principle** (Eqs. 5–7, controls list, CV rule all given) but **not independently reproducible** without the proprietary Thomson Reuters archive, the frozen 2010 topic-word distributions, and the Online Appendix → overall signal marked **`underspecified` for reproduction, specified for understanding**.

## Required data

- **Instrument / universe:** common stocks currently in the **S&P 500**, point-in-time membership from **CRSP index constituents**; 887 names over 1996–2022; inclusion only on index-member days.
- **Venue / market type:** U.S. listed equity, continuous session **09:30–16:00 ET** (NYSE hours used for the session split, §2); **spot equity, no futures/options/perpetual component**.
- **Timeframe:** daily open and close prices (CRSP); annual aggregation for estimation, daily aggregation for portfolio evaluation.
- **Fields — prices:** daily **open and close** from CRSP; **dividends included in close-to-open returns**; **splits and buybacks assumed to take effect at the close** (§2). **No volume, no bid/ask, no quote, no order-book field is used anywhere in the paper** → any implementable version would need them, but the source does not.
- **Fields — news:** **Thomson Reuters News Feed Direct (TR) archive**, full text, **with the TR timestamp**; article→company mapping and chain handling follow the pre-processing of Glasserman, Li & Mamaysky (2025); ~2.4M articles 1996–2022.
- **Fields — characteristics:** size, book-to-market (inverse-hyperbolic-sign transformed), investment, profitability, 12-month momentum (built **separately** for intraday and overnight monthly returns, skipping the most recent month, compounding the prior 11), annualized volatility (daily session vol × √252, computed separately per session), computed following **Green, Hand & Zhang (2017) using their code** (§2).
- **Point-in-time / availability:** the **2010 topic model** is the honest one — topic-word distributions frozen at end-2010, document-topic distributions for post-2010 articles inferred with the frozen distributions, so 2011–2022 is *"fully out-of-sample with respect to the topic model"* (§3). The **2020 topic model** uses 1996–2020 text and is labelled by the authors themselves as **"semi-out-of-sample"** because topic construction sees future news (§3, §4.1). The branching-LDA **model-selection step regresses firm returns on exposures and keeps the Gibbs run with the highest out-of-sample `R²` on a hold-out article sample** (§3) → **topic selection is supervised on returns**, which is a real (source-described) selection channel.
- **Timestamp / timezone:** article timestamps are TR's, converted into the NYSE close/open buckets; **timezone conversion rule and late/revision handling are not printed in v1 → `data gap`.**
- **Missing-data:** firms with no qualifying article in a session contribute zero exposure `z` for that session (implied by Eq. 2 summing over an empty set); **explicit null/stale/suspension/delisting handling is not stated → `data gap`.** Delisting-return treatment for names leaving the index is **not stated → `data gap`.**
- **Funding / fee / spread needs:** **entirely unaddressed by the source → `data gap`.** An implementer must supply commissions, spread, slippage, **overnight financing and short-borrow/locate costs** (the strategy is short intraday and long overnight, every day), none of which appear in v1.

## Execution assumptions

- **Signal-to-order timing:** the source is a return study; it never specifies an order. `research-proposed` (ours, not the source's): place the overnight buy at/near the 16:00 close and the cover at/near the 09:30 open, and the intraday short at/near the 09:30 open with the cover at/near the 16:00 close, on the re-selection date and **maintain the same 25 names daily**.
- **Order type / fill model / latency:** **not stated in v1 → `data gap`.** No market-vs-limit assumption, no partial-fill or failed-fill treatment, no latency or auction-vs-continuous treatment.
- **Fees / spread / slippage / impact / capacity:** **not stated in v1 → `data gap`.** Verified by full-text search (see Provenance). **Do not read the reported basis-point returns as net.**
- **Funding / leverage / margin:** **not stated → `data gap`.** Overnight longs and intraday shorts each leave the book flat for roughly half of every calendar day; the cash/financing leg of that pattern is never modelled.
- **Borrow / shorting:** **not stated → `data gap`.** `SS^i` requires a borrow on 25 large-cap names every day of the year; locate availability, recalls and hard-to-borrow fees are absent.
- **Turnover / capacity:** only the qualitative statement *"extreme turnover required to trade the over-intra effect"* (§1). **No turnover percentage, no ADV participation cap, no capacity number exists in v1 → `data gap`.**
- **Source-vs-Scout boundary:** the source assumes **frictionless, gross, equal-weight, always-fillable** execution implicitly (by silence). Everything in the bullet marked `research-proposed` above is ours and is labelled as such.

### Negative evidence

1. **The source itself rejects strategy viability (§1, verbatim):** *"Because of the extreme turnover required to trade the over-intra effect, our findings fall short of being a viable trading strategy."* This is a direct, source-reported statement against tradability and must not be softened.
2. **Zero cost model in v1** (verified full-text search): every basis-point number above is **gross**. The overnight leg round-trips daily (buy at close, sell at next open) and the intraday leg short-covers daily, so a two-sided cost ladder is the first-order threat; a 1-bp-per-side charge would erase ~2 bp of the OOS `LS^o − LNS^o` spread of 2.9 bp/day (our arithmetic, `research-proposed` ladder, not a source claim).
3. **Our inferred turnover structure:** ~100% one-way turnover per day inside each leg (see Signal) with **no turnover figure, no ADV cap, and no capacity analysis printed**. For 25-name equal-weight large-cap books this is the difference between a paper result and an implementable one; the source acknowledges it qualitatively and stops there.
4. **The short leg does not survive the fully out-of-sample window:** `SS^i` is starless in **all four** Table 4 rows and the authors say so in footnote 6. The short-side of the trade is therefore the weakest component exactly where it matters most.
5. **Attenuation between semi-OOS and fully-OOS:** `LS^o` falls 11.0 → 6.9 bps/day and `LS^o − LNS^o` falls 7.6 → 2.9 bps/day when moving from the 2020 (topics see post-2010 news) to the 2010 frozen model — i.e. **most of the headline is estimated on a topic model that would not have existed in real time.**
6. **Supervised model selection on returns:** branching LDA (§3) picks the Gibbs run with the highest **out-of-sample `R²` of return regressions** and repeats over several rounds of 10 samplers. The 2010 model freezes distributions before 2011, but the *selection criterion itself* was fit on 1996–2010 returns → residual selection-on-returns contamination for the whole sample, acknowledged only as "avoiding the disastrous overfitting" of putting returns inside the sampler.
7. **The "disappearing over-intra effect" result is a null, not a profit:** `LNS^o − SNS^i` is not significant in any table. The paper's core explanatory claim is that once you cannot pick names, there is **no residual spread to harvest** — which also means the effect is not accessible without the (costly, proprietary-data) news signal.
8. **Fully paper-specific data dependency:** the signal requires the **Thomson Reuters News Feed Direct archive back to 1996**, point-in-time article timestamps, the authors' frozen 2010 topic-word distributions, and the Online Appendix (not in v1). A second party cannot rebuild the input without a commercial vendor and unpublished artifacts.
9. **Universe/selection concerns:** only firms that were **both** index members **and** named in the archive enter; delisting-return treatment and stale/suspended handling are unstated; the 887-name pool with index-membership conditioning invites survivorship/selection questions the paper does not address.
10. **Sample ends December 2022**; no evidence spans the 2023–2026 regime, and no post-2022 robustness appears anywhere in v1.
11. **No Sharpe, drawdown, turnover, hit-rate, or capacity statistic exists in v1** — a strategy claim whose only reported statistic is a mean daily basis-point return with Newey-West SEs.
12. **No code, no data, no journal reference, single preprint version** — reproducibility rests entirely on the text.
13. **Publication-bias / spec-search concern (our observation):** 16 headline specification variants across two tables with no family-wise correction, plus four Table 6 columns and six Table 7 columns of the same exercise, all unadjusted.
14. Adjacent literature cited by the source itself cuts against a clean mechanism: **Lou–Polk–Skouras (2019)** attribute overnight earning of momentum to investor-type timing, **Berkman et al. ## Falsification plan

Every threshold below is **`research-defined falsification threshold`** or **`research-proposed`** — none is set by the source.

- **F1 — Independent-vendor replication (data):** rebuild the exposure/forecast pipeline on a different point-in-time news archive (e.g. Bloomberg or Dow Jones wires) over a window never used here. **Threshold (`research-defined`):** the fully out-of-sample `LS^o − LNS^o` spread must be positive with Newey–West `|t| ≥ 2.0`. **Action on failure:** classify the effect as Thomson-Reuters-specific and stop.
- **F2 — Cost ladder (execution):** charge 0 / 1 / 2 / 5 / 10 bp **per side** on both legs, plus an explicit overnight financing line and a short-borrow fee. **Threshold (`research-defined`):** the strategy fails if the net `LS^o − LNS^o` spread is ≤ 0 at **5 bp per side**, or if the net `LS^o` pick premium falls below **+1.0 bp/day**.
- **F3 — Turnover realism:** instrument the actual daily one-way turnover of both legs under the stated holding rule. **Threshold (`research-defined`):** if realized annual turnover exceeds **100×** the leg capital and net Sharpe (annualized, on the combined overnight-long/intraday-short book) is **< 0.5**, reject implementability regardless of the gross spread. (`research-proposed` Sharpe floor.)
- **F4 — Placebo / label shuffle:** permute firm labels on the exposure vectors within each year, 1,000 draws, re-running the full selection. **Threshold (`research-defined`):** the real `LS^o − LNS^o` must exceed the 95th percentile of the null (`|z| ≥ 2.0`).
- **F5 — Long-only ablation (removes the borrow problem):** drop the intraday short leg entirely and hold only `LS^o` overnight versus the equally weighted index overnight. **Threshold (`research-defined`):** must deliver **≥ +1.0 bp/day with `t ≥ 2.0` after costs** to justify any long-side implementation.
- **F6 — Real-time freshness test (the paper's own weakest point):** freeze the topic model, lasso rule, and top-25/bottom-25 rule **before** a post-2022 window and run it forward with no refit other than the annual β re-estimate. **Threshold (`research-defined`):** OOS post-2022 net performance must be **≥ 50% of** the 2011–2022 gross-equivalent level; otherwise the effect is judged expired.
- **F7 — Competing-mechanism ablation (hybrid):** orthogonalize the news forecast against (i) an attention/volume proxy à la Berkman et al. (2012) and (ii) an inventory/reversal proxy à la Boyarchenko–Larsen–Whelan (2023). **Threshold (`research-defined`):** the news signal must retain **≥ +1.0 bp/day incremental** net spread; if the incremental term is indistinguishable from zero, reclassify the mechanism as attention/inventory rather than news content.
- **F8 — Multiple-testing control:** compute a deflated Sharpe ratio over the full printed specification grid (2 topic models × 2 windows × 4 forecast models, plus the Table 6/7 variants). **Threshold (`research-defined`):** DSR **≥ 0.95** on the frozen, pre-registered winner only.
- **F9 — Vendor/price-source replication:** rebuild the session split from a second daily OHLC source with its own open/close conventions. **Threshold (`research-defined`):** sign of `LS^o − LNS^o` must reproduce and the level must agree within **20%** (`research-proposed` tolerance), since a 1-minute open-auction difference can move a close→open return.
- **Action on failure (all tests):** record the failing gate, keep the record `research-only`, do not promote to `candidate`, and log the negative result against this source identity rather than retuning the rule.

## Crypto portability

**`unproven`.**

- The mechanism is defined by a **09:30–16:00 ET session boundary versus an overnight gap** — crypto has **no close and no open**, so "overnight vs intraday" does not exist as a structural partition; any port must first define a substitute boundary (e.g. UTC day, U.S. equity session overlap, or exchange maintenance window) and that choice is entirely `research-proposed`.
- **News coverage:** the signal needs a 24/7, timestamped, company-mapped wire archive from 1996. Crypto coverage in commercial wires is thinner, younger, and issuer-centric (protocols do not file the equivalent of index-constituent news), so the exposure vector would be sparsely populated.
- **Session-specific repricing:** there is no specialist/open-auction/after-hours liquidity cycle to create the asymmetric `β^i` vs `β^o` response the paper identifies as the dominant component; the mechanism's key ingredient may simply be absent.
- **Market-structure differences:** 24/7 accrual, **8-hour funding** on perps, spot-vs-perpetual basis, venue fragmentation across many exchanges with disjoint candle boundaries, listing churn and thin cross-sectional breadth (a "top 25 of ~500 large caps" design has no liquid crypto equivalent), and `mark/index` price conventions for the session split.
- **Short leg:** shorting perps is mechanically easier than borrowing S&P 500 shares intraday, but funding, liquidation and isolated-margin rules replace the borrow cost the source never modelled.
- **Not `adapted`** — no evidence in this source tests crypto in any form; nothing in the record should be read as crypto empirical support.
