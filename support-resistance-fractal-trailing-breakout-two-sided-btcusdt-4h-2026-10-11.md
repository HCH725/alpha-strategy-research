## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

Fractal-confirmed trailing support/resistance with an averaged-extreme offset band (prose plus code): highs and lows are admitted only when a 5-bar fractal confirms them (`high[3]` higher than its two neighbours on each side, mirror for lows — the 2-bar confirmation lag the prose discloses), each admitted extreme valued at `high[3]`/`low[3]`; the trailing levels are the running averages of admitted extremes over the last 21 signals, widened into an outer band by `r` (the absolute gap between the average admitted high and the average admitted low); the `trend` latch follows closes relative to both outer levels, and entries demand both the latch state and a close beyond the inner averaged extreme. The design bets that only confirmed pivots — not raw wicks — deserve to anchor support/resistance, and that the averaged band absorbs single-extreme noise; it ships no stop, no target, and no flat state — the book always carries the last signal. The prose frames entries as breakout-of-SR and exits as opposite bars exceeding the averaged high/low difference.

### Research interpretation

Confirmed-pivot memory machine, edge-triggered, two-sided — the strictest admission policy among the pool's breakout family: Donchian admits every raw extreme, Chandelier admits raw extrema smoothed by ATR, SSL admits every bar through SMA smoothing, but this machine admits an extreme only through a 5-bar fractal gate and then averages admissions over 21 signals before any level exists. The `r` band is then a pure function of admitted memory (average-high minus average-low), so the outer levels breathe with realised pivot dispersion rather than with ATR or time — chop with small pivots tightens the band, trending stretches with large pivots widen it. The double gate on entries (latch state AND close beyond the inner averaged extreme) means a flip of the latch alone never trades without a breakout print, unlike pure state-flip machines (SSL #92, SuperTrend #63) where the flip is the order. Unlike oscillator-gated records there is no bounded oscillator anywhere; unlike stop-anchored records (Williams-fractal fixed-RR) the fractal here is the signal, not the risk anchor. The bet is on confirmed-pivot averaged breakouts as regime-shift evidence under `length = 21`, not on any raw level, volatility multiple, slope test, calendar effect, or second timeframe.

## Signal

Exact rule as pinned (`length = 21` frozen — any other value is a different, unpinned rule; there are no other inputs):

- Declarations (derived): two-sided breakout machine over `BTCUSDT 4h` with same-bar-close execution and house sizing/costs (see Execution assumptions). State ∈ {flat, long, short}; flat exists only before the first post-warmup order; at most one unit is ever held.
- Fractal admission, pinned verbatim: `h = bar_index>5 and high[5]<high[4] and high[4]<high[3] and high[3]>high[2] and high[2]>high[1] ? 1 : 0` (top pivot, valued `high[3]`, confirmed with a 2-bar lag); `l` mirrors on `low` valued `low[3]`. A bar can admit at most one top and one bottom by construction (strict inequalities on the same five prints cannot satisfy both).
- Averaging block, pinned verbatim: `ln = sum(l, length)`, `hn = sum(h, length)` (admission counts over the trailing 21 signals); `hval = h>0 ? high[3] : 0`, `lval = l>0 ? low[3] : 0`; `lsum = sum(lval, length)`, `hsum = sum(hval, length)`; `r = ln>0 and hn>0 ? abs((hsum/hn) - (lsum/ln)) : 0` — the zero-guard is written in the source, so a signal drought yields `r = 0`, never a divide-by-zero.
- Trailing levels, pinned verbatim: `lvalc := lval and r>0 ? lval : lvalc[1]` (inner support = last admitted low), `lvalr := lval and r>0 ? lval+r : lvalr[1]` (outer support), `hvalc := hval and r>0 ? hval : hvalc[1]` (inner resistance), `hvalr := hval and r>0 ? hval-r : hvalr[1]` (outer resistance) — four persistent rails that freeze whenever no extreme is admitted or `r` is zero.
- Latch, pinned verbatim: `trend := close > lvalr and close > hvalr ? 1 : close < lvalr and close < hvalr ? -1 : trend[1]` — both outer rails must agree; an inside-the-band close keeps the prior state, so chop between the rails prints nothing.
- Orders, pinned verbatim: `strategy.close("Long", when=trend==-1)`; `strategy.close("Short", when=trend==1)`; `strategy.entry("Long", strategy.long, when=trend==1 and close>hvalc)`; `strategy.entry("Short", strategy.short, when=trend==-1 and close<lvalc)` — entries need the latch AND a close beyond the inner averaged extreme, evaluated on every completed bar. From flat the first gated breakout opens; from the same side the call is a no-op under the default no-pyramiding construction (no `pyramiding` argument ships anywhere); from the opposite side the close-plus-entry pair reverses the full unit in one step — reversal is the sole exit path, with no separate exit leg (zero `strategy.exit`/`strategy.order` legs anywhere). On any bar at most one of the four `when=` legs' long side and one of the short side can hold, and the latch admits only one side — disclosed; under single-unit no-pyramiding construction at most one order event resolves per bar, never stacking.
- Exits and risk, pinned verbatim: there are none besides reversal — zero `strategy.exit`/`strategy.order` legs, zero `stop=`/`limit=`/`profit=`/`loss=` qualifiers anywhere. No take-profit, no trailing beyond the flip rails themselves, no time exit, no flat signal — pinned as written, explicitly none, not invented.
- Direction: two-sided with resting exposure. A long-only reading would contradict the `"Short"`/`strategy.short` leg and is explicitly not this record.
