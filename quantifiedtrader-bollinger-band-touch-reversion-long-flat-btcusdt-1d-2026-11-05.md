## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

Band-touch mean reversion, long side only. The 20-bar SMA with ±2σ bands frames the recent range; the position opens when the close touches or breaks below the lower band (dislocation expecting reversion to the mean) and ends it when the close reaches the upper band (the round trip complete). Between the bands the machine holds its posture — a recovery toward the middle that never prints the upper band keeps a long alive, and chop that never touches the lower band never enters. The equity-index universe and the unmodeled-costs stance are source facts, not this record's venue or economics. No performance figure is carried anywhere.

### Research interpretation

Touch-level reversion machine — the reversion mirror of the lane's breakout family and the band cousin of the pool's edge machines. Unlike the pool `bollinger-band-breakout` record (which buys upper-band *penetration* — `close[t-1] ≤ upper[t-1], close[t] > upper[t]` — and exits at the *midline* cross, mult 1.5, same-close fills), this machine buys the *lower-band touch* (`C[t] ≤ lower[t]`, no prior-bar read, touch-not-penetration) and exits at the *upper-band touch* — diametrically opposed entries, different exits, different multiplier, different fills: every trade event differs, never a twin. Unlike dual-band breakout machines (Donchian rolling extremes, Keltner EMA+ATR) the bands are σ-widths around a mean, so they breathe with volatility instead of ratcheting with extremes — entries cluster in volatility expansions, not trend continuations. Unlike edge readers no crossing bar is required (a close sitting exactly on a band fires — planted flat tape enters on the first post-warmup bar with no transition, as pinned). The failure mode is plain: a dead-flat tape pins σ at zero so both bands equal the close and every bar fires — alternating daily round-trips while flat (planted flat tape: 35 entries / 34 exits ending LONG — disclosed churn, not patched); a steady ±1%/day drift never prints a touch in either direction because the σ-bands ride the drift (planted ramp and bleed tapes: 0/0 ending FLAT — opportunity cost both ways, disclosed); a dip-and-recover that never reaches the upper band enters once and holds indefinitely with no stop and no target (planted trip tape: 1 entry / 0 exits ending LONG — disclosed hold-forever degeneration by construction, deterministic, not patched); chop that spans both bands prints full band-to-band round trips (planted chop tape: 3 entries / 3 exits ending FLAT — disclosed).

## Limitations

- Flat-tape churn by construction: zero volatility pins both bands onto the close, so every bar fires — alternating daily round-trips while flat (planted dead-flat tape: 35/34 ending LONG — disclosed, not upgraded).
- Never-enters opportunity cost by construction: a steady drift never prints a touch in either direction because the σ-bands ride the drift (planted ±1%/day tapes: 0/0 ending FLAT both ways — disclosed, not patched).
- Hold-forever degeneration by construction: a dip-and-recover that never reaches the upper band enters once and holds indefinitely with no stop or target (planted trip tape: 1/0 ending LONG — disclosed, not upgraded).
- Chop-whip by construction: a range spanning both bands prints repeated full-width round trips with no stop or target (planted chop tape: 3/3 ending FLAT; live probes show the same band-race texture) — disclosed, not patched.
- Deviation-freeze residual: the `ddof=1` sample-σ pin is a disclosed researcher choice over an unprinted helper — a population-σ (`ddof=0`) build prints narrower bands and different touches (a different, unpinned rule); single-venue port residuals (backtesting.py pandas arithmetic vs Binance spot execution, prints, fees, open-definition across 24/7 sessions, return-denomination comparability with the source's equity tapes) are unclaimed, disclosed.
