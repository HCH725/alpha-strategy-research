# Probability Sorted Portfolio (ProSP): Tail-Probability Dual-Sort for Crypto Perpetual Futures Cross-Sectional Long-Short

## Provenance

- **Authors:** Jiahao Zhang (XJTLU), Yinze Ji, He Ma, Yi Cao (XJTLU)
- **Paper:** "Probability-based portfolio in crypto-perpetual futures market"
- **Journal:** *Physica A: Statistical Mechanics and its Applications*, Volume 700, 15 October 2026, Article 131911
- **DOI:** [10.1016/j.physa.2026.131911](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physa.2026.131911)
- **Source URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378437126006473
- **Data source:** ForthTech (Hong Kong crypto hedge fund), hourly futures contract data, September 2019–May 2025
- **Sample period:** Rolling out-of-sample test, January 2021–May 2025
- **Funding:** XJTLU Research and Development Fund, Grant RDF-24-02-002

## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The authors argue that in low signal-to-noise environments like crypto perpetual futures, the translation of model outputs into tradable positions matters more than predictive accuracy. Point forecasts compress rich distributional information into a single scalar and are easily dominated by noise. By using predicted tail probabilities (top-tail and bottom-tail) as ranking statistics, the methodology preserves distributional granularity that is lost in point-forecast sorting or hard-label classification.

Under a location-scale return family, tail probabilities are monotone in the conditional mean: higher P̂(T) corresponds to higher conditional expected returns, and higher P̂(B) corresponds to lower conditional expected returns. This makes probability scores economically meaningful for cross-sectional ranking.

A second mechanism is volatility-induced probability intersection: when conditional volatility rises, increased dispersion inflates both tail probabilities for assets whose expected returns lie in an "uncertainty region", creating overlap between long and short candidate sets. Removing this intersection acts as a structural volatility filter.

### Research interpretation

The hypothesis is that cross-sectional tail-probability ranking produces a systematic long-short spread in crypto perpetual futures that is orthogonal to market direction. The mechanism is:

- **Primary signal:** Three-class probabilistic classification (top-tail / middle / bottom-tail) of forward returns
- **Portfolio construction:** Dual-sort by predicted top-tail probability (long) and predicted bottom-tail probability (short)
- **Volatility filter:** Remove overlap between long and short candidate sets when both tail probabilities are elevated
- **Model-agnostic layer:** Any ML model producing calibrated softmax probabilities can be embedded

This is a portfolio-construction methodology rather than a single-signal alpha. The alpha source is the cross-sectional dispersion in tail probabilities generated by the underlying ML models, filtered through the dual-sort mechanism.

## Signal

- **Formation timestamp:** At each rebalancing time t, using features available up to t
- **Lookback window:** Not explicitly stated in the accessible sections; features are derived from hourly close price, trading volume, and turnover
- **Long entry:** Select top 25% of assets ranked by predicted top-tail probability P̂(r_{i,t+1} ∈ T | X_{i,t})
- **Short entry:** Select top 25% of assets ranked by predicted bottom-tail probability P̂(r_{i,t+1} ∈ B | X_{i,t})
- **Overlap removal:** Remove any asset appearing in both long and short candidate sets
- **Position sizing:** Dollar-neutral long-short (long leg and short leg equally weighted within their respective sets)
- **Holding period / rebalancing:** 4-hour and 8-hour horizons (two specifications tested)
- **Parameters:**
  - Tail quantile k: top 25% / bottom 25% of cross-sectional return distribution
  - Rebalancing frequency: 4h or 8h
  - Feature set: Close price, total trading volume, turnover (three fundamental variables)
  - Model classes tested: Decision Trees, Random Forests, XGBoost, Deep Neural Network
- **Specification status:** Partially specified. The paper provides the framework and parameter ranges but the exact feature engineering pipeline, lookback windows, and model hyperparameters are in the full text (not fully accessible from the abstract/preview). The 25% quintile cutoff and overlap-removal are explicitly specified.

## Evidence

### Source-reported

- ProSP "consistently outperforms regression and hard-label classification benchmarks across feature sets, horizons, and model classes"
- "The strongest implementation achieving markedly higher Sharpe ratios than both regression- and label-based benchmarks"
- Factor regressions against BTC, ETH, and broad market index indicate profitability is "largely orthogonal to market direction and manifests as economically and statistically significant alpha, consistent with a market-neutral cross-sectional mechanism"
- ProSP preserves "superior robustness relative to benchmarks for low-cost trading environments" under transaction cost sensitivity

### Negative evidence

- High turnover makes net performance implementation-dependent under realistic transaction costs
- The probability-based approach preserves relative superiority to benchmarks but absolute net performance depends on fee environment
- The paper notes that "predictive accuracy is modest" — the alpha comes from portfolio construction, not from strong point forecasts
- Cross-sectional depth is insufficient in the early sample period (September 2019–early 2021), which may affect early-period results
- Crypto perpetual futures markets are non-stationary; the rolling-window protocol is designed to address this but regime shifts remain a risk

## Falsification plan

- **Out-of-sample separation:** The paper uses rolling-window training with out-of-sample testing (January 2021–May 2025). This should be extended with a holdout period not used in the original study.
- **Cost sensitivity:** Stress-test under realistic Binance/Bybit fee schedules (maker 0.02%, taker 0.05%) and estimated slippage. If Sharpe drops below 0.5 net of costs, the strategy is not economically viable.
- **Ablation on overlap removal:** Test ProSP without the volatility-induced overlap removal step. If performance degrades significantly, the overlap removal is a necessary component, not a heuristic.
- **Universe restriction:** Test on top-10 liquid perpetuals only vs. full universe. If alpha concentrates in illiquid names, capacity is limited.
- **Regime breakdown:** Separate results by bull/bear/sideways regimes. If alpha is concentrated in a single regime, it is regime-dependent.
- **Model complexity:** Compare XGBoost vs. Decision Tree vs. neural network. If the simplest model performs comparably, the methodology is robust to model choice. If only the most complex model works, overfitting risk is high.
- **Failure threshold:** If cross-sectional IC is not significantly different from zero (t-stat < 2) in the holdout period, reject the hypothesis.

## Crypto portability

direct

The strategy is designed natively for crypto perpetual futures. The methodology is directly applicable to Binance, Bybit, OKX, and other centralized perpetual venues. Key portability considerations:

- **Spot vs. perpetual:** The strategy uses perpetual futures features; not applicable to spot markets without modification
- **Funding:** Funding rate costs are present but not explicitly modeled in the strategy; they affect net returns
- **24/7 session:** The hourly rebalancing (4h/8h) aligns naturally with continuous crypto markets
- **Venue fragmentation:** The ForthTech dataset may span multiple venues; venue-specific liquidity effects are not addressed
- **Liquidity:** The broad universe includes less liquid contracts; capacity constraints may bind for larger capital
- **Contract specification:** USDT-margined perpetuals are assumed; coin-margined or inverse contracts would require adjustment
