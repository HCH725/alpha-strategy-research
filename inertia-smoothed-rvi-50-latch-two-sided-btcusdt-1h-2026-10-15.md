## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

Volatility-ratio trend acceptance with midline-latch reversal discipline (header prose plus quoted Pine, learn-or-paper-only warning fenced above): each bar's close-to-close move is classified by direction — the up leg `u` takes the bar's StdDev(10) when the close rises, the down leg `d` takes it when the close falls or holds — and both legs are Wilder-smoothed (`(13·prior + leg)/14`). Their ratio `100·nU/(nU+nD)` prints near 100 when recent volatility is all upward (trend acceptance) and near 0 when all downward, with 50 the exact balance point. Smoothing that reading with EMA(14) and latching it against 50 means a leg is entered only when smoothed inertia escapes to its side, and held while it merely touches or hesitates at the line — the opposite-side escape is the exit. There is no stop, no target, no flat state by design after the first signal — the opposite escape is the exit. The design bets that direction-split volatility, smoothed twice (Wilder then EMA), separates durable trends from chop better than any fixed-average cross, and that the highest-expectancy posture is to always hold the side of the last escape.

### Research interpretation

Single-series oscillator-latch system, two-sided, always-in-position after the first signal, with a sticky hold on exact-50 prints. Unlike cross systems that trade the moment one line passes another, this rule has no second series at all: the only moving part is the smoothed inertia reading itself, and the 50 line converts it into a three-state latch (long / hold / short) where the hold state inherits the last escape. Unlike symmetric-band systems (PFE ±50), a decaying leg exits on the mere crossing of the midline — there is no tolerance zone, so midline flicker prints repeated full reversals with no flat state to hide in, while the hold branch fires only on the measure-zero event of an exact-50 print. The double smoothing (Wilder-14 on the legs, EMA-14 on the ratio) makes the reading slow to turn but also slow to whipsaw relative to the raw ratio. The price of the always-in posture is indefinite adverse rides: a leg whose opposite escape never prints is held forever. The bet is on smoothed volatility-direction consensus, not on any momentum extreme, volume print, second series, or calendar effect.
