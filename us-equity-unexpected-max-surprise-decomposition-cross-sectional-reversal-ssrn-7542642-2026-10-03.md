## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The authors' stated rationale (PDF Sections 1, 2 and 5):

- Background: `stocks experiencing unusually large positive daily returns subsequently earn lower future returns, a phenomenon commonly referred to as the MAX anomaly (Bali, Cakici, and Whitelaw, 2011)`, and `the economic mechanism underlying this anomaly remains an open question`.
- Competing explanations the paper sets up: (a) **lottery demand / probability weighting** (Barberis and Huang 2008; Bali et al. 2011) - investors overweight small probabilities of large gains, demand inflates prices, prices revert; plus **attention** (Barber and Odean 2008) where salient extreme returns pull in retail interest and reinforce speculative trading; (b) **compensation for predictable tail risk** - if MAX were a priced risk exposure, the *expected* component of future extremes should carry the premium.
- Identification idea: realized monthly MAX `aggregates these different forces into a single statistic`, so it mixes a persistent, forecastable component with a short-lived shock. The paper forecasts next-month MAX with five models and defines two objects: the expected surprise `D_i,t = MAX_i,t - E(MAX_i,t+1 | t)` (available at month-end t, a predicted direction of change, explicitly `not a statistical surprise`) and the **unexpected MAX** `UMAX_i,t+1 = MAX_i,t+1 - E(MAX_i,t+1 | t)` (the forecast error, available only after month t+1 completes).
- Stated finding and interpretation: `expected MAX has little predictive power once unexpected extremes are accounted for`, `stocks experiencing unexpectedly large extreme returns earn significantly lower subsequent returns`, and `the evidence indicates that the MAX anomaly is driven by unexpected extremes, consistent with temporary mispricing rather than predictable exposure to tail risk`. The causal story offered is salience-driven: `unexpectedly large extremes are precisely the events likely to generate disproportionate attention, media coverage, and retail interest`, leading to extrapolation and lottery demand, temporary price pressure, then reversal; the paper frames this as `a mispricing phenomenon rather than a reward for bearing systematic tail risk`.
- The source is explicit that this is an interpretation, not a demonstrated cause: it lists `the role of retail trading, investor attention, and speculative demand` and international / other-asset extensions as **future** work, and it runs no test that separates attention from liquidity shocks, earnings news or market-wide jumps.

### Research interpretation

Hypothesized mechanism in falsifiable form: a **surprise-driven attention reversal** at the individual-stock monthly horizon.

- Component roles (source-specified unless marked otherwise):
  - *Regime / state*: the sign of the expected change in jump intensity, `D_i,t > 0` (model predicts quieter month) versus `D_i,t < 0` (model predicts a jumpier month); source-specified.
  - *Primary signal*: `UMAX_i,t+1`, the forecast error of next-month maximum daily return under an ARMA(1,1) expectation model (the source's best forecaster); source-specified as a characteristic, not as a tradable rule.
  - *Confirmation / conditioning*: the sign split `UMAX > 0` (realized extremes larger than expected, the candidate overpricing state) versus `UMAX < 0`; source-specified.
  - *Risk / exit*: no stop, no exit rule and no sizing rule are given by the source; the horizon is a one-month rebalance (`Portfolios are held for one month.`). Any long/short construction, sizing or stop is `research-proposed`.
- Falsifiable claim: conditional on the same realized MAX level, the cross-sectional return is decreasing in the *unanticipated* part of that maximum; i.e. the anomaly is state-dependent on the forecast error rather than on exposure to expected tail risk. A risk-premium account predicts the opposite pattern (a stable price for expected tail exposure regardless of the sign of the surprise).
- What would make this a mechanism rather than a correlation: the sign split must survive industry/size neutrality, the effect must be concentrated in the `UMAX > 0` leg, and it must survive a cost ladder. None of these is run by the source; they are set as `research-defined` falsification gates below.
- I do **not** treat the paper's narrative (attention, retail, extrapolation) as established: the source runs no attention, retail-flow or news test, so that link remains `unproven` and is recorded as such.

## Signal

Normalized construction, as far as the source specifies it. Anything not explicitly printed is marked `research-proposed` or `underspecified`.

- **Formation timestamp.** Characteristics are monthly, formed at month-end using that month's daily returns. The forecast `E(MAX_i,t+1 | t)` is produced `using only information observable at the end of month t` (PDF Section 3.5). The expectation is therefore known at the close of month t, but `UMAX_i,t+1` is known only after month t+1 has completed (Section 3.6(b): `Once month t+1 has fully realized, we observe the actual maximum daily return`). Time zone, calendar, closing-price convention and the exact lag between formation and evaluation are **`underspecified`** (`not stated in source`), and the printed captions conflict (see contradiction 4). `research-proposed` convention for any test: compute UMAX after the last trading day of month t+1, form portfolios at that close, and hold them through the close of month t+2; run the source's literal wording as a look-ahead control (Gate F2).
- **Lookback / warm-up.** `MAX_i,t = max over the days d of month t of the daily return` (Section 3.2, block maxima). Expectation models: (1) martingale `E = MAX_i,t`; (2) AR(1) on monthly MAX, estimated `using an expanding window ... requiring a minimum of 120 non-missing month-to-month pairs`; (3) ARMA(1,1) on monthly MAX, `estimated monthly using a rolling window of up to 120 months, with refitting every 12 months to manage computational costs`; (4) quantile-tail approximation using a `rolling 252-day 99th percentile`, a rolling standard deviation and the expected next-month draw count; (5) EVT peaks-over-threshold with GPD parameters from exceedances above `the rolling 95th percentile of daily returns over a 500-day window`. Forecast sample inclusion requires `at least 36 monthly observations` per firm (Section 4.1.1); the RMSE evaluation requires `at least 24 out-of-sample months per firm` (Section 3.5); forecasts are `clip[ped] ... to a plausible range [0,0.5]` / winsorized at economically reasonable bounds (Section 4.1.1), and Section 3.5 states the evaluation `clip[s] forecasted MAX values to a plausible range [0,0.5]`, i.e. `we clip forecasted MAX values to a plausible range [0,0.5]`. Section 4.1.1 also says parameters are estimated `using the last ten years of monthly MAX observations`, which conflicts with the AR(1) expanding-window description -> `underspecified`.
- **Entry / sort rules (source-specified).** At each month-end: univariate quintile sorts (5 portfolios) on realized MAX, on each model's `E(MAX)`, and on each model's `UMAX`, run separately for the full sample and within the sign subsamples `D>0` / `D<0` and `UMAX>0` / `UMAX<0`; double sorts are 5x5 = 25 portfolios on `UMAX x MAX` and `UMAX x EMAX` with the ARMA implementation used in the printed tables; portfolios are held for one month; returns are computed both equal-weighted and value-weighted. Ties, minimum stock counts per quintile, whether sorts are done within exchange or NYSE breakpoints, and rebalance price are `not stated in source` -> `data gap`.
- **Entry / exit for a tradable rule.** The source reports portfolio spreads, not a strategy: it gives no order type, no execution price, no sizing, no stop, no re-entry rule and no treatment of delistings while held. A long-short implementation (short the top UMAX quintile, long the bottom, or long/short within the `UMAX>0` conditioning) is `research-proposed`.

## Crypto portability

**Portability: `adapted` (sign `unproven`, and there is prior repo evidence pointing the other way).**

- The mechanism is universe-agnostic in the abstract: a surprise-driven attention/lottery reversal in a salient extreme should in principle operate on any asset with retail attention and constrained arbitrage. Nothing in the construction is equity-specific except the inputs.
- What does not port directly: the expectation models are fit on a 252-session equity calendar (MAX is a calendar-month block maximum), there is no crypto equivalent of the paper's VIX-free but equity-specific control set (FF6, q-factor, Amihud on CRSP), and `UMAX` needs a clean, liquid, point-in-time daily return series per symbol.
- Crypto-specific frictions the source never addresses, all `data gap`: perpetual funding and settlement, mark/index price divergence, liquidation and auto-deleveraging, 24/7 sessions changing the meaning of "month", venue fragmentation and per-venue maxima, listing/delisting churn (a far larger survivorship problem than in CRSP), stablecoin quotation, and borrow/short availability on individual tokens.
- Sign risk is real: the repository's `crypto-cross-sectional-max-daily-return-lottery-momentum-2026-08-31` record reports that in crypto the MAX effect runs the *opposite* way (high-MAX coins outperform), so a naive port of "short the high-surprise names" is `unproven` and could be exactly wrong. Any crypto test must re-establish the sign first, then apply the same surprise decomposition.
- Conclusion: `adapted` for mechanism, `unproven` for the sign and magnitude; crypto portability is not authorization to trade.
