## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

MA-centered ratcheted-band trend flip (mirror theory prose plus code header, no performance section ships): an ATR envelope (`Multiplier × ATR(10)`) is hung around a selectable moving average (default EMA-10 of `hl2`) instead of around price, and each side's stop ratchets (long stop never falls while the average holds above it; short stop never rises while the average holds below it). A `dir` latch selects the active side, and the published PMax line is the active stop. When the moving average crosses above its own trailing band, the prior downtrend is deemed exhausted and the system holds long; when it crosses under, it holds short. The MOST-plus-SuperTrend fusion claim is the author's: MOST contributes the average-centered stop-changing construction, SuperTrend contributes ATR price detection — the combination is meant to stay quiet through sideways chop that whipsaws either ancestor. There is deliberately no stop, no target, and no flat state — the prose lists no risk leg anywhere, by design gap rather than by hidden leg.

### Research interpretation

Average-vs-own-band cross state machine, two-sided with reversal exits. Unlike price-vs-band flip systems (SuperTrend, UT Bot, Chandelier, Nick Rypock) that trade the moment price touches a trailing level, this rule never compares price to the band for orders at all: its only order-gating comparisons are average-versus-band (`crossover(MAvg, PMax)` / `crossunder(MAvg, PMax)`), so a price spike that never drags the EMA-10 across the ratcheted stop changes nothing, and the system sits through chop that would flip a price-touch system repeatedly. Unlike cross-of-two-averages systems, there is only one average — the second line is the ratcheted stop itself, which freezes flat for stretches (the ratchet holds the prior extreme while the average stays onside), so entries demand the average to travel the full multiplier distance and back across a frozen rail. Note the two pinned asymmetries, stated plainly: first, the band is average-centered, so the trigger implicitly smooths twice (once in the average, once in the RMA-ATR) — whipsaw suppression at the cost of symmetric lag, and a market that trends hard without dragging the average back across the frozen rail is ridden, not trailed; second, the frozen `changeATR = true` selects RMA-ATR while the frozen `Normalize = false` keeps stops in raw price units — flipping either would re-geometry every band print and would be a different, unpinned rule. The bet is on average-exhaustion flips at frozen `ATR 10 / mult 3.0 / EMA 10 / hl2` geometry, not on any price level, volume print, mean-reversion extreme, or calendar effect.
