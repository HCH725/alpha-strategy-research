## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The source's stated mechanism has one root cause and four measured channels.

**Root cause.** AMM liquidity providers are passive: "their offered price quotes in response to requested trades only update as a result of a trade", so "when an asset's fair value ... moves, arbitrageurs therefore trade against the stale quotes on AMMs, i.e., liquidity providers are adversely selected", suffering loss-versus-rebalancing (LVR, attributed to Milionis et al.). A **propAMM** is "an on-chain program whose pricing function executes in a smart contract, as in an AMM, but whose prices are set by a single operator. The operator supplies the inventory themselves and (importantly) revises the quoted prices by writing new parameters to the contract, i.e., without a trade." The design intent is to "keep on-chain costs low while avoiding adverse selection", while "active quoting also brings about tighter spreads and thereby better prices to traders". By 2026 propAMMs "accounted for more than half of SOL/USDC volume on Solana".

**Four channels of the measured edge (Section 7, combined with the decoded Tessera implementation on Base).**

1. **Frequent and inexpensive quote updates (Section 7.1).** Updating the anchor price `p` and spread `s` reprices every ladder level at once, so an update costs far less than a swap (Base: 26,774-32,606 gas for a `p`/`s` update versus 201,243-402,881 simulated gas for a single swap; Monad: 33,428-50,839 versus about 162,503 with an access list and 330,103 without; Solana: 485-676 CU versus at least 16,938). Because chains order transactions by priority fee per unit of compute, operators bid strongly for position specifically on time-critical updates (Solana 304K-2.2M micro-lamports per CU on updates against 5.6K-22K on trades; Base Tessera pays 2.37x base fee for a price update but 0.18x for a book update and 0.77x for a default-fee update). Tessera additionally enforces **quote freshness**: each price update records a reference block, a trader gets a grace period, then an increasingly worse price, then no fill at all once the quote expires. Measured consequence: correlation between successful price-update counts and ETH realized volatility is **0.56** (Pearson, non-overlapping five-minute windows, volatility from five-second log returns, calendar-day and hour-of-day effects removed, non-price and unsuccessful updates excluded).
2. **Pricing by counterparty (Section 7.2).** Non-aggregator flow is charged a wider realized round-trip spread than aggregator-routed flow on five of the six largest Solana propAMMs (Table 1: HumidiFi +0.40, BisonFi +0.10, Tessera +0.10, SolFi V2 +1.58, GoonFi +3.59 bps, all at p<0.001 by exact sign test; ZeroFi -0.05, not significant), with the direction holding in 68-83% of matched (pool, day, size bucket) cells. The decoded Tessera contract implements six fee components -- base, age, priority, mode, default, address -- with four address classes (blacklisted cannot trade, whitelisted/default-exempt, penalized, remaining) and bytecode fingerprinting of new contracts, so pricing by counterparty is contract-enforced rather than heuristic.
3. **Being on the winning leg of cross-venue arbitrage (Section 7.3).** When a propAMM and a stale AMM (or another propAMM) are jointly arbitraged, the repriced venue captures its spread against the stale pool: before a propAMM-DEX arbitrage the reference price moves about 4.9 bps on Solana, 1.9 bps on Base and 7.3 bps on Monad, "The propAMM reprices in response, whereas the AMM does not."
4. **Spoofing (Section 7.4).** Operators quote better prices in the state aggregators read off-chain (end of block) and fill worse inside the next block: default-fee decreases concentrate in the final tenth of the block and increases in the first tenth, so "the effective spread is wider right after the fee increase early in the block, and narrower at the very end, i.e., in exactly the state aggregators read."

The source's own summary (Section 9): propAMMs "update prices cheaply and frequently without a trade, charge by counterparty, do not lose to arbitrage against other on-chain venues, and spoof ... The edge does not come out of retail."

### Research interpretation

The falsifiable hypothesis is a **quote-freshness alpha (or freshness-premium) mechanism on on-chain spot venues**:

- **Roles.** Regime/reference: an external fair-value process (Bybit microprice of the USDT pair). Primary mechanism: a single inventory-bearing operator reprices without a trade, so the venue stops being the stale counterparty. Channel 1 supplies the *capacity* to reprice (cheap, frequent, prioritized updates). Channel 2 supplies *flow selection* (charge toxic/non-aggregator counterparties more). Channel 3 supplies *positioning* (always be the non-stale leg). Channel 4 is a *taker-side tax* (quoted state != executed state), which is an edge for the operator and a cost for anyone routing on stale quotes.
- **Falsifiable predictions.** (i) The sign of the short-horizon maker markout should flip between passive pooled AMMs (negative after the fill) and actively repricing venues (positive after the fill), with the flip surviving changes of reference venue. (ii) The gap should be largest in high-volatility, high-reference-move states and on chains with cheap updates. (iii) A taker that routes on the previous-block state should lose approximately the measured quote-to-fill gap (1.08 bps average on Tessera) unless it re-quotes inside the transaction. (iv) If the edge were only a fee artefact, the markout should vanish when the fee-inclusive and fee-exclusive prices are separated -- the source does **not** perform this separation, which is a data gap.
- **Boundary of the claim.** This is a *market-structure measurement*, not a return premium and not a strategy anyone outside the operator set can buy: the operator supplies the inventory, so an outside researcher can only (a) test the measurement, (b) adopt taker-side routing discipline, or (c) attempt to replicate operator behaviour on a venue that permits it. Any concrete trading rule derived from it is **research-proposed**, never source-reported.

## Signal

The source specifies **no entry, exit or sizing rule for an outside participant**. Its operational content is a set of measurement protocols, which are specified precisely enough to reconstruct; the tradable operationalizations at the end of this section are explicitly **research-proposed**.

**Source-reported measurement protocols (reconstructible).**

- **Markout formation timestamp `t`.** Per-fill execution time reconstructed per chain (Appendix A.2): Solana from validator-vote sub-second boundary calibration against Anza validator metrics (votes cast within about a 0.22 s window, boundary slots as anchors, interior slots linearly interpolated in slot number, tail by local slot cadence); Base from a two-point approximation (the first 26% of a block's transaction positions stamped `b-1.8s`, the rest `b`, derived from Flashblock overlap statistics: FB1+FB10 carry 45.71% of propAMM ETH/USDC volume, median FB1 receipt about 200 ms after the previous block timestamp); Monad from MonadBFT consensus-layer timestamps.
- **Reference price.** Bybit public 200-level spot order-book archive: size-weighted mid `(bid*q_ask + ask*q_bid)/(q_bid + q_ask)` on the USDT pair (SOLUSDT, ETHUSDT, MONUSDT), converted to USDC by the Bybit USDCUSDT mid; collected 1 Sep 2025 to 31 Aug 2026.
- **Markout definition.** Relative difference between the microprice `tau` seconds after execution and the execution price; horizons plotted from -5 s to +15 s; headline reported at **+2 s**; aggregated as a notional-weighted average per venue, then pooled per chain. Sign convention: positive = the maker gained.
- **Realized round-trip spread (Table 1 construction).** Two consecutive opposite-side fills on one pool within **25 slots**, sizes within a factor of **two**, with **no intervening quote update**, both legs of the same flow class, compared within (pool, day, size bucket); metric is the relative price gap; significance by exact sign test (`*` p<0.05, `**` p<0.01, `***` p<0.001).
- **Retail proxy ("quiet" flow, Section 8).** A fill is quiet when the absolute Bybit weighted-mid move from `t-5 s` to `t+1 s` stays below **1 bps**, moving otherwise; quiet fills "arrive independently of what the market is doing and carry no information the maker has to price against".
- **Route classes (Section 7.3, Appendix B).** Per transaction: `single` (one decoded propAMM fill, no other swap), `prop-prop` (atomic arbitrage against a second proprietary pool), `prop-DEX` (against a public pool), `other` (splits, unresolved legs). Atomicity is detected by a balance-dictionary rule -- aggregate the trader's balance deltas `{token_i -> Delta_i}` and flag the transaction when `exists i: Delta_i > 0 and all i: Delta_i >= 0`; classification self-checks at 92.9% of Tessera ETH/USDC volume for `tx.to` addresses and 99.1% for `tx.from`.
- **Quote-versus-fill protocol (Section 7.4, Table 3).** For every Tessera WETH/USDC swap, compute the contract output twice -- state at the end of the previous block (what an aggregator routing on that state expects) versus state immediately before execution (what the trade receives) -- and assign each swap to the first applicable cause in the fixed order: nothing changed, different default fee, operator updated price/spread/book, earlier swap in the same pool, other fee or pricing difference.
- **Penalty/fee telemetry (Section 7.2, Appendix C.1).** Nominal marginal cost `c_nom = 10^4 * (p_a - p_b)/(p_a + p_b)` and effective marginal cost `c_eff` with the same expression after applying the penalty vector `phi` (ppm) that the contract withholds from the output, `Y_exec = Y_book * (1 - phi)`; fee revenue per component `r = Y*phi/10^6 - phi^T 1` as printed.

**Research-proposed operationalizations (NOT source-reported; every threshold is `research-proposed`).**

- **R1 -- taker routing freshness gate.** Only execute against a venue when the venue's quote was refreshed within the last `K` seconds, or route through an in-transaction quoter that reads state inside the swap, to avoid the measured quote-to-fill gap. `K = 1 block (research-proposed)`; the source itself notes that "An on-chain router that queries each venue inside the swap transaction would remove this gap" but sets no threshold.
- **R2 -- block-edge fee-window timing.** Large traders were observed timing trades into "the low-fee window spanning the end of one block and the start of the next"; a rule that only submits in the final `X` fraction of a block is **research-proposed** (`X = 0.1 (research-proposed)`, taken from the source's descriptive decile, not from any tested rule).
- **R3 -- active-repricing LP replication.** Supply liquidity only on venues that reprice without a trade, or replicate operator behaviour (update anchor/spread before the reference moves, prioritized by fee). Not executable from the outside without operator rights on these venues -- **underspecified** for a third party, and any sizing, stop or requote threshold is **research-proposed**.
- **R4 -- toxic-counterparty avoidance.** Do not route non-aggregator-sized flow to venues that price by counterparty; the observed premium ranges from 0.10 to 3.59 bps across venues, and picking a venue on that table out-of-sample is a **research-proposed** selection rule with no source-specified cutoff.

- **Missing data / gaps the source states itself:** no historical quote states on Solana, so spoofing cannot be corroborated there; several venues are recognised but not pool-resolved (Pump.fun, Phoenix, OpenBook, Lifinity v2, Meteora Pools, Raydium Route on Solana; Curve, Fluid, PancakeSwap Infinity, Virtuals, 1inch/Kyber limit orders, RFQ fills on Base) and are each counted as an unknown pool; update counts on Solana are estimates because any non-swap program interaction is counted as an update and "we cannot observe whether a given update moved the venue's quote"; imputation is not used and not endorsed.

## Execution assumptions

**Measurement-side (source-reported):** maker-side, price-only markout against a single external reference venue (Bybit USDT pair, converted to USDC); no gas, priority-fee, update, capital or inventory cost is subtracted; no fill model is needed because every observed fill is used as-is; latency is handled only through per-chain timestamp reconstruction and the observation that "transactions are by default ordered by priority fee per unit of compute", i.e. operators buy ordering rather than beating a latency race; no leverage, no borrowing, no shorting, no margin and no funding -- this is spot liquidity provision funded from "its own vault".

**Assumptions the source does NOT make (all data gap, never read as zero):**

- net-of-quoting-cost profitability (the decisive missing number for the operator edge);
- whether the execution price used in a markout is fee-inclusive for AMM LPs (Section 6 never says), so the AMM-side and propAMM-side fee treatment is **underspecified**;
- capacity, participation limits, maximum position or inventory bounds (census: `capacity` 0);
- partial fills, order rejection, reorg handling, MEV reordering risk to the operator itself;
- venue failure, operator counterparty risk, custody/withdrawal risk, upgrade/proxy risk of decoded contracts;
- a taker-side execution model for R1-R4 (order type, gas bidding, in-transaction quoting success rate) -- **research-proposed** in every detail.

**Explicitly excluded by the source rather than left unstated:** Ethereum (RFQ-like behaviour via builder ordering), Monad CLOB volume, non-proprietary permissionless vault venues (Clober Vault, Poe).

### Negative evidence

1. **The edge is never shown net of its own cost.** No figure nets gas, priority fees, update frequency or capital cost against the 0.37 / 1.19 / 1.69 bps headline (census: no `net`-of-cost statement; `transaction costs` appears once and only historically). An operator's edge can be positive gross and negative net.
2. **No statistical inference on the headline numbers.** `sharpe`, `cagr`, `drawdown`, `backtest`, `p-value`, `bootstrap`, `confidence interval` and `walk-forward` all occur **0** times; the only significance tests in the paper are the Table 1 sign tests. Every headline markout is an untested point estimate.
3. **Two-second horizons rest on coarse timestamp reconstruction**, most aggressively on Base where the first 26% of a block's transaction positions are stamped `b-1.8s` and the rest `b` -- up to 1.8 s of quantization against a 2 s horizon -- and the source itself calls this a "coarse approximation" that "retains timing uncertainty, particularly for trades in intermediate Flashblocks".
4. **Single reference venue and cross-pair basis.** All markouts use Bybit USDT-pair microprices converted to USDC; no second reference venue, no venue-weighted composite, and the USDT/USDC conversion basis is never quantified.
5. **Fee-inclusiveness of the execution price is not stated**, so it is unclear whether AMM-side and propAMM-side markouts are measured on the same fee basis (Section 6 gives only a price difference).
6. **Mechanism decoding covers the tail of the sample only.** The decoded Tessera implementation was activated **12 Aug 2026** and Metric/Elfomo logic is from **August 2026**, while the measurement window runs from **1 Sep 2025**; the source warns that undecoded implementations "may differ". Channel attribution therefore extrapolates a late-sample decode across a full year.
7. **Solana update counts are estimates** (any non-swap program interaction counts as an update; the source cannot observe whether a given update moved the quote) -- yet update frequency and the 0.56 update-volatility correlation are core to channel 1.
8. **Spoofing cannot be corroborated on Solana** -- "we lack the historical quote data to corroborate it"; only Tessera on Base has both states available.
9. **One year, one regime, shrinking market.** Sep 2025-Aug 2026 only, during which Solana propAMM volume fell from $1.82B to $530M per 90 days (a 71% decline) and Base/Monad propAMM markets are only months old; no subperiod, seasonality or stress-regime breakdown of the markout gap is reported.
10. **Heavy venue concentration.** HumidiFi above 50% then BisonFi 40.3% of Solana propAMM volume, Tessera the whole decoded Base story; no leave-one-venue-out robustness of the pooled markout.
11. **No capacity, participation or inventory analysis** (`capacity` 0), so the edge per fill is never converted into edge per dollar of inventory or per unit of queue position.
12. **Scope exclusions shrink generality**: Ethereum excluded as RFQ-like, Monad CLOB (Kuru) excluded, permissionless vault venues excluded, several swap venues only recognised not resolved (each counted as an unknown pool, which caps route-classification completeness).
13. **Route-classification self-check is a heuristic**, not a proof: the atomic-arbitrage flag is a balance-dictionary rule validated only by address-set overlap (92.9% / 99.1% of Tessera volume), and any transaction containing an undecodable swap is forced into `other` (25-53% of notional), which is also the class with the largest markouts on Base (+1.82 bps).
14. **The retail conclusion and the spoofing cost are never reconciled** (contradiction 1): retail is said to win while users are simultaneously said to bear 1.08 bps of spoofing cost, on different baselines.
15. **Third parties cannot buy the edge.** The operator supplies the inventory from its own vault; there is no passive instrument, no reported LP token performance, no fee-APR or impermanent-loss series -- the measurement says nothing about what a deposit into these venues would earn.
16. **No code, no data availability, no repository, no license text in the body** (census all 0 or reference-only), so exact reproduction requires rebuilding the entire pipeline from the Appendix A description.
17. **Publication status is preprint only** (cs.CE, no journal-ref, no publisher DOI, no peer-review statement) with Category Labs affiliation for two of three authors -- the measured venues include the authors' own ecosystem interest, a potential though unstated conflict (no competing-interests statement exists either way).
18. **Prior external claim unverified by this source**: the Solana quote/execution gap is attributed to a third-party blog post the source cites but does not test, and the ">half of SOL/USDC volume" claim is cited to an August 2025 blog post while the paper's own data covers 2025-2026.

## Crypto portability

`direct`

The source is crypto-native: it measures on-chain spot liquidity provision on Solana, Base and Monad, so no porting is required for the mechanism itself. Boundaries and risks that still must be stated:

- **Market type:** spot on-chain DEX only. There is **no perpetual-futures, funding, mark/index-price, liquidation or ADL content** anywhere in the source (`perpetual` 0, `funding` 0, `leverage` 0, `liquidation` 0 occurrences) -- those remain **data gap**, not "not applicable by proof".
- **24/7 sessions and timestamps:** the source handles them explicitly and per chain (validator-vote interpolation, Flashblock two-point approximation, MonadBFT consensus timestamps); any replication inherits their error bars, which are material at the 2 s horizon.
- **Venue fragmentation is the mechanism, not a nuisance**: the edge depends on being the non-stale venue relative to a reference price, so cross-venue basis, reference-venue choice and atomically routable companion venues are all first-order.
- **Contract/venue risk:** propAMM pricing is proprietary, upgradeable and partly opaque; only three Base implementations were decoded and the source warns others may differ. Proxy upgrades, operator keys, address-label manipulation and blacklisting are live risks for anyone transacting on these venues.
- **Taker-side spoofing risk** (1.08 bps average gap on Tessera) applies directly to any crypto strategy that routes through aggregators reading previous-block state.
- **Custody, withdrawal, stablecoin (USDC/USDT) conversion basis and operator insolvency** are not modelled -- data gap.
- **Access asymmetry:** the edge accrues to inventory-owning operators, so crypto portability of the *mechanism* is `direct` while portability of the *edge into an outside strategy* is `unproven`.

## Limitations

- **not independently reproduced**; no backtest, simulation, on-chain query or market-data download was performed this run -- only arithmetic identities over printed values.
- **underspecified:** what "gross" excludes in "gross maker markout"; whether the execution price is fee-inclusive; how the 14 programs map to the 13 named Solana venues; how the Section 7.1 price/book/default-fee update classification is possible on chains whose venues are not decoded; which gas corners produce the "5 to 15 times" band.
- **data gap:** net-of-quoting-cost PnL; Solana historical quote states; fee-APR/impermanent-loss series for an actual LP position; capacity and inventory bounds; code and data availability; funding/borrow/liquidation for any perp-shaped extension; a competing-interests statement.
