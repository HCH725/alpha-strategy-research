## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The authors introduce **oracle-parametrized automated market makers (OP-AMMs)**: quoting rules that depend jointly on the pool's reserve ratio `r = y/x` and an external oracle log-price `pi`, extending the information-agnostic AMM axioms to settings such as tokenized securities where price discovery happens off-chain (Sections 1-2).

- **Definition 2.1** - an OP-AMM is a `C^1` map `p(r, pi)` satisfying (i) strict monotonicity in `r`, (ii) surjectivity in `r`, (iii) a **non-decreasing and non-expansive oracle response** `0 <= p_pi <= 1`. Section 2.1 then lists four *optional* structural properties: (iv) surjectivity in the oracle (which excludes information-agnostic AMMs), (v) **boundary divergence** `g_0(pi) = g_inf(pi) = -inf` (a finite trade cannot drain either reserve), (vi) symmetry `p(r,pi) = -p(1/r,-pi)`, (vii) re-denomination invariance.
- **Proposition 2.3** - for fixed `pi` the OP-AMM behaves like a standard AMM: it admits a conditional invariant `U(x,y,pi) = log x + G(y/x, pi)` whose unit superlevel set is closed, convex and upward closed, and whose marginal price reproduces `p`.
- **Three constructions.** *Price-Tracking* `p_PT = pi + log phi(r)` (full pass-through, `p_pi = 1`); *Inventory-Tracking* `p_IT = pi + log phi(r e^{-pi})` (degenerates back to the plain CPMM when `phi(r)=r`); *Geometric-Average* `p_GA = lambda*pi + (1-lambda)*log phi(r)` with `lambda in (0,1)`, so `p_pi = lambda` is constant (Example 2.4, 2.5, 2.9). For the CPMM baseline `phi(r)=r` the GA curve is the level set of a **CES** utility, i.e. `y^lambda + e^{lambda*pi} x^lambda = const`, and `lambda -> 0` recovers Uniswap v2 while `lambda -> 1` becomes fully oracle-driven.
- **Assumption 2.6 + Proposition 2.7 + Theorem 2.8** - under a *strict* contraction `p_pi <= c_r < 1`, Banach's theorem gives a unique inventory-implied equilibrium price `pi*(r)`, and every such OP-AMM price is the convex interpolation `p(r,pi) = lambda(r,pi)*pi + (1-lambda(r,pi))*pi*(r)` with `lambda(r,pi) in [0, c_r]`. This is the paper's central structural claim: **the quote is always part oracle, part inventory.**
- **Lemma 2.10 / Example 2.11 - capital efficiency.** Matching the local curvature of the trading curve to an equally capitalized CPMM gives equivalent liquidity `L_OP = 2*sqrt(S) / (p_r * (S+r)^2) = 4*S*L / (p_r * (S+r)^2)`; the OP-AMM is locally deeper iff `p_r < 4S/(S+r)^2`. With an accurate oracle the GA is deeper than the baseline by exactly `1/(1-lambda)`; for a CPMM-based GA the depth condition is `|log S - pi| < ((1-lambda)/lambda) * log((1+sqrt(lambda))/(1-sqrt(lambda)))`.
- **Theorem 3.1 / Remark 3.2 - LVR.** With `d zeta = d s - p_pi d pi`, the instantaneous loss-versus-rebalancing rate is `ell_t = (S_t x_t) / (2 (S_t + r_t) p_r) * d<zeta>/dt >= 0`, and `ell_t / V_t = (1/8) (L_OP/L) d<zeta>/dt`. For **any** information-agnostic design (`p_pi = 0`) the residual variance is pinned at `sigma^2`, so `ell/V` per unit relative depth is fixed at `sigma^2/8` - meaning concentrated-liquidity designs move depth and LVR up together, while **oracle information is the only lever that moves the design off that line**.
- **Perfect oracle (Example 3.3)**: the CPMM gives `ell = (1/8) V sigma^2`; the GA has `ell_GA/V_GA = (1-lambda) ell_AMM/V_AMM`; full pass-through (`p_PT`) gives `d zeta = 0` and `ell = 0`.
- **Noisy oracle (Corollary 3.4, Example 3.6)**: with an OU oracle error the residual variance becomes `(1-p_pi)^2 sigma^2 + p_pi^2 (sigma_eta)^2 - 2 rho_eta p_pi (1-p_pi) sigma sigma_eta`. The GA beats the baseline locally iff `sigma_eta < sqrt((1-lambda)/lambda) * sigma`, and beats an **equally deep** information-agnostic design iff `sigma_eta < sqrt((2-lambda)/lambda) * sigma`. The paper stresses these are *local, state-matched* comparisons.
- **Discrete updates (Proposition 3.8, Example 3.9)**: `LVR = integral ell dt + sum Delta LVR`, where the jump at each oracle refresh is the **baseline oracle extractable value (OEV)**; a stale oracle also displaces the reserve state between updates.
- **Sandwich around an update (Proposition 3.11, Corollary 3.12, Lemma 3.14, Remark 3.15)**: absent fees the supremal front-run/back-run value is `Pi* >= Delta LVR` and can equal the **entire pool value**; for a GA pool it is attained by a *finite* trade because GA **fails** property (v). With a proportional fee `gamma` (band `Gamma = -log(1-gamma)`), front-running creates no incremental value whenever `p(r,pi+) - p(r,pi-) <= Gamma`; for GA the sufficient condition is `lambda*|Delta pi| <= Gamma`, and a deviation-triggered update policy `tau_{k+1} = inf{t > tau_k : |s_tilde - pi| > eps} wedge (tau_k + h)` is protected whenever `lambda*eps <= Gamma`.
- **Remark 3.10**: letting the sensitivity decay with oracle age, `lambda(Delta t)` decreasing to 0, acts as a **soft circuit breaker** from price-taking back to price-making - described but not simulated.
- **Section 4.1**: for a CPMM-based GA under an uncorrelated noisy oracle, relative depth `L_GA/L = (1/(1-lambda)) * 4 m/(1+m)^2` with `m = exp((lambda/(1-lambda)) k alpha)`, and relative arbitrage loss `ell_GA/ell = ((1-lambda)^2 + lambda^2 alpha^2) * L_GA/L`, where `alpha = sigma_eta/sigma` and `k` is the initial misalignment in units of stationary oracle-error standard deviations. At `lambda = 0` both equal 1 with derivatives `+1` and `-1`, so a small-`lambda` dominance interval exists; as `lambda -> 1` the three cases (noise-free / aligned-but-noisy / misaligned) behave differently and dominance is **non-monotone** (Figure 1d).
- **Section 4.2**: counterfactual backtest of the GA on a simulated tokenized-SPY pool against one-second NBBO over 2023, across three oracle regimes and a `lambda x gamma` grid, with metrics tracking error (TE), realized LVR net of collected fees, and average relative market depth (details and numbers under Evidence).
- **Sections 5-6**: the design is claimed to **separate** flow into stale-price arbitrageurs (disincentivised) and liquidity-sensitive traders (given deeper quotes), and the authors explicitly scope the benefit to *stale-price* adverse selection, state that informed-flow adverse selection is **not** addressed and would now face a deeper market, and list an equilibrium order-flow model, volatility oracles and finite-maturity RWA designs as future work.

### Research interpretation

The falsifiable hypothesis is **liquidity-provision economics**, not a directional premium: an AMM pricing rule that partially passes an external price feed through to its quote can simultaneously (a) concentrate executable depth at the prevailing price and (b) reduce the stale-quote adverse selection that an information-agnostic pool loses to, **but only up to an oracle-quality-dependent limit, beyond which oracle-error variance is imported and the design underperforms a plain CPMM.** Decomposed by component role:

- **Regime / signal source**: the external oracle, stylised into three regimes that differ only in heartbeat `h`, deviation threshold `eps` and posting delay `delta`.
- **Primary pricing rule**: the oracle sensitivity `lambda in [0,1)` of the geometric-average (GA) quote `p = lambda*pi + (1-lambda) log r` - the paper's one genuine new control variable.
- **Secondary control**: the proportional fee `gamma` (escrowed to LPs, Uniswap v3 convention).
- **Risk / exit**: **none**. There is no entry, no exit, no holding period and no position-sizing rule; liquidity provision here is a continuous state, and pool withdrawal is never modelled.
- **Absent**: no forecast, no cross-sectional ranking, no directional view, no stop, no volatility targeting, no hedging leg.

Three economic channels are consistent with the reported numbers, and the source does not discriminate between them:

1. **Stale-quote adverse-selection reduction** (the channel the paper proves): `p_pi > 0` shrinks the residual innovation `d zeta`, which is exactly the LVR driver.
2. **Depth concentration near the market price** (Lemma 2.10): a `1/(1-lambda)` multiplier on equivalent CPMM liquidity, which should let an LP capture more fee volume per unit of capital - **but the counterfactual contains no liquidity-motivated flow, so this channel is never monetised in the source.**
3. **Risk transfer, not risk removal**: Corollary 3.4 shows the LP swaps market-lag variance for oracle-error variance and its covariance with the market. Deeper books also face **more** informed flow, which Section 5 concedes is unaddressed.

The **missing link** is channel 2 vs channel 3: the source measures the *cost* side (LVR) and the *capacity* side (depth) but never measures *fee revenue from real order flow*, so the LP's net expected return is **not established anywhere in the paper** - only the components are. `lambda` is a design parameter, not a prediction; nothing here claims return predictability.

Not established by the source: any equilibrium fee, any capacity or AUM limit, any gas-inclusive net figure, any multi-year or multi-instrument robustness, and any crypto-market demonstration.

## Signal

**Fully specified parts (source-reported).**

- **Formation timestamp / refresh rule**: candidate signal `s_tilde_t = s_t - delta` where `s_t = log((B_t + A_t)/2)` is the NBBO log mid; updates follow Equation (3.7), `tau_{k+1} = inf{t > tau_k : |s_tilde_t - pi_{tau_k}| > eps} wedge (tau_k + h)`, with the infimum taken over the **one-second observation grid**, and `pi = s_tilde` at the update (Section 4.2; footnote 4 states that `eps = 0` is used in Section 4.2 to denote an update at every observation).
- **Three oracle regimes, exact parameters**: **L1** (high-latency L1 chain) `h = 1 hour`, `eps = 25 bps`, `delta = 60 seconds`; **L2** (lower-latency L2) `h = 1 hour`, `eps = 5 bps`, `delta = 2 seconds`; **Pull** (Chainlink-style pull oracle) `h = 1 second`, `eps = 0`, `delta = 1 second` (Section 4.2).
- **Quote formation, every second**: fee-free pre-trade mid `P^mid_t = exp(lambda*pi_t + (1-lambda) log(r_{t-1}))`, then `P^ask_t = P^mid_t / (1-gamma)` and `P^bid_t = (1-gamma) P^mid_t` (Section 4.2).
- **Counterparty / crossing rule**: arbitrageurs trade **whenever `B_t > P^ask_t` or `P^bid_t > A_t`**, and at each second execute the **minimal** trade that brings the relevant quote onto the corresponding NBBO boundary, "thereby extracting the maximal guaranteed profit available from the pool" (Section 4.2).
- **Initialisation and horizon**: each of the **250 trading days of 2023** starts with both the oracle and the internal reserve-based price exactly at the market-open mid; statistics are aggregated at the market close; the simulation is **reset daily** to avoid the "overnight oracle problem" (Section 4.2).
- **Parameter grids**: `lambda in {0%, 1%, ..., 99%}` (100 values) and `gamma in {0, 1, ..., 50} bps` (51 values) - research-checked 100 x 51 = **5,100 cells per oracle regime, 15,300 cells over the three regimes**, each cell being an independent 250-day counterfactual.
- **No entry, exit, holding period, re-entry, stop, take-profit or sizing rule exists** in the source; this is a quoting policy, not a position rule.

**Research-proposed operationalisation (NOT source-reported; required to make the rule deployable, and labelled research-proposed here and nowhere presented as the paper's rule):** a choice of `(lambda, gamma, eps, h, delta)` *ex ante* rather than by search over the grid; a pool-withdrawal and re-entry rule; a capital-allocation cap per pool; a second-signal confirmation (e.g. quote only when the NBBO spread is inside a research-proposed band); and any inventory haircut. The source itself explicitly leaves the optimisation of the update policy (3.7) and the design of variance-linked fee schedules to future research, so **no selection rule for `lambda`, `eps` or `gamma` is source-reported.**

**Reconstructibility verdict:** the *quoting rule* is reconstructible from the paper down to the grid; the *reported performance* is **not** - all headline results live in contour plots (Figures 1-3) with no printed numeric table, the pool's initial size is never stated, the NBBO session boundaries and the second-count per day are not stated, and there is no code and no data-availability statement, so nothing can be regenerated from the source alone.

## Execution assumptions

**Source-reported.**

- **Signal-to-order timing**: the arbitrageur observes the **contemporaneous** NBBO at every second with no delay of its own, while the pool is handicapped by the oracle delay `delta` - an intentional asymmetry that makes the experiment a *worst-case* for the pool but a stylised one.
- **External fill model**: `we assume that the external market is infinitely deep at the NBBO and impose no additional slippage in the external market` (Section 4.2). So the arbitrageur's leg has **zero spread cost beyond crossing the quoted NBBO, zero impact and zero size limit**.
- **Pool fill model**: the pool always fills at its own quoted ask/bid for the minimal size needed to clear; partial fills, re-quotes during the trade, mempool ordering and competing searchers are **not modelled** (data gap).
- **Order type**: the pool *quotes* a bid and an ask; the arbitrageur's order type is not stated (underspecified). The only `market order` in the document (1 occurrence) is the **rebalancing benchmark** of the LVR metric, `executed via market orders at the NBBO bid and ask prices`.
- **Fees**: proportional fee `gamma` charged on the shares of the asset sold to the pool, **held in escrow for liquidity providers and not reinvested in the pool** (Uniswap v3 convention, stated twice); grid `0..50 bps`.
- **LVR metric definition**: `the difference between the value of a continuous rebalancing strategy (executed via market orders at the NBBO bid and ask prices) and the realized pool value net of collected fees` (Section 4.2) - i.e. **net of pool fee revenue, but that revenue comes only from arbitrage trades.**

### Negative evidence

1. **Single instrument, single year.** SPY only, calendar 2023 only. No second ETF, no second asset class, no second year, no regime split, no sub-period breakdown anywhere in the 45 pages.
2. **The order flow is adversarial-only by construction.** `we impose a strictly adversarial trading environment in which all trading volume originates from arbitrageurs exploiting stale quotes`, and later `the simulation contains no liquidity-motivated demand`. Consequence: **fee revenue from real, uninformed flow is never measured**, so the LP's net expected return - the number an actual liquidity provider cares about - does not exist in the paper. LVR is "net of collected fees", but those fees are paid only by arbitrageurs.
3. **The fee optimum is a boundary solution throughout.** `across this entire frontier, the optimal fee level sits at gamma* = 50 bps, i.e., the upper boundary of the tested grid`. A parameter whose optimum is the grid edge is **not identified**, only bounded from below; the source concedes it "should not be read as an equilibrium fee recommendation".
4. **The backtest never simulates the sandwich the paper itself quantifies.** Section 3.2 assumes arbitrage "begins only after the update" and Section 3.2.1 then shows a pre-update front-run gives `Pi* >= Delta LVR`, with `Pi*/Delta LVR = 9.565` in the paper's own Remark 3.13 example (research-computed). Section 4.2 returns to the after-only model, so the reported realized LVR is a **lower bound on extractable value whenever atomic front-running is possible** - and the GA curve used for the whole empirical case study **fails the paper's own boundary-divergence property (v)**, which is exactly what makes the drain finite (Remark 3.13).
5. **The external leg is frictionless.** `infinitely deep at the NBBO` with `no additional slippage`, zero impact and zero competition, while the pool absorbs `delta = 60 s` of delay in L1. This is a stylised worst-case asymmetry in both directions, not an executable venue model.
6. **Overnight and closed-market risk is explicitly excluded.** `we reset the simulation daily so as to avoid the overnight oracle problem ... this experiment does not measure overnight or closed-market oracle risk.` The gap that kills most real oracle designs (weekend gaps, halts, opening auctions) is therefore untested.

## Falsification plan

All thresholds below are **research-defined falsification thresholds** unless explicitly attributed to the source; none of them is a source claim, and none of the operational rules below is source-reported. Each gate has a failure condition and a fixed action. **No-retuning rule:** once gates F2-F14 are opened, `lambda`, `gamma`, `eps`, `h`, `delta`, the instrument set and the cost assumptions may not be changed to rescue a failure; a failed gate downgrades the record to untradable research and must not be re-tested with adjusted thresholds on the same data.

- **F1 - Printed-value reproduction (blocked on code).** If code, the oracle signal and the NBBO extract are ever released at a pinned commit, regenerate Figures 1-3. *Fail:* the Figure 3a Pull-oracle minimum LVR at `TE <= 7.5 bps` differs from `0.001 bps` by more than `0.0005 bps`, or the Figure 3b `lambda*` at that point differs from `0.84` by more than `0.01`. *Action:* reject the Evidence section entirely.
- **F2 - Multi-year, multi-instrument gate (research-defined).** Re-run the Section 4.2 counterfactual unchanged on 2019-2023 and on a pre-declared set of 20 US ETFs ranked by quoted-spread decile. *Fail:* the Pull-oracle Pareto ordering (relative LVR `<= 1` at equal-or-lower TE than the CPMM) holds in fewer than `4 of 5` years or fewer than `14 of 20` ETFs. *Action:* restrict the hypothesis to SPY-like ultra-liquid underlyings.
- **F3 - Second-quote-source gate (research-defined).** Rebuild `s_t` from an independent quote feed with a different vendor and session convention. *Fail:* sign agreement with the printed LVR ordering in fewer than `4 of 5` years. *Action:* attribute the result to vendor-specific NBBO construction.
- **F4 - Latency / noise stress ladder (research-defined).** Sweep `delta in {1, 2, 5, 15, 60, 300} seconds` and `eps in {0, 1, 5, 25, 100} bps` on the frozen 2023 grid. *Fail:* no `lambda > 0` simultaneously attains relative depth `>= 1` and relative LVR `<= 1` in at least half of the cells with `delta <= 15 s`. *Action:* confine the claim to the pull-oracle class only.
- **F5 - Sandwich-inclusive gate (research-defined).** Extend the simulator so a research-proposed attacker may trade immediately **before** an oracle update (capital capped at the pool value, modelled per Proposition 3.11), then re-run Figure 3. *Fail:* the LVR advantage of `lambda = 0.84` over `lambda = 0` shrinks by more than `50%`, or the pool's post-attack position is worse than the CPMM's. *Action:* publish Figures 1-3 only as sandwich-free lower bounds and withdraw the headline.
- **F6 - Fee-revenue gate (research-defined).** Add liquidity-motivated uninformed flow (research-proposed: arrivals calibrated so that volume at `gamma = 50 bps` is at least `60%` of volume at `gamma = 0`) and compute LP net P&L = fee revenue - LVR - gas. *Fail:* LP net P&L at every `lambda > 0` is `<= ` the CPMM's. *Action:* reject the economic claim; the design is then only a cost reduction, not a return improvement.
- **F7 - Gas / infrastructure gate (research-defined).** Charge oracle-maintenance and swap gas at `0 / 0.1 / 1 / 5 / 10` bps of pool value per day. *Fail:* the net-of-gas LVR advantage disappears at `<= 1 bp/day`. *Action:* mark the result as infrastructure-dependent and unquantified.
- **F8 - Fee-boundary gate (research-defined).** Extend the fee grid to `0..200 bps`. *Fail:* the optimum still sits on the boundary, i.e. no interior optimum exists. *Action:* withdraw every fee recommendation and report `gamma` as unidentified rather than optimal.
- **F9 - Informed-flow gate (research-defined).** Introduce a research-proposed informed trader with a one-bar private edge and re-run LP LVR at `lambda in {0, 0.5, 0.84}`. *Fail:* at `lambda = 0.84` the LP's informed-flow loss exceeds the CPMM's by more than `20%`. *Action:* restrict the claim to stale-price adverse selection only (the source's own scope) and record the deeper-book risk as realised.
- **F10 - Boundary-divergence / drain gate (research-defined).** Because GA fails property (v), impose a research-proposed reserve floor and replay Proposition 3.11's optimal front-run on every update event at `lambda >= 0.84`, `gamma <= 50 bps`. *Fail:* an attacker extracts more than `10%` of pool value in at least `1%` of update events. *Action:* require a redesigned (boundary-divergent) curve before any deployment; no deployment on the GA family.
- **F11 - Cross-market portability gate (research-defined).** Repeat the frozen experiment on 20 crypto spot pairs with venue-specific fees and 24/7 candles. *Fail:* the Pareto ordering holds in fewer than `12 of 20` pairs. *Action:* mark crypto portability as refuted for this mechanism.
- **F12 - Multiple-testing gate (research-defined).** Apply Benjamini-Hochberg at `q < 0.10` to any cell quoted as "optimal" across the 15,300-cell grid, and, if a P&L series exists, a deflated-Sharpe adjustment over the configuration search. *Fail:* no cell survives. *Action:* downgrade all optimality wording to uncorrected exploratory search.
- **F13 - Depth-realisability gate (research-defined).** Measure executable depth as the size obtainable within `10 bps` of mid on a real book, at the source's pool value, for `lambda = 0.84` versus `lambda = 0`. *Fail:* realizable depth is `< 2x` the CPMM's (against the claimed `6.25x` multiplier). *Action:* withdraw the capital-efficiency claim as a curvature artefact.
- **F14 - Frozen forward gate (research-defined).** From `2026-10-01` run at least `12` months with `lambda`, `gamma`, `eps`, `h` and `delta` frozen at the Section 4.2 values and no re-search on the evaluation window. *Fail:* relative LVR at `lambda = 0.84` in the Pull regime is `> 1`. *Action:* mark the mechanism non-stationary.

## Crypto portability

**`adapted`** (mechanism is on-chain-native and ports mechanically, but the source demonstrates it **only** on a US equity NBBO counterfactual; crypto performance unproven).

- **What ports mechanically**: an AMM with an external oracle input, a proportional escrowed fee and a sandwich threat are native DeFi objects - `p`, `lambda`, `gamma`, the conditional invariant and Propositions 3.8 / 3.11 / 3.14 are venue-agnostic once a spot pool and an oracle feed exist. The mechanism was **born in crypto-adjacent AMM theory**, so this is not a pure TradFi import.
- **What the source does *not* show**: there is **no crypto market data anywhere in the paper** (census `bitcoin` 0, `ethereum` 0, `perpetual` 0, `24/7` 0; `crypto` 3 occurrences, all in the introduction and one reference title). The only simulation is one-second **SPY NBBO over US equity hours in 2023**, with a daily reset at the equity market open - a calendar with no crypto analogue.
- **The source itself flags the crypto regime as the more dangerous one**: `The vulnerabilities documented for crypto oracles are largely concentrated in long-tail, low-liquidity assets for which the spot price (and the time-weighted average price) is inexpensive to move`, contrasted with an NBBO "subject to regulatory surveillance". The market into which this would port is therefore exactly the one the paper identifies as more manipulable, and no manipulation stress test is run.
- **Crypto-specific porting risks**: 24/7 sessions (no close to aggregate against, no daily reset, no "overnight problem" to hide behind); venue fragmentation and oracle-feed selection (which index, which venues, stale-index risk); funding, mark and index-price differences for any perpetual-based implementation (all zero-occurrence in the source); gas auction and priority-fee dynamics plus multi-searcher MEV competition beyond the single stylised arbitrageur; thin or absent liquidity on tokenized-RWA pools where the design is claimed to matter; and stablecoin / depeg effects on the numéraire leg.
- Crypto portability is **not** authorization to trade; it states only that the mechanism can be *evaluated* in crypto with new data, a 24/7 clock, a real fee/gas model and an oracle-manipulation stress test (F11 above).
