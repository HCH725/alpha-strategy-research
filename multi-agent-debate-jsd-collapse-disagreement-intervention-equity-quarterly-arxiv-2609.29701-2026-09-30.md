## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The paper is an **evaluation study of LLM multi-agent debate as a portfolio-allocation mechanism**, organised around three research questions: whether measured reasoning quality predicts financial performance (RQ1), whether structured debate improves reasoning and portfolio outcomes (RQ2), and whether runtime interventions repair debate failures (RQ3). Specialised LLM agents (`macro`, `value`, `risk`, `technical`, `sentiment`, `devil's advocate`; experiments "primarily use subsets of 3-4 roles") run a `propose → critique → revise` loop orchestrated in LangGraph, a CRIT evaluator scores each revised argument on four pillars (logical validity, evidential support, alternative consideration, causal alignment), a PID controller can regulate critique "contentiousness" β, and a judge or an unweighted mean aggregates the agents' last revisions into one portfolio.

The authors' own explanation of where value comes from is explicitly **diversity, not reasoning quality**:

- They identify **sycophantic convergence** as "a central failure mode, in which agents progressively abandon independent positions during critique-revision cycles and converge toward similar allocations"; in the 4-agent/5-round debate, cash allocations converge from 10–50% at first proposal to 55–61% by round 5 and "the largest JSD drop occurs between the initial proposal and the first revision".
- The stated mechanism for the positive result is **ensemble diversity**: "the central finding across all debate experiments is that mechanisms preserving disagreement improve financial outcomes, while mechanisms enforcing deeper reasoning do not", justified by Krogh–Vedelsby ensemble-error decomposition (ensemble error = mean member error minus a diversity term) and by Condorcet's jury theorem (majority improvement requires voter independence), with "Our multi-agent trading system functions as such an ensemble" and "The critique–revise cycle unintentionally introduces correlation because agents condition on each other's positions."
- The claimed economic channel for the intervention is therefore: **prevent premature agreement → keep the technical agent's independent signal → raise realised return**, with the source stating the mechanism explicitly: preserving "the Technical Agent's independent signal appears to be the primary mechanism through which the intervention improves performance".
- The source is equally explicit that reasoning-quality interventions do **not** pay: causal-scaffolded prompts lift CRIT scores without lifting returns, and the CRIT-causal intervention (A10) slightly *worsens* Sharpe and drawdown.

### Research interpretation

Stated as a falsifiable hypothesis rather than a fact:

- **Regime/filter:** none in the market sense — the "regime" is the *internal* debate state: cross-agent allocation disagreement measured as a Jensen–Shannon divergence between the pre-revision (proposal) and post-revision portfolios.
- **Primary signal (the only component that carries a source-reported economic effect):** a **disagreement-maintenance overlay** on a long-only LLM allocation ensemble. If post-revision disagreement collapses below a fixed ratio of pre-revision disagreement, the revision phase is re-run with a diversity "nudge" prompt (plus a hard cap on how far a portfolio may move during the retry), so that the averaged/judged portfolio retains genuinely independent member views instead of a correlated consensus.
- **Base allocation engine:** quarterly, long-only, weighted allocation over a small US-equity universe produced by 2–4 role-specialised LLM agents from a point-in-time quarterly memo; no single agent's directional view is itself claimed as alpha.
- **Hypothesised mechanism class:** *ensemble-diversity / anti-correlation* — the same family as "crowded-positioning / correlated-signal decay", but operating inside a decision ensemble rather than in the market: aggregation error falls when members stay decorrelated, and the critique–revise interaction artificially correlates them. The complementary null hypothesis the paper itself tests is that **process quality (measured reasoning) is not economically predictive** in a public-information setting, i.e. a structural information ceiling.
- **What is NOT claimed by this record:** that debate portfolios are profitable, that the JSD overlay beats an equal-weight or index benchmark, or that any of it survives transaction costs. The source reports the opposite on all three counts (see Evidence and Negative evidence).

1. **No transaction costs, spreads, taxes or liquidity constraints are modelled at all** — stated by the source (§5.1), not inferred; census shows `transaction costs` only in the two admissions, `slippage`/`bid-ask`/`turnover`/`market impact`/`borrow`/`funding`/`limit order`/`market order` all 0.
2. **The simulation explicitly removes timing**: "Quarterly rebalancing without transaction costs removes the dimensions where reasoning quality might matter most".
3. **Debate loses to naive equal-weight**: beats 1/N only 33% of 455 runs (p < 0.001; research-computed 1.55e-13), pooled −1.29pp.
4. **No configuration consistently outperforms SPY**: "excess returns cluster near zero with wide variance"; the best printed excess on the 3-scenario panel is +0.04% with SEM ±4.04% (SEM ≈ 100× the point estimate).

## Falsification plan

Every threshold below is **`research-defined falsification threshold`** (chosen by this record, not by the source) unless explicitly marked `source-reported`. Each gate has a stated action, and a pre-declared **no-retuning rule** closes the plan: once started, the JSD threshold `0.8`, `min_js_proposal 0.10`, round count, role set, model names, aggregator, universe and cost ladder are frozen; a failed gate may not be rescued by re-selecting them, and a failure of the primary gate forces **rejection of the diversity-maintenance hypothesis as a tradable mechanism** (retention as a research-only observation at most).

- **F1 — Printed-value reproduction.** Re-run A7 base vs `+JS` from the pinned repository over the same 35 scenarios (3 repeats per cell to absorb `temperature 0.3` noise). Pass: Sharpe diff within `+0.143 ± 0.05` **and** positive in ≥ 2 of 3 repeats. Fail: |diff| < 0.05, sign flip, or CI including 0 in ≥ 2 repeats → reject. Blocked today by API budget (`$5–8`/scenario) and absent seeds; label as pending, never as passed.
- **F2 — Multiplicity gate.** Pre-register A7 Sharpe as the *sole* primary endpoint; recompute the full Table 12 family (4 tests) with Bonferroni α = 0.0125 and BH at q < 0.10. Pass: the primary survives at α = 0.0125. Fail: `p ≥ 0.0125` → treat the headline as a selection artefact and reject.
- **F3 — Cost ladder (research-proposed; the source models none).** Charge 0 / 5 / 10 / 20 / 50 bps one-way on realised quarterly turnover from the intervention's extra revisions. Pass: net Sharpe diff ≥ `+0.10` at 10 bps **and** net excess vs SPY > 0. Fail: gain erased at ≤ 10 bps → reject as untradeable.
- **F4 — Benchmark gate.** On ≥ 50 fresh scenarios, debate-with-intervention must beat 1/N in `> 50%` of scenarios with two-sided binomial `p < 0.05`, and beat SPY on average with `p < 0.05`. Fail (current state: 33%) → reject the "debate adds alpha" reading and keep only the risk-management reading.
- **F5 — Mechanism ablation (diversity vs debate).** Replace the critique–revise loop with `K` independent single-agent runs averaged with the same JSD cap but **no cross-agent critique**. Pass: the independent-ensemble control matches or beats the debate arm within `±0.05` Sharpe → then the effect is generic ensemble diversity, not debate; if the debate arm is superior by `≥ +0.10` with `p < 0.05`, the debate-specific claim survives. Either outcome falsifies the *other* explanation.
- **F6 — Third-agent robustness (A8 replication).** Repeat A8 (adds `risk`) 3× at 35 scenarios. Pass: Sharpe diff `≥ +0.10` with `p < 0.05`. Fail: replicate the printed `+0.04, p = 0.53` → the mechanism is configuration-specific and must be reported as such; two failures in a row → reject generality.
- **F7 — Threshold dose–response.** JS threshold ∈ {0.6, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9} plus `min_js_proposal` ∈ {0.05, 0.10, 0.20}, all pre-registered. Pass: ≥ 2 of the 4 thresholds show a positive Sharpe diff with the sign stable across ≥ 60% of scenarios. Fail: only `0.8` works → reject (threshold fishing).
- **F8 — Cadence and universe transfer.** Same rule on (a) monthly rebalance and (b) a ≥ 100-ticker universe over ≥ 50 scenarios spanning 2021Q1–2026Q3 without the 2022 skew. Pass: Sharpe diff `≥ +0.10`, `p < 0.05`, sign-consistent in ≥ 3 of 4 regime sub-periods (research-defined sub-periods: 2021Q4–2022Q4, 2023, 2024, 2025Q1–2026Q3). Fail → reject portability beyond the original panel.
- **F9 — Leave-2022-out test.** Recompute A7 excluding every 2022 scenario (the deliberately over-weighted stress period). Fail: sign flip or |diff| < 0.05 → reject as regime-conditional.
- **F10 — Leakage / pre-training gate.** Re-run with a scrubbed memo (drop `RET*`, `MOM*`, `SHARPE`, `SMA*` — the fields most likely to trigger memorised answers) and with anonymised ticker labels. Pass: Sharpe diff `≥ +0.08`. Fail: effect vanishes → attribute the original to leakage and reject.
- **F11 — Non-determinism gate.** 5 repeats × 35 scenarios per arm; require the paired diff to keep its sign in `≥ 80%` of scenarios and the cross-repeat SD of the mean diff `< 0.05`. Fail → the effect is run noise.
- **F12 — Turnover / capacity bound (research-proposed).** Measure incremental quarterly turnover induced by intervention retries. Fail if incremental turnover exceeds `2×` the baseline turnover while the Sharpe gain stays `< +0.14` → the overlay trades away its own edge under any realistic cost.
- **F13 — Statistical power gate.** Expand to ≥ 120 scenario-quarters and require two-sided `p < 0.05` on the pre-registered primary **plus** a deflated-Sharpe style adjustment for the number of configurations tried (Table 2/3/11/13 families). Fail → reject.
- **F14 — Frozen forward window.** From **2026-10-01**, run the frozen configuration on newly accrued quarters for **≥ 12 months** with no parameter changes; pass only if the cumulative paired Sharpe diff is positive with `p < 0.05`. Fail → reject; the record stays `research-only` regardless.

## Crypto portability

**`adapted` (mechanism ports mechanically; trading performance in crypto is `unproven`).**

- The mechanism under test — *preserve cross-agent allocation diversity by triggering on a Jensen–Shannon collapse ratio* — contains no asset-class-specific content: it operates on agent portfolios, not on prices. It could in principle be layered on a crypto allocation ensemble with the same `0.8` ratio.
- The source nevertheless demonstrates it **only** on US-equity quarter panels with a SPY benchmark: `bitcoin`, `ethereum`, `perpetual`, `funding`, `crypto` and `24/7` each occur **0 times** in the pinned text. This record therefore treats any crypto claim as a ported hypothesis, not as crypto evidence.
- Crypto-specific risks that the source does not address: spot vs perpetual basis and **funding** settlements; 24/7 sessions that break the "first day of quarter" convention and the memo's calendar; venue fragmentation and per-venue LBM/spread differences; thin-book impact for the small, concentrated books this design produces; mark/index-price and liquidation mechanics if any leverage is introduced; LLM inference latency relative to crypto session volatility; and the total absence of a cost model in the source (a cost ladder is mandatory before any crypto claim, see F3).
- Operational note (`research-proposed`): a quarterly long-only book with 7–17% idle cash is a poor fit for crypto's fast regime rotation; any crypto port would need a re-derived cadence, which would be a new hypothesis requiring its own falsification, not a transfer of this record's evidence.
