## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The paper's claim is a measurement claim, not a return claim, and it is stated as such (Sections 1, 6, 8):

1. **The strategy exists and is executed at scale.** One-way arbitrage (OWA) converts A -> B indirectly through an intermediary coin I (A -> I -> B) when the product of the two leg prices beats the direct A -> B price, without closing a loop back to the starting coin (Section 2.2, contrasting it with triangular arbitrage). The authors detect **more than 402 M likely OWA sequences on Binance and almost 2 M on Kraken** after thresholding, against **580 M and 2.7 M candidates** before thresholding (Sections 4.3, 4.5), corresponding to **0.94 percent of Binance volume and 0.13 percent of Kraken volume** (Sections 5, 8). The source's own summary: "OWA is not merely a theoretical possibility" (Section 6).
2. **The economics are scale economics, not per-trade economics.** Gross profits are estimated at **$73.3 M on Binance and below $2.0 M on Kraken**; after two assumed fee rounds they fall to **$31.2 M and $975 k** (Section 5.1). Mean profit per sequence after fees is **$0.15 (maker/taker) and $0.04 (taker/taker) on Binance** and **$0.68 and $0.36 on Kraken** (Section 5.1), i.e. "net profits are merely cents" and single sequences are "less than $1 on average after accounting for trading fees" (Abstract, Section 1). The source therefore concludes OWA "has become professional enough that it is out of reach of hobbyist traders" because it requires speed, volume and possibly negotiated fees (Section 6).
3. **Competition is the stated causal force.** OWA volume migrated from an initial 70-80 ms concentration to 0-10 ms between the two orders on Binance maker/taker, and the profit-dominance profile turns negative beyond 180 ms (taker/taker) and 325 ms (maker/taker) on Binance (Sections 4.4.2, 5.2), while per-sequence gross returns fell from a 6 bps mean on Binance to 3.8 bps in Q2 2023 (Section 5.1). The source reads this as "increased competition among arbitrageurs" driving speed up and price discrepancies down (Sections 5.2, 6, 8).
4. **Candidate mechanisms for why the discrepancies arise at all** are offered as hypotheses, not findings: order-book depletion by an abnormal single-pair order (for example a pump-and-dump) making one pair inconsistent with the others, and exchange price-step granularity binding in one pair but not another (Section 6). The source explicitly leaves "the precise circumstances" for future work.

### Research interpretation

- **Mechanism class:** transient cross-pair price inconsistency inside a single centralized order-book venue - a law-of-one-price / microstructure-inefficiency channel, not a risk premium and not a forecasting signal. The falsifiable hypothesis is: *within one CEX, the executable price of an indirect two-leg conversion exceeds the executable direct price by more than two fee rounds often enough, and at large enough notional, that a latency-constrained two-order programme produces positive expected net profit after fees, spread, depth and latency costs.*
- **Component roles (kept separate for later ablation):**
  - Signal / trigger: an observed cross-pair price discrepancy between the direct pair and the two-leg route through a non-anchor intermediary coin (source-described in Section 2.2; no numeric entry threshold is given).
  - Confirmation: none in the source - there is no depth check, no order-flow check, no second-venue confirmation.
  - Execution: two separate orders sent in quick succession, since neither venue supports atomic batch trading; the source hypothesizes bots that send the second order proactively rather than waiting for the fill notification (Sections 2.2, 5.2).
  - Risk / exit: the source models none. Inventory risk of the intermediary coin is argued away only by the assumption that arbitrageurs "avoid holding volatile (non-anchor) coins" and "hold multiple anchor coins regardless" (Sections 2.2, 4.1).
- **What this record does NOT claim:** the source does not propose a tradable rule, does not run a backtest (0 occurrences of `backtest`), reports no Sharpe, CAGR, drawdown, win rate or p-value (all 0 occurrences), and does not claim the measurement generalizes beyond the two venues or beyond May 2023. The record captures a *capacity and feasibility measurement* for one arbitrage family, which is exactly what the incremental-write threshold allows ("new falsification or negative evidence").
- **Why it is still useful as an alpha hypothesis record:** the same repo carries DEX cyclic arbitrage, CEX-DEX latency arbitrage and cross-exchange spatial arbitrage records; none of them measures whether *executed, intra-venue* indirect conversions were actually profitable at scale on a centralized spot venue. This source is the first to answer that from settled trades rather than from quoted opportunities, and its answer is predominantly negative at the per-trade level.

## Signal

Every operational item below is tagged `source-reported`, `research-proposed` or `data gap`. Nothing tagged `research-proposed` may be quoted as the source's rule.

- **Economic trigger (source-reported, Section 2.2):** take the indirect route A -> I -> A2 instead of the direct A -> A2 whenever the product of the two leg prices produces more A2 than the direct conversion, after fees. The paper illustrates with 1 BTC buying 30 ETH or 582,012 DOGE, so a DOGE/ETH price of 0.00005155 or above makes BTC -> DOGE -> ETH cheaper than BTC -> ETH (Figure 1).
- **Entry threshold in basis points:** `data gap` - the source states no minimum edge, no quote-refresh rule and no monitoring rule. `research-proposed`: enter only when the measured gross discrepancy exceeds two fee rounds plus a fixed buffer, and recompute the discrepancy on the live book rather than on VWAP.
- **Formation timestamp:** `data gap` - the source never states when a trader would observe the discrepancy, nor any timezone convention for its own timestamps. `research-proposed`: use exchange server timestamps in UTC, evaluate on book updates, and require the second order to be submitted within the same observed window.
- **The source's thresholds are a DETECTION instrument, not a trading rule.** This distinction is load-bearing: Section 4.3-4.5 defines how the authors *matched* historical trades into likely OWA sequences. `source-reported` (Table 2, Sections 4.3, 4.4, 4.5): route shape A1 -> I -> A2 with A1 and A2 anchor coins (BTC, ETH, BNB, stable coins, and USD on Kraken) and I a non-anchor coin; initial quantity-difference window `dq` in [-10, 1] bps on Binance and [-40, 1] bps on Kraken, tightened to [-0.1, 0.1] bps on both; initial latency window `dt` <= 2 s, tightened to [0, 400] ms for Binance maker/taker, [0, 200] ms for Binance taker/taker, [0, 1000] ms for Kraken maker/taker and [0, 2000] ms for Kraken taker/taker; the sequence must END with a taker operation (the first operation may be a maker order so maker rebates can be captured); ambiguous operations are resolved by keeping only the sequence with the best price conversion; matching complexity is O(n^2) and took months on a shared cluster for Binance, three days for Kraken. The threshold values were chosen by *maximising profitability* of the resulting candidate set, which the paper declares is a precision-over-recall choice made in the absence of ground truth (Sections 4.2, 4.4).
- **Reference price for the direct leg (source-reported, Section 4.4):** the VWAP of all direct A1 -> A2 operations in the 2-second window preceding the first operation of the candidate sequence, on the stated ground that the opportunity "should be visible to the arbitrageur prior to the execution of the initial operation".
- **Profit definition (source-reported, Sections 4.4, 5):** absolute gross return `R = output(indirect) - output(direct at reference price)`; relative gross return `dp = p(A1->I->A2) / p_ref(A1->A2) - 1`. Refinement-stage profitability is computed **without** fees ("best case"); Section 5.1 adds the two fee scenarios.
- **Mark-to-market (source-reported, Section 3.1):** one-minute volume-weighted average price per coin; on Kraken every token trades directly against USD; on Binance 92.5 percent of tokens trade against BTC, so volumes are converted to BTC using the pair's quote-coin BTC VWAP and then to USD with the Kraken BTC/USD one-minute VWAP, with BNB or USDT/BUSD used as the quote leg when BTC is unavailable.
- **Position sizing:** `data gap` - never stated for the traders being measured. Median sequence volume is $97 (maker/taker) and $160 (taker/taker) on Binance with means of $411 and $500; Kraken medians $291 and $216 with means $907 and $844; the paper notes "the capital used in an OWA sequence is approximately half that" (Section 5).
- **Exit / holding period:** `data gap` for a tradable rule; for the measured sequences the holding period is the sequence itself, with `dt` bounded at 200-2,000 ms by Table 2.
- **Risk management:** `data gap` - no stop, no inventory limit, no notion limit, no venue-failure handling, no partial-fill handling anywhere in the paper.
- **Fully specified?** No. The strategy is reconstructable only at the level of economic trigger and measured sequence anatomy; entry threshold, sizing, exit, monitoring cadence and risk limits are `underspecified`, and the paper's numeric thresholds belong to its detector rather than to a trading rule. This record does not present the work as a reproducible strategy.

### Negative evidence

1. Per-sequence economics are pennies: after two assumed fee rounds the mean is $0.15 and $0.04 on Binance and $0.68 and $0.36 on Kraken (Section 5.1) - the abstract's "less than $1 on average" is the ceiling, not the typical case.
2. The edge is thinner than the fee: Binance mean gross return is 6 bps against a 4.8 bps round trip at the lowest public taker tier for two taker legs, and only 3.8 bps in Q2 2023 - below that round trip - so a taker/taker programme on Binance is net-negative on average in the most recent data of the sample.
3. Net profitability is entirely fee-scenario dependent: $73.3 M gross becomes $31.2 M with fees, and the source states "even small differences can change aggregate profits by the order of millions of dollars" while conceding some arbitrageurs must be paying below the published tiers (Section 5.1).
4. Binance taker/taker cumulative net profits declined between end-November 2021 and October 2022 under the double-fee assumption, yet volume stayed high - the source resolves this by assuming unobserved fee arrangements rather than by measuring them.
5. The trend is adverse: per-sequence gross returns and dispersion have fallen since Q1 2021, extreme mispricings have become rarer, and latency has compressed to 0-10 ms - the source's own reading is that competition has eaten the discrepancy (Sections 5.1, 5.2).
6. No ground truth: matching trades is heuristic, and the source states it "cannot quantify unprofitable sequences or failed OWA attempts (e.g., due to slippage), which prevents us from estimating losses" (Section 6.1) - the loss distribution is unobservable by construction.
7. Thresholds are selected on profitability (Sections 4.2, 4.4), so the detector is definitionally biased toward finding profitable-looking sequences; precision over recall is an explicit design choice, and no precision or recall figure is reported.
8. Excluded by construction: in-band-fee sequences, sequences with multiple inputs or split outputs, anchor-only intermediation, triangular arbitrage, and any sequence failing the +/-0.1 bps quantity or latency windows (Sections 4.4.1, 6.1) - the 0.94 percent and 0.13 percent shares are floors for one narrow definition, not a census of arbitrage.
9. Reference-price construction uses executed trades in a 2-second VWAP window rather than quotes, so the "direct" comparator is itself a traded-price average and can be stale relative to the book an arbitrageur would face (Section 4.4).
10. No order-book data at all: depth, spread, slippage and queue position are unreconstructable (Section 6.1), and `market impact` appears 0 times - gross returns are quoted-price returns, not executable returns.
11. No capacity analysis: `capacity` appears 0 times; median sequence size is $97-$160 on Binance, which bounds deployable notional per sequence without any aggregate ceiling being computed.

## Falsification plan

Every threshold below is a `research-defined falsification threshold` (Scout-chosen, not the source's), and every reproduction step is `research-proposed`. Data: the two venues' historical public spot trade feeds as the source used them, plus - where noted - L2 order-book snapshots and post-2023 trades, none of which the source had.

- **F1 Printed-value reproduction.** Rebuild the candidate and selected sets from the pinned thresholds and recompute 580 M / 2.7 M candidates, more than 402 M / almost 2 M selected, $190 B and $1.7 B volume, 0.94 percent and 0.13 percent shares, $73.3 M / $2.0 M gross and $31.2 M / $975 k net. **Threshold:** every count within 5 percent relative, every dollar figure within 10 percent relative, both volume shares within 0.05 percentage point. **Action:** on failure, mark every number in Evidence "not reproducible from the published description" and stop all downstream use of those figures.
- **F2 Detection precision.** Blind-hand-label 1,000 randomly drawn detected sequences by an independent reviewer with access to the raw trades, and separately inject 10,000 synthetic OWA sequences with known ground truth into a one-month slice and measure recovery. **Threshold:** precision >= 90 percent on the drawn sample and injected-sequence recall >= 70 percent. **Action:** fail -> prevalence, profit and timing conclusions are artefacts of the matcher; the record's mechanism claim is withdrawn.
- **F3 Selection-window cost audit.** Quantify the candidate volume and gain mass discarded by the +/-0.1 bps quantity window and by the 200/400/1000/2000 ms latency caps. **Threshold:** discarded candidate volume <= 10 percent of total candidate volume. **Action:** fail -> 0.94 percent is a floor far below the true figure and per-sequence profitability is conditioned on the best-matched tail; restate as a biased subsample.
- **F4 Fee-scenario robustness.** Replace the lowest-public-tier assumption with a fee ladder (0, 1.2/2.4, 4.8, 10 bps) and with any observable proxy for negotiated tiers, on both venues. **Threshold:** aggregate net profit stays positive under the double-lowest-public-taker scenario on both venues, and mean net per-sequence profit stays positive in at least 3 of the final 4 quarters of the sample. **Action:** fail -> the headline is a fee-assumption artefact, not measured profit.
- **F5 Frozen forward-window replication.** Apply the frozen Table 2 thresholds, unchanged, to Binance and Kraken spot trades from 2023-06 through 2026-09. **Threshold:** mean net per-sequence profit > 0 after the double-fee model and detected OWA share >= 0.30 percent of volume on Binance. **Action:** fail -> the mechanism was real historically but is now competed away; downgrade to "measurement of a decayed edge" and reject any tradability reading.
- **F6 Timestamp-permutation placebo.** Shuffle operation timestamps within 1-hour blocks on the same pairs to destroy true sequences while preserving marginal volume and price distributions, then re-run the matcher. **Threshold:** the real profit-dominance value in bins at or below 200 ms on Binance exceeds the 95th percentile of 1,000 shuffled runs. **Action:** fail -> the "fast sequences are profitable" pattern is a matching artefact.
- **F7 Executable-depth gate.** With L2 snapshots, replay each detected sequence against the book and charge observed spread plus depth-based slippage on both legs. **Threshold:** at least 90 percent of sequences remain executable within 10 bps of the quoted discrepancy, and aggregate net profit stays positive. **Action:** fail -> quoted-price returns are not executable returns; all profit figures become `data gap`.
- **F8 Matcher-specificity check.** Run the identical matcher for triangular arbitrage (three legs, closed loop) on the same data. **Threshold:** OWA sequence count exceeds triangular count by at least 3x, matching the source's qualitative claim that triangular is considerably rarer (Section 2.2). **Action:** fail -> OWA prevalence is an artefact of the two-leg search-space restriction.
- **F9 Third-venue generalization.** Apply the frozen thresholds to one further centralized exchange with free historical trade data (for example Coinbase or Bitstamp). **Threshold:** net per-sequence profit has the same sign and detected share is within one order of magnitude of Kraken's 0.13 percent. **Action:** fail -> venue-specific artefact; the mechanism claim is restricted to the two measured venues.
- **F10 Anchor-set sensitivity.** Relax the anchor/non-anchor constraint to allow anchor-to-anchor intermediation and re-run. **Threshold:** total detected sequences and volume change by less than 25 percent. **Action:** fail -> prevalence is an artefact of the search-space restriction rather than a property of the market.
- **F11 Concentration and sub-period stability.** **Threshold:** no single intermediary coin contributes more than 30 percent of net profit, and at least 3 of 4 equal sub-periods are net positive under the double-fee scenario. **Action:** fail -> the result is one coin or one episode; report it as episodic rather than structural.
- **F12 Capacity ceiling.** From the observed per-sequence size distribution, compute the capital that could have been deployed at 80 percent participation in the matched sequences, and compare its annualised net profit against plausible fixed costs (infrastructure, colocation, engineering). **Threshold:** the capacity-bounded annual net exceeds those fixed costs by at least 2x in the final sample year. **Action:** fail -> the finding stands only as a measurement; it is not, and was not, an investable strategy.

**No-retuning rule:** from 2026-10-01 forward, freeze the Table 2 thresholds (quantity window +/-0.1 bps; latency caps 400 / 200 / 1000 / 2000 ms; initial windows +/-10 bps, +/-40 bps and 2 s), the 2-second direct-leg VWAP reference window, the 1-minute VWAP mark-to-market, the anchor/non-anchor definitions, the "sequence must end in a taker order" rule, the best-price-conversion tie-break, the lowest-public-fee-tier assumption, the 2023-05 sample end for in-sample claims, and every F1-F12 threshold. Any change invalidates the corresponding gate and requires a fresh record.

## Crypto portability

`direct` - the source is crypto-native end to end: spot trading pairs on two centralized cryptocurrency exchanges, anchor and stable-coin taxonomy, crypto-specific intermediary coins (ADA, DOGE, SOL, LUNA, XRP) and a mechanism that lives entirely inside crypto market microstructure. Nothing needs to be ported from traditional assets to state the hypothesis.

Portability caveats that remain inside crypto: the study is **spot-only**, so none of the perpetual-specific frictions that dominate our other records - funding payments, mark/index price divergence, liquidation cascades, leverage - are present or tested; porting the same measurement to perpetual cross-pair or cross-venue routes would be `adapted`, not `direct`. The sample is single-venue-pair (Binance, Kraken) with no cross-venue, no cross-exchange and no on-chain component, and it ends in May 2023, before the current market structure. Venue fragmentation, custody and withdrawal risk, API rate limits and trading halts are untouched. The paper also does not claim its findings transfer to any venue other than the two it measured. Crypto portability is not authorization to trade.

## Limitations

- `underspecified`: entry threshold, monitoring cadence, position sizing, exit rule, risk limits, fill model, timestamp precision and timezone convention for a tradable reading of OWA; the paper proposes no trading rule at all.
- `data gap`: no order-book, depth, spread, slippage, market-impact, participation or capacity model; no observed fee attribute in the data, so all costs are assumed schedules; no point-in-time, look-ahead or survivorship audit; no released code, data set, repository, manifest, seed or data-availability statement; no peer review, no journal reference, no competing-interest statement.
- `not independently reproduced`: every count, dollar figure, basis-point figure and latency figure in this record is the source's own; only arithmetic identities were checked (0.94 percent of $20.16 T = $189.5 B against the printed ~$190 B; 0.13 percent of $1.25 T = $1.63 B against $1.7 B; $19.4 M / $31.2 M = 62.2 percent against the printed 62.1 percent; $590 k / $975 k = 60.5 percent; $44.6 M / $73.3 M = 60.8 percent against 60.9 percent; 127 M x $0.15 + 275 M x $0.04 = $30.4 M against the printed $31.2 M using the 68.3 percent taker/taker sequence share).
- `unproven`: whether the discrepancy is executable at the observed sizes, whether it survives depth and spread, whether it persists after May 2023, whether it generalizes to a third venue, and whether the detector's precision is high enough for the prevalence figure to mean what it appears to mean.
- Source-quality limits: arXiv preprint v1 only, single-version, no journal reference, no released artefact, a detector with no ground truth whose thresholds were tuned on profitability, and an explicit statement that unprofitable and failed attempts cannot be recovered.
- Internal ambiguity kept visible rather than repaired: the sample-start months (Section 3 prose versus Table 1) and the dataset-volume attribution (Introduction candidate sets versus Sections 5-6 selected sets). See `contested: true` with two recorded contradictions.
- Scope: two spot venues, data ending May 2023, one narrow two-leg definition of arbitrage, and a census methodology with no uncertainty quantification anywhere.
- This record is research material. It is not a validated signal, not a backtest we ran, and not a recommendation.
