## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

- The paper is a **controlled text-layer comparison inside the narrative asset-pricing pipeline of Bybee, Kelly and Su (2023)**, which the source adopts "without change from the shock construction onward" (Section 2.1). The stated economic premise is that "systematic risk can be reflected in the language of financial news" (Section 1, citing Bybee et al. 2023): a shock to attention around a narrative (oil supply, Fed rates, technology) affects different stocks differently, producing a cross-section of stock exposures to that narrative, from which a small number of latent systematic risk factors can be estimated.
- **Pipeline as printed (Figure 1 caption and Sections 3.1-3.7):** 394,661 FNSPID news articles (2015-2023) are mapped into daily narrative attention; attention shocks are `z_t = theta_t - mean(theta_{t-5:t-1})` (deviation of daily topic attention from its trailing five-day mean); weekend and holiday shocks are carried forward to the next trading day; rolling stock-level covariances between each stock's excess return and each narrative shock give one characteristic instrument per narrative (252-trading-day lookback, 60-trading-day exponential half-life in the baseline); with `L = 60` narratives plus an intercept the instrument vector has 61 entries; **Sparse IPCA** (Kelly, Pruitt and Su 2019) maps these instruments into stock-specific loadings on `K = 3` common factors through a group-sparse loading map `Gamma`; an out-of-sample minimum-variance-equity tangency portfolio `w = Sigma_f^{-1} mu_f` in factor space is then evaluated over the 41 evaluation months.
- **The comparison:** the **LDA** text layer (bag-of-words, collapsed Gibbs sampling, `tomotopy`, 800 iterations, seed 0) is replaced by a **frozen sentence transformer** (`paraphrase-MiniLM-L3-v2`, never fine-tuned) whose passage embeddings are clustered with mini-batch k-means into the same `L = 60` topics and assigned softly with temperature `tau = 0.07`. Both branches read the same articles, share the same 15,000-term vocabulary, produce the same output shapes (daily attention `theta` summing to one), and feed the identical downstream financial machinery (Sections 3.2-3.4). The stated rationale is that contextual encoders let "the same word receives different representations in different sentences", so narratives with similar meaning but different wording are grouped, which a bag-of-words model cannot do (Sections 1 and 2.2). The encoder is deliberately frozen so that neither text layer observes returns (Section 3.3).
- **Two declared exploratory extensions (Sections 3.5-3.6, reported separately in Table 1):** (i) **spherical k-means**, which rescales every cluster centre to the unit sphere so that the centroid update matches the cosine criterion implied by unit-length embeddings, plus a **sparse-soft c-TF-IDF term head** that only changes how topics are *described* (labelled terms and NPMI) and not the daily attention that reaches pricing; (ii) **multi-horizon IPCA**, which concatenates covariance instruments at half-lives `H in {20, 60, 120}` trading days and imposes the group penalty jointly across a narrative's three horizon rows, using excess rather than total returns.
- **Declared boundary of the claim:** the source states throughout that "the available tests do not establish outperformance" (Abstract, Section 3.7, Section 4, Section 6) and that improved labels and improved portfolio performance are separate findings (Section 4).

### Research interpretation

- **Mechanism class:** a **risk-based narrative-premium hypothesis**, not an arbitrage. The falsifiable claim is that *unexpected daily shifts in news attention to a narrative carry covariance information that survives a 252-day exponential window, prices the US equity cross-section through three latent factors, and can be held in a tangency portfolio that earns a positive monthly Sharpe net of nothing*. If attention shocks carry no covariance information, the instruments collapse to noise and the factor returns should be indistinguishable from an equal-weight market portfolio.
- **Component roles (normalised):**
  - Text layer / signal source: daily topic-attention vector `theta_t in R^60` (LDA or frozen sentence transformer).
  - Primary signal: the trailing-5-day attention shock `z_t`.
  - Exposure construction: trailing 252-day exponentially weighted stock-narrative covariances (half-life 60 baseline; 20/60/120 in the extension).
  - Selection: group-sparse `Gamma` (group lasso over the 60 narrative blocks, unpenalised intercept).
  - Portfolio: factor-space tangency weights, mapped back to stock weights through the ridge reconstruction `B (B'B + I_K)^{-1} w`.
  - Regime filter: **none** - the source has no explicit regime, volatility or trend filter component.
  - Risk management / sizing: **none printed** - no stop, no risk budget, no gross-exposure cap, no turnover control.
- **Do not assume every component contributes alpha:** Appendix B shows the topic count moves the headline Sharpe by 0.3178 and moves the LDA single-horizon arm by +0.8485 (research-computed), so text layer, topic count, covariance horizon and return definition are all load-bearing and must be ablated before any attribution.
- **Competing explanations the record must keep alive:** (a) the book is a large-cap momentum/size/value tilt rather than a narrative premium (the equal-weight market on the same universe prints 0.8166 total / 0.7164 excess); (b) specification selection on the 41-month window (the source admits it); (c) future-sample information in vocabulary, clustering and instrument scaling (the source admits it) and in a pretrained encoder whose training text overlaps the evaluation period (the source admits it); (d) label/coherence effects that never touch pricing (the source isolates them).
- **Risk management vs alpha:** every operational choice below that the source does not print is `research-proposed`, and every acceptance/failure cutoff is a `research-defined falsification threshold`.

## Signal

- **Formation timestamp:** daily attention is the term-count-weighted average of article topic weights across articles **by calendar day of publication**; shocks recorded on weekends or holidays are carried forward to the next trading day (Section 3.1). Intraday publication timestamps are absent - the source states in Section 4 that "Missing intraday article timestamps also prevent verification that each day's news would have been available before the trading decisions that a live strategy would require" (typographic apostrophe normalised to ASCII in this quotation). So the tradable timestamp of a shock is `data gap`, not "close of day d".
- **Lookback:** shock baseline = trailing 5 trading days of daily attention; covariance instruments = 252 trading days ending before the priced month, exponentially weighted with a 60-trading-day half-life (baseline) or the three half-lives `{20, 60, 120}` (extension), requiring at least 120 usable observations (Section 3.6); stock exposures in Figure 1 are labelled `trailing 252d`. Endpoints: the window "ending before the priced month" is explicit; whether the shock window endpoints are inclusive is `not stated in source`.
- **Long / short entry:** there is no entry trigger. Each evaluation month produces factor-space weights `w = Sigma_f^{-1} mu_f` from **training** factor returns and stock weights `B_{t+1} (B_{t+1}' B_{t+1} + I_K)^{-1} w`, so a "long" is a positive stock weight and a "short" is a negative stock weight of the same construction. Whether the resulting book is shortable in practice, and whether weights are constrained in sign or magnitude, is `not stated in source` (the objective printed in Equation 8 has no weight constraint).
- **Exit:** none printed - the position is re-formed monthly by the next month's refit/eval step. No stop, no take-profit, no maximum holding period (`stop`, `take profit` and `maximum holding` do not appear in the pinned text).
- **Holding period / rebalance cadence:** monthly evaluation over 41 consecutive months (September 2020 - January 2024); the financial model is re-estimated on an **expanding** sample every 12 months starting September 2020; within each estimation sample the last 25% of months are held out chronologically for penalty selection.
- **Parameters (all fixed values printed by the source):** `L = 60` topics (Appendix B sweeps `L in {50, 60, 70, 80}`); `K = 3` factors; vocabulary 15,000 terms = 7,467 words + 7,533 word pairs, built from 60,000 randomly sampled articles with seed 0, terms appearing in at least 10 sampled articles and at most 40% of the sample; article body capped at 4,000 characters before combination with the headline for LDA; the transformer receives paragraphs split into chunks of at most 200 words, chunks shorter than 8 words dropped, **at most three leading eligible chunks per article**, each truncated by the encoder to 128 subword tokens (about the first 110 words of running text); `1,183,874` passages result; LDA Gibbs 800 iterations seed 0; mini-batch k-means batch 4,096, 10 initialisations, seed 0; soft-assignment temperature `tau = 0.07`; spherical run converged after 24 of 25 permitted iterations at tolerance `1e-5` with mean passage-centre cosine 0.5915; sparse-soft head median decisiveness `kappa = 0.1013`; penalty search 12 values (single-horizon) or 8 values (multi-horizon), selection "maximizes validation portfolio Sharpe, preferring the largest penalty among scores within a tolerance of 5% of the absolute best score", then refits on the whole estimation sample; ridge term `I_K` in the factor-return reconstruction; penalty scaled as `lambda * N`.
- **Position sizing logic:** `data gap` - no risk budget, no volatility target, no leverage cap, no per-name cap, no turnover control. The 10%-volatility presentation plots are explicitly said to "rescale returns for display using an ex-post scale" and to be "not the returns of a prospectively risk-managed, cost-adjusted strategy" (Section 3.7).
- **Multi-timeframe dependencies:** single return horizon (one month, stated in Section 3.6: "the return horizon remains one month"); "multi-horizon" refers only to covariance decay rates, not to multiple trading horizons - the source states this explicitly to avoid the misreading.
- **Specification completeness:** `underspecified` for execution timestamp, weight constraints, shortability, delisting/missing-return treatment, tie handling between simultaneous shocks, and the paper's own universe rule (see Required data).

## Required data

- **Instrument / universe:** US common stocks. **The paper never prints a universe rule** - it reports only a CRSP return panel with monthly stock returns compounded from daily observations requiring at least 15 valid daily observations per month, giving 95,071 stock-month observations over 102 months (August 2015 - January 2024). The universe rule is `not stated in source` in the paper; the **same-source code README at the pinned commit** states it instead: CRSP `common shares on NYSE, AMEX and NASDAQ, the 1,000 largest stocks by June market capitalisation each year (kept for that calendar year), 2014-2024`, written to `data/crsp_daily_2014_2024.parquet` with columns `date, permno, ticker, ret, prc, shrout, me`. That README rule is a June-formation, calendar-year-held top-1000 construction; whether delisted names are retained after exit is `not stated in source`.
- **Venue / market type:** US equity cash market (CRSP via Wharton Research Data Services). No options, no futures, no perpetuals, no funding.
- **Text data:** **FNSPID** (Dong, Fan and Peng, arXiv:2402.06698, 2024), 394,661 retained articles dated January 2015 - December 2023; retained articles must have a valid date, stock ticker and article text, be at least 500 characters, and be de-duplicated by URL and by matching date+title (Section 3.1). The code README adds a per-day cap of 120 articles and the Hugging Face path `huggingface.co/datasets/Zihan1004/FNSPID` (`Stock_news/nasdaq_exteral_data.csv`, 23 GB streamed, about 2 GB kept) - a preprocessing choice **not printed in the paper**.
- **Fields:** daily stock returns, price and shares outstanding / market equity from CRSP; the Fama-French daily factor file (public, Kenneth R. French Data Library) supplies the risk-free rate; news article text, headline and publication date.
- **Timeframe:** daily news attention; daily stock returns compounded to monthly; monthly pricing and evaluation.
- **Point-in-time / availability:** **materially incomplete by the source's own admission** - vocabulary selection draws from the full news period rather than the initial estimation sample (Appendix A note), both text models are fitted on the full January 2015 - December 2023 corpus, instrument columns are divided by standard deviations computed over the full panel including evaluation months, and the frozen encoder's training text overlaps the evaluation sample (Sections 2.4, 3.1, 3.7, 5). Only the shocks (trailing 5 days), the covariance windows (ending before the priced month) and the financial refits are correctly lagged.
- **Missing-data:** monthly returns require at least 15 valid daily observations; covariance instruments require at least 120 usable observations; articles without a usable passage receive uniform weight `1/L` on every topic (Section 3.3); weekend/holiday shocks carried forward. No imputation rule is printed beyond these.
- **Cost / fee / spread / funding needs:** `not stated in source` - see Execution assumptions.
- **Access barrier:** CRSP is licensed and not redistributed; reproducing the pricing stages requires WRDS access (Section 7 and code README).

### Negative evidence

1. **The source disclaims its own headline.** The Abstract, Sections 3.7, 4 and 6 all state that the available tests do not establish outperformance; Section 4 says the findings "do not establish that the extensions outperform the original paper or deliver a cost-adjusted trading advantage."
2. **Every printed test fails to reject.** p = 0.22 (paired mean returns, ST vs LDA), p = 0.493 (paired, spherical vs preceding), p = 0.521 (Newey-West with 3 lags), and a 95% bootstrap interval `[-0.903, 1.682]` for the Sharpe difference that contains zero and is 2.585 wide against a point difference of 0.3729 (research-computed: 0.69 standard errors).
3. **The headline portfolio is barely risk-managed and deeply volatile:** raw annualised volatility 81.68%, maximum drawdown 62.23%; the published 10%-volatility plots are an **ex-post rescale** that the source explicitly says is not an implementable, cost-adjusted strategy.
4. **The simplest benchmark beats the narrative book in three of the four Table 1 rows.** Equal-weight market 0.8166 (total) beats LDA single-horizon -0.0810 and frozen-ST single-horizon 0.3059 outright, and equal-weight **excess** 0.7164 (Table B.2) beats frozen-ST multi-horizon 0.6605 - a like-for-like excess comparison the prose never makes (contradiction 2).
5. **Topic count alone can reverse the conclusion.** LDA single-horizon total moves from -0.0810 at L = 60 to 0.7675 at L = 80 (research-computed +0.8485) with the downstream pipeline unchanged, and the headline spherical row falls from 1.0334 to 0.7156 (research-computed -0.3178). Appendix B concludes "more topics do not help the pricing stage" and that neither the L effect nor the L = 80 layer gap is distinguishable from zero.
6. **The text-layer ranking is not stable across specifications.** At L = 60 single-horizon the transformer arm leads by 0.3869; at L = 80 single-horizon the printed points put LDA ahead by 0.0456, and Appendix B calls the two layers "indistinguishable" - compounded by the fact that every L = 80 transformer row uses a different clustering variant (contradiction 3).
7. **Coherence improvements are decoupled from pricing.** The sparse-soft term head raises NPMI by 0.0989 while the two variants' attention series agree to within `1 - 6e-14`; the source states the coherence gain "cannot account for any part of the difference in portfolio performance" and that higher coherence "does not necessarily indicate better asset-pricing signals".
8. **Selection on the evaluation window is admitted:** Section 2.4 - "Our own evaluation window is 41 months and our specifications were chosen after inspecting it" - with the Bailey and Lopez de Prado (2014) deflated-Sharpe reference cited in the same paragraph; Section 3.7 adds that the extension "was selected after inspecting the same 41 OOS months".
9. **The "out-of-sample" label is narrower than advertised:** vocabulary and clustering are built on the full 2015-2023 text sample and instrument columns are scaled by full-panel standard deviations that include evaluation months (Sections 3.1, 3.7, 5) - contradiction 1.
10. **Pretrained-encoder leakage is admitted:** Section 2.4 - "A pretrained encoder has seen text from a period that overlaps the evaluation sample... Our encoder does not meet that standard", referencing NoLBERT (Kakhbod and Li, 2025) as the standard it fails.
11. **Same-day tradability is unverifiable:** missing intraday article timestamps (Section 4) mean it cannot be shown that the day's news preceded the day's trading decisions.
12. **The controlled comparison is not controlled on text supply:** LDA reads the full cleaned article (body capped at 4,000 characters) while the transformer reads at most three passages truncated at about 110 words each (Section 3.4) - research-computed, 99.99% of articles hit that three-passage cap - and the term-ranking rule differs between branches, so the coherence comparison "mixes the effect of the topic assignment with the effect of the rule used to summarize it."
13. **Group sparsity does not bite:** all 60 narratives are retained at later refits for both transformer arms (34 only at the first refit), so the evidence does not support "a small set of priced topics" (Sections 3.7 and 4).
14. **No cost, turnover, leverage or capacity model of any kind** (Methods-level census above), so all four printed Sharpe ratios are gross while the book is a full cross-sectional weight vector re-formed monthly from an unconstrained tangency optimisation.
15. **Universe and survivorship are not stated in the paper at all**; the only rule available comes from the code README (top 1,000 by June market cap, held for the calendar year), and neither surface states how delistings, halts or the news-coverage filter enter - `data gap`, and the equal-weight "same universe" row inherits the same ambiguity.
16. **In-sample deterioration is visible in the appendix:** spherical ST in-sample Sharpe falls 1.34 -> 0.87 across refits at L = 80 while 77-80 of 80 narratives stay active and instruments grow 180 -> 240 on a fixed panel - the source's own "signature of a larger instrument set fitted to a fixed panel".
17. **Single seed, single topic count, single corpus, single market:** Section 4 states that the reported runs fix `k = 60` and the clustering seed rather than measuring sensitivity to either choice (the HTML extraction renders the symbol twice as `k = 60 k=60`, so the sentence is quoted only in paraphrase); the Gibbs sampler is a single draw (Section 2.1 notes a single fit is one posterior draw), and there is one news corpus (FNSPID) and one asset class.
18. **The coherence metric itself is contested by the source's own citation:** Hoyle et al. (2021) is cited for automated coherence agreeing poorly with human judgment "in exactly the regimes where competing models are usually compared"; the source therefore reports NPMI as descriptive only.
19. **Reproduction is gated on licensed data:** CRSP via WRDS is not redistributable (Section 7), so independent verification of the pricing stages requires institutional data access; the repository ships results and weights but not the panel.
20. **Source quality / publication status:** arXiv v1 preprint, no journal reference, no venue, no peer-review statement, cited as a working paper by its own project page and README; 0 stars, 0 forks, 0 issues on the code repository at read time (2026-09-29).

Pass = net excess Sharpe still exceeds the equal-weight excess market (0.7164) at 10 bps. Fail = REJECT as non-investable.

- **F10 - Availability gate (next-session tradability).** Rebuild daily attention using only articles with a verified timestamp before the prior close (or, if only dates exist, lag every shock by one full session - research-proposed). ## Crypto portability

**adapted** (performance in crypto: `unproven`).

- **Why not `direct`:** every empirical result in the pinned source is US equity cash-market evidence over August 2015 - January 2024 with an FNSPID news corpus; `crypto`, `Bitcoin` and `Ethereum` are not an experimental domain anywhere in the pinned text, and the CRSP/Fama-French inputs are equity-specific.
- **What plausibly ports:** the mechanism has no equity-only primitive - daily topic attention, trailing-5-day attention shocks, exponentially weighted stock-narrative covariances, group-sparse conditional factor loadings and a tangency portfolio can be written for any return panel. A monthly, cross-sectional, long/short-capable construction is directly representable on crypto spot or perpetual panels.
- **What does not port as stated:** (i) **FNSPID has no crypto coverage** - a crypto-native corpus with reliable timestamps is a new material data dependency; (ii) the equity calendar (weekends/holidays carried to the next trading day) is replaced by 24/7 sessions, which changes what a "daily" attention shock and a 252-day window mean; (iii) the `top-1000 by June market cap, held for the calendar year` universe has no analogue - crypto listing churn, delistings and survivorship are far more severe and must be re-specified (`research-proposed`); (iv) crypto adds funding, mark/index price, liquidation and venue fragmentation that the source never models (all cost/friction fields are `data gap`); (v) crypto spreads are wider, interacting directly with the missing cost model (F3); (vi) a monthly rebalance with unconstrained tangency weights in a market where borrow for the short leg is often unavailable is a harder executability problem than in US equities.
- **Crypto-specific risks to state explicitly:** spot vs perpetual basis and funding carry; mark/index vs last price at rebalance; 24/7 session and candle-boundary alignment; listing/delisting survivorship; venue fragmentation and depth; stablecoin quote-currency effects; custody and withdrawal constraints.
- Crypto portability is **not** authorisation to trade, and no crypto result exists for this source.
