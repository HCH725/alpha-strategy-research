## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

The instrument-level mechanism is the funding payment: a perpetual has no expiry, so a recurring payment between longs and shorts - longs pay when the contract trades above its reference, shorts pay when it trades below - substitutes for maturity convergence (Section 2). The paper's contribution is that **the quoted funding rate and the quoted basis are not sufficient statistics for a basis trade**, because they say nothing about what the *next* payment will be when the position is opened.

Two design features carry the argument (Sections 1, 3, 4):

1. **Funding timing / entry-time information.** A position opened immediately after a settlement faces three logically possible cases: the next payment itself is fixed at entry (`known payment`), part of the state that determines it is already accumulated at entry (`state-inherited`), or nothing beyond the published rule is known (`interval-sampled`). None of the seven venues delivers a known payment. **Only legacy Drift is state-inherited**, because its rate is computed from mark-price and oracle-price EMA-TWAPs that already contain pre-entry observations - although the protocol refreshes both TWAPs with post-entry prices before settlement, so amount and sign remain path-dependent. The other six build the premium input from observations realized during the coming interval.
2. **Mechanical bound / Layer 2.** All seven enforce a finite outer cap on the realized per-interval rate, so every venue is `bounded but uncertain`: magnitude bounded, sign unknown. No venue supplies a **signed payment floor** (a strictly positive lower bound on what one named side receives), and no venue occupies the "path-dependent and unbounded" cell either. The paper keeps three devices apart: an **inner deadband** clamps a term inside the formula (Binance, Bybit, OKX, Hyperliquid at +/-0.05% per 8-hour equivalent; Kalshi zeroes any rate below 0.01% absolute because it has no interest-rate leg), an **outer cap** clamps the realized rate itself, and a **signed payment floor** would guarantee a cash flow (absent everywhere).

A third layer is explicitly **descriptive only**: bound tightness, i.e. how close realized funding sits to the cap. The paper refuses to let tightness define the classes, so the classification depends on contract rules rather than price history.

The paper states its central relation without estimating it: `entry-time arbitrage discipline = f(funding-rule information at entry, execution costs, basis risk)`, written as a function because only a known payment yields a signed amount to net against costs and basis risk (Section 2).

Supporting source-reported findings: **cap prospectiveness** - dYdX's clamp factor changes only through on-chain governance with a 259,200-second (three-day) voting period (`prospective`), Binance, Bybit and OKX reserve real-time adjustment (`administratively adjustable`, with documented mid-interval changes), and Kalshi, Hyperliquid and legacy Drift are `unaudited` on this question; **architecture risk channels** (Table 3) separate price anchor, asset control, rule changes, execution and tail event across offshore CEX, regulated CEX and DeFi; **scale** (Figure 1, August 1-31, 2026 UTC, average daily one-sided USD notional of BTC/ETH/SOL perpetuals): Binance $19.27 billion, Bybit $12.14 billion, OKX $7.07 billion, Hyperliquid approximately $3.78 billion (reconstructed), dYdX $37.3 million, with Kalshi and Drift omitted; the **Drift incident** of April 1, 2026 cost approximately $296 million across collateral assets; and **regulatory change** - the CFTC approved KalshiEX's BTCPERP contract in May 2026 (release 9240-26), with gold and silver perpetuals following in September 2026 through Rule 40.2(a) self-certification.

### Research interpretation

Falsifiable hypotheses derived from the source's framework. Component roles are named so each can be ablated; nothing below is claimed as a source-asserted trading rule.

- **H1 (cross-venue equivalence is false - the paper's own claim, restated as a testable statement).** Regime = venue funding design class (state-inherited vs interval-sampled, plus cap prospectiveness). Signal = the quoted basis and quoted headline funding rate at entry, which the source says are identical in the comparison it constructs. Transfer = the realized next-interval funding payment. Risk/exit = none specified by the source; the paper lists forced-closure constraints (margin call, liquidation-engine trigger, funding-driven cash drain, rule or governance change) without giving a rule. Falsifiable form: for matched assets with the same quoted basis and headline funding, the distribution of the realized next funding payment differs across design classes, so the venue choice changes the trade even when the quotes match.
- **H2 (entry-time forecastability differs by class - research-proposed).** Under the source's interval-start convention, an entry-time-only forecaster (premium observed at entry plus any inherited TWAP state) has lower forecast error for the next payment on a state-inherited venue than on interval-sampled venues, because the latter start every calculation from post-entry observations. The source supplies the classification and explicitly concedes that its own relayed evidence (the companion paper's band) evaluates funding at the hourly update, which is *not* the next payment for a newly opened position - so H2 is untested.
- **H3 (quoted-bound reliability differs by cap process - research-proposed).** A bound quoted at entry is informative for settlement only where the venue cannot revise it before settlement. The source documents that Binance, Bybit and OKX can (with dated instances), that dYdX can only through a three-day governance process whose block alignment to an hourly funding boundary is unconfirmed, and that three venues were never audited. Falsifiable form: the incidence of settlements governed by a cap that changed with less than one funding interval of notice differs materially between the `prospective` and `administratively adjustable` labels.
- No component is assumed to add alpha; F1-F12 are required before any hypothesis above is retained.

## Signal

The source proposes **no trading signal, no entry, no exit and no holding rule and no sizing rule**. Everything below is `source-reported` unless explicitly labelled `research-proposed`.

- **Signal object 1 - design class per venue (fully specified as a classification, Table 1).** Columns read as: reference price, funding input, pre-entry state inherited, cap change prospective, funding constraint, taxonomy class. Normalized per venue:
  - **Binance** - internal index (spot-weighted) plus premium index; 5-second premium TWAP over the settlement interval; pre-entry state not inherited; cap not prospective; inner +/-0.05%, outer +/-0.75 x MMR for major pairs or +/-2% for other pairs, a breach shortens the next settlement to 1 hour; class `interval-sampled, bounded`.
  - **Bybit** - Bybit index (volume-weighted across the top 6 spot exchanges); minute premium TWAP whose weight rises near settlement; not inherited; not prospective; inner +/-0.05%, outer +/-min[(IMR - MMR) x 0.75, MMR]; class `interval-sampled, bounded`.
  - **OKX** - OKX index (at least 3 exchanges) plus impact-price premium; premium sampled through the period and finalized in the last minute; not inherited; not prospective; inner +/-0.05%, outer per-contract cap between +/-0.375% and +/-1%; class `interval-sampled, bounded`.
  - **Kalshi** - CF Benchmarks BRTI regulated-exchange index; TWAP of 1-minute premium candles over an 8-hour period; not inherited; cap-change process unaudited; deadband zeroes any rate below 0.01% in absolute value, outer +/-2% clamp; class `interval-sampled, bounded`.
  - **Hyperliquid** - stake-weighted median validator oracle (multi-CEX); 5-second premium averaged over the settlement hour; not inherited; unaudited; inner +/-0.05% per 8 hours, outer +/-4% per hour; class `interval-sampled, bounded`.
  - **Legacy Drift** - external oracle, funding uses mark and oracle EMA-TWAPs; **pre-entry state inherited**, refreshed with post-entry prices; unaudited on cap process; tier cap on the realized rate (BTC about +/-0.125% per hour); class `state-inherited, bounded`.
  - **dYdX v4** - post-entry impact-price premium samples, accrued from premium observed after entry; not inherited; cap change prospective (three-day governance vote); outer +/-6 x (IMR - MMR), tier-dependent, BTC-USD +/-4.8%, and it never binds in the relayed sample; class `interval-sampled, bounded`.
- **Signal object 2 - funding formulas transcribed by the source (fully specified).** OKX BTC-USDT-SWAP: `F = clamp( P_bar + clamp(0.01% - P_bar, -0.05%, +0.05%), -0.375%, +0.375% )` where `P_bar` is the average premium over the coming eight hours sampled after entry. Legacy Drift hourly: `f = (1/24) x (M - I_bar + I_bar/5000) / I_bar` with `M` the mark-price TWAP and `I_bar` the oracle-price TWAP (the `/5000` term is a fixed 0.02% offset). dYdX: the short receives one-eighth of the average premium sampled over the coming hour, read as an eight-hour rate and clamped at +/-4.8% for BTC-USD, which is six times the gap between the 2% initial and 1.2% maintenance margin fractions (BTC-USD margin fractions from the dYdX v4 Indexer API `/v4/perpetualMarkets`, retrieved September 30, 2026).
- **Signal object 3 - the worked example (fully specified but stylized).** Bitcoin index price $100,000, perpetual $100,100 (a 0.10% premium), entered immediately after a funding settlement: buy 10 BTC spot, short 10 BTC perpetual, a $1 million reference position; funding reported on that notional; execution costs set aside; the same 0.10% premium is treated as each venue's premium input and legacy Drift's market-maker receipt-scaling rule is omitted. Table 2 (payments to the short): OKX 8-hour interval, range -$3,750 to +$3,750, +$500 if the premium persists; legacy Drift 1-hour interval, range about -$1,250 to +$1,250 with the entry-state formula giving +$50, +$50 if it persists; dYdX 1-hour interval, range -$6,000 to +$6,000, +$125 if it persists. Table 2's note adds the 8-hour equivalents: about $500, $400 and $1,000 of receipts, and ranges of +/- $3,750, about +/- $10,000 and +/- $48,000 - explicitly **not** same-horizon yield comparisons. Under a persisting 0.10% premium the OKX inner clamp binds at `F = 0.10% - 0.05% = 0.05%` (a $500 receipt); under an average 0.50% discount `F = -0.45%` before the outer cap and `-0.375%` after (a $3,750 payment); any average premium from -0.04% to +0.06% leaves `F` on the 0.01% interest leg (a $100 receipt); a running 0.05% per eight hours is "about 55% on a simple annualized basis" and "describes the first path, not a cash flow that can be locked in"; in the sell-off path the hedged trade is $2,750 down before costs even if the premium closes by settlement.
- **Signal object 4 - the due-diligence checklist (Section 6, fully specified as questions, not orders).** (1) What funding cash flow is actually known at entry, and can the bound move before settlement? (2) What price anchors the contract and who controls the anchor? (3) Who controls collateral and privileged administrative functions? (4) What can force the position to close before convergence? (5) What execution frictions separate the quoted basis from realized carry?
- **Research-proposed operationalization (NOT source-reported).** To turn the framework into something testable we would (a) freeze a venue set and a documentation snapshot date, (b) label each venue's Layer-1 class and cap-prospectiveness from archived documentation, (c) rank candidate venues for a long-spot/short-perpetual basis trade by the entry-time width of the payment bound per unit notional per unit time and by cap prospectiveness, (d) prefer the tightest prospectively-capped venue, and (e) treat venues whose caps can move without notice as carrying an unbounded-on-notice residual. The choice of ranking statistic, of the "tightest" rule and of any bound on tolerable notice time is `research-proposed`; the source supplies none of them.

## Falsification plan

Every threshold below is a `research-defined falsification threshold`; every rule is `research-proposed`. F1-F12 must all pass before any hypothesis in this record is retained.

- **F1 - printed-value reproduction.** Recompute every Table 2 payment, range, 8-hour sum and the "about 55%" annualization from the printed formulas. **Fail:** any printed cell does not reproduce. **Action:** reject the record's numeric content and re-pin the source.
- **F2 - classification re-derivation.** On a frozen documentation snapshot date, independently re-derive every Table 1 cell for all seven venues from first-party documentation. **Fail:** any cell cannot be sourced, or two independent derivations disagree on a class. **Action:** mark that venue `data gap` and drop it from H1/H3 panels.
- **F3 - entry-time forecast differential (tests H2).** For each venue, forecast the next funding payment using only entry-time observables (premium at entry plus inherited state where it exists); compare root-mean-square error across design classes over at least 90 days. **Fail:** the state-inherited venue's error is not lower than the median of the interval-sampled venues. **Action:** drop H2 and treat the state-inherited/interval-sampled split as a documentation curiosity rather than a tradable property.
- **F4 - matched-basis panel (tests H1).** Build a matched panel of the same asset across venues at instants where quoted basis and quoted headline funding agree within 1 basis point; compare the distribution of realized next-interval funding payments across design classes with a block bootstrap. **Fail:** the design-class differences' 95% interval contains zero, or the dispersion is below 0.5 basis points per interval. **Action:** reject H1 as economically negligible even if directionally true.
- **F5 - quoted-bound reliability (tests H3).** Archive every cap/floor revision with its announcement time and effective time for 90 days. **Fail:** more than 5% of settlements in any quarter are governed by a cap that changed with less than one funding interval of notice, or cap revisions cannot be reconstructed at all. **Action:** reclassify "bounded at entry" as unverifiable and stop quoting bounds as entry-time facts.
- **F6 - cost ladder.** Run the long-spot/short-perpetual basis trade at 0/1/2/5/10 basis points per leg per round trip, including two-leg synchronization of one funding interval. **Fail:** net carry is non-positive at 2 basis points per leg on the median venue. **Action:** demote the whole cross-venue carry framing to research-only with no implementable edge.
- **F7 - bound tightness versus carry.** Reproduce the paper's own named extension: standardized cap utilization across all seven venues over a common window. **Fail:** venues with tighter utilization show no lower funding-payment dispersion. **Action:** drop Layer 3 from any decision use; keep it descriptive only, as the source does.
- **F8 - forced-closure realism.** Simulate margin calls, liquidation-engine triggers and funding cash drains on the short leg at the venue's stated IMR/MMR. **Fail:** more than 20% of simulated paths close before one settlement interval at the illustration's 10-BTC scale. **Action:** mark the holding-period claim `underspecified` and forbid any carry extrapolation across intervals.
- **F9 - venue-continuity gate.** Re-check that legacy Drift's design still exists in a live, documented protocol (Velocity). **Fail:** Velocity's funding mechanism differs from, or is undocumented relative to, the audited legacy Drift design. **Action:** remove the only state-inherited member, state that the taxonomy currently has one populated class, and re-open the classification.
