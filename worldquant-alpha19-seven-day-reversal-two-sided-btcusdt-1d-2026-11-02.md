## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

Coarse mean-reversion/momentum building blocks. The paper frames its universe coarsely: `one can think of alpha signals as based on mean-reversion or momentum`, where `a mean-reversion alpha has a sign opposite to the return on which it is based`. Alpha#19 is a 7-day reversal block: the sign of the 7-day close change, negated, scaled by a 250-bar returns-validity factor. The paper prints the formula and the delay-1 next-day class and nothing else about its behaviour. The paper ships no stop, target, or risk leg of any kind.

### Research interpretation

Seven-day close reversal, two-sided overall. Write the single-asset score exactly as the pinned code reduces (bit-exact equivalence verified this cycle — max absolute difference `0.0` over 2,362 live bars and 90 tape-C bars): `score[t] = -sign(close[t] − close[t-7]) × 2.0`, i.e. sign `= sign(close[t-7] − close[t])`. When close sits below its print seven bars ago (7-day slide — weakness), the book goes long the next open (fade expecting snap-back); when close sits above (7-day run-up — strength), the book goes short (fade expecting give-back). The `2.0` rank leg is dead warmup (250-bar validity gate, never a signal) — disclosed, never a signal. Unlike sibling worldquant-alpha7 (an adv20-gated 60-bar time-rank-scaled contrarian with a `-1.0` short default — a gated magnitude object reading volume), this rule never gates and never scales: every defined bar takes a side at fixed magnitude. Unlike sibling worldquant-alpha9 (a 1-day delta-sign streak ternary — a streak object), this rule reads a single 7-day span. Unlike sibling worldquant-alpha12 (a volume-signed 1-day product — a volume×move object), this rule reads volume never. The failure mode is plain: the live window flips 440 entries / 439 exits across 2,357 post-warmup bars (~0.19 flips per bar) with no guardrail and no cost awareness — calmer than siblings worldquant-alpha47 (~0.37/bar), worldquant-alpha52 (~0.26/bar) and worldquant-alpha41 (~0.94/bar), but still a naked 7-day flip with no smoothing, plus a 251-bar dead-warmup tax that consumes ~10% of BTC history — disclosed, not upgraded. Score magnitude (fixed `2.0`) is ignored (single-unit book), so dollar/point scale never sizes anything.

## Signal

Exact rule as pinned (any other series, anchor, window, divisor, negation, fill, frame, book, or tie treatment is a different, unpinned rule):

- Inputs (paper A.2): `close[t]` live on completed `1d` bars only, plus `close`-derived `returns[t]` for validity only (the rank leg collapses single-asset as pinned — values never move direction, finiteness only). `open[t]`, `high[t]`, `low[t]`, `volume[t]`, and `vwap[t]` are never read by any order-gating line, not even for validity. No adv/returns-level/market-cap/sector beyond the pinned legs, no cross-sectional rank, no second asset or timeframe is read by any order-gating line.
- Level (paper Appendix A lines 431–432, pinned impl lines 314–316, bit-exact single-asset reduction proven this cycle): `score[t] = -sign((close[t] − delay(close[t], 7)) + delta(close[t], 7)) × (1 + rank(1 + ts_sum(returns[t], 250)))` — undefined exactly when any read input is non-finite per the NaN-propagation rule (any missing bar in the 250-returns window, any non-finite close print, or the `returns[0]` seed makes the whole score NaN — deterministically flat, never a fabricated side), never seeded or backfilled. Note `(close − delay(close,7))` and `delta(close,7)` are the same 7-day change, so the sign leg is `-sign(2 × delta)` = `-sign(delta)` exactly.
- Direction mapping (source-grounded, fenced in Negative evidence 1): `score[t] > 0` → long signal; `score[t] < 0` → short signal; `score[t] == 0` → flat signal (fires only on the exact print `close[t] == close[t-7]` — the dead-constant tape proves this path prints all-flat with `-0.0`; 3 such post-warmup flats on the sine tape, zero across the full 2,612-bar live window, disclosed); non-finite → NaN → flat signal by record rule, never interpolated. Both sides fire from the paper's own sign convention — neither side is disabled. The entry fills at the next bar's open, one single unit, only if the book is not already on that side (see position rule).
- Exit: the first opposite-side or flat signal reverses-to or flattens the full unit at the next-bar open — flip-or-flatten is the sole exit path by construction (a long holding into a short signal reverses across the next open, counting one exit plus one entry; into a flat signal it closes flat). No stop-loss, no take-profit, no trailing, no time exit (the paper ships no risk leg — carried over exactly, disclosed in Provenance adaptation (4), never upgraded into a source claim).
- Direction: two-sided overall (7-day slide longs; 7-day run-up shorts; exact-tie or undefined prints flat).
- Position rule (predeclared researcher pin): single-unit long-or-short-or-flat; an already-positioned book treats same-side signal bars as no-ops (no pyramiding, no averaging, no scaling by move distance); flat-regime bars while already flat are no-ops. Re-entry re-arms on the next signal bar after a flat exit (no cooldown specified — explicitly none, not invented). Pre-first-signal bars (before the adopted warmup completes) and any NaN-score bars are flat by record rule.

## Limitations

- Naked 7-day flip with no guardrail: the live window flips 440 entries / 439 exits across 2,357 post-warmup bars (~0.19 flips per bar) — the record's principal risk is churn against house taker fees and modeled funding with no smoothing before the next print, by the carried-over paper design.
- Single-print span sensitivity: the sign reads two closes seven bars apart with no averaging — single close wicks swing the sign bar to bar — disclosed, not smoothed.
- Dead-warmup tax: the 250-returns rank leg contributes nothing to direction yet withholds the first 251 bars (~10% of BTC history) — disclosed, not removed, because the paper prints it and the pinned code evaluates it.
- Fixed-magnitude decoration: the `2.0` scale never changes direction under the single-unit book (magnitude only) — disclosed, not repurposed into sizing.
- Next-open latency idealization: source-native delay-1 assumes trading `on the day subsequent`; any live gap between the prior close print and the realized open fill differs by construction — disclosed, research-only.
- Performance evidence is absent: nothing here is calibrated, fitted, or tuned to any backtest.
- Author scope: the paper presents production formulaic alphas as research objects, not a vetted retail trading system — this record is research-only and claims no live suitability.
