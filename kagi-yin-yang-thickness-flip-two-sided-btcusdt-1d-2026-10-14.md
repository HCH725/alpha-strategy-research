## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

Threshold-filtered trend speculation from Japan (mirror the StockCharts title): because a Kagi line reverses only on a minimum price change and changes thickness only on breaks of prior peaks/troughs, chop below the reversal amount leaves no mark on direction at all, and only certified breaks recolor the line. StockCharts converts that filtered chart into a two-action system — `buy on a new yang line, sell on a new yin line` — with thickness persistence (`remains in play until` the opposite break) as the holding rule. Quantower frames the same object as pure price-action trend highlighting (`tracks pure price movement and reversals to highlight trends`, `full time-independent and based on price action only`).

### Research interpretation

Thickness-gated reversal capture on a single 24/7 instrument, both sides coded. Unlike the pool's Three-Line-Break flip (which certifies reversals through 3+-line runs and fences alternation as non-trading state), this system carries no run gate and no alternation fence — selectivity comes solely from the fixed 4% threshold plus the peak/trough memory: a 3.9% V prints nothing at all, while a certified break recolors the book immediately. Unlike direction-following Kagi readings (long while the line points up), this system can hold long through a down reversal that fails to break the prior trough, and stays short through an up reversal that fails to break the prior peak — the bet is that thickness transitions on daily BTCUSDT precede multi-day directional drift often enough to survive sub-threshold whipsaw, with the threshold plus the ordered peak/trough memory as the sole noise defence — and with the sources' complete absence of any risk leg inherited undisguised (see Negative evidence).

## Signal

Exact rule as pinned (R = 0.04 fixed, strict `>` throughout, close-only — any other reversal value, tolerance, price field, filter, or stop is a different, unpinned rule):

- Notation: completed daily closes `C[i]`; forming line with direction `d ∈ {up, down}`, anchor `A` (running extreme close in the travel direction), thickness `T ∈ {yang, yin}`; stored `P` (prior peak: extreme close of the last completed up-line) and `Tr` (prior trough: extreme close of the last completed down-line), each undefined until its first completion.
- Seeding (pinned code, close-only normalization): anchor at `C[0]`; direction set by the first strict close move (`C > prev` → up, `C < prev` → down; equal closes extend nothing and scanning continues). First-line thickness convention (pinned code `startAs`): first up-line starts yang, first down-line starts yin. Position stays flat until the first thickness CHANGE (the initial assignment is continuity, never a signal — declared record rule).
- Per completed close `C[i]` in strict time order (three outcomes, pinned):
  - Continuation iff moving with direction beyond the anchor (up: `C[i] > A`; down: `C[i] < A`, both strict): `A = C[i]`; then thickness check — up-line with `T == yin` and `C[i] > P` (strict) → `T = yang`, BUY signal; down-line with `T == yang` and `C[i] < Tr` (strict) → `T = yin`, SELL signal; otherwise thickness unchanged.
  - Reversal iff contra move exceeds the fixed threshold (`|C[i] − A| > 0.04 × A`, strict, measured from the anchor exactly as the S&P 1951→1931 example measures from the extreme): complete the current line at `A` (completed up-line extreme `→ P = A`; completed down-line extreme `→ Tr = A`); open the opposite line from `A` with close `C[i]` and anchor `C[i]`, inheriting current thickness; then the pinned immediate check — new down-line with `C[i] < Tr` (strict) → `T = yin` (SELL signal iff previously yang); new up-line with `C[i] > P` (strict) → `T = yang` (BUY signal iff previously yin).
  - Otherwise (contra move within threshold, or equal close): explicit no-op — no line update, no thickness change, no signal.
- Position target (StockCharts signal pair, wired to the flip book): at the close of each completed bar `i` (post-warmup), the target for the next bar is long iff this close printed a thin→thick transition; short iff it printed a thick→thin transition; else unchanged (hold current side; flat only before the first-ever transition). Orders fire for the next bar's open: flat→long buys at next open on thin→thick; flat→short sells at next open on thick→thin; long-held + thick→thin flips full long to full short in one step at next open; short-held + thin→thick flips full short to full long in one step at next open. Same-direction continuation closes and sub-threshold moves are ignored (single-unit, no adds — declared port of one-event-per-bar-boundary). At most one order event per bar boundary; no intrabar evaluation, no intrabar orders, no partials.
- Mutual exclusion (proven, not assumed): both transitions on one close would require `C[i] > P` and `C[i] < Tr` simultaneously with `Tr ≤ P` — the ordering invariant holds by construction (a completed down-line starts at the prevailing peak and falls, so `Tr ≤ P`; a completed up-line starts at the prevailing trough and rises, so `P ≥ Tr`), hence impossible; the reversal branch assigns a single `changeTo`. Both legs can never fire on the same close and no priority ruling is owed.
- Degenerate tape (proven, not assumed): a zero-range history (`C` constant) never satisfies any strict inequality, so no line, no peak/trough, no transition, and no signal ever prints; the flat-until-first-transition state needs no extra guard.
- Risk legs (declared researcher posture, matching all three rule sources' shipped absence of risk): no stop-loss, no take-profit, no trailing, no time exit — the sole position-changing paths are thickness transitions. A vertical adverse move on/after the entry bar is held in full until the opposite transition prints. This is the coded trade, disclosed, not a tunable parameter here.
- Direction: two-sided flip system, always-in-market after the first signal, flat before it by construction.
