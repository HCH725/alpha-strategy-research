## Economic mechanism

### Source-reported

Dual-confirmation dip-reclaim reversal: the author’s prose states the whole thesis in two sentences — the strategy sells when price is above the upper Bollinger Band and buys when below the lower band, but it “only triggers when both the RSI and the Bollinger Band indicators are at the same time in a overbought or oversold condition.” The unconventional calibration is the point: a fast RSI(6) read against the neutral 50 line acts as a momentum-direction filter (not an overbought/oversold detector), while a very slow 200-bar ±2σ envelope acts as long-horizon price structure (not a short-term volatility envelope). A long prints only when momentum turns up through neutral on the exact bar price reclaims the lower rail from below — dip-buying with same-bar momentum confirmation; the short leg mirrors at the upper rail. The v1.1 note records that widening the basis to 200 periods produced fewer but more relevant opportunities.

### Research interpretation

Edge-triggered two-sided reversal system on a joint event, always-in-position after the first signal. Unlike single-trigger band systems (upper-breakout, dip-reclaim, midline-exit) where one edge moves the book, here two edges must coincide on the same bar — an RSI-50 momentum turn AND a band-rail reclaim — so each leg is rarer and more selective than either leg alone; chop that crosses one series without the other prints nothing. Unlike level-gated systems (RSI-above-50 permission, DMI-spread permission) where a state enables a second trigger, both conditions here are edges, so there is no “armed” state that persists across bars: the opportunity exists for exactly one bar. Unlike the always-live position-state machines (PPO-above-signal, CMF-velocity-sign), the trigger is the coincidence, never the state — a bar with RSI above 50 and price above the lower rail but no fresh cross on either series holds. The 200-bar basis moves glacially on daily bars, so the rails are regime structure and entries cluster where fast momentum turns align with slow-structure touches; the price of the always-in reversal posture is that every adverse excursion is held to the opposite joint print, which in trendless chop arrives quickly (whipsaw pairs) and in trends arrives late. The bet is on confirmed dip-reclaim persistence at the slow-structure scale, never on a breakout, squeeze, level, calendar, or volume print.
